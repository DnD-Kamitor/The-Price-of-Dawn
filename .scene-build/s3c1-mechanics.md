# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: Rules Reference & Mechanics

<details><summary>🎲 Movement & Difficult Terrain</summary>

**Crowd as difficult terrain:**
- Costs 2ft of movement per 1ft traveled
- Crowd depth: 30ft (full alley width, no passable edge)
- Alley width at Chandler's Block: 15ft
- Players attempting to hug an alley wall still move through crowd (no clear path at edges)

**Side lane — flanking route:**
- Branches north before the crowd begins (before players enter the crowd)
- Width: 8ft
- Obstruction: crates and stacked goods along east wall reduce effective width
- DC 12 Perception or Investigation to spot from south alley approach
- DC 10 Perception if player explicitly describes scanning the alley
- Automatic if player asks "is there any way around this?"
- DC 10 Acrobatics to move at full speed (narrow passage, uneven footing)
- Normal movement is possible without check — just costs double movement for that section
- Route exits at the building's north side; Isolde's door is accessible from there

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The side lane is not hidden. It is a physical feature of the alley. Players who look for it find it. Do not require them to think of it specifically — if a player describes their character looking for options, give them the lane on a DC 10. The DC 12 is for passive notice while walking straight toward the crowd.

The flanking route bypasses the crowd problem entirely. It does not solve the Senna problem — Senna is still in the alley when players emerge on the other side. Players who flank and don't engage Senna have chosen to leave that thread open.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎲 De-escalation DC Table</summary>

**DC 15 — Generic appeal (empathy, authority, urgency):**
Senna has heard all of this. "We understand your pain." "The situation is being addressed." "We're on your side." These register as more of what she has been told for two years. They do not reach her. DC 15 is functionally a wall — it's possible but costs a strong roll and leaves her unmoved at the level of engagement.

**DC 12 — After establishing genuine connection:**
Requires one of:
- Player names her daughters (Nia and Brek) specifically, by name
- Player acknowledges the specific fear that the ritual might not work even if the Dawnborn die
Either of these drops the DC because Senna is no longer performing for a stranger — she is talking to someone who knows something real about her situation.

**DC 10 — After both connection points are established:**
Player has named the daughters AND acknowledged the specific fear. Senna is no longer arguing. The Persuasion check at this point is essentially a formality — she is already deciding to step back. A natural 1 might mean she asks one more hard question before she does.

**No roll required:**
If a player says something true that Senna has not heard before — something that isn't in the social playbook — and it lands in the right place, GM may waive the roll entirely. Examples:
- "We found out someone has been suppressing the research that might save your daughters. Three years. We're going upstairs because of that."
- "We don't know if the ritual will work even if the Dawnborn die. That's why we're here."
Either of these is true, specific, and addresses Senna's actual situation. She didn't know she could ask for it in those terms. No roll needed.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The DC system here is a guideline, not a script. If players engage Senna with genuine specificity and their character would convincingly know the right things to say, don't make them roll. The roll is for when the outcome is uncertain. If they've done the work, the outcome isn't uncertain — it's just Senna deciding.

The daughters' names are on the back of the placard. This is player-accessible information. Any player who approaches Senna and looks at her placard can read the reverse. The GM should not hide this or make them work for it. It is there. The emotional weight comes from *using* the information, not from obtaining it.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎲 What Isolde Knows About the Crowd</summary>

Isolde has been watching from the second-floor window since the crowd arrived. She has not come down. She recognizes Senna.

If players reach Isolde and ask about the crowd:
> "She comes to the Dawnhall sometimes. The one at the front. She lost her husband two years ago." *(pause)* "She has two daughters. I don't know their ages."

If players tell Isolde the daughters' names:
> She is quiet for a moment.
> "She wrote their names somewhere, didn't she." *(not a question)* "I couldn't read it from up here."

If players ask why Isolde didn't come down:
> "Because if I come down, I have to tell her I don't have an answer yet. And I can't —" *(She stops.)* "I'm very close. Three years of close. I need to be certain before I tell anyone."

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Isolde and Senna have never spoken. They are two people in the same building with the same problem and no awareness of how much they need each other. This is a thread players can pull — or leave. If players connect them later (bring Senna's daughters' names to Isolde, or bring Isolde's work to Senna), it changes what both of them are willing to do.

This is a Session 4-5 opportunity, not a Session 3 requirement. Note it and let it wait.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎲 Crowd Escalation: Combat Rules Addendum</summary>

If players choose overt violence (attacking a crowd member, drawing weapons and advancing, shoving with clear aggressive intent):

**Escalation trigger:** First round of visible combat.
**Required check:** DC 10 Persuasion from any party member (free action, uses their reaction).
- Success: the crowd backs away. Difficult terrain continues. No escalation.
- Failure: crowd walls off. Impassable this round. Players can spend a full action forcing through — no other actions.

**Subsequent rounds:** DC increases by 2 per round of continued violence (DC 10, 12, 14...).

**If players attack a crowd member directly:**
- Crowd member: Commoner stat block (1 hp, AC 10, unarmed). They are not combatants.
- DC 15 Persuasion required immediately or the crowd becomes a mob:
  - Mob rules: difficult terrain becomes impassable until crowd disperses
  - Crowd takes 1d4 rounds to disperse (they are not trained fighters; they will flee)
  - Senna backs to crowd's far edge; does not engage; watches with expression players will remember
  - Davan Crel closes his notebook, stands, and leaves the café

**Senna's response to violence:**
She steps back. She does not engage. She does not call the crowd to fight. She watches, flat-faced, and says, once:
> "I told them this was going to happen."

She means: *not you specifically. The city. Someone was always going to do this eventually.*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The goal is not to make combat impossible — it's to make it clear what combat costs before players commit. If they choose it anyway, run it clean and let the narrative consequences follow. Do not add unexpected mechanical penalties. What you told them would happen: happens.
:::

</details>
