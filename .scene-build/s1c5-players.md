## Read-Aloud — Exiting the Dawnhall

*Read this as the last player steps through the Dawnhall door and it closes behind them.*

---

> The door closes. The warmth cuts off.
>
> Crestwick Lane stretches south. Two amber lanterns on iron hooks throw small overlapping circles of yellow light across the cobblestones. Between those circles: half-dark. Above: the city's permanent dusk, neither night nor day, the grey twilight that has been Varenhold's sky for fifty years.
>
> Fifty feet south, at the mouth of Ashring Alley, a man in dark robes is standing. He has not moved since the door opened. He is not blocking the lane. He gave you the option to go around. He can see from here that you have seen him.
>
> He waited. He has been waiting long enough for his breathing to be unhurried.
>
> He does not come toward you.

---

## Read-Aloud — "You Have No Idea"

*This is the first thing Edoran says when players are within range — approximately ten feet. Deliver it quietly, without buildup or dramatic pause beforehand. It is an observation, not a threat.*

---

*He watches you cross the fifty feet without moving. When you are close enough to see his face — grey at the temples, the particular weathering of a man who has been outside at odd hours for decades — he speaks. Quietly.*

> "You have no idea what you are looking for."

*He is not angry. He is not performing. He says it the way you say something that is true and precisely true and that you have been thinking about for a long time.*

*He waits.*

---

## Read-Aloud — The Living Conduit Moment

*Deliver this as part of Beat 4 — after players have shared what they found, after the calibration beat has run.*

---

> "You found the phrase 'living conduit.' You think it's a mechanism. It's not a mechanism."
>
> *He pauses. The pause is deliberate.*
>
> "It's ten people. Ten people who have been carrying something for fifty years that was supposed to have lasted a single night. You found Sera Voss tonight. Now you know two things simultaneously: what she is, and what she's chosen. Those two things together are the whole question."

*He is watching you to see if you follow. He is not testing you. He is calibrating how much you already understand.*

---

## Read-Aloud — "She Didn't" (Conditional)

*Use this only if a player has asked the daughter question — any version of: why didn't the people who died of grey sickness get a choice, or why didn't his daughter get to choose, or any question that lands on the asymmetry between the Dawnborn's consent and the deaths grey sickness has taken without asking.*

---

*He stops. Not the patient stillness he has had through this conversation — something different. This is the stillness of a man who has just heard the thing he carries said aloud by someone who is not him.*

*He does not answer immediately.*

*The lane is quiet. The amber lanterns do not move. Somewhere in the city two streets over, a cart. The sound of forty people in the Dawnhall, muffled through the door you closed behind you.*

*Then:*

> "She didn't."

*He does not add anything. He looks at you — or slightly past you, at the particular middle distance of someone whose attention has gone somewhere internal — and says nothing further.*

*The two words sit in the lane.*

---

## Read-Aloud — The Exit

*This is the scene's last moment. Deliver it after Edoran's exit line. Let it be the end.*

---

> "The Restorers will not trouble you again tonight."
>
> *He steps aside. Not backward — to the side, clearing the lane the way you clear a doorway for someone, clean and without ceremony.*
>
> *The south lane is open.*
>
> *He does not watch you go.*

---

## Session 1 Closing Paragraph

*Read this after Edoran is no longer in the lane — after players have moved away, or after the moment has settled. This is the GM speaking directly to the players, not a character. Read it slowly.*

---

> You have two documents you cannot fully decode. A man who built a movement on grief. A woman who serves soup and has chosen to die.
>
> You've been hired to investigate a fifty-year-old ritual failure. You know what the words mean now: *distributed sympathetic anchors, living conduit mechanism, the completion requires their release.* You know what those words mean.
>
> You still don't know what they mean.

*End Session 1.*

## Player Engagement Matrix

---

### The Action Player

This is a quiet scene. No fight, no chase — unless players make combat happen, and combat is the worst possible outcome for their information state. Some action players will feel the absence of something to do.

What to give them: frame Edoran as a problem that requires reading, not fighting. *"He has position and numbers and he hasn't moved. What do you do?"* Then: Insight checks, framed as active. *"You've been reading people in the field for years — what do you see in this one?"*

Also: the fifty-foot walk to him is not nothing. Action players who are paying attention will clock the zealots in the alley shadow, the unblocked western passage, his wet boots, his patient breathing. The observation is the action in this scene. Honor it as such.

The thing to give an action player: Edoran is not a fight. He is an assessment problem. He has been running a twenty-year operation with specific information advantages and specific force-doctrine decisions. Understanding him is the mission. Insight is the combat action here.

**When they try to make it a fight:** Let them try once. Let Edoran receive it with patience. Then have someone else at the table register that attacking this man in a quiet lane after they just left a room full of sick people is not the move they want on their record. Edoran does not escalate. The scene continues.

---

### The Investigation Player

Three open threads from this scene, each leading somewhere in Sessions 2-4.

**Thread 1: The Archive Has More**

He says it directly: *"The Archive has more than you've found."* He declines to say what. DC 15 Insight tells you he is withholding something specific, not guessing. He has been in the Archive for twenty years. Whatever he found: it is in Fragment 2 of the Notation Key, which you do not yet have. Session 4.

**Thread 2: "Tonight"**

His exit line is precise: *"The Restorers will not trouble you again tonight."* Not "ever." Not "again." Tonight. He is telling you there is more, later, without saying more about what more looks like. The word is a thread. Pull it in Session 2.

**Thread 3: The Grief Underneath**

DC 13 Insight gives you: his position was built on personal loss. Something happened. He will not volunteer it. His exit from the Auris priesthood twenty years ago is documented in district records if players go looking — a quiet departure, no stated reason. The mortality records from fourteen years ago are also accessible. A name: Mira Edoran, 17 years, grey sickness. The investigation player who goes looking will find the gap between that death and the founding of the Restorers, and the gap will be about two years, and that two years is where the theology came from.

---

### The Role-Play Player

The scene is built for them, but differently than Scene 4. Sera Voss gave more to questions about herself. Edoran gives more to questions that challenge his position on its own terms — not personal questions, but philosophical ones that start from where he starts.

What the role-play player needs to do: engage with the argument. Not agree with it — engage with it. Ask the question that is not on the investigation list: *"What would change your mind?"* or *"Is there a version of this where you're wrong?"* or *"When did you stop being afraid of what you're asking them to do?"*

He will answer these questions. Not easily — they are hard questions and he has not been asked them before, at least not by people who were genuinely asking rather than looking for a trap. He will answer them with the same precision he brings to everything, and the precision will be slightly less armored than usual, and players who are paying attention will hear what is underneath.

The crack is here for the role-play player who reaches it. *"She didn't."* Two words. Full scene.

**What they need from this scene:** An opponent who is real. Edoran is the most intellectually honest antagonist they will meet in this campaign. He deserves a real conversation. Give it to him.

---

### The Problem-Solver

The secondary objective for this scene is stated in the gm notes but can be felt here: get close enough to have a real conversation.

That is the win condition. Not agreeing with Edoran. Not finding his crack. Not extracting all his information. Just: approach and stay.

The problem-solver will want to know: what does winning look like? Winning looks like leaving this lane with the living conduit beat and the consent argument and the knowledge that there is more in the Archive. That is the information payload of this scene. It is available to any player who crosses fifty feet of cobblestone and stays.

The problem-solver who recognizes this will cross the fifty feet and stay. That is the correct solution.

**The harder version:** The problem-solver who realizes, mid-conversation, that the secondary objective of this scene is not information extraction — that Edoran is not a source to extract but a position to understand — and who stops trying to win the argument and starts trying to understand the argument instead. That player gets the most out of this scene. It requires stopping the extraction impulse. It is the hardest thing this player type is asked to do all session.

---

## Handling Silence After "You Have No Idea"

If the table goes quiet after the opening line — genuinely quiet, the specific silence of people who have just been told something they are trying to assess — do not fill it.

He does not fill it either. He has said the thing he opened with. He is waiting to see what kind of people he has in front of him.

If a player looks like they are about to speak but hasn't: wait. They are getting there.

If players ask *"What do you mean?"* — they are in the scene. Answer them as Edoran, directly:

> "I mean: you found documents. You found a woman who has made her peace. You have pieces. The pieces don't tell you what you're actually investigating, which is whether the Dawnborn are going to be asked or told. Those are different actions. They produce different ritual outcomes. The archive tells you this. You haven't reached that part yet."

The silence is not awkward. It is the scene doing its job. Sit in it.
