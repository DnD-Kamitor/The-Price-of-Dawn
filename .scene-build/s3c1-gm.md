# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: GM Execution Notes

<details><summary>🎬 Pacing: Keep It Moving</summary>

This scene should take **10–15 minutes** regardless of which path players choose.

The scene has one job: establish that the city is fracturing and that this fracture is personal, not statistical. Once players understand that (through Senna, through the crowd, through whatever path they take), the scene is done. Move to Isolde.

**Signs the scene is running long:**
- Players are negotiating with Senna in circles without reaching the crack
- Players are trying to "solve" the protest rather than engage with it
- Investigation of Davan Crel has become its own extended scene

**What to do:**
- If Senna negotiation has gone past three exchanges without progress: have someone in the crowd say Nia's name — another crowd member who knows Senna, who says *"Senna, Nia's waiting"* — and watch whether players respond to it.
- If Davan Crel investigation is extending: he gives his cover story competently and does not crack. Players can note him and move on. They will understand him fully in S3C5.
- If players are stuck on the flanking route trying to get through the crates quietly: it works. Give it to them. The point is that they're choosing to avoid the problem, not solve it. Let that choice be made cleanly.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**The scene ends when players are through the door.** All three paths reach the door. Don't let the approach become the whole session.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎬 The Secondary Objective: De-escalation</summary>

De-escalation is the secondary objective. It is not required. Both the flanking path and the forced passage path get players to Isolde's room.

**What de-escalation does:**
- Senna hears something true. She steps back. She says: *"If they die for this, I need to know they chose it."*
- The crowd disperses behind her, not as a scattered mob but as people who have been acknowledged.
- Senna appears in Session 5 at the Ashring plaza with forty people. They are witnesses, not protesters. Her presence stabilizes the plaza.

**What de-escalation does not do:**
- It does not solve the grey sickness.
- It does not stop Davan Crel's report.
- It does not make Isolde's research real or safe.

**How to reward it without overstating it:**
The reward is Senna's shift. The line: *"If they die for this, I need to know they chose it."* That's it. Don't editorialize. Don't have the crowd cheer. Senna says it once, quietly, and steps back. Players who heard it will carry it. It will mean something different in Session 5.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Senna arriving at consent-as-requirement on her own — through grief, not theology — is the scene's thematic payload. The players may not realize until later that Senna answered the campaign's central question in a Lowmark alley at mid-morning, before they had the language for it. That's fine. The payoff is deferred. Let it be.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎬 Forced Passage: Don't Punish, Follow Through</summary>

If players push through the crowd:
- Don't add hidden mechanical consequences they couldn't anticipate
- Don't have the crowd turn violent unless players strike first
- Don't have Senna curse them or become an enemy

**Do:**
- Run the phase checks as written (DC 10 Persuasion each phase; crowd escalation on failure)
- Have the window go dark as players reach the door
- Let Isolde's first line be: *"I heard the noise. I thought it was the Reckoning."*
- Apply the +1 difficulty to her first Transfer Apparatus check in S3C3

**The narrative consequence:**
Senna is still outside in S3C5 when the raid arrives. She is not hostile to players. She is watching. She wants to know if they're going to do the same thing again — push through the obstacle, get what they came for, leave the problem in the alley.

If players have already de-escalated her: she is there with forty people, stabilizing. If they pushed through her: she is there alone, or with five or six people who stayed, and her energy is different — not destructive, but watchful. She is testing whether this time is different.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The forced passage path is not a failure state. It is a choice with consequences. Players who make it may not understand the consequences until Session 5. That's the design. Trust it.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎬 Davan Crel: The Scene's Hidden Threat</summary>

Davan is this scene's invisible consequence.

**What players can know:** DC 13 Perception to spot him. DC 15 Insight to read him as professional. Cold tea is the tell — if players describe their character looking around the alley, give them the café, give them the man, give them the tea. Let them decide what to do.

**What players cannot change:** He has already sent the report. Isolde's location is in Harran Lecht's hands. The raid in S3C5 is in motion. Nothing players do to Davan in this scene prevents that. If they:
- Ignore him: fine. They learn why it mattered in S3C5.
- Confront him: he lies smoothly. He leaves when it's convenient. The raid still happens.
- Detain him: someone else already carried the message, or he sent it before players arrived. The raid still happens. (You can play this as: *he seems almost relieved to be detained; he's done what he came to do.*)
- Attack or incapacitate him: the raid still happens. Vyne is informed that players are actively interfering. Davan's absence is itself a report.

**The functional value of noticing him:**
Players who notice Davan Crel in this scene will understand something in S3C5 that players who didn't notice him will have to reconstruct: that the Amber Compact was watching long before Harran arrived. That the raid wasn't accidental or reactive. That it was planned, and the planning started here.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Don't tip Davan's hand. Let the cold tea sit there. If a player looks at him closely and asks about the tea, confirm it's cold. If they ask how long he's been here, he says "not long" — and that's a lie they can clock if they pushed for it. But don't push the scene toward him. He's there for players who are looking.

If no player notices him in this scene, that's correct. The raid in S3C5 will be more of a surprise. That's also correct.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎬 Senna in Session 5: What This Scene Determines</summary>

**If players de-escalated (Senna heard something true):**
- In S3C5 Ashring plaza, Senna arrives with forty people.
- They are not protesters. They are witnesses. They stand at the plaza edge and watch.
- Senna's presence tells the crowd that what's happening matters. Her credibility — dock worker, grieving parent, the woman who stood in Lowmark Alley and stepped back when someone said something real — keeps the plaza from tipping into violence when the pressure builds.
- If players recognize her and speak to her: *"I brought people to see. Whatever happens here — someone should see it."*

**If players flanked (Senna never knew who they were):**
- Senna appears in S3C5 as an unknown variable.
- She is there with her usual core group — fifteen, twenty people.
- She doesn't know the players. Players can approach her; she will respond based on how they approach.
- The crowd dynamic in S3C5 is uncertain in ways it wouldn't be if she knew them.

**If players forced through:**
- Senna is in S3C5 alone, or with five or six who stayed.
- She watches players with the specific attention of someone forming a revised opinion.
- She is not hostile. She is waiting.
- If players demonstrate in S3C5 that they are actually trying to give the Dawnborn a choice: *"So you learned something."* She doesn't say it gratefully. She says it like a verdict.

</details>

<details><summary>🎬 The Daughters' Names: How to Seed Them</summary>

Senna's placard reverse reads: *"My daughters' names are Nia and Brek. They are nine and six. I am not here for politics."*

This is player-accessible without prompting. Any player who approaches Senna and looks at her placard sees the reverse. The GM should not hide it or require a special action. It is there.

**How to make sure it lands:**
When players first approach Senna, describe the placard briefly:
> *"She's holding a placard at her side, the board face turned toward her hip. The crowd-facing side is large block letters. The back of the board has writing too — smaller, different hand, looks like charcoal."*

That's it. Don't say what it says unless a player asks or moves close enough. The information is available. Players who look for it find it. Players who don't look may never learn the daughters' names — and that's fine. The de-escalation still has other paths (acknowledging the ritual might not work). But players who do read the placard and use the names in conversation with Senna will get the best outcome, and they'll have earned it by paying attention.

**The payoff:**
In S4 or S5, when Lira Anwick's daughter is named, players who remember Nia and Brek will hear the echo. Two daughters. Two mothers. The same disease, opposite sides of the consent question. That structural parallel is the scene's long-game contribution to the campaign.

</details>
