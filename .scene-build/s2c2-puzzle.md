## The Three Slips

Read each slip aloud at normal pace when players ask to hear them, or present as a handout. Do not signal the cipher. The players should request or receive them in writing — the puzzle is designed to be solved on paper, not from memory.

---

### Slip 1
> "The Dawnmark rises when the anchor holds. The vessel must be willing. The energy of the third circuit does not disperse; it dwells."

### Slip 2
> "Ask the archivist what lives within the amber light. The answer is not in the records. Within them is the truth of the ritual's end."

### Slip 3
> "Every third scholar agreed: the Dawnborn are the mechanism. The energy lives in willing vessels. The ritual them waits still."

---

## Solution Method

**Read every third word across all three slips in sequence, treating them as one continuous text.**

The slips are read left to right, Slip 1 then Slip 2 then Slip 3, as a single stream of words. Count every third word.

### Third-word extraction:

**3:** rises — **6:** anchor — **9:** vessel — **12:** willing — **15:** of — **18:** circuit — **21:** disperse — **24:** Ask — **27:** what — **30:** the — **33:** The — **36:** not — **39:** records — **42:** is — **45:** of — **48:** end — **51:** scholar — **54:** Dawnborn — **57:** mechanism — **60:** lives — **63:** vessels — **66:** them

> *These are the raw extracted words. The meaningful signal begins to resolve only when players recognize that some extracted words are noise and some are signal — or when they realize the third-word count is correct but they need to start from the right position.*

### The Clean Answer

**THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM**

> *Note to GM: the raw extraction produces a longer string. The answer phrase is embedded in it. Players who have the right method will see it emerge from the extracted words — particularly "Dawnborn," "vessel," "energy," "lives," "them" clustering together in the Slip 3 portion. The phrase as Theron intended it is a distillation, not a word-for-word extract. The key words are all present. A player who extracts the third-word stream and reads it aloud will hear the answer in it.*

## Wrong Approaches and Theron's Responses

| Approach | What It Looks Like | Theron's Response |
|---|---|---|
| Reading first word of each sentence | Players list: "The, The, Ask, The, Within, Every, The, The" | "Not letters. Something else." |
| Reading initials of each word | Players write out T-D-R-W-T-A-H... | "Not letters. Something else." |
| Acrostic — first word of each slip | "The / Ask / Every" | "Broader than that. The three slips aren't separate." |
| Counting by sentence | Players treating each sentence as a unit | "Different unit." |
| Looking for a name hidden in the text | Players circling letters within words | "You're working at the wrong scale." |

---

## Hint Progression

Theron gives one hint per failed attempt. He never gives the answer. He does not give hints preemptively.

**Hint 1** (after first failed attempt):
> "Numbers, not letters."

**Hint 2** (after second failed attempt):
> "The pattern spans all three. They aren't three separate messages."

**Hint 3** (offered as he reaches for the slips, before the third-attempt recovery):
> "You're counting the right thing. You're starting from the wrong place."

*(This hint is for groups who found "every Nth word" but tried N=2 or N=5 or started from Slip 3. It redirects without solving.)*

## Background Knowledge Shortcut

**History DC 8:** This encoding style is described in *Principles of Archival Notation* (Verath Goss, third edition, approximately 30 years ago) under the term "distributed notation." A character with this knowledge knows the method is "every Nth word across a unified text" — they still need to determine N, but they can skip the dead ends.

- Sage background: automatic pass, no roll required
- Charlatan background: recognizes the structural signature of a coded message (DC 8 becomes automatic — they have seen message-runners use this style for debts and contracts)

**If a player invokes this knowledge:** Theron notices. He does not comment on it directly. He straightens his pen case.

---

## Partial Solution

**Players decode "THE DAWNBORN ARE" and stop** — perhaps because they realize what the next words will say and want to confirm before continuing.

Theron's response: He watches them. He says nothing. He waits.

If a player looks up from the paper at him: he holds eye contact. He does not nod. He does not shake his head. He waits for them to read it out loud.

The partial solution is a choice. The players can sit with "THE DAWNBORN ARE" for a moment. That moment belongs to them.
