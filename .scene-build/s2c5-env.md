## The Ashring — Scene 5 Entry State

The Ashring is not dramatic in the way of fortress gates or throne rooms. It is old. The stones are older than any living person in Varenhold. They stand in a rough circle on a rise above the Dusk Parishes, and in fifty years of sunlessness they have been worn smooth by the hands of people who come to touch them and feel nothing change. The amber-glow torches the city has placed at the circle's edge make the stones look warm. They are not warm. They are the temperature of the earth they stand in.

When Harran Lecht's column appears on the east road, the stones dim slightly.

Not from his intent. Harran does not know the stones respond to proximity. The resonance the Primer Stones carry is sensitive to coercive intent in the same way a tuning fork is sensitive to the wrong frequency — not dramatically, not visibly to the untrained eye, but measurably. The amber glow in each stone's core drops perhaps a fifth of its brightness. The circle becomes, very slightly, less itself.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM NOTE — THE DIMMING:** This is not a mechanical event yet. It is atmosphere. Players who have been paying attention to the stones across Sessions 1 and 2 may notice — DC 13 Perception passively, automatic for anyone specifically watching the stones when the column arrives. If noticed, do not explain it. Let players name what they observe. The mechanic consequences of Aldric leaving his stone are in s2c5-mechanics.md. The dimming on Harran's arrival is the scene establishing that something in this place recognises what is coming.
:::

## Five Senses — When the Column Appears

**Sight:**

East road. Torchlight first — four lanterns carried by soldiers in front, spreading amber light across the flagstones of the path. Then the shapes behind the light: grey tabards, the Reckoning's silver-thread running-sun badge visible on the breast when the torchlight catches it at the right angle. Eight Veterans in formation behind Harran, two abreast, boots hitting the flagstones in the coordinated rhythm of people who have drilled this walk so often it has become involuntary. Harran himself at the front, one pace ahead, not hurrying. He already has jurisdiction in his head. He is arriving at a place he considers he already controls.

Behind them, the Ashring: nine Primer Stones glowing amber, the faintest cold fire in their cores. Aldric Stone at Stone 9 on the west arc — he's been there since evening began, hand resting against the stone's face, looking at the approaching torchlight with the expression of a man who knows exactly what he's seeing and knew it was coming before the players did.

**Sound:**

Boots on flagstone in formation — not random, not the civilian shuffle of a group of people walking together. Formation march is a specific sound: four beats, weight evenly distributed, no scraping, spacing maintained by habit. Players who have been in or around military units will recognise the pattern before they see the torchlight clearly. Players who haven't been in military contexts may just register it as wrong — too rhythmic, too coordinated for a group of people on an evening walk.

The wind through the stone circle comes from the north tonight and moves through the gap between Stones 4 and 5. It has been doing this for fifty years. The sound it makes there is not melodic — a low breath, present at irregular intervals, the kind of sound a place makes when it is simply very old and the world moves through it.

Aldric Stone moving closer to his stone — the sound of a boot sole shifting on flagstone, deliberately, someone planting their feet. No words. Just the sound of an old guardsman taking his position.

**Smell:**

Torch oil burning — the particular smell of Varenhold's amber-infused lamp oil, slightly sweet, slightly resinous, distinguishable from common pine-pitch by the faint warmth underneath the smoke. The column carries it with them as they approach.

Cold night air with the edge that comes before autumn fully gives to winter — clean and dry, with the faint dustiness of the east road rising from the boots of eight people walking it simultaneously.

The ozone-metallic scent the Primer Stones emit when active — difficult to describe to someone who hasn't encountered it. Like the air before lightning, but stiller. Players who have been at the Ashring before will recognise it. Players who haven't will register it as something present that they don't have a word for.

**Touch / Feel:**

Ground vibration from the formation march — faint, transmitted up through flagstone and earth. Not seismic. The kind of vibration you feel in the soles of your feet when a group of people move in coordinated steps forty feet away. The human body notices this before the mind categorises it.

Aldric's hand on a player's shoulder — if any player is standing near him when the column appears. Not heavy. The grip of an old guardsman who has made the decision to stand his ground and wants one person to know they're not alone before things get difficult. He does not say anything when he does this.

**Taste:**

The dry cold air of the elevated ground where the Ashring sits — at this height above the Dusk Parishes, there is less moisture. Breathing through the nose gives the particular dry chill of high ground at night. Players who are nervous — or whose characters are — will notice it more. The body's response to tension makes the cold feel sharper.

## What Has Changed Since Scene 4

Players have just left Theron's study. They are carrying:

- The truth about the Dawnborn (the ritual requires their death or their free consent)
- Fragment 2 of the Notation Key
- Theron's exhaustion, his eleven years, the word EXTINGUISHED

The emotional register they bring into the Ashring is the emotional register they carry into this encounter. A party that has processed grief may be slower to engage. A party that is cold and practical may position immediately. Both are correct responses. The encounter will meet them where they are.

Aldric Stone is here. He is not a player character. He is not Theron. He does not know what the players know. He knows he has been standing at his Primer Stone for several months and that when he puts his hand on it something in the stone changes. He does not have a theological framework for this. He has a retired guard's framework: *stand your post, hold your ground, wait for orders from people who know more than you do.* The players are the people who know more than he does.

He needs them to make that clear tonight.
