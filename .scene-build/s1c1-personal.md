## Scene 1 — Personal Hooks by Archetype

These inserts are delivered *during* the general scene opening, slotted in after the read-aloud variant and before the first skill checks. Each is addressed directly to one player. Keep them brief. The goal is not to give information — it is to make the scene feel like it was *built for that character specifically*.

---

## Part 1 — Per-Archetype Sensory Inserts

Deliver each to the relevant player by name or by archetype. These are additions to the scene, not replacements for the read-aloud variants.

---

### To the Investigator

> Before the rest of the party has taken two steps into the junction, you've already read the exits. West alley: one figure, too still to be a pedestrian. South end of the queue: another one, facing the wrong direction for someone waiting on a food allocation. North lane: a broad man with his hands at his sides and nowhere he needs to be. Someone looked at this junction before you did. They looked at it, chose it, and placed people in it. This was not improvised.

*GM note: deliver this quietly, directly to the Investigator's player, before narrating the general scene beat. The geometry of the intercept is the tell — it reads as a professional read of the street, not a spontaneous block.*

---

### To the Believer

> When the figure at the north lane spreads his hands — that first moment, palms out, no weapon visible — you catch a glimpse inside his collar. The coat opens slightly with the gesture. There it is: a small circle of undyed thread stitched against the lining, a sun with a notch cut from its lower arc. You know that symbol. It is not standard Auris iconography. It belongs to a specific community within the faith — one that believes the sun's delay is not punishment but *grief*. You have encountered this tradition before, probably at a distance. Right now it is blocking your path.

*GM note: The sun-disk detail lives in s1c1-npc.md — visible in a grapple or when a collar opens. Advance it for the Believer because recognition is their specific emotional texture here. Do not yet name the Restorers or the Wounded faction — the Believer's Religion check does that.*

---

### To the Survivor

> Your eyes go to the food queue before they go anywhere else. Habit. You scan the line the way you have been scanning lines like this for months — looking for a posture you recognize. The woman three places back from the cart is standing in a way that is not quite standing. Her weight is slightly wrong. She keeps one hand against the building, not because the press of the crowd demands it but because she needs it there. Her coat is too large — the kind of too large that means she has lost weight, and lost it recently. Stage 2. You know what Stage 2 looks like. You know because you have been watching someone walk toward it, and you know exactly what is at stake if this job does not go the way it needs to go.

*GM note: The woman in the queue is not a named NPC. She is a mirror. She should land before the combat beats do. If the Survivor has shared their sick person's name in Session Zero, see the callback hook at the end of this file.*

---

### To the Scholar

> You are looking at the placement. You cannot help it. Four people, four positions — two exits blocked, one central axis controlled, one flanking the secondary objective. That secondary objective being, you notice, *you*. This is not a mob. This is a practiced intercept pattern — the kind of thing you learn by running variations until you do not have to think about it. Whoever designed this has run operations in confined urban spaces before. Which means they have run *several*, and this group has trained on them. Someone organized these people. Someone with tactical experience organized these people, and they did it *before the players arrived in this junction*, which means they knew you were coming. That is the part worth thinking about.

*GM note: The Scholar's register is analytical. Give them the interpretation, not just the sensation. The key word is "before" — the intercept predates their arrival.*

---

### To the Mercenary

> You've seen professionals work. You've been hired by professionals, run operations with professionals, been on the other end of what professionals do. What you're looking at right now is... almost that. The formation is sound. The positioning is thought through. But the woman near the west alley mouth is watching the center of the junction instead of her exit — which means either someone briefed her wrong or she's nervous enough that the briefing didn't stick. The zealot near the market crates is holding his hands too close to his body; he's managing breathing, not weight distribution. Whoever hired this crew hired people with belief and gave them structure. Belief and structure is dangerous. It is not the same as professional. You file that distinction away. It matters.

*GM note: Give the Mercenary credit for reading what is actually there — a competent but imperfect operation. The "almost professional" assessment is accurate and character-validating without being flattering.*

---

### To the Dawnborn-Adjacent

> You know this street. Not from maps — from Sera's letters. The distribution cart on the center-south lane: she mentioned it once, offhand, in a letter about a volunteer scheduling dispute she'd found frustrating. The food registry queue stretching to the east wall: she described standing in it herself, seven years ago, before she started running the Dawnhall, back when she was just another person in Varenhold trying to get through a winter. These are her people. The woman near the cart, the man with the grey in his beard near the back of the line, the child being held close by a parent as the figures start to move — Sera knows these faces. She has been feeding these faces for years. And right now they are about to watch a fight they did not ask for, in their street, at their food queue, and you are part of the reason it is happening.

*GM note: Do not frame this as guilt — frame it as belonging. The Dawnborn-Adjacent character is not a tourist here. This is the neighborhood of someone they care about, and that should land before the fight starts.*

## Part 3 — Check-Result Narrations

When a player succeeds on one of the scene's active checks, do not read a mechanical result. Read the *story* of what they perceive. These are the narrations.

---

**Insight DC 12 — on the Enforcer (emotional read):**

> His face does what controlled faces do when they are working not to let anything through — and you catch the one thing they cannot fully close off. Not anger. Not contempt. Something closer to the expression of a person attending a funeral for someone they knew well: present, composed, and tired in a way that has nothing to do with sleep.

---

**Insight DC 14 — on the youngest zealot (the uncertain one):**

> In the moment before movement — when the group tightens and every body commits to being there — his shifts. Just slightly. Not toward you. Away. One breath where he is somewhere else entirely, and then he closes it off and is here again. Whatever he is carrying, it is heavier than what he came to do.

---

**Investigation DC 14 — on the note (if retrieved, examining the handwriting):**

> The hand that wrote this is trained. Not a scribe's hand — a scholar's, or a liturgist's. The letters are formed with the particular deliberateness of someone who learned to write in a tradition that regarded handwriting as practice, not just communication. The ink is unhurried. Whoever wrote this was not in a rush, and was not afraid of what they were writing.

---

**Perception DC 15 — scanning the east alley (Edoran):**

> Thirty feet into the east alley, where the amber lanternlight doesn't reach, there is a person who is not moving the way people move. Not still the way someone leaning against a wall is still — still the way someone has made a decision to be still. Hood up. Plain coat. Watching the junction. Watching, specifically, *you*. They do not react to your gaze. They were waiting to be noticed, and they are entirely unsurprised that you did.

## Part 4 — Post-Scene Personal Questions

After Scene 1 ends — the Enforcer stands down, the zealots scatter into the queue — each archetype is left holding a different version of the same problem. These are the *personal* questions, not the campaign questions.

**Investigator:** *Who gave the Restorers our route — and does that mean whoever hired me has already had this conversation with them?*

**Believer:** *That sun-disk in his collar was grief-thread. A mourning devotional. The person in the alley who sent these people — are they a believer, or someone who learned to speak to believers? And does it matter?*

**Survivor:** *The woman in the queue, the one with Stage 2 — she is still in line, because she has no other option. If this investigation goes wrong, how many more people end up in that line? How much longer does the person I love have?*

**Scholar:** *I spent three years corresponding with Theron Waide. He sent me documents he chose. He built a picture of this city, this ritual, this investigation, that brought me here. Someone already knew I was coming before I arrived in Varenhold. Was any of that correspondence actually mine to initiate?*

**Mercenary:** *Someone put resources into this intercept — rehearsal time, people, coordination. My employer said routine document retrieval. Nobody rehearses a street intercept for routine. What is the actual job I've been hired to do?*

**Dawnborn-Adjacent:** *Sera wrote that something was happening she didn't feel safe committing to paper. She sent that letter six weeks ago. This intercept happened on a street she works on, with people who know this neighborhood. Does she know about the Restorers? Does she know they know about us?*

## Part 5 — Callback Hooks (GM Notes, Session Zero Dependent)

If the player shared their background hook in Session Zero, use these brief amplifications in your delivery of the sensory inserts above.

**Investigator** — *If the player named their patron:* When describing the intercept's organization, pause and add: *"Someone put time into this. The kind of time that takes resources. The kind of resources [patron's name] has."* Do not confirm anything. Let the thought land.

**Believer** — *If the player specified their tradition (Auris orthodox vs. Morthis or independent):* The sun-disk recognition lands differently depending on whether the Believer shares the Wounded faction's theology or opposes it. For orthodox Auris: the disk is familiar but marked as deviant — the notch cut from the sun is wrong to them. For Morthis: it is foreign iconography in a city they thought they understood. Adjust the delivery accordingly.

**Survivor** — *If the player named their sick person:* When describing the woman in the food queue with Stage 2 grey sickness, say her name once — quietly, without emphasis, without explanation: *"She stands the way [name] stands now, one hand on the wall, weight shifted."* Then continue the scene. Do not repeat it.

**Scholar** — *If the player named the documents Waide sent them:* As the Scholar reads the intercept formation, add: *"The same kind of systematic thinking that organized Waide's archive — who gets access to what, in what order, building a specific picture."* The implied comparison is deliberate.

**Mercenary** — *If the player named their employer or described the job's stated scope:* When the Mercenary clocks the "almost professional" quality of the intercept, add: *"This is the part that doesn't fit. [Employer's name/the job description] should not have warranted this response."* The mismatch is the tell.

**Dawnborn-Adjacent** — *If the player shared details of Sera's letter or their correspondence:* When describing the Lowmark junction as Sera's neighborhood, name one specific detail from the player's established correspondence: *"The distribution cart she wrote about in [month/year]. The one she said needed its wheel replaced and never did."* Specificity is the difference between atmosphere and belonging.

---

*Design note: the best use of this tab is to deliver one or two of these inserts, not all six. Read the table's energy. A player who leans forward when their archetype is named will get more from a direct personal moment than from a comprehensive delivery. The goal is not to complete a checklist — it is to make one player feel, once, that this scene could only have happened to their character.*
