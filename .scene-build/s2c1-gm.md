## The Three Beats of This Scene

### Beat 1: The Opening Image (before initiative)

The players open the door and see what's already happening. This beat exists entirely to give them information before the clock starts.

Do not rush to initiative. Read the five senses. Let players ask one or two questions about what they see. Let them count the soldiers. Let them see Bundle A being approached. Then ask: *"What do you do?"*

The secondary objective should be legible before a single die is rolled. Players who understand the problem from the first breath will make more interesting choices than players scrambling to catch up. The scene's tension is *prioritization*, not surprise.

**What to establish in this beat:**
- The fire is already going. This operation started without them.
- The soldiers are moving with purpose, not panic. They were briefed.
- The Lieutenant is at the far end, checking a bundle — he is the one with judgment. The soldiers are executors.
- Three bundles. The nearest is already being reached for.

---

### Beat 2: The Complication (Rounds 1–3)

The secondary objective is the engine. Every round a player spends fighting a soldier without someone also moving on a bundle is a round where the bundle timeline advances.

The complication is not the soldiers — it is the *split-focus problem*. Players cannot do everything. Someone will have to choose between securing a bundle and helping a teammate in danger. This is the scene's actual dramatic content.

**The Lieutenant is the additional weight.** He's not fighting aggressively — he's watching. But he knows things. He is worth capturing. Getting to him costs action economy. The temptation to grab him while securing bundles is real. That pressure is intentional.

**How to run it:**
- Announce bundle movement aloud each round: *"The soldier with Bundle A is now 10 feet from the fireplace."*
- Do not hide the timeline. Let players feel it.
- When a bundle goes in the fire, slow it down (see narration note below).

---

### Beat 3: Resolution

Combat ends on one of two paths (see `s2c1-combat.md`). Either the Lieutenant is in hand, or two bundles are secured and the Reckoning withdraws.

The resolution beat is about information: what did the players save, what did they learn, and what does the Lieutenant tell them (or refuse to)? Theron emerging from the alcove is the transition — his entrance confirms the scene is over and Scene 2 can begin.

**Don't linger in the aftermath.** Once the soldiers are gone, give players a brief moment to catch breath, then: the alcove door opens.

---

## On Narrating a Bundle in the Fire

When a bundle enters the fireplace:

> Pause. Don't describe the fire catching immediately. Describe the moment before: the soldier's arm pulling back, the arc, the weight of it in the air. Then: it lands. The leather binding curls first. Then the edges of the pages. The smoke changes quality — less woodsmoke, more paper ash. Something that took years to accumulate becomes unreadable in seconds.

One sentence. Then let silence sit for half a beat before continuing. This moment matters. Let it cost something at the table.

---

## The Lieutenant as the Interesting Choice

He has information. Getting it requires capturing him, which costs action economy during a time-pressured scene.

The scene is designed so that capturing him is *possible* but *expensive*. He is not trying to die. He will negotiate a graceful exit if offered one. But the window for that negotiation is narrow — once the bundles are secured or gone, he moves toward withdrawal.

**If players focus entirely on the Lieutenant:**
- Soldiers complete the burn on Bundles A and B.
- Players end the scene with the Lieutenant but only Bundle C.
- This is a valid trade. He knows things Bundle C doesn't say.

**The correct play doesn't exist.** There is no optimal path through this scene. Some bundle is probably going to burn. The question is which one and what the players chose instead.

---

## If Players Kill the Lieutenant

He dies without giving testimony. That's a real loss.

What they find on his body:
- A sealed document authorizing the burn, signed with a Reckoning administrative cipher (not Harran's personal signature — deliberately distanced)
- A folded note, handwritten: *"Commander's copy secured. Proceed to secondary phase when confirmed."*
- He wrote "confirmed" in his own hand. He was going to send this note after the burn.

**What secondary phase means:** Unknown to players. Unknown to the soldiers. The note exists to confirm that Harran has already acted — this burn is cleanup, not discovery. The phrase will resurface later in the campaign.

---

## If All 3 Bundles Are Saved

Small bonus for Scene 2. Theron emerges from the alcove faster — he opens the door during the withdrawal rather than waiting for the room to clear. He has watched the players protect what he's been protecting for weeks. That shifts the way he speaks.

He is still shaking. But he looks at the players differently. When he produces the slips, he sets them on the table rather than holding them back. He has decided something.

**Note for GM:** Don't announce this as a reward. Just play Theron slightly more open. Let players feel the difference without naming it.

---

## If Only 1 Bundle Is Saved

The damaged state. Players have one bundle and a gap.

- If Bundle C was the one saved: they have two colophons but no institutional correspondence. Theron's verbal testimony becomes load-bearing in a way it wasn't before.
- If Bundle A or B was the one saved: they have partial information. Scene 3's puzzle has a missing component. This is recoverable — but Theron will need to be pressed harder, and the resonance box puzzle will require creative problem-solving rather than the direct approach.

Do not apologize for this outcome. The scene was designed to be difficult. The gap is part of the story now.

---

## Session Energy: The Cold Open

This is the first scene after a week's gap. Players may be slightly out of the room. Hit the five senses hard in the opening image. Make them smell the smoke. Make them feel the warm door handle.

**Opening ritual:** Before you read the scene-opening paragraph, ask the table: *"Your character has been thinking about what you found in the Archive last session. What one thing stuck with you?"* One sentence per player, then go directly into the scene description.

This does two things: it re-grounds everyone in the previous session, and it primes players to care about what they're about to fight to protect.

The combat is the cold open. There is no slow entry, no social scene before the stakes arrive. The stakes arrive when the door opens. That is intentional — Session 2 is about the players discovering that someone else was already moving.
