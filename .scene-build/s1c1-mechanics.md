## 2. Terrain Mechanics

### Food Queue Crowd (~50 people, north side of junction)

**Baseline state (Round 1):**
- The crowd occupies a 20 ft × 40 ft zone along the north street edge, tightest near the distribution cart.
- Difficult terrain for anyone moving through it.
- Creatures *inside* the crowd zone gain half cover (+2 AC, +2 Dexterity saving throws) from ranged attacks originating outside it.

**Density zones:**
- Within 10 ft of the distribution cart: heavy difficult terrain (every foot costs 3 ft of movement). AC bonus applies to attackers *outside* this zone.
- Street edges and alley mouths: lighter crowd; treat as standard difficult terrain only.

**Round 2 trigger (Initiative Count 20, start of Round 2):**
The crowd breaks. Civilians scatter in all directions — move them off the map. Crowd cover is gone. The junction becomes sparse difficult terrain (overturned baskets, dropped packs, scattered grain) rather than dense crowd. GM may place 1d4 prone civilians in random squares as obstacles.

**Civilian injuries:**
- Each civilian is AC 10, 4 HP.
- If a player's AoE or attack strikes a civilian, note it. Two or more civilian casualties triggers a City Watch investigation within 24 hours (see Section 4D below).

---

### Market Crates (east end of junction, stacked 3–4 high)

**Default state:** Half cover (+2 AC, +2 Dex saves) for creatures crouching behind them.

**Upending a crate (Action):**
- DC 10 Athletics.
- Success: crate falls; creates a 5 ft radius difficult terrain patch at the landing square. Creatures in the path make a DC 12 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d6 bludgeoning and are knocked prone.
- Failure: crate shifts but does not fall; no terrain change.

**Improvised weapon (pushing a crate onto an adjacent creature):**
- DC 12 Athletics check (not an attack roll).
- Success: target takes 2d6 bludgeoning and must succeed on a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone.
- Failure: crate tips but target steps clear — no damage, but crate now counts as half cover for them.

---

### Lantern Posts (spaced ~15 ft along street edges, 6 ft tall iron posts, open-flame oil lamps)

**Passive hazard:**
- Any creature pushed, shoved, or knocked into a lantern post must succeed on a DC 12 Acrobatics saving throw or take 1d6 fire damage as the lamp tips and splashes burning oil.
- The lantern post itself is AC 13, HP 8; destroying it removes the hazard and plunges a 15 ft radius into dim light.

**The burn zone:**
- A zealot holding the note who is adjacent to a lantern post has an *opportunity action* available on their turn: hold the note to the flame (no action cost if already adjacent; uses their Action if they must move to reach it).
- Trigger condition: Round 2 or later, note still in zealot's possession.
- Contesting the burn: a player within reach can use their Reaction to make an Athletics or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check (DC 13) to knock the zealot's hand aside before the note catches.
- If no player contests: note ignites at end of zealot's turn. It burns to ash by end of *next* turn unless smothered (DC 10 Dexterity as a bonus action while adjacent).

---

### Distribution Cart (centre of junction, north edge)

**Object stats:** Large object, AC 11, HP 27, immune to psychic and poison damage.

**As blocking terrain:** Blocks movement and line of sight for Medium and smaller creatures crouching behind it. Standing creatures are visible above the cart bed.

**Pushing the cart (Action):**
- DC 15 Athletics.
- Success: cart moves 10 ft in a straight line. Any creature in its path must make a DC 13 Strength saving throw or be knocked prone and take 1d8 bludgeoning.
- Failure: cart shifts 2 ft; no terrain change.
- The cart cannot be pushed through walls or other Large objects.

---

### East Alley Mouth (10 ft wide, runs east from junction)

**Lighting:** Dim light (vs. amber street lanterns). Perception checks made from the street into the alley have disadvantage.

**Edoran's position:** 30 ft into the alley from the junction mouth. He does not move toward the street under any circumstance.

**Time window:** Edoran waits until end of Round 2. If no player has entered the alley by then, he takes a step back into the darkness and is gone. Players who move into the alley on Round 1 or early Round 2 can reach him before he withdraws.

**Dim light in the alley:** Creatures inside the alley making Perception checks back toward the lit street have advantage (bright light behind the subjects).

## 3. Crowd Mechanics (Detailed)

### Pushing Through

- **Check:** DC 12 Athletics or Acrobatics (player's choice).
- **Success:** Move through the crowd zone at half speed (difficult terrain, every foot costs 2 ft). The crowd parts grudgingly.
- **Failure:** The crowd presses back. No movement through the crowd zone this turn. The player can still move in open areas outside the crowd zone.
- **Special:** A player who declares they are *forcing* through (not navigating) can push through automatically but displaces 1d3 civilians who stumble — roll to see if any are in a hazard square.

### Crowd as Cover

- A player who moves into the crowd zone and stays still (does not attack) may attempt to blend in.
- **Check:** DC 13 Stealth, rolled with disadvantage (active combat, enemies are actively searching).
- **Success:** Player gains half cover from ranged attacks this round. Enemies must guess which figure in the crowd is the player; melee attacks against the player have disadvantage until the player attacks or casts a spell.
- **Failure:** No benefit. The player is conspicuous.

### AoE Spells / Indiscriminate Force Into Crowd

- If a spell or effect's area of effect includes crowd squares:
  - Each civilian in the blast is AC 10, 4 HP. GM resolves their saves normally.
  - On any civilian being reduced to 0 HP: one civilian is visibly injured (screaming, down). Track this.
  - Two or more civilians at 0 HP: witnesses flee and report to City Watch. Investigation arrives in 1d4 hours.
  - Player-character reputation: the Dawnhall, Sera Voss, and any Lowmark faction contact hears about this within the session.

## 4. Creative Play Responses

### A. "I try to negotiate before any combat starts"

**Check:** Persuasion DC 16 (Enforcer is under orders; persuasion is fighting his chain of command, not his personal feelings).

**Approach matters:**
- Threatening: disadvantage on the roll.
- Appealing to his fear (requires DC 13 Insight first, revealing he's afraid not angry): advantage on the roll.
- Offering to leave the district immediately and abandon the investigation: automatic success — the Enforcer signals a hold. The zealots stay blocking. Players get 2 minutes to leave before the hold breaks.

**Success:** Enforcer holds position; says nothing. A tense standoff. He gives players 1 minute to leave the district. If they leave, he withdraws. If they don't, combat starts on his initiative.

**Failure:** Enforcer shakes his head and signals his people. Roll initiative. Players who attempted negotiation have already declared their position; they do not get a free action before Round 1.

---

### B. "I run directly for Edoran without fighting"

**No check required to run.** Movement is automatic. But:

- The crowd is difficult terrain (costs double movement, or DC 12 Athletics/Acrobatics to push through at full speed).
- Two zealots will use their Reactions to make opportunity attacks if the running player passes within 5 ft.
- The Enforcer may use his turn to Dash and grapple the running player (Athletics +3 vs. player's contested roll).

**Reaching the alley (30 ft to Edoran):**
- Player needs ~30 ft of movement after clearing the crowd zone. With difficult terrain, a 30 ft speed character needs 2 turns minimum unless they succeed on the crowd push check.
- A player with 35–40 ft speed who clears the crowd with a successful DC 12 check can reach Edoran in 1 round.

**What happens:** Edoran does not flee immediately. He says: *"You have no idea what you are looking for. When you find it, you will understand why I did not want you to."* Then he steps back into the dark. He cannot be grappled or detained — if a player attempts it, he simply is not there when they reach out. He is already gone.

---

### C. "I try to turn the crowd against the zealots"

**Check:** Persuasion or Performance DC 14, rolled with disadvantage (the crowd is frightened, not angry; they want to flee, not fight).

**Success:** 1d6+2 civilians throw debris (sacks, baskets, produce) at the nearest zealot. Each hit deals 1 damage and imposes disadvantage on that zealot's next attack roll. The Enforcer turns his attention to crowd control for 1 round (loses his Action).

**Failure:** The crowd starts to panic early — treat Round 2 crowd scatter as happening at the *end of Round 1* instead. The early scatter removes cover and exposes civilian AoE risk sooner.

**Critical success (rolled 5+ above DC):** A civilian with some physical presence (broad shoulders, dock worker build) physically grabs a zealot and bear-hugs them — the zealot is grappled until it succeeds on a DC 10 Athletics check on its turn.

---

### D. "I cast an area spell into the crowd / use indiscriminate force"

**No check — this just happens.** Resolve the spell normally.

**Civilian consequences:** Per Section 3. Two civilian casualties = Watch investigation. Three or more = the scene ends with players fleeing the district, not completing it cleanly.

**Faction consequences:**
- Sera Voss hears. Scene 4 (the Dawnhall) begins with her standing with her arms crossed. She still talks to them. She is not warm.
- The Chancellor hears. She does not comment. She files it.

**Combat consequence:** The Enforcer will not yield to players who harmed civilians. Persuasion to end the fight becomes DC 20 for the rest of the scene.

---

### E. "I try to knock out all zealots non-lethally"

**Rules:** Melee attacks only. When a melee attack would drop a creature to 0 HP, the player declares non-lethal. The creature is unconscious, not dead. Ranged attacks and spells (without specific "non-lethal" flavour) cannot be declared non-lethal under 2014 rules without GM agreement.

**Practical note:** Cultists have 9 HP each; they drop fast. The Enforcer has 52 HP — non-lethal is viable but he fights to half HP before the surrender trigger anyway.

**Restraint check:** Each non-lethal KO costs a bonus action to secure (tie hands, etc.). If players don't secure them, unconscious zealots regain consciousness in 1d4 minutes (1 HP) and may flee or alert others.

**Reward:** Players who knock out all zealots non-lethally and secure them can interrogate. The zealots know: they were paid, not devout (despite the pins); the Enforcer is the only one with a name to give (he won't); they met their contact in the Ashring, not the Lowmark.

---

### F. "I try to intimidate everyone into leaving immediately"

**Check:** Intimidation DC 18 to affect the Enforcer (he's seen worse). Cultists break at DC 13 — they're hired, not fanatics.

**Split resolution:** Roll once. On a 13–17, the zealots flinch and hesitate (disadvantage on their next attack), but the Enforcer holds and they hold with him. On an 18+, the Enforcer takes a half step back; the zealots break and run; the Enforcer fights alone (reducing the combat to 1v party).

**Failure (below 13):** The Enforcer smiles, briefly. His people step forward. Roll initiative with players at disadvantage on their first initiative check (they overplayed their hand).

---

### G. "I try to bribe the Enforcer"

**No check required — the offer is made.** But:

- If the bribe is less than 50 gp: Enforcer ignores it. Proceed to check.
- If 50–100 gp: DC 16 Persuasion to make the bribe work (he's taking a professional risk).
- If 100+ gp: DC 12 Persuasion (the money is real and significant to him).

**Success:** Enforcer pockets the coin without acknowledgment. He calls a tactical retreat — "nothing to be done here." Zealots withdraw. No combat.

**Failure:** Enforcer glances at the coin. Does not take it. Says: *"Put that away before someone else sees it."* He does not elaborate. This is not a refusal on principle — it is a refusal of *this* bribe in *this* public place. A private approach (alley, later, DC 12 Persuasion) might work. The current scene proceeds to combat.

---

### H. "I try to grab a zealot as MY hostage / use them as leverage"

**Check to grab:** Athletics or the Grapple action (contested Athletics, zealot +2). On success, player has zealot grappled.

**Check to use as leverage against Enforcer:** Intimidation DC 15 (Enforcer is calculating whether this zealot is worth more than his contract).

**Success (Intimidation 15+):** Enforcer freezes. Standoff. He has 1 round to decide. If player maintains grapple and holds position, Enforcer signals withdrawal at the start of his next turn. He says nothing.

**Failure (Intimidation below 15):** Enforcer gestures to the remaining zealots. They move to flank the player with the grappled zealot — they are going to *take* their colleague back. The grappled zealot fights to break free (Athletics +2 on its turn).

**Special case:** If the player explicitly threatens to kill the hostage and the Enforcer believes them (DC 14 Intimidation with a credible weapon at the zealot's throat): automatic withdrawal. But word travels — the players threatened to murder a captive in the Lowmark. Someone saw.

## 5. Rulings Cheat Sheet

### Most Likely Edge Cases at the Table

**1. "Does the crowd count as cover for the zealots / Enforcer too?"**

Yes, while they are inside the crowd zone. Half cover for everyone, both sides. Once the crowd scatters (Round 2 trigger), cover is gone for all. The GM does not need to track individual crowd figures — zones are enough.

---

**2. "Can I delay my turn to contest the note burn?"**

Yes, but only if the player has already declared *before* the zealot's turn that they are readying an action to intercept. A Readied Action (DC 13 Athletics or Acrobatics as the Reaction trigger) is the correct mechanic. Players cannot retroactively contest after seeing the zealot succeed — they must declare the ready before the zealot acts.

---

**3. "I want to shove the Enforcer into a lantern post."**

Shove action: Athletics vs. Enforcer's Athletics or Acrobatics (Veteran Athletics +5, Acrobatics +2 — player rolls against whichever is better, so +5). On success, Enforcer moves 5 ft in a straight line. If that line ends at a lantern post square, the GM calls a DC 12 Acrobatics save for the Enforcer or he takes 1d6 fire. The shove itself requires the player to be within 5 ft and uses their Attack action (or one attack if multi-attack).

---

**4. "The zealot dropped the note — it fell on the ground. Who owns initiative on picking it up?"**

The note is a loose object on the ground. Picking it up costs a free object interaction (no action required) on a player's turn if they are in or adjacent to the square it occupies. If two creatures want the same object, the one whose turn comes first gets it — no contested roll needed. If both are within reach on the same initiative count, the player wins the tie (friendly GM ruling; players are protagonists).

---

**5. "Can I use magic to solve the crowd problem non-violently?" (Charm Person, Sleep, Calm Emotions, etc.)**

**Charm Person / Calm Emotions:** Valid. Each zealot is a valid target (humanoid). The Enforcer has Veteran stats, which include no special save bonuses beyond Wisdom (modifier unknown — treat as +1 Wisdom for MM Veteran). The DC is the caster's spell save DC (typical Gestalt L3: 13–14). On success against the Enforcer, he is charmed — he will not attack but also will not order a withdrawal (the charm makes him friendly, not obedient). This effectively ends the threat while leaving the Enforcer present and awkward. DC 13 Insight for other zealots to notice the Enforcer's behaviour has changed — if they notice, they may break and run on their own.

**Sleep:** Cultists (9 HP) go down easily. The Enforcer (52 HP) is almost certainly above the Sleep pool at L3 — he will not be affected unless he is already heavily wounded. Casting Sleep into the crowd risks civilians — GM applies the civilian HP rules per Section 3 for civilians caught in the burst radius.
