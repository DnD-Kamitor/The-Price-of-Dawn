## Narrative Function: The Reversal

Session 1 positioned the players as investigators arriving at a mystery. They found documents in the restricted stacks, understood that something was encoded, left with Fragment 1 of Corven's key.

Session 2, Scene 1 reverses the dynamic entirely.

They return to follow a thread. What they find is that someone is already cutting that thread. The Reckoning acts before the players do. The burn order existed before the players knew what they'd found. The soldiers are already inside when the players arrive.

**This is the story of Session 2:** The players are not ahead of anything. They are behind. How far behind is the question that drives every subsequent scene.

The scene does not tell players they are losing. It shows them what it looks like when another party has been operational longer, with more institutional support, running a parallel investigation in the opposite direction: not toward understanding, but toward controlled ignorance.

---

## Three Threads This Scene Activates

### Thread 1: The Reckoning Is Operational, Not Theoretical

The Reckoning was a concept in Session 1 — a named faction, a known position, an antagonist in the abstract. This scene makes them concrete. They have soldiers. They have floor plans. They have burn orders with specific manifest lists. They have a Commander who already has a copy of the documents they're destroying.

The players now know what they're dealing with: not an opposing viewpoint, but an active opposing operation.

### Thread 2: Keseph Vyne's Bureaucratic Interference Has a Paper Trail

Bundle B, if saved, is the first documentary evidence of Spire involvement in suppressing the ritual record. Keseph Vyne's name appears in a clerk's annotation — not as a central actor yet, but as a contact who shut down a formal review process eight months ago. This thread is quiet. Players may not clock it immediately. Theron will, in Scene 2. The name will matter more later than it appears to matter now.

### Thread 3: The Documents' Content Is Now a Matter of Who Has Copies

Before this scene, the question was "who found the documents." After this scene, the question is "who has copies of what they say." The original documents are no longer the only version in play. Harran has read them. The Lieutenant has read them. Players may have saved some. The knowledge has dispersed — and the Reckoning's attempt to control it through burning is already complicated by that dispersion.

This reframes the campaign's central information problem: it is not about finding hidden truth, but about understanding who already knows the truth and what they're doing with it.

## Theron as the Scene's Hidden Element

He is in the alcove. He has been there the whole time.

He heard the soldiers arrive. He heard them pull the bundles. He heard the Lieutenant confirm the burn order. He heard whatever complication the players caused when they came through the door.

He has not made a sound.

When he emerges in Scene 2, the players will understand that he was present for everything they just went through. He is not a neutral party who needs to be convinced the Reckoning is serious. He watched it happen from behind a door, alone, not knowing if they would find him.

**What surviving that does to him:**

Theron has been afraid of exactly this for weeks. The Reckoning using force — or its proximity — to control the ritual's narrative. He knew it was coming. Knowing and watching are different things. He watched it come and he survived it and he did not act, because he could not act, because he is a scholar not a soldier and the only thing he could offer was staying hidden and not burning.

He is shaking when he opens the door. But something has resolved in him. The worst thing he feared has already happened and he is still here. The documents he couldn't save are gone. The documents the players saved are real. He has the slips. He is ready to talk.

**The GM note:** Theron's emergence is the transition point from combat to narrative. Don't rush it. Let players process the aftermath of the fight for one beat before the alcove door opens. Then: a creak, a pause, and a man's voice from the dark.

> *"Are they gone?"*

## Bridge to Scene 2

Theron emerges with the slips. He confirms what the documents said — because he was there when they were compiled. He was one of the people who put the bundles together before the Reckoning arrived. He knew they would come. He was trying to preserve what he could in the time he had.

Scene 2 opens with an answer and a new set of questions. The bridge is the slips — small paper squares with notation that can't be burned because Theron has been carrying them against his body for three days.

He sets them on the reading table. Whatever else burned, these survived.

---

## What the Table Should Feel Coming Out of This Scene

Not relief. Not victory, even if they saved all three bundles.

**The feeling is this:** They moved as fast as they could and something was already ahead of them. They won the immediate fight and they still don't know when the race started or what the finish line looks like.

The Reckoning is not mysterious anymore. They are real and they are operational and they were here before the players were. That knowledge changes the tempo of everything that follows.

The question that should be alive in the room, unspoken, as Theron opens the alcove door:

*How long have they known?*

And its corollary, which players won't voice yet but will feel:

*If Harran already has a copy — what is he planning to do with it?*
