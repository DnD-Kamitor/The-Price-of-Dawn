## Three Outcome States

---

### Variant A — Full Success

**Conditions:** Aldric stays on Stone 9 for all combat rounds. Harran exits via Tactical Retreat.

**Stone 9 status:** Fully preserved. Amber core at normal brightness.

**Aldric's state:** He is not unhurt — he has been in a fight — but he is upright and on his stone. He sits down against it after combat ends. Not dramatically. He just sits down, because his knees have been making their usual complaint for the last six minutes of combat and he was ignoring them until now. He does not make a speech. If a player checks on him, he says:

> "Three more months before I retire from this too."

He will be back at his stone in Session 3. He will not need anything from players before he stands there again.

**Inversion status:** Full Inversion option (Ending B) remains intact at 10/10 Primer Stones. This is the only outcome state where Ending B is fully achievable without Session 3-4 remediation work.

**What players know:** Harran knew about the Inversion inscription. Someone in the Spire is watching the investigation and reporting to the Reckoning. The investigation has a leak. Players carry this into Session 3.

**Forward hook:** Varren Aldiss at the Spire is the source. He will not be hard to find if players look for him. He will not confess easily. He has reasons for what he did that he believes are good.

---

### Variant B — Partial Success

**Conditions:** Aldric was pulled off Stone 9 at some point during combat, but players got him back before combat ended — he returned to his stone and re-attuned.

**Stone 9 status:** Flickered but recovered. Aldric's hand on the stone brought the amber back. The re-attunement is visible and real.

**Aldric's state:** Physically present, stone occupied. But something is different about the way he's standing at it. He was physically removed — two soldiers grabbed him and pulled — and that is in his body now in a way that the intellectual knowledge of Reckoning opposition was not. He was there, and then he wasn't, and then he was again. It is not the same as having held the stone all night.

In Session 3, if players attempt to organise any gathering at the Ashring, Aldric will be there. He will not visibly hesitate. But there will be a moment — before he places his hand on the stone — where he pauses. One breath. He is not going to ask anyone for anything. He is waiting for acknowledgment that what happened tonight was seen.

A player who says *"I know what it cost you to come back here"* (or any genuine variant) unlocks him fully. A player who simply proceeds without noting the moment will find him functional but reserved. He is a retired guardsman; he does not make his discomfort into a scene. But it is there.

**Inversion status:** Full Inversion option technically preserved, but carries the Aldric attunement cost (see above). Session 4 remediation: requires one player moment with Aldric before a 10/10 Inversion attempt.

**Forward hook:** Players need to return to Aldric with acknowledgment before Session 4's ritual-decision scenes. The moment is small and specific — it does not require an extended conversation. One sentence, genuinely meant, in his vicinity. He will receive it without ceremony.

---

### Variant C — Failure

**Conditions:** Aldric was pulled off Stone 9 and did not return before combat ended. Stone 9 is dim at scene close.

**Stone 9 status:** Dark. The amber core has gone grey. It is not destroyed — the Primer Stones cannot be destroyed by physical means — but Aldric's attunement has broken and the stone has returned to its baseline dormant state.

**Aldric's state:** He is somewhere in the Ashring — or at its perimeter if he moved there during combat — and he is not at his stone. He looks at it from wherever he is standing. He does not go back to it immediately. He has been standing at that stone for months. He was taken away from it by force. It is, for him, a very specific kind of failure in a man whose career was about standing posts.

If players talk to him in the aftermath, he will say:

> "I'll go back. Just — not right now."

He means it. He is not done. But right now he is a man who was physically carried away from the thing he was supposed to hold, and he needs a moment that is not provided by the rush of post-combat adrenaline. Players who try to move him back immediately will find him immovable in a different way — not stubborn, exactly. Just not yet.

**Inversion status:** Stone 9 is dim. Inversion now requires 9/10 minimum. This means:

- Ending B (full Inversion, no deaths) cannot be achieved unless Stone 9 is re-lit before the ritual decision point in Session 4.
- To re-light Stone 9: Aldric must return to the stone willingly and attune. This requires a player conversation that acknowledges what happened tonight without pressuring him. Once he returns, the stone re-lights within one round of contact.
- If Stone 9 is not re-lit before Session 4's decision point: Ending B shifts to a 9-stone partial Inversion attempt (DC higher, some energy release incomplete, carries a 15% chance of partial failure per Corven's equations). This is not Ending B anymore. It is something between Ending A and Ending B.

**Long-term consequence:** Players will feel this in Session 4 when the ritual options are calculated and the math is one stone short. The gap is traceable to tonight.

**Forward hook:** Aldric can be recovered in Session 3 if players seek him out. He visits the Ashring most evenings — he cannot quite stop. Players who go there and find him and give him the conversation he is waiting for will see Stone 9 re-light. Players who do not go looking will find, in Session 4, that he has re-attuned on his own — but incompletely. The stone at 60% brightness is not enough.

## Chancellor's Letter Outcomes

The letter arrives regardless of the combat outcome. What it means depends on where players are in the investigation.

**Players who have been slow or thorough (Scenes 1-4 took multiple in-world days):**
Seven days is catastrophically short. Rationing begins before players can complete the next major investigation thread. The clock is now inside the investigation's timeline. Session 3 opens under pressure.

**Players who have been methodical (Scenes 1-4 completed in two in-world days):**
Seven days is tight but workable. Players have enough runway to reach Isolde Maren, gather the Dawnborn, and present findings to the Chancellor before rationing begins. Session 3 opens with urgency but not emergency.

**Players who have been fast (Scenes 1-4 completed in one in-world day):**
Seven days is enough. The Chancellor's trust — *"I am told you have been thorough"* — is matched by the timeline. Session 3 opens with the players ahead of the clock, which is the best position available in this campaign.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM NOTE:** The Chancellor's letter is not a mechanical timer that triggers specific failures. It is a tonal beat that reframes the investigation's stakes. "Seven days to rationing" means the city is going to visibly change — empty shelves, queues, arguments — and that change will be background texture in Sessions 3-5. Players who solved this scene quickly feel ahead; players who worked slowly feel behind. Both experiences are valid. Neither locks the campaign into a specific outcome.
:::

## Session 3 Forward Hooks

**Hook 1 — The Spire Leak (Variant A/B/C all carry this):**
Harran knew about the Inversion inscription. Varren Aldiss at the Spire is the source. Players following this thread in Session 3 will find a mid-level administrator who convinced himself that giving the Reckoning advance warning would prevent violence at the Ashring — and who is now looking at evidence that the opposite occurred. He is not a villain. He made a bad decision that he told himself was protective. He is waiting for someone to help him understand what to do now that the decision has been made.

**Hook 2 — Aldric's Recovery (Variant B/C carry this, Variant A dormant):**
Aldric Stone needs one thing before Session 4's ritual decision scenes: acknowledgment from a player that what he did tonight was seen and valued. He will be at the Ashring most evenings. He will not ask. He will be waiting. Players who visit him in Session 3 and give him this moment unlock his full cooperation for the Inversion attempt.

**Hook 3 — The Chancellor's Reply:**
Players have the option to send a reply to Chancellor Ostenveld with Sera Moth. What they tell her shapes Session 3's opening. If they tell her the full truth (Dawnborn must die or freely consent, Harran is moving to coerce), she moves into crisis mode and begins direct intervention in the Reckoning's operations. If they tell her partial truth, she works with what she has. If they say nothing, she assumes they need more time and the seven-day clock runs in silence.

**Hook 4 — Isolde Maren (Session 3 open thread):**
Theron gave players Isolde's location in Scene 4: Weavers' Terrace, Dusk Parishes, Ashring district. If players go to her in Session 3, they find a researcher who has been waiting for someone with archive access and investigative reach. She has been aware, for three years, that someone would eventually come. She has prepared for it. She has notes that will change how players understand the timeline entirely.

**Hook 5 — What the Dawnborn Don't Know:**
Players leave Session 2 holding a truth that the Dawnborn do not have: the ritual requires their death or their free consent. Every conversation with a Dawnborn in Session 3 is now a decision: do they tell them, when, how, and in what order. Session 3's Dawnborn scenes are built for this exact weight. There is no guidance on the right answer. There is only what players choose and what follows.
