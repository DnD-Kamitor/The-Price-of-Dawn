## Archetype Inserts

Use these during or immediately before the opening image. Deliver as internal observation framing — what the character notices first based on who they are.

---

### The Soldier / Fighter / Martial Type

> The soldiers move well. Not parade-ground well — field well. They've spaced themselves without being told to. Someone designated the blocker before the room was entered. The east soldier is carrying, not fighting. The one nearest you isn't going for a weapon — he's going for position.
>
> You clock it without deciding to: two exits (west door, west window), one bottleneck (the tables), one fire hazard that's already active. The Lieutenant hasn't drawn his sword. He doesn't need to yet.

**The hook:** What does your character read first — the exit routes, the weapons, or the documents? The answer tells you something about who they've become.

**Scene question:** Have you ever burned something on someone else's order? What was it?

---

### The Scholar / Wizard / Investigator

> Those aren't random papers. The bundles are tied — not with Archive cordage, which uses blue ribbon, but with plain waxed thread. Someone prepared these outside the Archive and had them placed here. Or someone inside the Archive prepared them for someone outside.
>
> The labels are in a notation hand you recognize from last session. These were organized by someone who understood what they were organizing. The burn order had a manifest. The question is who wrote it.

**The hook:** Someone did the advance research. Someone knew which documents were the right ones. That work was done before the players arrived, before this operation began. How long before?

**Scene question:** If you could only save one bundle, which would you choose — and how would you know which was most important without reading them first?

---

### The Rogue / Infiltrator

> Bundle C is nearest the door. One action. You could have it and be through the west window before the blocking soldier takes two steps. The question isn't whether you can do it — the question is what's in the other two.
>
> You notice: the Lieutenant is not watching the door. He's watching the east end of the room. He expected the threat to come from the inside, not the outside. That's an assumption. He hasn't corrected for you yet.

**The hook:** You have the clearest escape path in the room. The question is whether you use it for yourself, for a bundle, or not at all.

**Scene question:** At what point does taking the easy win cost more than it gains?

---

### The Cleric / Religious Type

> Above the east shelf — almost behind the fireplace — there's a devotional marker. Small, carved, faded. Auris symbols, the sun-circle in its old form, pre-schism. It's facing the fireplace.
>
> Someone placed it there to face this direction. The Archive has dozens of these markers, distributed at the entrances to stacks, above reading alcoves — they face outward, toward the readers, toward the light. This one faces the fire.
>
> Someone put it there to watch this happen. You don't know if that means they knew this day would come, or if they wanted it to.

**The hook:** The marker is not an accident. This scene has a religious dimension that the secular players around you may not clock. What does it mean to have a symbol of Auris facing a burning?

**Scene question:** Is this a desecration of the Archive, or a completion of something the Archive was built around?

---

### The Social / Face Character

> The Lieutenant is not performing. There's no theater in how he moves — no demonstration of authority, no gesture toward the documents to make a point. He picked up Bundle B, read a page, set it down. He signaled the burn without announcing it. He is doing a job the way someone does a job they've done before.
>
> That tells you something about the organization behind him. This is not improvised. This is not reactive. This is a standing procedure that someone finally authorized. The question is when that authorization came down — and what it was waiting for.

**The hook:** You read people for a living. What you see here is a professional executing a prior decision, not a man making a choice in this room. The choice was made elsewhere. By someone else. Someone you haven't met yet.

**Scene question:** Is there anything you could say to him that would make him stop — not through persuasion, but through showing him information that changes his calculation?

## Passive Knowledge Triggers

These activate automatically — no roll required. Deliver them as observations in the opening image, before initiative.

| Background / Feature | Automatic Knowledge |
|---|---|
| **Criminal background** | You recognize the Reckoning tabard insignia immediately — grey field, black sun-circle. You've seen it on wanted posters, on checkpoint notices, on the doors of houses where people stopped coming out. The Reckoning has institutional authority but no formal city jurisdiction. They operate in the space between city law and Spire mandate. They've been in that space for a long time. |
| **History or Noble background** | The Archive holds institutional protection under City Charter, Article 14: *No armed body may conduct operations within Archive premises without published warrant and Archivist countersignature.* There is no notice posted on the door. There is no Archivist present. What is happening in this room is technically a charter violation — the kind that gets buried quietly, not prosecuted, but real enough to matter if someone puts it in a formal record. |
| **Military background** | The soldiers knew this room before they entered it. The spacing, the blocker position, the fact that one soldier is already at the east table while another covers the door — that's briefing, not improvisation. Someone gave them a floor plan, or someone who knew the room walked them through it. This operation was planned at least 24 hours in advance. Probably longer. |
| **Arcana or religious background (Auris)** | The devotional marker above the east shelf is not standard Archive placement. Archive markers face toward reading areas — they're oriented toward the people using the space. This one faces the fireplace. In old Auris theology, that orientation is used in completion rituals: the symbol is placed to witness an ending, not to illuminate a beginning. |
| **Investigation proficiency (passive 13+)** | The bundles were pre-sorted by someone who understood the Archive's internal organization system — the classification marks on the labels use archival notation that isn't public. This list was written by someone with restricted catalog access. Either an Archivist, or someone with an Archivist's cooperation. |

## Cross-Archetype Moment

If two or more players engage the Lieutenant simultaneously — one physical, one social:

> He looks between you. For the first time, he is not reading a document. He is reading a situation. He says, quietly: *"You already know what these say. Otherwise you wouldn't be here this fast."* It's not a question. He's filed it away. Whatever he does next, he does it knowing that.

This moment costs him nothing tactically. But it's the first time he has acknowledged that the players may be ahead of the burn order rather than behind it. Let it sit in the room.
