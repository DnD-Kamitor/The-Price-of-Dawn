## Crestwick Lane — The Exit

The Dawnhall door closes behind the last player. The warmth ends. What replaces it is not cold exactly — Varenhold is not a cold city, only a dim one — but the absence of warmth is immediate and physical. The lane is stone and the stone has had all evening to settle into the temperature of the perpetual dusk.

Crestwick Lane runs south from the Dawnhall door. Fifty feet ahead, where the lane meets the mouth of Ashring Alley, a man is standing.

He is not hiding. He is not blocking the lane. He is positioned slightly to the right of the lane's center, at the alley mouth, in dark robes. He has been there long enough that his breathing is unhurried. He is looking at you. He was looking at you when the door opened.

He waited. That is the first thing players understand about him: he chose this moment. He could have intercepted the players at the Archive door this morning, at the Lowmark gate, at the Dawnhall entrance an hour ago. He waited until after Sera Voss. He is standing in the place where players would see him from the doorway, not closer. He gave them the option to avoid him. He did not take the option away.

## Arrival Read-Aloud

*Read this as the Dawnhall door closes behind the last player:*

> The door closes. The warmth cuts off and you're standing in Crestwick Lane with cold stone under your boots and the sound of the soup kitchen muffled behind two inches of oak.
>
> The lane runs south. Amber lanterns on iron hooks — two of them, one on each side, forty feet apart — throw small overlapping circles of light against the cobblestones. Between them and the Dawnhall door behind you there is a pool of half-dark where the lanterns don't quite reach. The sky above the lane is its usual twilight, the permanent not-quite-dark of a city that has been waiting fifty years for the light to make a decision.
>
> At the mouth of Ashring Alley, fifty feet south, a man is standing.
>
> He is not in your way. He is standing slightly to the right, in dark robes, with his hands at his sides. He has not moved since the door opened. You can see, behind him and further into the alley mouth, the shapes of four more people standing in shadow. They are not advancing.
>
> The man at the front looks at you across fifty feet of empty lane. He waited. He is still waiting.

## Five Senses

**Sight**

Crestwick Lane is narrow — fifteen feet across, three-story buildings on both sides, no windows at street level. The amber lanterns on their hooks cast warm light that does not reach the alley mouth where Edoran stands. He is visible in outline: dark robes, moderate height, grey at the temples, standing still. His posture is not aggressive. It is patient.

The four Restorer Zealots behind him are visible as shapes in the alley shadow, not as individuals. They are not dressed for violence — robes like Edoran's, darker. One of them has his hands folded. They did not advance when the door opened. They will not advance unless Edoran moves.

Fifty feet of empty cobblestone separates players from Edoran. The lane is completely open. There is a passage to the west at thirty feet that exits onto a wider street — players could go around him with minimal detour. He has not blocked it.

**Sound**

The Dawnhall door sealing: a solid sound, not a slam. The soup kitchen noise reducing to near-silence through that door — the occasional scrape of a bench, the bell distant and already past. The city beyond Crestwick Lane: cart wheels on stone two streets over, a conversation somewhere above, the indeterminate sound of forty thousand people managing an evening.

Edoran is not speaking yet. What you hear from him, as you cross those fifty feet, is his breathing — unhurried, even, the breathing of a man who has been standing here for some time and is not troubled by it.

The silence between the Dawnhall door and his first word belongs to whoever is walking it.

**Smell**

Cold lane air: stone, dust, the faint chimney smoke of a district that heats its buildings with wood and whatever trades through the eastern gate. The soup kitchen smell on the players' clothing — broth and woodsmoke — is noticeable in contrast to the cold air of the lane.

From the alley mouth: nothing alarming. The zealots are simply people in robes. The smell of the lane here is the smell of an old city at night: stone that has absorbed rain and years, and the particular mineral quality of alleys that never see direct light.

**Touch**

Cold on exposed skin — the back of the hand, the neck, anywhere the Dawnhall's warmth was sitting. The cobblestones under the boots are uneven; Crestwick Lane is old and was not laid by anyone who expected it to be much used. Footing is reliable but requires attention.

At the alley mouth, if players close to within ten feet of Edoran: the faint warmth of four bodies in a confined space behind him, the air slightly less cold than the open lane.

**Taste**

The lingering warmth of the Dawnhall — broth absorbed into the air — fades with each step south. By the time players reach Edoran's position, that warmth is gone. What replaces it is the clean mineral cold of a lane in Lowmark at night, and the particular taste of nothing, which is the city's baseline flavor for fifty years.

## What the Lane Tells Players (Before Edoran Speaks)

- He is alone at the front. The four zealots are behind him, in shadow, positioned as presence rather than threat. They have not advanced. This is a different formation than the intercept at Lowmark Junction.
- He gave players an out. The western passage at thirty feet is visible and unblocked. He positioned himself where they can see him and where they can also avoid him. The choice to approach is theirs.
- He waited. The door has not been closed for five seconds. He has been here longer than that — his breathing and stillness are not the stillness of someone who just arrived. He waited through their entire time in the Dawnhall.
- He waited specifically until after Sera Voss. Not before. This is deliberate.

---

## Scene Duration Note

This is the final scene of Session 1. It should last 10–15 minutes at the table. The session does not extend past it — when Edoran steps aside and says his last line, the session is done. Do not begin anything new. Let the scene close the night.
