## Scene 3 — Personal Hooks by Archetype

These inserts are delivered during puzzle engagement — after the players have seen the star-wheel mechanism and the chart on the wall, but before the first ring is turned. Each is addressed quietly to one player while the others are absorbing the room. The goal is not to tell each character what to do — it is to make the mechanism feel like it was built for the particular person now standing in front of it.

---

## Part 1 — Per-Archetype Sensory Inserts

Deliver each to the relevant player individually, as a brief aside during the first examination of the star-wheel. These are additions to the scene setup, not replacements for the general narration.

---

### To the Investigator

> The chart on the wall is not a concealment. Everything you need to read this mechanism is here, legible, waiting. The star-wheel system, the ring correspondences, the constellation names — all present, all labeled. Corven did not try to hide how the lock works. He put the instructions on the wall. That is a specific professional choice: he did not want to prevent the wrong person from finding this room. He wanted the right person to be able to find it without needing help from anyone who might obstruct them. He built a self-contained investigation — the evidence and the method in the same room. Someone who expected to be investigated but wanted to control how the investigation proceeded. A man who trusted process over gatekeeping. Whatever is behind shelf 4-17-3, he intended it to be found. He intended it to be found correctly. He did not intend it to stay sealed.

*GM note: The Investigator's read here is professional and precise. Corven's design philosophy is itself evidence — about his intentions, his fears, and about who or what he was trying to route around. The Investigator should feel, in this moment, a kind of posthumous respect across the work. Do not elaborate on what Corven feared. That becomes clearer when the shelf opens.*

---

### To the Believer

> The system is not Auris. No solar calendar, no grief-thread notation, no martyrs' days. It's pure astronomical — the sky as it is, not the sky as a theological frame. Ancient. The alignment predates the Auris calendar by at least a hundred years. But then you look at the notation key excerpt pinned below the chart, the fragment about weight borne willingly, and you know that language. "The weight borne willingly is not the same weight as the weight imposed." That is the Litany of the Open Hand, translated into engineering notation. Corven was not writing theology. He was writing physics. But he arrived at the same place the Litany arrived at, from a completely different direction. He was measuring a force. The Litany is measuring a moral category. They are describing the same thing. Whether you believe that means Auris was right, or that Corven was right, or that they were both observing something real that neither fully understood — this is where that question lives.

*GM note: If the Believer is Penitent-faction, the convergence of empirical and devotional framing is a form of confirmation that will be complicated rather than comfortable. If they are Wounded-faction, this reads as Auris speaking through unlikely instruments, which is standard Wounded theology. Either way, the consent gloss is not incidental. Corven understood what the Litany understood. The question of why the ritual happened anyway — if he understood consent as an engineering requirement — is the question the Believer will be carrying out of this scene.*

---

### To the Survivor

> The cedar smell hits you before you identify it. The north end of the stacks. Shelf 4-17-3 — the one the mechanism is aimed at. It smells like someone stored something there with care. Cedar is a preservation choice. You know what you choose to preserve and what you let go — you have been making that calculation for long enough that it has changed how you think about rooms. The Archive around you is not preserved. It is maintained — filed and ordered but not *tended*. There is a difference. This one shelf, in this restricted room, was stored with cedar because whoever put something there wanted it to last past their own lifetime. They were thinking about people who would come later. They were thinking about whether those people would find what they needed. You are standing in the room. The smell is telling you: someone thought about you before you were here.

*GM note: Do not resolve this with sentiment. The Survivor's calculation is always existential — resources, time, what can be saved. Let the cedar observation be a moment where someone else's long-term thinking about the future makes contact with the Survivor's very immediate version of the same concern. The shelf was packed for people who would come when the person who packed it was gone. The Survivor knows something about how that preparation feels.*

---

### To the Scholar

> You have been corresponding with Theron Waide for three years. Letters, documents, supplementary folios, occasional careful questions answered with more careful not-quite-answers. You are now in the same room with him for the first time, working a puzzle, and you have been watching him for four minutes. He has not looked at shelf 4-17-3 once. Not casually, not by accident. He is oriented slightly away from it. When the mechanism clicks on the first ring, his eyes move to the chart — not to the shelf. He knows where the shelf is without looking at it. He knows this room. He knows this puzzle. He knows the answer, and he is standing very still, watching you work toward it, and he has not said so.

*GM note: The Scholar's three years of correspondence are the ground under this entire scene. Theron chose what documents to send, chose what research threads to encourage, chose what questions to answer obliquely rather than directly. The Scholar was not just corresponding — they were being guided toward this room. Theron will not volunteer this explanation during the scene. If the Scholar confronts him directly — "You know how to solve this" — he will not deny it. He will say: "Yes." And wait to see what the Scholar does with that.*

---

### To the Mercenary

> You have been assessing this room since you walked in. Here is what the room tells you: the mechanism is not a deterrent. It is a filter. Someone designed it so that getting to the documents inside requires — at minimum — literacy in historical astronomical calendars, enough knowledge to work a three-ring correspondence system, and the ability to cross-reference a star chart. That is a specific profile. Whatever is in this shelf was not locked away from everyone. It was locked away from everyone who couldn't demonstrate a particular kind of understanding first. You can solve this puzzle. You have enough to work it. But you were not the intended audience. You are qualified by accident or by preparation you didn't know was preparation. The mechanism is telling you: whoever arrives at shelf 4-17-3 was supposed to already understand why they needed to be there. You are in the room. The question is whether that's enough.

*GM note: The Mercenary's read is accurate and professionally unsettling. The job was stated as routine document retrieval. Routine document retrieval does not require navigating a Corven-designed astronomical filter system inside a restricted archive. The gap between the stated job and the actual job is visible and the Mercenary can see exactly how wide it is. Let this register as intelligence, not alarm. They file it.*

---

### To the Dawnborn-Adjacent

> The skylight is directly overhead. You can see stars — the same stars on the chart. They are the same stars visible from anywhere in Varenhold. From the Dawnhall. From the street outside. You have the specific thought: Sera has looked at those stars. She looks at the sky every evening when she locks up; you know this about her the way you know small habitual things about people you are close to. She has looked at those specific stars, probably many times, without knowing they were the key to this room. The chart on the wall is a map of something she has seen. And she wrote you a letter she could not finish because there was something happening she could not commit to paper. Whatever the mechanism leads to — whatever is in shelf 4-17-3 — the stars overhead connect this room to the same sky she stands under. She has been that close to this answer without knowing it was an answer.

*GM note: Sera Voss's letter is the Dawnborn-Adjacent's reason for being here. This insert creates an astronomical continuity between the Archive skylight and the sky visible from the Dawnhall — a small specific detail that makes the investigation feel like it has been surrounding Sera's world without her knowing it. When the shelf opens and the documents mention "living conduit mechanism," the Dawnborn-Adjacent will be the player most likely to think immediately of Sera's unfinished sentence.*

## Part 3 — Check-Result Narrations

When a player succeeds on one of the scene's active checks, deliver the story of what they perceive, not the mechanical result.

---

**Insight DC 13 — on Theron (observing him during puzzle engagement):**

> He is not reading the chart. His eyes rest on it but they are not moving across it the way a person reads something unfamiliar. He already knows what it says. What he is watching is you — tracking your progress, tracking the moment when the alignment becomes clear to you. He has the particular stillness of a man who has rehearsed a scene many times in private and is now watching someone else perform it for the first time. When you look at him directly, he looks at the chart. The redirect is practiced. He has been managing his attention in this room for a while.

---

**Insight DC 15 — on Theron when shelf 4-17-3 opens:**

> The expression that moves across his face when the shelf rotates into the room is not relief. It is not what you expected — not satisfaction, not vindication. It is closer to what a person looks like when they set down something heavy they have carried a long distance. Not joy. The specific exhaustion and lightness of a weight that is finally not in your hands anymore. He has been carrying the knowledge of what is in that shelf for eleven years. He is watching someone else pick it up. There is grief in it, but the grief is old. What is new is that it is no longer only his.

---

**Investigation DC 12 — on the star chart (annotated instruction):**

> Below the main chart legend, in a smaller hand that matches the notation key style: *"Rise order, west to east. As above, so the key turns."* You could read it as instruction — which ring first, which direction. But it is more than instruction. Corven put the sky on the wall so the answer would always be literally overhead. On any clear night, anyone who could read the chart could look up through the skylight and find the answer without any additional reference material. He did not seal this room with secret knowledge. He sealed it with the requirement to look up. The chart is not a hint. It is a design principle: the system explains itself to anyone who is willing to observe it.

*GM note: "As above, so the key turns" is the annotation. No further check is needed to read it — it is legible to anyone who examines the chart carefully. The DC 12 Investigation is to perceive that the annotation is not merely procedural but is a statement of Corven's design philosophy: he built the lock so the answer was overhead, always available, requiring only attention. This matters later when the documents discuss the ritual — Corven believed in making things findable by the right process, not hiding them from the wrong people.*

---

**Arcana DC 14 — on the notation key fragment:**

> The technical entries are clipped and precise — one clause, one term, no elaboration. The consent glosses are different. They are longer. They qualify themselves. They loop back and restate. "The weight borne willingly is not the same weight as the weight imposed" is followed by three additional sentences that say the same thing in different terms, as if Corven was not sure the first formulation was sufficient. He was not documenting a known principle. He was working something out as he wrote. The difference in register between the technical entries and the consent entries is the difference between writing down what you know and writing down what you are still trying to understand. Corven built the consent requirement into the ritual's architecture, but he did not fully understand it yet when he wrote this. He was still arriving at it.

---

**History DC 12 — on the phrase "distributed sympathetic anchors" (from Corven's public summary):**

> Technical term, ritual architecture register: a sympathetic anchor is a ritual component that holds a sympathetic bond in a specific location — think of it as a fixed point in a network of linked effects. Distributed means multiple anchors maintaining a single shared effect across a geographic area rather than in one place. The architecture distributes the ritual load so no single anchor bears the full weight. You understand the mechanics. What you do not yet understand is what the anchors were made of — or who. The public summary uses the technical vocabulary without the human component. The phrase is clear. What it describes is not yet.

*GM note: This is an accurate technical reading, intentionally incomplete. "Distributed sympathetic anchors" in the context of the Dawn Ritual means the anchors were people — Dawnborn volunteers who held portions of the ritual's sympathetic load. The History check gives the players the mechanical understanding; the documents in the shelf will supply the human cost. Do not elaborate beyond the mechanics on this check. The gap between what the phrase means technically and what it meant in practice is the horror the scene is building toward.*

---

**Perception DC 14 — cedar smell as shelf 4-17-3 rotates:**

> When the mechanism completes and shelf 4-17-3 rotates toward the room, the cedar smell intensifies — concentrated, immediate, the smell of a cedar-lined box opened after years of keeping. It is not ambient. It is specific to this shelf. The documents here were stored with deliberate preservation care: cedar against damp, against insects, against the slow decay that takes paper in fifty years if no one attends to it. Someone packed this shelf for people who would not arrive for a long time. The preservation was not maintenance. It was an act of intention directed at an unknown future reader. The smell is what that intention has been doing for fifty years.

## Part 4 — Post-Scene Personal Questions

After Scene 3 ends — after the shelf opens, after the documents are in hand, after Theron's expression has been read and the cedar smell has faded — each archetype is left holding a different version of the same problem.

**Investigator:** *The document retrieval was the stated job. Shelf 4-17-3 required navigating a Corven-designed astronomical lock inside a restricted Archive section, after a street intercept involving trained operatives and a detour through a notation-sealed door. At what point does completing the commission require me to understand who actually hired me and what they were actually trying to retrieve — and why they needed someone else to do it?*

**Believer:** *Corven built consent into the ritual as an engineering requirement. The notation key says it: willingly-given weight is not the same as weight imposed, and this is physics, not philosophy. The ritual happened anyway. Someone who understood that distinction well enough to write it into a lock built the thing that broke that requirement. What broke — the understanding, or the person who had it?*

**Survivor:** *"Living conduit mechanism." The public summary uses that phrase. The anchors were distributed. The word "living" is the tell — not inert materials, not fixed locations. Something that was alive was used as a component. If it was living and willing, is that different from being consumed? At what point does choosing to be part of something that kills you slowly stop being consent and become something else?*

**Scholar:** *Three years of letters. Documents chosen and sequenced. A research relationship built carefully enough that I can read the notation on a fifty-year-old lock because of folios Theron sent me in Year 37. He was not corresponding with me. He was preparing me. He knew this room. He knew what he was doing. The question is whether he knew he was doing it — and whether it matters if he did.*

**Mercenary:** *"Routine document retrieval." The documents are behind an astronomical puzzle lock inside a restricted Archive section that required a notation-sealed door to access, which required a separate bypass of Archive staff, which was preceded by a street intercept involving operatives who knew the route in advance. The phrase "routine document retrieval" does not cover any of that. Whoever hired me either did not know what the job would involve — or knew exactly.*

**Dawnborn-Adjacent:** *The public summary mentions a ritual that used living willing participants as distributed anchors. Sera wrote a letter she could not finish. She runs the Dawnhall — the place where Dawnborn gather, where people with the specific capacity the ritual required gather. She has access to the exact population the ritual needed. She couldn't commit something to paper. Does she know what the ritual cost the people who volunteered for it? Does she know someone who did?*

## Part 5 — Callback Hooks (Session Zero Dependent)

If players established specific background details in Session Zero, use these amplifications when delivering the sensory inserts above.

**Investigator** — *If the player specified a prior case or established a professional methodology:* When describing Corven's design choice to put the instructions on the wall, connect it to the Investigator's own practice: *"You have done this. Left the method legible to the right investigator, obscure only to the wrong one. Corven ran a clean investigation. The evidence is here. He trusted the process more than the lock."* The Investigator recognises a colleague's professional philosophy across fifty years of distance.

**Believer** — *If the player specified their Auris faction (Penitent or Wounded):* The consent-gloss convergence lands differently by tradition. For a **Penitent**: Corven arrived at Auris doctrine through empirical measurement, which is the Penitent model — observation precedes belief. The notation key is a data point in a theological argument the character has been making in a different register. For a **Wounded**: Corven was tending the same wound Auris tends — the gap between what was freely given and what was taken. He just called it physics. For **any faction**: the specific formulation "willingly borne" appears in three separate Auris texts; if the character has read any of them, they will recognise the clause before they finish reading the line.

**Survivor** — *If the player named their sick person in Session Zero:* When delivering the cedar-smell insert, name the person once, quietly, without pause: *"Someone packed this shelf for people who would come when they were gone. You know what that kind of thinking feels like. You have been thinking about [name] the same way — what to arrange, what to prepare, for the possibility that the arrangement matters more than the preparation."* Do not wait for a response. Continue. The player holds it.

**Scholar** — *If the player named specific documents Waide sent them or identified a research focus:* When describing Theron's deliberate not-looking at shelf 4-17-3, make the correspondence specific: *"He sent you the [folio name/subject] in [season, year]. The notation spacing in that margin is the same notation spacing on the star chart. He sent you the key to this room without telling you the room existed. Whether he knew he was doing it — that is the question you are now standing in front of."*

**Mercenary** — *If the player named their employer or established the stated scope of the commission:* When the Mercenary reads the mechanism as a qualification filter, make the employer specific: *"[Employer's description of the job]. 'Routine.' The filter on this shelf was designed by the most significant ritual architect in the city's history. Either [employer] does not know what is behind it — or they do, and they needed someone expendable enough to go through it first."* Do not resolve which. Leave it in the file.

**Dawnborn-Adjacent** — *If the player shared specific details of Sera's letter or their relationship history with her:* When describing the skylight connection to the Dawnhall sky, use a detail from the established relationship: *"She mentioned the skylight in [specific context if established]. Or she would have — you know her well enough to know she notices that kind of thing. The same sky. She has been standing under the answer without knowing it was an answer, and she wrote you a letter she couldn't finish, and now you are holding the beginning of what she couldn't write."*

### Theron Waide and the Shelf Opening — The Moment of Weight Set Down

This beat happens when shelf 4-17-3 rotates into the room. Theron is in the stacks, present since his guided entry during the puzzle. The whole table sees his expression. The Scholar, if present and watching, gets the full version.

The mechanism completes. Cedar smell. The shelf rotates.

Theron does not move toward the shelf. He stands where he is and watches it open.

> His face does something that takes a moment to name. It is not relief. Not satisfaction. It is closer to the specific expression of a man who has been carrying a stone for a long time, and has now given it to someone else to carry — and is standing in the moment between having held it and no longer holding it, in the exact space where the weight used to be.

> He says nothing for several seconds.

> Then: *"Those documents have been in there for fifty years. Corven put them there before anyone understood what they were documenting. He put them there for people who would come when the city needed to know."*

> He looks at the shelf. Not at the party.

> *"I have been the only person who could open that shelf for eleven years. I did not open it."*

> He looks at the party now.

> *"You should read what is in there before you decide what to do with it."*

*GM note: Theron's statement — "I did not open it" — is not an apology and is not intended as one. It is a statement of fact about the choice he made. He does not explain the choice during this scene. If the Scholar or anyone else asks why, he says: "Because I was not certain anyone would know what to do with it. I am still not certain." He goes to wait in the reading room. He is available for Scene 4 conversation if the party seeks him out.*

---

*Design note: Scene 3 is a puzzle scene that is also a research scene — the mechanism filters for astronomical literacy, but the documents inside filter for something harder, which is the capacity to understand what they describe and what it means. The personal hooks exist to ensure that when the phrase "living conduit mechanism" appears in Corven's public summary, each player has a different specific reason to stop at the word "living." The Investigator stops because it is evidence about the nature of the crime. The Believer stops because it is evidence about what broke the consent requirement. The Survivor stops because they are thinking about what willingness costs. The Scholar stops because they are thinking about Theron standing in front of this shelf for eleven years. The Mercenary stops because the phrase is not in the job description. The Dawnborn-Adjacent stops because of Sera. The puzzle has one solution. The document has six different first sentences, depending on who is reading it.*
