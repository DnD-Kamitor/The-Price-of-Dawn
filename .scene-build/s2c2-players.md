## What This Scene Offers

- **Intellectual satisfaction:** A solvable cipher with clear inputs and a revelatory output
- **Emotional weight:** The moment of decoding — reading the truth aloud and watching the man who has carried it for eleven years hear it said by someone else for the first time
- **Character revelation:** Theron is one of the campaign's most important NPCs. This is his introduction as someone with agency, not just exposition

This is the scene where players realize what they have been hired to investigate. The early sessions positioned this as a historical mystery. Scene 2 of Session 2 is where it becomes a present-tense moral problem.

---

## Primary Engagement: Solving the Cipher

Give the players the text in a form they can work with. Handout preferred. VTT text drop acceptable. Reading aloud twice if necessary.

The puzzle has four designed entry points:
1. Structural recognition (the text feels encoded — DC 8 Investigation)
2. Method identification (every Nth word — DC 12 Investigation or History DC 8 background)
3. Parameter finding (N=3 — confirmed by "third circuit" Arcana, or by trial)
4. Full extraction (reading the answer aloud)

Most tables will move through these stages in roughly 10–20 minutes with the hint system available. The puzzle is designed to be solvable, not gatekeeping.

---

## Secondary Engagement: Reading Theron

The puzzle is the door. Theron is the scene.

Players who are watching him while others work the cipher will find things. His stillness is not neutral. His pen case is not just a prop. His word choice is precise in ways that reward attention.

Insight checks are available throughout (see Mechanics). The GM should invite this engagement by describing Theron's posture and small actions at intervals — not constantly, but at natural pause points when the puzzle work has quieted for a moment.

> *"Theron watches the table. He has not moved except to straighten his pen case once."*

That is enough. Players who want to read him will ask. Players who want to solve first will finish the cipher and come back to him.

---

## Player Type Matrix

### Puzzle Players
This scene is designed for them. The cipher is fair, multi-layered, and solvable without GM assistance if they think systematically. Give them the slips in writing and step back. Let them work.

If they solve it quickly: honor the speed. The moment of reading the answer aloud carries the same weight at five minutes as at twenty.

### RP Players
Theron is the scene. The cipher is the door. They may spend their energy trying to get him to just tell them — a reasonable strategy that will not quite work, but will produce meaningful conversation about why he encoded it this way and what it cost him.

Let those conversations happen. They will decode Theron as a person even if they do not decode the slips. Either path leads to the same truth.

### Combat Players
There is nothing to fight here. The combat was Scene 1.

Frame this scene as intelligence work — the extraction of information from a difficult source, under time pressure (implied: the Reckoning knows the Archive now, and this location is compromised). Combat players respect intelligence-gathering as a skill set. They do not need to be told the puzzle is interesting; they need to be told why the information matters and who it affects.

> *"You just fought off a group willing to die to suppress these documents. What you're holding now is what they were willing to die to suppress."*

That framing helps.

### Cautious Players
They may be suspicious of Theron. Why is he helping now, right after a raid? Why does he have a cipher ready? Was the raid staged to create exactly this vulnerability?

These are good instincts. They should be rewarded with Insight check opportunities, not dismissed. Theron's genuine complexity — the cowardice, the correction of the third slip, the eleven years — is visible to players who are suspicious enough to look.

He is not a trap. But he is not simple. Cautious players reading that will trust him more than cautious players who were reassured too quickly.

### Impatient Players
The puzzle is the scene. If table energy is flagging and the cipher work has gone too long, give them the number three as a direct hint:

> *Theron says, quietly: "Count by threes."*

This breaks the formal hint structure but preserves table momentum. The answer matters more than the solve. If they race to the answer from that hint, they still have to read it aloud. The "Yes" still lands.

---

## Post-Decode: The Open Moment

After the answer is read and Theron says "Yes" — before moving to Scene 3 setup — ask the table:

> *"What does your character do with this information in the next five minutes?"*

Not as a Scene 4 setup question. Not as a mechanical prompt. Just as a moment of open space.

Let each player answer briefly. Some will have reactions they've been holding since "THE DAWNBORN ARE." Some will say their character is processing. Some will want to ask Theron another question immediately.

All of these are correct. The open moment is a brief exhale before the next scene. Let it breathe, then move to Scene 3 transition when it feels natural.

Do not rush from the "Yes" to the next room. The "Yes" is a scene in itself.
