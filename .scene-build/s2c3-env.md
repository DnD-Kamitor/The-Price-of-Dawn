## Physical Space: Theron's Private Study, Upper Archive Floor

The study is one flight of stairs above the reading room where Scene 1 took place. The staircase is narrow, stone, unlit — the only lamp is at the top landing, and it was already burning when the players arrive. Theron did not light it to welcome them. It is always lit when he is in the study. He has been here since the soldiers left.

### Room Dimensions and Layout

Small. The kind of room a person fills just by being in it. Approximately five paces wide, seven deep. The door opens inward on the south wall. One high, narrow window on the north wall — barely wide enough to admit a hand. Bookshelves line the east and west walls from floor to ceiling, packed tight enough that the spines lean slightly outward from the pressure of the books behind them. The shelves have that particular Archive smell: aged vellum, dried leather, the dry mineral scent of ink that has not been wet in decades.

**The desk** is centered between the bookshelves, facing the door. It is not a large desk — one working surface, two drawers, no adornment. The desk has been cleared of everything except the box, which Theron placed there himself before the players arrived. He did not do this in a hurry. He did it deliberately.

**The chair behind the desk** is Theron's — the only padded chair in the room. Two plain wooden chairs sit in front of the desk, slightly angled toward each other, as if whoever arranged them expected two people to sit and talk. Theron arranged them. He did not arrange a chair for himself outside his desk position. He has not decided yet which side of the desk to be on when they arrive.

**The shelf gap** on the east wall, at eye height behind the desk: an obvious absence where the box used to stand. The shelf bracket shows a slight impression, a rectangle of fractionally less dust than the space around it. The box has been in this exact spot for eleven years. That gap will be visible once the box is on the desk.

### The Box: First Impression

The box is dark wood — walnut, or something that has aged to look like walnut. It is approximately the size of a large book laid flat, perhaps twenty centimeters long, twelve wide, eight deep. The corners are worn smooth. The lid has been touched many times; not opened, but touched. The wood there is slightly warmer in color than the sides, where less handling has occurred.

**Three circular indentations** on the lid, arranged left to right at even intervals, each about the diameter of a finger. They are shallow — a few millimeters at most — and smooth-edged, as if machined. They are not decorative. There is no visible hinge. There is no visible seam between the lid and the body of the box. It appears, at first glance, to be a solid piece of wood.

When a player picks it up: it is heavier than it looks. Not shockingly heavy — the weight of something with dense contents, or internal mechanisms. And it is warm. Not body-warm, not room-warm — warmer than either, the particular warmth of a mechanism under low continuous tension. If a player mentions this to Theron, he does not react with surprise. He has known about the warmth for eleven years. He stopped noticing it at some point. It is only strange to someone touching it for the first time.

### Starting Positions

When players arrive at the study:

- **Theron** is standing behind his desk. Not sitting. Standing, slightly to the left of center, so the box is between him and the door. He is looking at the door when it opens. He was waiting.
- **The box** is on the desk surface, centered. It has been positioned, not set down casually.
- **The two chairs** in front of the desk are available. Theron will not invite players to sit. He will not tell them to stand. He will wait for them to decide.
- **The door** behind the players closes on its own — the frame is slightly warped and it falls shut. The latch catches. The room is quiet.

### Five Senses

**Sight:** Dusty afternoon amber through the narrow north window — one bar of light falling across the corner of the desk, catching the box's left side. The lamp at the door landing is behind the players when they enter. The room is dim but not dark. Theron's face is readable. The box is the most visible thing in the room: centered, bare, deliberate.

**Sound:** Silence. Complete Archive silence — the soldiers are gone, the reading room below is empty. No voices from outside. The building has returned to itself. The only sound is Theron breathing, slightly too carefully, the controlled breath of a man managing something. If players are quiet enough, they may notice he has been waiting here long enough that his breathing has its own rhythm.

**Smell:** Old wood. Old paper. The smell of a room that belongs entirely to one person — the specific accumulation of one person's particular preferences in oils and teas and the particular books they choose to keep nearby. There is no woodsmoke up here. That is below. Up here it is only Theron's room, and it smells like eleven years of a man alone with a decision he has not made.

**Touch:** The box, warm against the palm. The desk edge, cold. The chairs are plain wood, no cushion. If a player touches the bookshelf, it is solid, immovable, packed tight.

**Taste:** The metallic ghost of the combat below has followed them up the stairs. The adrenaline. The dust the fight knocked loose. It will fade within a few minutes of being in this room. The room wants to settle them. That is its nature.

### What Players Know Walking In

- They know Theron has a box. He mentioned it at some point during or after Scene 2 — if he did not say so directly, he gestured toward the stairs, or simply led them here, which communicates the same thing.
- They know it has been sealed for eleven years, that Theron placed it there himself after the night of the ritual redirection.
- They have the documents from Scene 1 (some or all, depending on what was burned). They may or may not have examined the colophons.
- They do not yet know the names are the key. They do not know what is inside. They may not know what a resonance lock is.

**What they do not know:** Why Theron placed the box on the desk before they arrived. Why he is standing rather than sitting. Why the box is already in the center of the desk, as if it has been waiting for an audience.

That last question has an answer: he put it there because he could not open it himself, and he has finally stopped pretending he ever could.
