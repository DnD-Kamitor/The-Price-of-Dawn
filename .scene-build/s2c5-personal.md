## Per-Archetype Resonance — Scene 5

---

### The Soldier / Martial PC

**You've served under commanders like Harran.**

You know what "Emergency Powers" means when it's used on civilians. Not in the abstract — in the practical sense: it means jurisdiction claims that override civilian consent, documentation that covers action after the fact, and a commander who is personally insulated from accountability because the charter said he could. You have watched this framework applied to people who did nothing wrong except be in the way of what the city needed done quickly.

You also know Harran is right about the clock. Military logistics are not a mystery to you. The food estimates he's working from match your own reading of the situation. Six months is probably accurate. Possibly optimistic. A city that cannot feed its population in six months does not survive in a recognisable form regardless of what principles were honored.

This is the specific torture of this scene for a martial character: he is the enemy you recognise. Not because you agree with him, but because you understand the framework he is operating from, and you know there are things he is right about, and you know that being partially right does not excuse what he is doing to Aldric.

**The beat:** If combat gives you a moment near Harran — particularly if you're the one whose blow triggers his Tactical Retreat — you have the option of a line before he goes. You don't have to say it. But the game will give you the space. What does your character say to someone whose logic you follow and whose methods you reject?

**What to ask this player:** *"You've served under commanders who made calls like this. What did you do then? What are you going to do now?"*

### The Scholar / Investigator PC

**Harran knows about the Inversion inscription.**

He said *"The Reckoning holds jurisdiction over the Ashring site"* as his opening line. He walked directly to the stones with targeted Veterans. He knew where Aldric was. He knew what Aldric's presence at Stone 9 meant. He came prepared for an Inversion attempt.

The Inversion inscription is recent information — players only found it when the resonance box was decoded. The only people who knew it existed were in the Archive, and then in this circle. Theron hasn't told anyone. The players haven't told anyone. Aldric doesn't understand the full technical parameters.

Someone told Harran. They told him specifically enough that he knew which stone mattered, which Dawnborn to target, and what would happen if he pulled one away.

Someone in the Spire has been watching the Ashring and reporting to the Reckoning. The investigation has a leak. Your investigation. The documents you've been assembling, the resonance you've been following — someone with access to that information thread is feeding it to the commander of the city's emergency enforcement arm.

You don't know who. You know that they know. That changes what you say, who you say it to, and what you write down for the rest of this campaign.

**The beat:** This is not a combat beat — it's an observation. During the encounter, if you watch Harran's positioning, his Veterans' targeting, and the specificity of his opening claim, you can see that he came prepared for exactly this situation. The DC 14 Insight check is not hard. The question is what you do with the knowledge. Varren Aldiss's name will come up if you pull the thread in Session 3.

**What to ask this player:** *"Your investigation has a leak. Before you ask who, ask this: what have you written down, and who could have read it?"*

### The Healer / Cleric PC

**Aldric Stone has bad knees and a Primer Stone he has been standing at for three sessions.**

He is sixty-three years old. His retirement was medically necessitated — chronic joint damage from thirty-one years of guard patrol on Varenhold's uneven flagstones. He is not fragile. He is also not built to resist two trained soldiers performing a controlled extraction on him while the rest of the Reckoning keeps anyone from helping him.

You can see this coming before it happens.

The Drag mechanic becomes visible the moment the Veterans start their approach: two people moving directly toward an elderly man who is standing at a stone he has been told is important. Their body language is coordinated. They are not moving to talk.

If you act on this before they reach him — if you move toward Aldric's stone, interpose yourself, use Help action on his Athletics — you may prevent the Drag entirely. If you hesitate to see how the situation develops, you will watch him get pulled.

He will not call for help. He's a retired guardsman. Calling for help is not in his vocabulary. But you can see what his face looks like when he hears the boots heading his way, and you know what it costs a person to stand quietly in that posture and wait.

**The beat:** Your contribution to the secondary objective is potentially the single most impactful action of the encounter. The Help action on Aldric's Athletics costs your action. It means you're not healing anyone. It means a player character might go down who could have been stabilized if you'd moved differently. That's the trade. What does your character do with it?

**What to ask this player:** *"You see someone who is going to need help before they ask. You always have. What does your character do when they can only help one person and there are two who need it?"*

### The Thief / Rogue PC

**Harran has documentation for his jurisdiction claim.**

The Emergency Powers charter is a real document — it grants the Reckoning authority over ritual sites during emergency declarations. But authority in a legal document is not uniform: it comes with specific conditions, specific limitations, specific language that either covers this situation or doesn't.

He is carrying the charter. He has it annotated in his own hand — you can see the folded papers in his coat pocket when the torchlight catches the bulge. He referred to the charter in his opening line with the confidence of someone who has read it recently and believes it says what he claims it says.

If you can get close enough during combat to lift it, read it, or copy its relevant clause — object interaction as you move through adjacent space, or a Sleight of Hand check against his Perception if you're more direct — you will know exactly what legal standing he actually has. And doesn't have.

There is a clause the charter does not cover: forced physical extraction of a civilian from an undesignated civic space. The Ashring is not a restricted site. It is not listed in the emergency declaration. Harran's jurisdiction extends to ritual oversight — he can supervise. He cannot legally remove a citizen from a public space without a separate order that he does not currently have.

This is not a weapon that ends the encounter. It is information. Information that could be used in Session 3 to challenge his authority through channels other than combat.

**The beat:** The complication table (s2c5-combat.md, result 3) gives you an opportunity even if you don't attempt the lift — the charter falls from his coat during combat. But a character who goes looking for it in the middle of a fight is a character who understands that information is the long game, not the round she's in.

**What to ask this player:** *"He walked in here with jurisdiction papers and eight soldiers. You usually win those fights before they start, with something small. What does your character reach for first?"*

### The Noble / Diplomat PC

**Harran said "investigators don't have standing here." That's an argument.**

It is, technically, a legal argument — standing is a formal concept in Varenhold's civic charter, and it means something specific about who has the authority to contest a jurisdiction claim in a designated site. He is correct that investigators-for-hire do not automatically have standing to contest Reckoning jurisdiction in an emergency declaration zone.

But he is also making a legal argument in a civic space to a person who knows the law, and legal arguments have counter-arguments.

Counter 1: The Ashring is not listed in the current emergency declaration. The declaration covers *"sites designated for ritual administration under active investigation."* The Ashring is under active investigation — but investigation by the investigators standing in front of him, hired by the Chancellor. The investigation authorises their presence here. The investigation is Chancellor-sanctioned. The Reckoning does not outrank the Chancellor.

Counter 2: Emergency Powers charter, Clause 7 (you know this if you have any background in civic law): jurisdiction over ritual sites grants the Reckoning *supervisory authority*, not *exclusive authority*. Supervisory authority means they can observe and regulate, not unilaterally remove participants.

He knows this too. He is hoping you don't.

**The beat:** A formal counter-claim delivered mid-combat — not a combat action, a statement, something that requires him to respond — throws his Veterans off their rhythm. They are trained to execute orders, not to manage a suddenly legally complex situation. DC 15 Persuasion or DC 13 Intimidation (using legal authority rather than threat) causes the Drag Pair to hesitate one round before resuming. One round is often enough.

**What to ask this player:** *"You've been in rooms where people with more resources than you tried to establish authority by walking in confidently and expecting you to believe them. What does your character say?"*

### The Restorer-Adjacent / Religious PC

**Edoran's movement and Harran's Reckoning want the same outcome — and they despise each other for how the other plans to get there.**

Edoran Maris believes the Dawnborn must die, but their deaths must be willing. The ritual requires consent as an engineering specification — Corven built it that way — and Edoran has built an entire theology around this truth. He believes coerced completion will either fail or damn everyone involved. He is not wrong about the mechanism. He may be right about the theology. He is grieving his daughter, who is one of the ten.

Harran Lecht believes the Dawnborn must die, and the timeline does not permit waiting for all ten to consent willingly. He will use persuasion, documentation, legal pressure, and the city's survival as leverage. He calls this operational necessity. He knows it will look like coercion in the historical record and has decided that is acceptable if the city survives.

Both of them are in Varenhold right now, working toward the same endpoint through opposing methods, each certain the other is going to cause the very failure they're trying to prevent.

You may have already met Edoran. You may have his note. You know his position.

**The beat:** This scene gives you information Edoran doesn't have yet: Harran has moved to actively control the Ashring, and he's doing it now. Before Session 3, you have the option of taking this to Edoran — not as an alliance, but as a report. What he does with it will surprise you. He does not rejoice that Harran is overstepping. He is frightened. A coerced inversion is his nightmare, and Harran is building one in real time.

**What to ask this player:** *"Both of them are right about the math. Both of them are wrong about consent. You've been in the middle of this since Session 1. Who, if anyone, does your character think can actually fix this?"*
