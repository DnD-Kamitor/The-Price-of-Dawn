# S3C2 — The Frequency Lock: Puzzle Structure

<details><summary>🔒 The Riddle — Full Text & Analysis</summary>

**The card reads:**

> *I am the same at the beginning and end of a prayer.*
>
> *I live between noise and music.*
>
> *You cannot hold me, but you can give me away completely.*
>
> *What am I?*

*Below, smaller, later addition:*
> *Please don't misfire it. The neighbours have complained twice.*

---

**Answer: a tone.**

Any player who describes producing a clear, sustained musical tone triggers the lock. This includes:
- Humming a single note
- Singing a note (wordless or otherwise)
- Whistling a sustained pitch
- Playing a musical instrument (a single sustained note suffices)
- Saying "I hum a note" — the physical act described, not just the word "tone"

What does **not** work:
- Speaking the word "tone" aloud
- Knocking on the door rhythmically
- Tapping a rhythm
- Shouting
- Describing what a tone is without producing one

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The riddle is solvable. It is not meant to be hard. Isolde is not trying to filter for genius — she is filtering for people who think in more than one register, who hear "you cannot hold me but you can give me away completely" and follow it past the abstract toward the physical. Anyone who has ever started a prayer with a note, or sung in any context, has the answer.

The clue line is "you cannot hold me" — a tone cannot be held (it dissipates) but it can be given (produced and transmitted). If players are circling the correct answer without committing, the hint ladder handles the rest.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🪜 Hint Ladder</summary>

**No hints yet — first engagement with the riddle:**

Let players work it. The average group will get it within two to three minutes of real-time discussion. Do not rush to hints. If the table is having fun with wrong approaches, let them.

---

**Hint 1 — if players ask for one, or after two wrong attempts:**

Repeat the line: *"You cannot hold me."*

Then: *"What can you give completely to someone, and have none left for yourself, and have given nothing physical?"*

This is the pivot. A tone produced is gone. You cannot keep it after producing it. You can give it — anyone nearby receives it. You retain nothing.

If players get stuck on "give away completely": *"What do you do with your voice when you start a service? Before the words begin?"*

---

**Hint 2 — if players are past three wrong attempts without escalation:**

*"The lock responds to what's in the room. Not what you say about it. What you do with it."*

This is the final bridge: the lock is not a word puzzle. It is a physical puzzle. The answer must be produced, not named.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** If a player has said "the answer is a tone" but hasn't produced one yet, that is the puzzle's final moment. Isolde, listening from behind the door, says nothing. She does not prompt. She waits. If the player has arrived at the answer intellectually and is waiting for something else to happen: nothing happens. The door is silent. The lock requires the physical act, not the word.

This is intentional. Knowing the answer and doing the answer are different things. The puzzle rewards doing.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🎯 Wrong-Answer Responses</summary>

**Speaking the word "tone" aloud:**
Nothing. The door doesn't respond. The hum from beneath it continues unchanged.
*If players seem confused:* the card doesn't say "say the answer." It says what it says.

**Knocking:**
Nothing. The lock is not mechanical. Knocking produces no tone.
*Escalation:* counts as a wrong attempt for escalation purposes (see below).

**Trying to pick the lock / searching for a keyhole:**
There is no keyhole. The iron plate at the frame is solid continuous metal. A DC 15 Investigation confirms: there is no mechanical locking component visible anywhere on or around the door. The lock is not where you're looking.

**Reciting the riddle back at the door:**
Nothing.

**Saying a prayer aloud (words only):**
Nothing — unless the player describes intoning or chanting it with a sustained vocal pitch, in which case this counts as producing a tone. *"I said a prayer"* does not trigger it. *"I chanted a prayer on one note"* does.

**Sliding something under the door:**
The gap is a quarter inch. Paper slides under. Nothing happens. (Isolde picks it up from the inside. She doesn't respond through the door to notes — she's been watching their feet and she knows who's out there.)

**Playing a rhythm rather than a sustained note:**
Nothing. A rhythm is not a tone. The lock responds to sustained pitch.

**Shouting:**
Nothing. Loud is not tonal.

**Attempting to Arcana-check the door:**
DC 10 Arcana identifies the lock mechanism as a resonance-keyed lux array — it responds to a specific frequency range of sustained acoustic input. This doesn't tell players what frequency, but it confirms: the lock is sound-responsive, and specifically tone-responsive, not just any sound.

**Threatening the door / Isolde through the door:**
Isolde does not respond. She is listening. She has heard threats before. She will wait.

---

**Isolde's reactions from behind the door (things she says, quietly):**

She does not volunteer these. Players who press their ear to the door on a DC 12 Perception check can hear them.

After the second wrong attempt: *"...not a knock."* (to herself, flat)

After the chemical-irritant jar incident (forced entry): *"I'm sorry — I didn't mean to throw it that far."* (immediately, before she's even assessed the situation)

</details>

<details><summary>⚠ Wrong-Attempt Escalation</summary>

**First wrong attempt:**
Nothing happens. The door is silent except for the low hum beneath it. The card remains pinned. The lock does not respond.

**Second wrong attempt:**
A faint vibration in the door — felt in the palm if touching the door, heard as a brief resonant buzz that lasts less than a second and then stops. This is the lock's near-field feedback: something is listening. It almost registered. It didn't.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM note:** The second-attempt vibration is the puzzle's signal that players are on a wrong path and the system is paying attention. It is not a warning in the danger sense — it is a calibration note. Players who feel it and think about it have a clue: the lock responds to *something in the acoustic range*, but it didn't register their last attempt. What were they doing? What should they try differently?
:::

**Third wrong attempt — FULL VOLUME:**
The lock fires.

A tone blasts from the door — pure, sustained, at a volume that fills the stairwell and carries through the building's walls. It lasts exactly three seconds. Then silence.

In the alley below, it is audible as a clear, sharp note with no obvious source. Davan Crel, still in the café across the alley, now has what he needed: not just "second floor" but the specific room's acoustic signature, identifiable against the alley's ambient noise profile. His report updates.

The neighbours know. Two of them have already complained. Whoever is directly above or beside Isolde's room now knows it fired again.

Isolde opens the door thirty seconds after the tone. She looks at players with the expression of someone who is tired of this specific problem.

> *"Second floor, left of the stairwell. Everyone in the block knows now."* *(pause)* *"Come in."*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The third-attempt consequence is real — Davan Crel's report updates — but Isolde doesn't turn players away. The scene continues. What changes is downstream: in S3C5, Harran Lecht's soldiers arrive with the room number, not just the floor. The search step is eliminated. Players who misfired the lock made the raid faster.

Do not announce this consequence now. Players will understand it in S3C5.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>💥 Forced Entry</summary>

**Forcing the door:**
DC 18 Strength. The door is heavy, iron-banded, set into a reinforced frame. It will give. It takes effort.

On success: the door swings open. Isolde, startled, throws a glass container from her nearest worktop. It is not a conscious decision — it is the reflex of someone who has been alone for three years and has one too many threat-response pathways active. The container shatters against the door frame and releases a chemical irritant (her current experiment's solvent solution — the amber-tinged substance that stains her fingers).

**Chemical irritant:** DC 12 Constitution saving throw or disadvantage on Perception checks for 1 hour. The irritant is not toxic at this concentration. It is genuinely unpleasant.

Isolde apologizes immediately — before she's checked whether anyone was hit, before she's assessed the situation. *"I'm sorry — I didn't mean to throw it that far."* She retrieves an antidote from the shelf behind her within thirty seconds. It's there. It was already labeled. She has done this before.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The chemical irritant is an involuntary startled reaction, not a trap. Isolde did not set this up as a defense. She was working at her bench, heard the door give way, and her hands threw the nearest container before her brain caught up. She is embarrassed. She apologizes multiple times in the first minute after.

The antidote is real and works. The Perception disadvantage clears completely after treatment. Do not punish players who forced entry for more than the Constitution save — the scene continues normally once Isolde has apologized and offered the antidote.

Forced entry also counts as noise: Davan Crel, if still in the alley, hears the impact. This triggers the same consequence as the third wrong attempt — his report updates with the specific room.
:::

**Thematic resonance note:**

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The riddle is the campaign in capsule form. *"You cannot hold me, but you can give me away completely."* The Dawnborn carry the sun. They cannot hold it forever — fifty years is not forever, it is a question that eventually becomes urgent. The question the campaign asks is whether they can give it away without losing themselves. A tone is the smallest possible version of that question: you produce it, you give it, it is gone from you, but while it was in the air it opened a door.

Do not say this to players. It is there if they notice. The riddle's second line — *"I live between noise and music"* — is also true of the Transfer Method: it lives between the ritual death that is merely noise and the solution that might be something like music. Isolde didn't mean it that way. Or she did. She has been alone with this for three years and she wrote the riddle herself.
:::
