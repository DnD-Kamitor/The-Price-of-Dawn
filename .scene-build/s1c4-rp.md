## Full Scene Flow

The scene has seven beats. They run in order as a default. Players can push beats forward or backward by how they engage. Beat 6 (THE PERSON BEAT) is conditional — it only triggers if a player asks something non-investigative.

---

### Beat 1 — THE ARRIVAL

*She sees them. She finishes the bowl she's working on. She hands the ladle to the teenager.*

The GM should not have to prompt this. The arrival read-aloud in s1c4-env.md handles it. When players enter the Dawnhall, Sera is already in motion toward the conclusion of the task she's doing. The handoff to the teenager is seamless — not a signal, not a gesture, just a result. The teenager takes the ladle. Sera removes her cooking gloves.

She walks toward the players — not fast, not slow — pulling a bench out from the nearest empty table section with one foot as she approaches. Sits on the bench. Looks at them.

> "You're from the Chancellor's office. Word travels faster in Lowmark than the Chancellor probably prefers."

*(No accusation. She is not performing suspicion. She is simply stating what she already knows.)*

She does not wait for confirmation. She gestures at the benches across from her.

> "Sit down. You look like you've been in the Archive all day."

*(Players who have indeed been in the Archive all day may or may not register this as an implicit acknowledgment that she knows their route.)*

---

**If players ask how she knew:**

> "There are three people in Lowmark who know Theron Waide by name. One of them told me an hour ago that someone had been in the restricted stacks. The rest was simple."

---

**If players are guarded or don't sit:**

She does not push. She remains seated. She is not performing openness — she simply does not close. If they want to stand, they can stand.

---

### Beat 2 — THE QUESTION

*She wants to know what they've found. This is not courtesy.*

Sera leans forward slightly — elbows on the table, hands loose, the posture of someone genuinely paying attention.

> "Tell me what you found. I'm not asking because I don't know. I'm asking because I want to know what you know."

*(This line requires delivery. She is making a distinction: she has her own knowledge; she is asking after theirs. She is not performing ignorance. She is gathering information about what the investigation has reached.)*

She waits. She does not prompt further. She is comfortable in the space where someone is about to answer a question.

---

**GM cue:** Players may hedge here — be vague about what they found, probe for what she already knows before offering information. She will not play that game. If players ask "what do you know?" she says:

> "More than you expect. But tell me what you have. I want to know what you found, not what I already know."

She is not being cagey. She is trying to establish a shared information state before the conversation continues. She finds people who play information as a negotiating chip mildly exhausting. She will still answer their questions. But she notices the hedging.

---

### Beat 3 — THE ANSWER

*Players tell her what they've learned. She listens without interrupting.*

This beat belongs to the players. Let them speak.

Sera's behavior during the answer: she listens. She does not nod along, does not interject with recognition, does not signal how the information is landing. She is paying attention in the specific way of someone who is hearing something they know and calibrating how much the speakers understand.

If players use specific language from the Archive documents — *"distributed sympathetic anchors," "living conduit mechanism," "the completion requires their release"* — she is very still for a moment.

If players have decoded more than she expected — if they understand the consent architecture, if they know about Fragment 1's language about *"willing heart" as engineering specification* — she goes still longer.

She does not interrupt. Not once.

---

**GM note:** If players are brief or vague here, she asks one follow-up question:

> "The phrase about the conduit — did you find the full sentence, or only the fragment?"

She is establishing what they have. This is not strategic. She is calibrating how much she needs to explain.

---

### Beat 4 — THE BEAT

*"I've been waiting to be told for a long time."*

When players have finished telling her what they know — when there is a natural pause, when the last thing one of them said has settled — she says it:

> "I've been waiting to be told for a long time."

**GM note on delivery:** Do not rush this. Let the silence before it be the silence after an answer, not the silence of a transition. She is not saying this as a response to any specific piece of information. She is saying it because someone else now knows, and she has been the only person in her life who knew, and there is relief in that.

The "telling" she waited for was not her learning what she is. She figured that out herself, eight months of Archive research, three years into running the Dawnhall. The "telling" she waited for was someone else knowing. Someone with the authority and reach to do something about it. She has been alone with this knowledge and she is, quietly, not alone with it anymore.

She does not say all of this. She says the one sentence.

---

**If players ask "you already knew?":**

> "Years ago. I found the same documents you found. It took me longer — I didn't have a Notation Key fragment to start with. But the conclusion is the same."

---

**If players ask how she felt when she found out:**

She touches the pendant, once.

> "I finished reading the page I was on. I went home. I made dinner. The next morning I came back and opened the kitchen."

*(Pause.)*

> "I've never been sure if that means I'm very calm or very stubborn."

---

### Beat 5 — THE CONSENT CONVERSATION

*She is clear. She does not perform her clarity.*

Players will ask about willingness. They will ask if she consents. They will ask in various framings — *"are you willing," "have you agreed," "do you accept what has to happen."*

Her answer is always the same, in her own words:

> "Yes."
*(Pause.)*
> "I've had years to make that decision. I didn't make it once. I keep making it."

---

**On whether other Dawnborn have consented:**

> "My yes is mine. Theirs is theirs. I would not tell you their answer if I knew it. I don't always know it."

She says this firmly. Not defensively — it is simply a principle she holds with the same steadiness she holds everything else. Her consent is hers. Theirs is theirs. She will not speak for them, will not speculate about them, will not pressure them.

---

**If players push: "but do you think they'll agree?":**

She touches the pendant.

> "I think they'll answer the question for themselves. That's all I know."

---

**If players try to convince her not to consent:**

> "It's not yours to change."

Not hostile. Not cold. Settled. This is not a negotiating position. It is a fact about who owns this decision. She does not explain further. She waits for the next question.

---

**If players argue that she might be wrong about what the ritual requires:**

She listens. She does not dismiss the argument.

> "If you find evidence that the mechanism works differently — bring it to me. I'll look at it."
*(Pause.)*
> "My answer will be the same. But I'll look at the evidence."

She means this. She is not closed to new information. Her consent is not contingent on certainty about the mechanism — it is her answer about what she would do with her life regardless.

---

### Beat 6 — THE PERSON BEAT

*Conditional. Triggers only if a player asks something non-investigative.*

This beat does not come from a player who is doing their job. It comes from a player who is, for a moment, a person talking to another person.

Any of the following triggers it:
- *"Are you all right?"*
- *"What's the yellow scarf for?"*
- *"How long have you been running this place?"*
- *"What do you cook for yourself when it's not forty people?"*
- Any question that has nothing to do with the investigation.

---

**If a player asks how she is (genuine):**

She pauses. Long enough to be visible. Then:

> "I'm all right. Most days."

*(If pushed:)*
> "Ask me again after."

*(She means: after the ritual question is resolved. "After" is a specific future she is already thinking about.)*

---

**If a player asks about the yellow scarf:**

She glances at the hook.

> "My mother's. I don't wear it in here — too precious. I touch it when I arrive and when I leave. That's enough."

She does not elaborate. She does not look away from the player when she says it. She is not inviting further inquiry — she is simply answering, the same way she answers everything.

---

**If a player asks what she cooks for herself:**

> "Whatever I want. That's the point. Here I'm always feeding forty. At home it's — I can make something small. Something that doesn't have to serve a purpose."
*(Pause.)*
> "I've been making rice with herbs lately. Very simple. I forget it's on and eat it cold. I don't mind."

---

**If a player asks about her faith:**

> "Auris is the one who left something behind when they went. I've had a long time to think about what it means to be the thing they left it in. I don't have a complete answer. I keep serving soup."
*(Very slight, dry. Not quite a joke. Close.)*

---

**If a player asks about the Ashring stones:**

> "I've been there once. Stone 1 — there was a particular quality to standing near it. Like being recognized. Not seen. Recognized."
*(Pause.)*
> "There's an inscription at the base. I've never been able to read it. But I know it's there."

*(This is the forward thread. Do not explain it further. Let it sit.)*

---

**The weight of this beat:**

Players who reach this beat get a Sera who gives more than she was planning to give. She does not broadcast this. She simply answers the questions that are actually asked, fully, rather than adequately.

This relationship — Sera who has been asked something real — pays forward in Sessions 3-5. She will recognize these players. She will tell them something she hasn't told the others.

---

### Beat 7 — THE END

*The bell. She has work. She does not apologize.*

At whatever point the scene has run its natural course — not on a timer, not interrupted — the bell above the kitchen door rings once. A clear tone, not loud.

Sera stands. She does not rush it.

> "That's lights-out. I have work."

She looks at each player individually — a single, direct look. Not a farewell, not a signal. An acknowledgment.

Then she goes. She picks up a cloth from the nearest table as she passes it, and she is already moving toward the far tables before she has fully turned away.

---

**GM note on the ending:**

She does not apologize for ending the scene. She does not say "it was good to meet you" or "I hope that helped." She does not add warmth as a send-off. The lack of a ceremonial ending is not rudeness — it is the particular quality of a person who has work to do and trusts that the conversation was what it was without needing to frame it.

Players who found the scene unsatisfying will feel that absence acutely. Players who were genuinely present in it will understand that the ending is appropriate and will not want her to do it differently.

---

**If players have a final question as she's leaving:**

She stops. Turns back. Answers it. Then she goes. One question. She gives it its full weight. Then she is gone.
