## What Players Know After This Scene

Regardless of how the box was opened — cleanly, partially, or by force — players now know the following if they read the documents:

**From Corven's key passage:**
- The Dawnborn are not an accident. They are the result of an intentional ritual redirection.
- There are ten of them. They were born at the moment of inversion — meaning the night of the ritual, when the solar energy was redirected instead of released.
- Each one is a Lux Anchor: a living vessel holding a portion of redirected solar energy.
- The ritual is technically incomplete. It was not failed. It is waiting.
- Completing it requires their release. Players now need to discover what "release" means technically — Fragment 2 gives partial insight; Fragment 3 (Session 3) completes the picture.

**From Fragment 2 (if acquired):**
- The notation vocabulary for the energy/anchor/vessel system is now partially decipherable.
- The ten vessels are arranged in a specific geometric pattern — the Ashring plaza configuration is visible in the energy distribution diagram on page 1.
- The "pending completion" phase marker exists in the notation and can now be read.
- "Release" in the technical notation carries the same meaning as theological release: the living vessel ends, the energy passes on.

**From the anonymous sentence:**
- Someone else was present at or after the sealing who understood what Corven had done.
- This person believed Corven did not know they would write it down.
- Their identity is unknown and remains a thread.

**What they still do not know:**
- The completion mechanism (how "release" works in practice)
- The timeline (when the window for completion closes, which is urgent)
- Whether the Dawnborn themselves know what they are
- What the Reckoning intends to do and on what schedule

## Theron's Relationship State: Three Versions

### State 1 — Graceful Open (Puzzle solved cleanly, secondary objective achieved)

Players solved the puzzle, the box opened without damage, and at least one player asked Theron why he never opened it himself.

**What this means:** Theron is in the most open state he can reach in this scene. The act of solving the puzzle — of someone else saying those names, clearly, toward the box, in his presence — has done something that eleven years of private guilt could not. He has witnesses now. Not to the original act, but to the act of finally giving it up.

**His disposition going forward:** Cooperative, forthcoming. He will tell players things they do not ask about. He will arrange for them to have access to restricted Archive sections if they need them. He trusts that whoever is holding those documents is using them carefully. He will not impede Scene 4 — he will, in fact, begin Scene 4 himself if the players do not start it.

**Consequence tracker entry:** Theron Waide — Trust: Open. Access: Full Archive. Relationship: Witness.

**What he can provide in later sessions:** Archive access; knowledge of Ostenveld-era suppression; one name he has held back (he knows who commissioned the original burn order before it reached the Reckoning). He gives this name in Session 3 if players maintain the relationship.

---

### State 2 — Partial Solve (One bundle burned; players needed third name from Theron or investigation; box opened but harder-won)

Players had only two names from surviving documents. They needed Theron's help for the third name, or they succeeded on a DC 14 Investigation of surviving annotations.

**What this means:** The box opened, but the path was narrower. Theron had to give something — the third name — without speaking it to the box himself. He said "Wren" to the players, not to the lock. That distinction mattered to him. Players may not understand why. His disposition is slightly more guarded — not unfriendly, but aware that the process was imperfect, that something was lost in Scene 1 that cannot be recovered.

**His disposition going forward:** Willing but careful. He is not withholding, but he is watching. He wants to know what players intend to do with the information before he opens additional doors. If players have been straightforward with him, he is straightforward in return. If they've been evasive about their goals, he is evasive about his resources.

**Consequence tracker entry:** Theron Waide — Trust: Conditional. Access: Reading Room + named restricted sections on request. Relationship: Cautious ally.

**What he can provide:** Same as State 1, but on a slower timeline and with one additional requirement: players must tell him something honest about their intentions before he gives them the Ostenveld commission name.

---

### State 3 — Forced Open (Box opened by Thieves' Tools or brute force)

The mechanism was bypassed. The documents are readable. The seal is fractured.

**What this means:** The box opened, but not the way Theron spent eleven years hoping it would. The scorched edges of the documents are physical evidence of an act of impatience — or urgency, or frustration, or simply practicality, depending on how the player framed the action. Theron does not blame the players. He sat down when it happened. He said "Eleven years. I could have just—" and did not finish.

His reaction is not anger. It is a specific grief: the recognition that he protected something so carefully for so long that it became more valuable than what it contained, and someone else saw clearly that the right thing to do was just open it.

**His disposition going forward:** Functional but personally closed. He will work with the players — he will not obstruct them, and the documents are in their hands. But the moment of opening that he has imagined for eleven years did not happen. The ceremony was skipped. He is not sure he cares about the ceremony as much as he thought. He is sitting with that uncertainty.

**What he provides:** He answers direct questions with direct answers. He does not volunteer information. If players need Archive access, they can have it — but they need to ask, because he is not going to offer it. The Ostenveld commission name: he gives it, but only if asked the right question. He will not route them to it himself.

**Consequence tracker entry:** Theron Waide — Trust: Functional. Access: Standard Archive access. Relationship: Professional. Scene 4 requires more effort to begin — he does not start it himself.

## Fragment 2 Acquisition Note

Fragment 2 is the same regardless of how the box was opened. Its condition is not affected by force-open methods — it was stored in the center of the document stack, protected by Corven's notes on all sides. The scorching from a force-open only affects the top document (the cover sheet of Corven's notes, which is a title notation, not a content page).

Players who receive Fragment 2 should be told explicitly: "This is a separate document from Corven's notes. It is in a different handwriting." This signals that it has a different origin and invites them to consider where it came from and who Sali was.

Fragment 2 status feeds directly into the Session 3 puzzle. Players who have both Fragments 1 and 2 enter Session 3 with a significant mechanical advantage on the ritual diagram reconstruction. Mark this clearly in whatever consequence tracking system the table uses.
