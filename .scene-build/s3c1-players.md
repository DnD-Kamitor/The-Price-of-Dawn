# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: Player Engagement & Read-Alouds

<details><summary>📢 Opening Read-Aloud: South Approach to Lowmark Alley</summary>

> Lowmark Alley opens in front of you into Chandler's Block — a wider section, maybe fifteen feet across, where three streets meet before narrowing again to the north. You can smell it before you see it: close-packed wool, lamp oil from a café somewhere to the east, the cold-stone smell of a street that never quite dries out.
>
> The crowd is thirty people. Maybe more. They're not moving. Not shouting, either — that's the first thing you notice. Whatever sound they were making earlier, they've moved past it. Now it's the ambient noise of bodies standing together: a cough, someone adjusting their grip on a painted board, the tapping of wood against a shoulder.
>
> Three placards visible above the mass of heads. One of them reads: *WE GAVE THE DAWNHALLS EVERYTHING / WHAT DID IT GIVE US BACK.*
>
> At the crowd's center — not elevated, not louder than anyone else, just the point that everyone else is orbited around — there's a woman. She's holding a placard of her own, but she's got it at her side, face turned in toward her hip. The back of it has writing you can't quite read from here.
>
> Above her, above all of it: a second-floor window, amber glass, warm light coming through. Someone up there has been watching since before you arrived.
>
> And across the alley — café, cloth awning, one table visible through the window — a man with a cup of tea he hasn't touched.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Deliver this at normal pace. Don't rush the atmosphere. Players need to feel the weight of the crowd before they start problem-solving it. The read-aloud establishes four things: (1) crowd character (exhausted, not violent), (2) Senna's position and placard, (3) Isolde's window, (4) Davan's presence. All four are seeds. Let players choose which ones to pull.

Pause after the read-aloud. Wait for a player to speak or act. Do not volunteer information. If they sit in silence for more than a moment, Senna's voice carries to them from the crowd's center: *"They're still in there."*
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📢 Senna Approach Read-Aloud: She Sees Them First</summary>

*This fires when players move toward the crowd or toward Senna specifically. She notices them before most of the crowd does.*

> She turns her head before you're halfway across the block — not fast, not startled, just the way someone turns when they've been watching an entrance for three hours and something finally came through it.
>
> She looks at your group for a long moment. Her placard is still at her side.
>
> Then, to no one in particular — though everyone nearby hears it:
> *"You're not here for chandler's supplies."*
>
> She doesn't ask you to leave. She doesn't step back from where she's standing, twenty feet from the building's door. She just waits. The crowd around her has shifted its collective attention without anyone visibly directing it — thirty people and you're the thing they're all looking at now.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** After this beat, the scene is open for player-led dialogue. Senna will not initiate further. She has made her opening statement (*"You're not here for chandler's supplies"*) and she is waiting to see how players respond to it. This is her tactical default: she makes one clear observation and then watches what happens.

If players stay silent: after a beat, she adds: *"I don't care why you're here. Just don't get between me and that door."*

That's her limit statement. She will not move from this position without something that reaches her.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📢 The Placard Reverse: What's Written There</summary>

*This fires if a player moves within 10ft of Senna or specifically asks to read the placard's back.*

> You can read it now. Smaller than the front — charcoal, not paint, like it was added later or in a different mood. Her handwriting.
>
> *My daughters' names are Nia and Brek.*
> *They are nine and six.*
> *I am not here for politics.*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Read this quietly. No added commentary. Let it land.

If a player then addresses Senna and names the daughters, her reaction is in s3c1-npc.md — she stops, a beat, then: *"You read the back. Most people don't bother."*

Do not prompt players to use this information. It is there. Players who look will find it. Players who use it will reach her.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📢 De-escalation Beat: The Shift</summary>

*This fires when Senna's crack has been reached — daughters named, or specific fear acknowledged, or both. Play it slow.*

> Something changes in her face. Not softening — more like the argument she was ready to have has been taken away from under her. She's still standing in the same place. She hasn't moved toward you or away from you. But the quality of her attention is different.
>
> She looks at you for a long moment.
>
> Then:
>
> *"If they die for this, I need to know they chose it."*
>
> She doesn't elaborate. She doesn't ask you to confirm it. She says it once, and it sits there in the space between you, and then she steps back — one step, then another — and the crowd around her shifts without anyone saying anything, and there is a path to the door.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Do not follow this with explanation. Do not have a crowd member cheer or react. Do not summarize what happened. Let the moment close.

The path to the door is open. The window above is still lit. Move directly to players reaching Isolde's stairwell.

If players say anything to Senna at this moment — thank her, ask her anything, try to continue the conversation — she looks at them and says, once: *"Go upstairs."* That's all. She means: *you have your path; use it.*

What she said — *"If they die for this, I need to know they chose it"* — is the thing she came here to say and didn't know how to say. She said it to the players because they gave her the terms for it. She will be thinking about it for the rest of Session 3. So will the players, if you've played this right.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📢 Forced Passage Failure Read-Aloud: Isolde's Reaction</summary>

*This fires when players reach the second floor after forcing through the crowd.*

> The stairwell smells like candlewax and old paper. At the top, a door — unlocked, or close enough that one push opens it.
>
> Inside: a workroom. Shelves of reference volumes, a desk covered in calculation sheets, two lamps that were burning. One of them has been turned down.
>
> A woman at the far wall, her back to you, reorganizing papers. She has been reorganizing the same papers since before you came up the stairs.
>
> She doesn't turn immediately. When she does, she looks at each of you in sequence — not hostile, not afraid, something else. Like she is deciding.
>
> *"I heard the noise."*
>
> A pause.
>
> *"I thought it was the Reckoning."*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Isolde heard the crowd scatter. She knows what it means when thirty people stop making noise that quickly. She has been in this room for three years. She has thought, more than once, that the knock at the door was going to be someone sent by Vyne to take her papers.

She is not angry at the players. She is recalibrating. She had a version of this meeting in her head — she would open the door when she was ready, she would have her notes organized, she would present the Transfer Method from a position of control. That version is gone. This version starts with: *"I heard the noise. I thought it was the Reckoning."*

Apply the +1 difficulty modifier to her first Transfer Apparatus check (S3C3). Do not announce it. Just track it. Isolde's hands are not quite steady.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📢 De-escalated Arrival: Isolde Opens the Door</summary>

*This fires when players reach Isolde's door after successfully de-escalating the crowd.*

> The stairwell is quiet. At the top, the door opens before you knock.
>
> She's been watching from the window. She saw.
>
> She looks at your group — not all at once, person by person — and then she steps back to let you in.
>
> *"I saw what you did. With the woman outside."*
>
> A pause. She goes back to her desk. She doesn't sit.
>
> *"Come in."*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Isolde's tone here is not warm. It is recognition. She watched someone finally handle the thing that has been outside her window since morning. She is still cautious — three years of hiding will do that. But she opens the door instead of waiting to see if you'll knock, and that's something.

Do not editorialize on this. Just run the scene forward. Isolde is ready to talk.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📋 Player Engagement Matrix</summary>

**Action-oriented players:**
The flanking route is the action track. DC 12 Perception (or DC 10 for active looking) to spot the side lane north of the crowd. DC 10 Acrobatics if moving at full speed through crates. They bypass the crowd entirely, reach the building's north side, access Isolde's door. Clean, fast, no social friction.

The secondary action track: Davan Crel. DC 13 Perception to spot him. If they move on him, he gives his cover story. The action is in reading whether he's lying (DC 14 Insight) and deciding what to do about it. He doesn't fight. But the cold tea is a tell, and the paper at his elbow is a thread.

**Investigation-oriented players:**
Two threads: Davan Crel (who is he, why is he here, what has he already done) and Senna's placard reverse (daughters' names, "I am not here for politics"). Both are accessible through observation. Neither requires confrontation. Both pay off later — Davan in S3C5, the daughters' names as a running echo through Sessions 4-5.

**Role-play-oriented players:**
Senna. All of her. She is the scene's core. She has fifteen lines of dialogue across different approaches, a clear crack condition, and a shift that is one of the scene's most resonant beats. Role-play players who engage her fully and reach the crack will get the best version of this scene.

**Problem-solving players:**
The optimal path is flanking route + de-escalation. They're not mutually exclusive — players can scout the side lane and still choose to engage Senna directly. Players who do both: find the lane (know they have an exit), go to Senna anyway, reach her crack, get her shift, then proceed to the door through the parted crowd. They've solved both problems. Senna shows up in Session 5 as a stabilizing force. The lane is there if they need it later.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Let players discover their path without steering. If a pure action group flanks and never talks to Senna, that's a valid scene outcome. If a pure role-play group spends twelve minutes on Senna and never notices Davan Crel, that's also valid — they'll meet his consequences in S3C5. The scene has multiple tracks because players have multiple instincts. Trust all of them.
:::

</details>
