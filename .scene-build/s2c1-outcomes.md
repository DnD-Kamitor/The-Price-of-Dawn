## Document Bundle Contents

### Bundle A — Public Ritual Proposals

**Physical description:** Thick, formal binding. Civic seal on the cover page. Archive classification mark: Public Holdings, Civic Records, sub-category Ritual Administration.

**Contents:** Public-facing proposals for ritual completion, drafted over a period of approximately 30 years following the original event. Language is careful, bureaucratic, and vague. These documents establish *that* the ritual needs to be completed — a problem acknowledged formally by civic and Spire institutions — but say nothing about *how*. The mechanism is referred to only as "the established method" or "pursuant to the original design."

**What they prove:** The city knew there was an unresolved problem. Institutional knowledge of the ritual's incomplete state goes back decades. This is not new information that surfaced recently. The incompletion was managed, not forgotten.

**Critical content:** The third witness colophon — WREN — appears only in this bundle, on a folded interior document signed by Corven. If Bundle A is burned, WREN cannot be confirmed through documentary evidence alone.

---

### Bundle B — Administrative Correspondence

**Physical description:** Lighter than Bundle A. Thinner pages, institutional letterhead. Two seals: the Archive's, and one players may not recognize — a Spire administrative cipher.

**Contents:** A series of letters between the Archive's administration and someone at the Spire, over a period of approximately 18 months ending eight months ago. The correspondence concerns what's described as "pending institutional review" of certain ritual documentation. The tone is evasive in a formal way — clearly written by people who expect the letters to be read by third parties.

**What they prove:** Someone at the Spire was monitoring the status of ritual documents actively. The correspondence references a review process that never concluded — it simply stops. The last letter, dated eight months ago, is from the Spire contact and says only: *"The review will proceed under revised institutional authority. No further correspondence required on this matter."*

**Critical content:** The Spire contact's letters are signed with a cipher, but the internal Archive copy bears a clerk's annotation in the margin: the name *Keseph Vyne*. This is the first time that name surfaces in the campaign. Players may not recognize it yet. Theron will, in Scene 2.

---

### Bundle C — Two Witness Colophons

**Physical description:** Smallest bundle. Worn leather wrapper, tied carefully. Older paper — not fragile, but clearly from a different era than the other bundles.

**Contents:** Two of the three original colophons from Corven's notation — documents that name the witnesses to the notation's creation and verify its authenticity. Each colophon is signed in the witness's own hand.

- **CORVEN** — the author's own witness mark, self-attesting. This is unusual; notation keys don't normally carry the author's personal witness. Corven signed their own work as if expecting it to be disputed.
- **SALI** — a second witness. The name is full: *Sali Marchev*. Players may recognize this as a historical name if they have History or Archive knowledge (DC 14 History: Sali Marchev was a Spire administrative clerk active approximately 50 years ago, at the time of the ritual event).

**What they prove:** Corven's notation was formally witnessed. It is not a private document — it is a verified institutional record. Someone with standing and authority attested to it. The documents are not forgeries.

**Critical content:** These colophons are load-bearing for the resonance box puzzle in Scene 3. The box responds to named voices. Without CORVEN and SALI confirmed, one of the box's three activation sequences cannot be run through document authentication — it requires direct testimony or a different approach.

## Lieutenant Capture: Interrogation

If the Lieutenant is alive and in players' custody, he will answer limited questions. Use the DC table in `s2c1-mechanics.md` for persuasion/intimidation thresholds.

**What he gives willingly (no roll):**
- He identifies himself as a Reckoning field officer. He will not give his name. (GM name: Carrow. Players will not learn this here.)
- He confirms this was an authorized operation.
- He confirms the documents are what players think they are.

**What he gives under pressure (DC thresholds apply):**
- Three weeks. The Reckoning has known about these specific documents for three weeks.
- *"The Commander already knows what's in them."* He does not say this lightly. When he says it, he pauses first.
- He will not give Harran's location. He genuinely does not know Harran's current position.
- He will not explain "secondary phase." He doesn't know what it is. His silence on this is honest, not strategic.

**What he will not say under any circumstances:**
- Keseph Vyne's name
- Who provided the document manifest
- Any detail about the Reckoning's operational structure above his tier

**His final word, if players push him past his limit:**
> "You're asking the wrong person. I was sent here to burn paper. Whoever made the list that told me which paper — that's the person you should be looking for."

He does not know who made the list. But this line confirms that a list existed. Someone selected these documents with specificity. That is new information, even if he doesn't know who holds it.

## Note on His Body (If Killed)

Found in his inner coat pocket:

> A folded paper, sealed with the Reckoning administrative cipher. Inside, two lines in his own handwriting:
> *"Commander's copy secured — proceed to secondary phase when confirmed."*
> The word "confirmed" has not yet been crossed through.

He was going to cross it out after the burn was complete. He didn't get to.

**What this tells players:**
- Harran already has a copy of what was in these documents. The burn is cleanup, not discovery.
- Something called "secondary phase" exists. No one in this room knows what it is.
- The note was written before this operation began. The structure was already in motion.
