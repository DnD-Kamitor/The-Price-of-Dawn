## Session 1, Scene 2 — The Archive Door: Riddle Tension System

**Design intent:** There is no combat in this scene. The tension system replaces initiative with a guess-tracking clock. The door is not a puzzle to be bypassed — it is a threshold that asks the players a question the entire campaign is built on. Players who solve it quickly are already thinking correctly. Players who fail are given Theron, which is its own reward.

**Emotional target:** The word should feel earned. When the door opens, the players should register that the answer was not arbitrary — it was *right*.

**Dramatic question:** Can you name the thing the city lost before you understand why it was lost?

---

## 1. The Riddle

Read this aloud, slowly, as if the door speaks it — because it does. The notation carvings move when the players first approach the door's brass speaking plate. This is not magic in a flashy sense. It is mechanism. The carvings shift. The words appear. The door is waiting.

> *"I am given only when no one compels it.*
> *Forced, I become something else entirely.*
> *I am the difference between a gift and a theft.*
> *What word names the thing that makes the difference?"*

**Answer:** CONSENT

**The GM note that must not be skipped:** This is the campaign's central word. Everything in Sessions 1–5 turns on it — the ritual's cost, the Dawnborn's right to refuse, Edoran's theology, the Inversion ending. The door is asking the question before the players know there is a question. Players who answer correctly before Theron arrives are not just solving a puzzle. They are, without knowing it, already asking the right thing.

---

## 2. Guess Tracking — The Tension Clock

### The Physical Setup

The Archive's restricted stack entrance is a heavy brass-fitted door on the upper landing of the main reading room's east staircase. The door has no handle visible on the outside. Centered at eye level: a brass speaking plate, 6 inches wide, with a ring of notation carvings that shift to display the riddle when a character approaches within 5 feet.

There is no time limit. The clock is social, not mechanical — it is driven by what wrong answers trigger, not by rounds.

### Guess Tracking Table

| Guess # | What Happens Mechanically | What Happens Narratively |
|---------|--------------------------|--------------------------|
| **Correct (any attempt)** | Door opens. Notation carvings complete a full rotation and go still. The restricted stacks are visible. | See Section 9: The Peak Moment. |
| **Wrong — 1st attempt** | A low resonance tone sounds through the door. Single frequency, brief (3 seconds). Audible from the main reading room floor below. | Junior clerks on the floor below look up. A chair scrapes. Footsteps on the stairs begin. |
| **Wrong — 2nd attempt** | The tone repeats. Slightly higher. Audible from the stairwell landing. | Theron appears on the landing one floor below, looking up through the banister. He has ink on his sleeve. His coat is slightly askew. He does not call out. He watches. |
| **Wrong — 3rd attempt** | Tone sharpens to a thin, high note. A crack of pale light appears along the full perimeter of the door frame, then fades. The door goes inert — it will not accept a new attempt for one hour. | Theron reaches the landing. He looks at the door. He looks at the players. He says nothing immediately. After a moment: *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."* This is not a failure state. This is how the RP scene begins. |
| **Additional attempts after lock** | The door does not respond. No tone. No carvings. It is simply a door. | Theron is now present and available as guide. The one-hour lock runs in the background while they speak. |

**Note:** A second wrong attempt before Theron reaches the landing means he arrives to find them at attempt three, not attempt two. Compress the narrative: he sees the light crack, not just hears the tone. He moves faster. His expression is harder to read.

### No Partial Credit

The door does not respond to near-misses. CHOICE, FREEDOM, WILL, GIFT, AGREEMENT — these all produce the same low tone as STONE or FISH. The door is not interested in being close. This is important: players should not be able to guess-by-proximity. The answer is specific. If players start narrowing by synonym, let them — this is intelligent play, and they will reach CONSENT through that method. They have earned it.

## 3. The Door — Physical Mechanics

### Notation Shift (Correct Answer)

When the correct word is spoken aloud against the speaking plate, the notation carvings — a ring of 40 or so interlocking symbols — do the following in sequence:

1. Each carving rotates one quarter-turn clockwise. Not all at once — starting from the top, moving around the ring, one by one, in a wave that completes in roughly three seconds.
2. A low harmonic note sounds — entirely different from the wrong-answer tone. The wrong-answer tone is a warning bell. This is something closer to a chord. It rises slightly, then resolves.
3. The door swings inward on its own. No click of a latch. No handle. It simply opens.
4. The restricted stacks are visible beyond: dimly lit by ceiling-mounted amber lanterns, tall shelves, the smell of old paper and something faintly metallic.

**GM note on delivery:** Pause at the door swing. Let the action rest for a moment. If a player made the guess, look at them. If they got it first try, add one sentence: *"Like the door has been waiting."* If they got it on the second or third try after Theron's guidance: let the silence stand. The door doesn't know the difference. It just answers.

### The Wrong-Answer Tone

The tone is audible, not painfully loud. Think: a deep bell struck once, resonating. The kind of sound that carries through stone. Each subsequent wrong answer raises the pitch fractionally — the third wrong answer is noticeably sharper, thin enough to be slightly unpleasant. These are the physical tell that tells the floor below something is happening at the restricted entrance.

### The Crack of Light

Third wrong attempt only. The crack appears along the door frame — all four edges simultaneously — as pale, slightly cool white light. It lasts approximately two seconds and fades completely. It looks like the door considered opening and changed its mind. This is accurate: the door's mechanism registers the attempt, tests for a possible partial match in some older interpretation of the riddle's answer, finds none, and locks.

**Players who ask what the light was:** Theron, if present, says: *"It does that. The builder was thorough."* He will not elaborate in this scene. The light is referenced in the deep archive documents in Session 3 if players pursue it.

## 4. The One-Hour Lock

### What Triggers It

Third wrong attempt. The door goes inert immediately. The carvings do not shift for anyone who approaches. The speaking plate does not activate.

### Duration

Exactly one hour from the third wrong attempt. The door resets on its own. Players can attempt again after the hour.

### What Players Can Do During the Wait

| Activity | Available? | Notes |
|----------|-----------|-------|
| Talk to Theron | Yes | This is the primary intended use of the wait. See Section 5. |
| Search the non-restricted stacks (main floor, open shelves) | Yes | DC 14 Investigation. Success: they find Corven's public index — lists what was submitted, not what it contains. Points toward three shelf numbers they cannot access without the restricted door. DC 20 or with Theron's guidance: they find a cross-reference note in Theron's own handwriting pointing to shelf mark 4-17-3. |
| Explore the current floor (this landing and adjacent rooms) | Yes | Two reading alcoves with locked cabinets (DC 15 Thieves' Tools or DC 17 Sleight of Hand). Cabinets contain: index supplements from the Year 7 archive expansion. Nothing plot-relevant, but one cabinet contains a handwritten note from a former Archivist addressed "To whoever inherits this" that describes the door's history: *"Built in the third year after the Eternal Dawn. The Chancellor-Consort of the time requested a door that could tell the difference between taking and receiving. I was not told why she needed one."* This is a gift to give players who look. |
| Attempt magical solutions | Yes | See Section 6. |
| Ask Theron directly for the answer | Yes | See Section 5 — he knows the answer. He will give it, at a cost. |
| Leave and return in an hour | Yes | The door resets. They may attempt again. Theron will be in his office and available if they seek him out when they return. |

## 5. Theron as Guide — Staged Arrival and Information

### Arrival Timing

Theron's position in the building at the start of Scene 2: his office, one floor below the restricted landing. He hears the first wrong-answer tone from his desk. He does not move immediately. He has heard that tone before. He waits to see if it stops.

Second tone: he stands. Ink on his sleeve from a letter he was writing. Coat not fully buttoned. He moves to the stairwell.

Third tone and the crack of light: he is already on the stairs. He reaches the landing quickly enough to see the light fade.

### What Theron Says at Each Intervention Point

**First wrong answer (he is not yet present — this is pre-arrival):**

Describe the staircase: a chair scraping below, the sound of someone standing. Give the players a moment to process that they have been heard.

**Second wrong answer (he appears at the lower landing):**

He looks up through the banister. His expression is not alarmed — it is the particular watchfulness of a man who has been anticipating something for a long time. He does not speak yet. He is reading the situation. DC 13 Insight (passive qualifies): he is calculating how much they already know.

**Third wrong answer (he is on the landing with them):**

He looks at the crack of light as it fades. He looks at the players. Silence for a moment — long enough to be deliberate. Then:

> *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."*

This is not a surrender. It is not a rescue. It is Theron beginning a negotiation he has been rehearsing for eleven years: someone is finally here to investigate, and he has been deciding since the first tone whether to help them or stall them, and the third tone means the door itself has made part of the decision for him.

### What Theron Volunteers (as guide)

If players accept his offer and follow him — likely to his office or to the main reading room's back alcove — he tells them the following, in this order, at whatever pace the conversation naturally reaches:

1. **The riddle's history:** The restricted door was installed in the third year of the Eternal Dawn, commissioned by Chancellor-Consort Elara Voss (Sera Voss's distant ancestor — do not flag this; let it surface naturally in Session 3). The builder was a locksmith-mage from the old Accord guild. The answer has not changed in 250 years. He knows the answer because he researched the door's provenance when he became Master Archivist.

2. **The answer itself:** He will say it. *"CONSENT. The word is CONSENT."* He says it with a particular flatness, as if he is aware of the irony of handing over the word that the city lost. *"The Chancellor-Consort wanted a door that only opened to something freely given. Her word for it was consent."* He pauses. *"Fitting, given what the restricted stacks contain."*

3. **What the restricted stacks hold:** Corven's original ritual documentation. His observation logs. A sealed letter addressed to the Archivist at the time — unopened until Theron read it eleven years ago. Theron does not say what the letter contains at this stage. This is what he withholds.

4. **What he withholds:** The cost. He knows the ritual requires the Dawnborn to die — specifically, that the Lux Anchors are extinguished by completion. He will not say this yet. He uses language like *"the documentation contains passages I have struggled with"* and *"the notation system requires careful interpretation."* DC 14 Insight: he is stalling, not confused. DC 17 Insight: the stalling is protective — he is deciding whether to make the players carry the same weight he has been carrying. He wants absolution from them, not just for his silence, but for being the one who tells them.

**Getting Theron to tell them everything:** See the Lantern Walk Confession skill gate in npcs.md. In this scene, he can be pressed with Persuasion DC 15 — but success only gets one additional step: *"The letter describes a mechanism. Not just a ritual structure. A cost."* He will not name the cost here. That is Session 2 territory.

### After Theron Guides Them Through the Door

If Theron brings the players to the door and speaks the word himself, the door opens exactly as described in Section 3. The carvings rotate. The chord sounds. The door swings.

**Theron:** He does not go in with them immediately. He stands in the doorway. After a moment:

> *"I have read everything in there. Several times. I know what you're going to find."*

He steps aside. He does not block the entrance. He is not going to stop them. He simply says:

> *"When you have read it — come back and tell me if I was right to be afraid."*

This echoes Theron's dialogue from the Lantern Walk Confession (npcs.md). If players remember it, they will understand: he is not offering to help them interpret what they find. He is asking them to give him a verdict on the last eleven years of his life.

## 6. Creative Approaches — What Works, What Fails, What Theron Says

### Knock (Spell, 2nd-level)

**What happens:** The spell fires. The door does not open. A faint amber shimmer runs across the door's surface — the notation carvings pulse once, then settle. The Knock spell registers as a mechanical attempt against the door's locking mechanism. The door has no lock in the mechanical sense. The carvings are the mechanism. The spell has nothing to grab.

**Side effect:** The loud knock sound that Knock produces (audible 300 ft) fires normally. This absolutely brings Theron upstairs — regardless of which wrong-attempt stage they are at. It is, in effect, the loudest possible wrong answer.

**Theron's response (arriving at pace):** He rounds the corner at a near-run, lantern in hand, and stops when he sees what happened. He looks at the carvings. He looks at the players. He is not angry. He is the specific expression of a man who has just watched someone try to pick open a combination lock with a hammer.

> *"The door was built by someone who anticipated that approach. The mechanism is not mechanical."*

He waits for them to ask the obvious next question.

### Detect Magic

**What happens:** The door radiates conjuration and abjuration magic. The carvings radiate transmutation. The speaking plate radiates divination — faint, old, deeply embedded. The magical auras are layered and old (250 years). A DC 14 Arcana check while examining the auras identifies that the divination aura on the speaking plate is a listener effect: the plate is assessing the spoken word against a stored conceptual key, not a phonetic one. Synonyms in other languages would also work. This is a significant discovery.

**Theron's response (if present):** Mild approval. *"The notation has always suggested the mechanism was semantic. I never attempted to verify it directly."* If players try a synonym in another language: it works. CONSENTEMENT (Elvish-derived Common dialect), SAMINATHAS (Dwarven formal), SVALA (Ashfen word for the act of voluntary giving) — any of these open the door. Theron will file this away and find it interesting.

### Dispel Magic

**What happens:** DC 19 Arcana check (the magic is old and deeply set). On success: the carvings go dark. The door is now a door. It can be opened with Thieves' Tools (DC 22) or forced (DC 20 Athletics, the door is heavy brass-fitted hardwood). On failure: the magic suppresses for one round, then resumes. The carvings relight. The door is exactly as it was.

**Consequence of forcing or picking after a successful Dispel:** The door opens. The restricted stacks are accessible. This bypasses the riddle entirely. However: Theron, if present, goes very still. He does not try to stop them. His expression is the expression of someone watching a consent violation happen in real time. He will not confront them in the moment. He will not forget it. Trust with Theron drops by one full step, and his eventual Session 2 confession will be harder to earn (raise Persuasion DC by 3). The irony should be available to players in retrospect: they bypassed the CONSENT door by not giving consent to the process.

**Theron's response:** If they forced the door, he says nothing immediately. Later, in his office, if pressed: *"The door asks something. You chose not to answer."* He does not elaborate.

### Physical Force (Without Dispel)

**What happens:** The door is hardwood with brass reinforcement. AC 20, HP 60, immunity to psychic and poison damage, resistance to all non-magical damage. It does not move under a slam, kick, or shoulder. Players with Strength 18+ can attempt a DC 22 Athletics check — on a success, the door cracks and eventually gives over three rounds of sustained effort. It will never open cleanly.

**Theron's response:** He arrives at the noise (loud, persistent, structural). He does not intervene physically. He watches. If they succeed: *"You have broken a 250-year-old door commissioned by Chancellor-Consort Voss."* Long pause. *"I will need to explain that to the Council."* He is not joking. He is filing this in the same category as Dispel: you chose not to answer the door's question.

### Asking Theron Directly for the Answer (Before He Arrives Naturally)

**What happens:** Players go looking for Theron before touching the door, or before any wrong attempts. They find him in his office (DC 10 Investigation or asking any junior clerk). He will answer the question. He does this without hesitation, which is itself slightly surprising.

> *"CONSENT. Speak it clearly against the plate. The carvings will shift."*

Then he looks at them with a kind of careful attention.

> *"You didn't try first. Most people try first."*

If players ask why he finds this notable: *"Because the door is asking something, and you came to ask someone instead of guessing. There's a difference."* He does not say what the difference is. DC 15 Insight: he is testing whether they understand the irony. He is also, very slightly, more willing to trust them than if they had arrived with wrong attempts behind them.

### Research in the Non-Restricted Stacks

**What happens:** DC 16 Investigation to find material directly relevant to the door. On success: a folio in the civil architecture records section, dated Year 3 of the Eternal Dawn, describes the Archive extension and the commissioned security mechanisms. The language is bureaucratic, but one line reads: *"The restricted entrance mechanism was built to require spoken response to a propositional question; the key concept is to be spoken freely, without prompt, as a demonstration of understanding."*

This is not the answer, but it is the frame: the door wants understanding, not guessing. Players who have read this description and then correctly answer CONSENT will feel the weight of that framing land in the same moment the door opens.

### Attempting to Bribe or Threaten a Junior Clerk for the Answer

**What happens:** There are three junior clerks on the main floor. None of them know the answer. This is genuine ignorance, not concealment — the answer is not in any staff handbook. Theron never told them. One clerk mentions, if asked, that *"Archivist Waide knows. He's read everything."* Bribery produces this referral. Intimidation produces the same referral plus a DC 12 Perception check to notice the clerk is now watching the players more carefully — and will mention the incident to Theron.

## 7. The Secondary Objective — Theron's Withheld Document

### What It Is

Eleven years ago, when Theron read Corven's sealed letter for the first time, he found a second document folded inside it — not part of the ritual documentation itself. A letter from the former Restorer leadership (pre-schism, before the movement formalized under Edoran) addressed to the Archive and marked: *INTERCEPTED — DO NOT FORWARD — Filed under standing order 7.*

It is a warning letter. Specifically: a formal warning from a previous generation of Restorers, sent to the Archive, stating that an ongoing investigation into the Ritual of Eternal Dawn risked uncovering information that could be weaponized by the city's ruling faction. The letter argues that certain archival materials should be sealed pending a Council review of who is authorized to commission investigations into the ritual's reversal.

**The letter never reached the Council.** The Archivist at the time — Theron's predecessor — intercepted it under an obscure administrative order, filed it with Corven's materials, and wrote one sentence in the margin: *"Filed. Not my decision to make."*

Theron found it when he found the sealed letter. He has been sitting on it for eleven years.

**What it reveals:**

- The Restorer movement did not originate with Edoran. It existed in an earlier form, fifty years ago, and was already arguing about consent and the ritual cost in organized terms.
- Someone in the city administration knew about the letter's interception and chose to leave it buried.
- The standing order cited (Order 7) still exists. It authorizes the Archivist to intercept and hold communications regarding the Ritual of Eternal Dawn pending Chancellor review. Theron has technically been operating under this order by filing it and never forwarding it.

This is not the smoking gun — that remains Corven's sealed letter. But it tells players that the suppression of information about the ritual is not new, is not solely Theron's doing, and has institutional infrastructure behind it.

### When Theron Produces It

He does not produce it automatically. He produces it if one of the following occurs:

| Trigger | Notes |
|---------|-------|
| Players answer the riddle correctly on their first or second attempt, without wrong answers | He meets them at the door as it opens or just after. Seeing them solve it — especially quickly — he retrieves it from his coat pocket. He has been carrying it. |
| Players name the word CONSENT and then ask Theron a specific question: *"Was this the first time someone tried to investigate the ritual?"* | This is the right question. He retrieves the document. |
| Players ask Theron what he has been withholding | DC 15 Persuasion. On success, he produces the document along with a partial account of why he kept it. |
| Players impress Theron through the Sorting Table Margins check (Investigation DC 15, npcs.md) | He produces it as part of the trust escalation. |

### How He Hands It Over

He does not explain it. He sets it on the table, or places it in a player's hands, and says:

> *"I intercepted this eleven years ago. I found it inside Corven's materials. It was already there when I arrived — I didn't put it there. I have been meaning to forward it to the Council since the day I found it."*

He sits down.

> *"I haven't. I keep finding reasons not to."*

If players ask why: *"Because forwarding it requires deciding that the investigation should proceed. I was not comfortable making that decision. You seem to be making it for me."*

**What he does not say at this stage:** That he knows what Corven's letter contains. That the decision he couldn't make was whether to tell anyone that the ritual kills ten people. The document is a breadcrumb toward that revelation — the warning letter argues against investigation precisely because *"full disclosure of the mechanism's requirements would cause irreparable social harm."* Players who read carefully will note that phrase. It is not clear yet what it means.

## 8. Archetype-Specific Hooks

| Archetype | How the Riddle Lands |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **The Investigator** | Wants to solve it. Will attempt research before guessing. Has probably already noticed the Detect Magic option. Let them work through the frame — they're doing exactly what the scene is designed for. |
| **The Believer** | Has faith training. DC 10 Religion: the riddle reads like an Auris catechism question. The word CONSENT appears explicitly in Auris liturgy on the doctrine of the willing heart. The Believer may name it confidently before others have a chance to think. If they do: the door's response lands as confirmation of something they already understood. |
| **The Survivor** | Is not interested in the riddle as an intellectual exercise. Is interested in whether the people making decisions about this ritual asked anyone. May name CONSENT from that angle — not as a theological term but as a practical absence. The door opens. They may not think of that as solving a riddle at all. |
| **The Scholar** | Knows the notation system is the mechanism (Arcana or History DC 12 to recognize Corven's cipher in the carvings). Will likely suggest Detect Magic. Will proceed methodically. Will be slightly frustrated by the door's refusal to respond to synonyms — then intrigued. |
| **The Mercenary** | Tries the door. Gets a wrong answer. Looks for Theron. Practical. The scene's tension system is not maximally interesting to them unless they engage with the secondary objective (the withheld document as actionable intelligence). Frame the document as leverage. It is. |
| **The Dawnborn-Adjacent** | May already know something is wrong with how the ritual has been described to the public. When the answer is CONSENT, they are not surprised. They are tired. The door asking for CONSENT in a city where the ritual's cost was never explained to the people who pay it is the correct emotional note. Let the moment breathe. |

## 9. The Designed Peak Moment — When the Door Answers

This is the moment the scene is built toward.

### Setup

The players have either solved the riddle themselves or been brought to the door by Theron, who speaks the word. The carvings are shifting. The chord is sounding. The door is swinging inward.

### What the GM Says

Deliver this flat, without dramatic build:

> *"The carvings rotate — all of them, one after the next, starting at the top and moving around the ring. There is a sound. Not a bell — something with more resonance than that. Like a room with good acoustics answering a tuning fork. The door opens inward on its own.*
>
> *The restricted stacks are in there. Tall shelves. Dim light. The smell of old paper, and something else — something metallic and cold — the smell of a room that has been sealed a long time.*
>
> *The door doesn't know if you're the right people. It doesn't know why you came, or what you'll do with what's in there. It just answered the word you said."*

### Why It Matters

The door's indifference is the point. It was built 250 years ago by someone who understood that the difference between a gift and a theft is consent — and that consent means the giver's reasons are their own. The door doesn't ask why you want in. It asks if you understand the word. That is an important distinction in a campaign about whether the Dawnborn get to choose.

**If players solved it on the first try:** The door answered the right question before they had any context for why the question mattered. This is the correct version of events. They are, right now, further ahead than they know.

**If Theron had to guide them:** The door still opened. Theron is standing in the doorway. The chord is still resonating. The fact that they needed help to answer the question is not a failure — it is how most of the city has lived for 250 years: not because they couldn't have named the word, but because no one asked them the question.

**If players bypassed the door (Dispel, force):** The Peak Moment does not fire. The door is just open. There is no chord, no rotation, no resonance. They got in. The scene has nothing to give them that they didn't take. Note this and let it sit. It will matter when they reach Session 3 and have to decide whether the Dawnborn's consent is required, or merely preferred.
