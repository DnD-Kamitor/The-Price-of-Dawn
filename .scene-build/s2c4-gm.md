## Pacing

This scene runs long. That is correct. Do not rush it.

Scene 4 is the pivot of Session 2. Everything in Scenes 1 through 3 has been preparation for this room. The combat rescued the documents. The riddle decoded the first layer. The puzzle opened the physical proof. This scene is what the session was actually for.

A scene this structurally important is allowed to take thirty minutes. It is allowed to take forty-five. It can run until players are ready to leave. There is no encounter clock. Nothing is arriving. The Archive is empty. Give the room its due.

---

## Do Not Rush Past EXTINGUISHED

The word EXTINGUISHED is the scene's emotional peak. It is also the word that will be remembered after the session ends — the one players will quote to each other weeks later when they're talking about this campaign.

What "do not rush past it" means in practice:

- Deliver the word at the end of a sentence. Do not continue immediately after it.
- After you say it, stop.
- Let the table process.
- If the room goes quiet, that quiet is the scene working. Sit in it.
- Do not say "so anyway" or use any transitional phrase that signals "moving on now."
- When Theron continues after the pause, he sits. The chair sound marks the transition. That is the beat structure. Use it.

If a player speaks immediately after EXTINGUISHED — responds before the silence can land — let them. Then let the response settle. Then the scene continues from where they took it. You do not need to force the silence if a player has something they need to say right now. What you do need to do is not fill the space yourself with explanation or elaboration.

The word explains itself. Theron chose it after eleven years. Trust it.

---

## Handling a Player Who Tries to Skip to "What Do We Do"

Some players will receive the EXTINGUISHED beat and immediately want to move to problem-solving. This is a valid response to the information — it is how some people process weight — and the scene should honor it.

What to do: acknowledge it by having Theron answer it. The answer is: *"That is no longer my question to answer."* That is not a deflection. It is a complete answer about where the agency now lives. It does not prevent the player from asking follow-up questions.

Then, once the practical response has been received and honored, check with the rest of the table. Not by breaking the fiction — stay in scene — but by allowing a beat of silence after Theron's response that creates space for a different kind of reaction from another player.

Not every player processes information the same way. The practical player and the grief-struck player are both correct. The scene holds both simultaneously. Do not flatten it to one register by following only the fastest-moving player.

## The Secondary Objective — The Date Question

If any player asks what Theron would have done if no investigators had arrived — or if he ever intended to act — the answer is the scene's most revealing moment.

He had a date. Three years from now. He was going to go to Sera directly.

This answer does several things:

1. It prevents Theron from being purely passive. He was not simply waiting indefinitely. He had a plan, even if the plan was years away and uncertain.
2. It reveals something honest about the difference between resolve and capacity. He does not know if he could have done it. He made the date anyway.
3. It shifts how morally legible he is. He is not someone who abandoned his responsibility entirely. He is someone who could not complete the act yet and scheduled a future self to try.

Deliver it slowly. The line about not knowing if he could do it then either is the key sentence. Let it land before the scene moves.

---

## What Theron Does If a Player Mentions Sera Specifically

Theron has watched Sera build the Dawnhall for years. He knows who she is not as a symbol but as a person — the specific person he would have gone to if players had never come. If a player names her in this scene, his response is different from his response to any other Dawnborn name.

He is still for a moment. Longer than usual.

Then:

> "She will take this better than she should. That is the thing I most fear about telling her."

He means: Sera is likely to consent. Her faith is real and her care for the city is real and she will probably say yes, and Theron knows this, and he has spent eleven years with the knowledge that the person he admires most is the person most likely to agree to be extinguished.

He does not say all of this. He says the one line. Players who push will get more. Players who don't will carry the line anyway.

---

## What Theron Does If Players Are Hostile Throughout

Some parties may arrive at this scene with anger already present from Scene 3 (particularly if the box was forced, or if a document bundle was lost in Scene 1's fire). That anger is legitimate. Theron in Scene 4 does not manage it, deflect it, or perform contrition to dissolve it.

What he does: he receives it. He answers honest questions honestly. He does not raise his voice, does not become defensive, does not try to frame his choices in the most favorable light. If they call him a coward, he does not argue.

The scene does not require a warm ending. Players can leave this room angry. They will still have Isolde's location if they asked, Fragment 2 if they found it, and the full truth of what the ritual requires. Their relationship with Theron is damaged — that is a real and trackable consequence. It does not prevent the session from moving forward.

## After the Word Lands — Reading the Room

After EXTINGUISHED, before continuing: look at your players. Not their characters — your players. Some tables will go very quiet. Some tables will start talking immediately. Some will have one person speak for several people.

What you are checking for: is there someone at the table who is sitting with something? A player who looks like they are processing something beyond the game? That player is the one to check in with after the session, or during a break if the scene runs long. This is a hard scene. The weight in it is designed to be felt. If someone is feeling it, that is the scene working correctly — and that player deserves a quiet word at the right moment.

---

## The Transition Out

Scene 4 transitions into Scene 5 (Harran's Arrival) when players are ready to leave the Archive. There is no mechanical trigger — the players decide when they're done.

Before they go: remind yourself (not them) that they are about to walk out of a building carrying documents that the Reckoning tried to burn today, and that Harran Lecht is going to be in the alley. The emotional state players are in when they leave this room is the emotional state they carry into that encounter.

A party that has just processed grief may be slower to engage. A party that is cold and practical may move fast. Both have different implications for how Scene 5 plays. Let the emotional register carry.

---

## What Theron Does After the Players Leave

He stays in the study. He does not go home. At some point he closes the box, not because it needs to be closed but because it is the thing at hand. He will be in this building when players come back — if they come back. He has nowhere else to be. The Archive is his. He opened it to them. He will keep it open.

If players ever return without having resolved the campaign, Theron has begun going through his suppressed research notes again — the ones he did not destroy — and compiling them into a single document. He does not know what it is for. He is not sure he will give it to anyone. He is doing it because he stopped burning things and has to do something with the space that left.
