## This Is the End of Session 1

Do not lose sight of this. Everything after Edoran steps aside is credits. The session ends in this lane. If the conversation runs well and there is more to say — let the conversation run, and then end it. If the conversation runs poorly and players are disengaged — end it sooner. The session does not extend past this scene.

10-15 minutes at the table. That is the target. The scene is not a puzzle, not a fight, not a revelation — it is a last voice in the players' heads before they go home. Give it the weight of a last voice, not the length of a chapter.

---

## The Scene's One Job

Players are leaving Session 1 with two voices in their heads:

**Sera Voss:** I have known for years what I am. I chose. I keep choosing.

**Brother Edoran:** You have no idea what you are looking for. The mechanism is people. The question is consent, not mechanics.

These are not the same voice. They are not in conflict — Edoran agrees with Sera's choice; Sera would recognize the validity of Edoran's concern. But they are different approaches to the same truth, and players will carry both.

This scene's one job is to deliver Edoran's voice cleanly and completely. Not to resolve it. Not to position him as wrong or right. To deliver it.

---

## Pacing: Three Modes

**If players engage immediately:** Walk the beats. Give Beat 3 its full weight — let players share what they found, let him calibrate. Don't rush to the living conduit line. The beats have a rhythm; trust it.

**If players are guarded or skeptical:** He is patient with this. He does not need them to be open. He needs them to be in the lane. Every moment they stand in front of him and argue or probe or push back is a moment they are receiving what he came to give. Let the skepticism happen. He will answer it, and the answering is the scene.

**If players are immediately hostile:** This is the hardest version. He does not escalate. He is calm. Give him the opening line and let the hostility land and let him choose not to match it. His patience in the face of hostility is itself information — players who are paying attention register that this is a man who has been practicing this patience for a long time.

---

## The Secondary Objective

Every scene in The Price of Dawn has a secondary objective. For this scene:

**Approach and stay long enough to have a real conversation.**

That is it. Players who approach Edoran and remain in conversation — even if they argue, even if they are wrong, even if they are rude — have achieved the secondary objective. Players who avoid him or fight through him have not.

The secondary objective is not about agreeing with him. It is about being present with him. The consent question, the living conduit confirmation, the oblique reference to the Inversion, the grief underneath the theology — all of these are available to players who stay. None of them are available to players who leave.

Do not announce the secondary objective. Players will feel the difference between a session that ends with Edoran's last line and a session that ends without it.

---

## The Living Conduit Beat — Delivery

> *"You found the phrase 'living conduit.' You think it's a mechanism. It's not a mechanism. It's ten people."*

This is the scene's intellectual center. Let it land before continuing.

The frame shift Edoran is making: players have been thinking about the Dawnborn as a system component. They are the mechanism. Edoran is repositioning: the people are not in the mechanism. They are what the mechanism is made of. That distinction changes what investigation means, what consent means, what the players are actually doing.

After this line: a beat. Then let players respond. Do not continue to the next line until the first has been received.

---

## The Consent Argument — Do Not Simplify It

Edoran's theological position is not simple. He is not arguing that the Dawnborn should be killed. He is not arguing that their consent is irrelevant. He is arguing something more complex: that they were born into a situation they did not choose, that they must choose what to do with it, and that the choosing is real and matters and has to be genuine.

This position is uncomfortable because it is partly right. Players who try to find the simple objection — "but they'll die" — will find that he has already accounted for this. Players who try to find the coercive reading of his position — "but you're pressuring them" — will find that he specifically disagrees with Harran Lecht on exactly this ground.

He is the most dangerous kind of antagonist: one who is partly right. Do not make him simpler than he is. Let his position be what it is, in full.

---

## The Daughter Question — Handle It with Care

If a player finds the crack — asks the question that touches Mira — the scene changes shape.

What to do:

1. Stop. Edoran goes still.
2. Let the stillness be visible — say "He stops" or "he goes very still — not the patient stillness from before, something different."
3. Wait. If players wait, he speaks: *"She didn't."*
4. Let "She didn't" sit. Do not add anything immediately from him. Do not add anything from you. Let the two words exist in the scene.
5. If players push, he gives them Mira's age and the grey sickness, and he stops. He does not explain the connection between her death and the movement. Players can see it or not.
6. He returns to the scene after a beat. Quieter. The precision is slightly gone.

**What not to do:** Do not have Edoran cry. Do not have him become emotional in any expressive way. The grief is not dramatic. It is the very still center of a man who has organized everything else around it for fourteen years. Stillness is the only appropriate register.

**What not to do:** Do not prompt players toward this question. If it doesn't come up in this session, it does not come up. Players who find it in Session 3 or 4 — when they have learned Mira's name — will have a different experience with it. That is also correct.

---

## The Exit Line

> "The Restorers will not trouble you again tonight."

**"Tonight."** Not "again." Not "ever." This is precise. He means it exactly as said — the Restorers will not interfere with the players during the remaining hours of this night. He is making no promise about tomorrow.

Players who notice the word will have a thread. Do not call attention to it. He says it and steps aside. The word is there for players who were listening.

He steps aside without ceremony. He does not wish them well. He does not say the conversation mattered, even if it did. He is already done — whatever he came to do, he has done it, and what players do with it is not something he can affect now.

---

## Session 1 Closing Hook

After Edoran is no longer in the lane — after the players are standing alone on Crestwick Lane with the Dawnhall behind them and the open city ahead — deliver this as the GM, directly to the players:

> You have two documents you cannot fully decode. A man who built a movement on grief. A woman who serves soup and has chosen to die. You've been hired to investigate a fifty-year-old ritual failure. You know what the words mean now. You still don't know what they mean.

Then stop. That is the end of Session 1.

---

## What Edoran Does After Players Leave

He does not watch them go. He turns back into the alley and speaks briefly with the zealots — quiet enough that players who are still within earshot can hear a low murmur but not words. Then the alley is empty.

He will spend the night writing. He has been writing since his daughter died. The pages are in his rooms in the Restorer hall in the Ashfen district. Players will not see those pages until Session 5.

He is not afraid of what the players will do next. He is waiting to see what they choose, the way he has been waiting to see what the Dawnborn choose. He has been practicing waiting for twenty years. He is patient about what is still unresolved.

---

## Do Not Roll for What Edoran Knows

He knows. He chose not to say. That is the choice, and it was made before the scene started, and it does not need a check to confirm. Let it be.

He knows about the Inversion pathway. He knows it is in Fragment 2 of the Notation Key, which he found in the Archive twelve years ago. He has been sitting with it for twelve years — the possibility that the ritual can complete without deaths if every Dawnborn freely and genuinely consents. He has not told the Restorers. He has not told the Chancellor. He has not told anyone. He is not sure why. He knows it is something he should tell the players. He did not tell them.

Players who find this in Session 4 will return to this scene in their memories. They will remember that he told them the Archive had more than they'd found. He was telling them where to look. He was not telling them what was there. They will have to decide what that means about him.

Let that question live. Do not resolve it tonight.
