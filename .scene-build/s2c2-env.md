## Physical Space: Archive Reading Room, Post-Combat

This is the same room as Scene 1. Nothing has been moved on purpose — only overturned by the fight. That distinction matters: the disorder is the Reckoning's, not the Archive's. The players are still standing in evidence of an attack.

### State of the Room

| Element | Scene 1 State | Scene 2 State |
|---|---|---|
| Chairs | Arranged at reading tables | Two overturned near the south wall; one dragged to block the aisle during combat |
| Lamp oil | All lamps lit | East lamp has been knocked crooked; still burning but casting light at an angle |
| Document bundles | Stacked on the central table | Some pulled, one partially open — the Reckoning was searching before the players interrupted |
| Fireplace | Burning normally | Still hot; the fire has settled from its peak but the coals are live and bright |
| Floor | Clean stone | A smear of blood near the south door (not a player's, unless it is) |
| Air | Warm, slightly dusty | Woodsmoke heavier now; the fireplace drew the smell of the fight into the room |

### Why This Room Works Dramatically

The players just defended these documents. The Reckoning came for something. Theron produces three slips the Reckoning did not come for — they were in a locked desk drawer, unknown to the raiders. This contrast is load-bearing: the soldiers were after the official record. The truth was hidden in something that looked like personal correspondence.

The room carries the memory of violence and has already returned to quiet. That quiet is not comfort — it is the Archive asserting its nature over what just happened. Paper and silence outlast soldiers.

### The Reading Alcove

A small wooden partition in the northeast corner of the room, barely wide enough for a chair and a standing shelf. The partition is old, dark with oil and age. The shelf holds three reference volumes and a spare inkwell. The chair inside is the only chair in the room with a cushion — Theron uses this alcove for close work.

He heard everything that happened in Scene 1 from behind this partition. He did not come out until the last soldier was down. He is not ashamed of this. He is shaking slightly because his body has been flooded with adrenaline and is now processing that it is over. The shaking is physiology, not cowardice — or it is both, and he knows it.

When he emerges, he does not look at the bodies or the mess. He goes directly to his desk.

### Lighting

This is the best-lit scene in the session. The amber lanterns run the full length of the south and west walls. The fireplace is on the east wall and still burning well. Everything in the room is visible. There are no shadows to hide in, no darkness to obscure expressions.

That is intentional. Theron chose this room for this scene, even if he never said so aloud. He is going to say something in the light.

### The Table Setup

Theron does not use his desk. He takes three parchment slips from the locked bottom drawer of his desk (the key is on his person, attached to a short chain on his belt), carries them to the nearest reading table, and sets them down side by side, left to right.

He then sits in the chair on the opposite side of the table from his desk — the visitor's side. He puts nothing between himself and the players. No desk, no authority. Just the table and three slips of paper.

**The three slips, physically:**

- **Slip 1:** Cream-colored parchment, approximately 10cm x 7cm. Folded three times — the creases have gone white. The ink is dark and even, written in a precise hand. This is the original.
- **Slip 2:** Same dimensions. Slightly yellowed at one edge — it was once near a damp surface, briefly. The fold lines match Slip 1 in depth. Also original.
- **Slip 3:** Same dimensions. The ink is *almost* identical to the others, but slightly darker. The fold lines match perfectly — it has been refolded to match. This slip was rewritten. The original was destroyed.

**Also on the table:**

- **The desk drawer** (still open, across the room, visible from the table) — empty except for a small brass key hook and a faded ribbon, the kind used to bind document rolls.
- **Theron's pen case** — he carries it in his left breast pocket at all times. He sets it on the table when he sits down, not because he needs it, but because setting things on tables is what he does when his hands are otherwise empty. He straightens it twice during the scene without noticing.
