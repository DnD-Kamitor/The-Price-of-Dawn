## Per-Archetype Inserts

These are prompts for the GM to deliver in-session — either as private asides to individual players or as public observations framed around their characters. Deliver at the most relevant moment during the puzzle work or its aftermath.

---

### Scholar / Wizard / Investigator

You have been decoding Corven's notation for a session now. Archival work. Obscured systems. Deliberate complexity.

This is different.

Corven's notation was dense because the field is dense — because resonance theory requires precision. These three slips are encoded simply. Too simply for the content. Someone who understood distributed notation well enough to use it chose a method that an intermediate archival scholar could crack in under an hour.

That is a choice about the intended audience.

The question is not just *what* is encoded. It is: what kind of person hides something this way, in a form designed to be found — and then waits?

*Prompt:* Does your character recognize what it means to encode something to be discoverable rather than to be kept? What does that psychology look like to someone who has spent their career in information systems?

---

### Rogue / Infiltrator

Third-word ciphers. You have seen simpler variants — usually in market-runner notes, debt acknowledgments, messages passed between people who couldn't be seen talking to each other. The kind of code that isn't trying to be unbreakable. It's trying to be plausibly deniable. If you are found carrying it, you can claim you don't know what it means.

This is that technique applied to something of considerably greater consequence.

It is almost insultingly simple. You could break this in the time it takes to drink a cup of tea. You suspect the archivist knows that. You wonder if the simplicity is the point — if the message is meant to be findable by anyone with even a passing familiarity with coded communication.

Which raises the question of why it was never found before. Who has been in this room? Who had access to that desk?

*Prompt:* Do you tell the table what you know about this encoding style? Or do you work it yourself first, and then share? What does it say about Theron that he used street-runner encryption to encode a discovery of this magnitude?

---

### Cleric / Religious Character

The phrase "willing vessel" appears in Slip 1: *"The vessel must be willing."*

Your order uses that phrase. The initiation rite for second-circle members includes a spoken affirmation: *"I am a willing vessel for the light of [deity name]."* You said those words. You were told they were a metaphor for spiritual receptivity — for the opening of the self to divine influence.

You were not told what it meant if the metaphor was literal. If "vessel" was technical rather than symbolic. If "willing" was a ritual requirement rather than a spiritual aspiration.

The phrase in the slip feels different in your mouth now than it did when you said it in the rite. You are not sure what to do with that.

*Prompt:* Do you say anything about this to the table? Does it change what you are looking for in the cipher? Does it change how you look at Theron?

---

### Social / Face Character

Theron is not asking for help.

Watch how he does it. He places three slips of paper on a table and sits down across from you. He says he encoded them intentionally to be found. He then does not say another word unless prompted. He calls this giving you the cipher. You would call this a performance of vulnerability that maintains total informational control.

He is deciding what you know by deciding what he encodes. He is deciding when you know it by deciding when he produces the slips. He has structured this moment so that you have to work for the truth, and so that he can say, afterward, that he gave it to you freely.

Watch his hands when you get close to the answer. The pen case. He straightens it when he is managing something. You have met people who do this — who organize their surroundings when their interior is in motion.

*Prompt:* Do you engage with the puzzle directly, or do you engage with Theron? Is there value in getting him to tell you instead of decoding it yourself — and what does it cost him if he has to do that?

---

### Martial / Soldier Character

You have been in situations where someone had information that could have changed outcomes. Someone who knew about the ambush and didn't send word in time. Someone who had a supply count wrong and didn't say so until the column was already moving. Sometimes it was cowardice. Sometimes it was hierarchy. Sometimes they genuinely thought they were protecting someone by not saying it.

It rarely helped.

You are watching Theron the way you watch people in that category — not with contempt, because you have learned contempt is a luxury, but with a specific kind of attention. You are calculating how long he has known and what has happened in the city during those eleven years that might have gone differently. You are not sure you want to finish that calculation.

*Prompt:* Does your character say anything about this to Theron, or do they hold it? If the table decodes the cipher and Theron says "Yes" — what is the first thing your character thinks, not says?

## Passive Knowledge Unlocks (Automatic, No Roll)

These apply without a check. Inform the relevant player before the scene begins or at the moment of relevance.

| Background / History | What They Know | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Sage background** | Distributed notation is a documented technique — *Principles of Archival Notation*, Goss, third edition. Pattern is "every Nth word across unified text." | Advantage on Investigation to identify N. Skips all dead-end approaches. |
| **Charlatan background** | Recognizes coded messages by structural signature alone — the text feels wrong at sentence level, like the surface meaning is load-bearing but hollow. | DC 8 Investigation check to identify the slips as encoded becomes automatic pass. |
| **Any character who decoded Fragment 1 in Session 1** | "The energy lives within them" in Slip 3 maps to "living conduit mechanism" from Fragment 1. The phrase is the same concept in two different registers. | Automatic recognition. Grants advantage on the next Arcana check in Scene 3. |
| **Guild Artisan background (amber-working)** | "Third circuit" is a workshop term — amber-workers use "circuits" to describe energy layers in raw amber. Third circuit is the deepest, the one that doesn't release during normal carving. | Grants advantage on Arcana DC 10. |
