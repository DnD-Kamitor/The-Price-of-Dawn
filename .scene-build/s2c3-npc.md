## Theron Waide — Master Archivist

*No combat stats. Theron does not fight in this scene or any scene unless circumstances become extreme. If violence reaches the study, he places himself in front of the box. Not heroically — it is the only thing he is willing to protect with his body, and he knows it.*

### Physical Description

Late fifties. Lean in the way of someone who has always worked indoors and forgotten to eat through the middle of the day. Grey-brown hair, cut practically, not vanity-trimmed. Glasses that he removes when reading close and replaces when looking at a distance — a habit so automatic he does not notice doing it. He is wearing his work coat: dark, well-made, with the slight asymmetry at the left breast pocket where his pen case lives. He has not removed the coat since the soldiers arrived. He will not remove it during this scene.

His hands are still. Not the stillness of calm — the stillness of someone who has learned to make their hands still when their mind is not. Watch them when a correct name is spoken into the box. They are not still then.

### OGAS

| **Occupation** | Master Archivist of the Civic Archive, thirty years in post. The longest-tenured archivist in the building's current history. He knows where every document in the Archive is, what it contains, who has read it, and in three cases, why its existence was suppressed. He has kept those three cases in his memory rather than the record system. |
| **Goal** | To be absolved without asking for absolution. He wants someone to hear what he has done and tell him it was not as bad as he thinks, or that they will handle what comes next, or simply to acknowledge that carrying it for eleven years was a form of doing something, even if that something was not enough. He will not say any of this. He will wait for it. |
| **Attitude** | Waiting. Patient in the way of someone who has been patient so long the patience has become structural. Quietly terrified beneath it — not of the players, not of what is in the box, but of the moment the box opens and the weight of it passes from him to them. He is about to become a person who told someone, and he does not know what kind of person that is. |
| **Secret** | He tried to open the box himself twice. The first time was six months after he sealed it — he pressed the first indentation and could not make himself speak Corven's name. Not because he did not know it. Because saying it out loud in this room, with the box under his hands, would have meant confronting what the name attached to. He sat with his finger in the indentation for what he later estimated was four minutes. He did not say it. The second time was three years ago, on the anniversary of the ritual. Same result. He has not tried a third time. He does not know if he ever could. He has not told the players this. He will, if asked directly. He will tell them in a flat voice, as if reporting a fact about someone else. |

### Want / Fear / Lie

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | For the players to open the box. Not to have to open it himself. For the burden to be transferred cleanly, with the evidence intact, to people who arrived with enough purpose to come here at all. For this to be over — not the consequence, just the keeping-it-secret. |
| **Fear** | That the players will open the box and use what they find to do exactly what he spent eleven years preventing someone from doing: treat the Dawnborn as a mechanism rather than people. That by handing over the evidence he becomes complicit in whatever comes next. He chose to wait for someone who would handle it well. He does not know if he chose correctly. |
| **Lie** | That he did not help the Reckoning find the documents in Scene 1. He did not — but he could have hidden them better. He told himself he left the bundles accessible because he trusted the Archive's security. He knows, and has not admitted, that he wanted someone to find them. Someone external. Someone who would force the issue that he could not. The soldiers finding the documents was not what he intended. But it was not a surprise. |

### Voice and Register

Theron speaks in complete sentences. He does not trail off mid-thought. When he pauses, it is because he is deciding what to say, not because he does not know. The pauses are long enough to be noticeable. Players who try to fill them are welcome to. He will wait.

He does not use diminutives. He does not perform warmth. When he is direct, he is direct without softening it. When he is evasive, he knows he is being evasive, and if pressed he will acknowledge it.

His register drops during the scene. When players arrive, he is formal and contained. By the time the box opens, he is speaking quietly, as if to himself as much as to them. This is not performance. He has stopped managing his presentation. That is the scene's emotional arc.

### Dialogue Lines

**When players first enter the study:**
> "I've been waiting up here since the soldiers left. I thought you'd come sooner."
> *(He doesn't say what he expected them to do in the interval — investigate, question the Lieutenant, catch their breath. He simply notes the wait.)*

**If players ask what the box is:**
> "Evidence. Everything else is copies or interpretations. That box contains the original notation, in Corven's hand. It is the only physical proof that the redirection was intentional and not a failure."

**If players ask why he never opened it:**
> *(Pause. A longer pause than anything else in the scene.)*
> "I could not say their names. Not out loud. Not here."
> *(He does not elaborate unless pressed. If pressed:)* "I knew the names. I know the method. I knew what would happen if I spoke them. But standing here, with my hand on the lid — no. Eleven years and I could not do it twice."

**If players ask who the names are:**
> *(He looks at the documents, not at the players.)*
> "You have the colophons. Three witnesses. The names are there."
> *(He will not say the names. He is not being obstructive — he is genuinely incapable of saying them in this room, with the box present. If players ask him to say them directly, he opens his mouth. He closes it. He says:)* "Find them in the documents. I need you to find them there."

**If players speak a wrong name:**
> *(The box sounds a low tone. Theron closes his eyes. He does not speak immediately. After the thirty-second reseal, quietly:)* "Not that one."

**If players speak a correct name:**
> *(The indentation lights faintly. Theron opens his hands at his sides, a small involuntary movement, the gesture of someone releasing something.)*
> He says nothing.

**When all three names are spoken and the box opens:**
> Theron stands. He does not approach the desk. He is standing at the back of the room, near the shelf gap where the box used to live. He looks at the open box. He says, after a moment:
> "Go ahead."
> *(He means: read it. He cannot be the one to read it again. He has read it. He needs them to read it now.)*

**If the box is forced open (Thieves' Tools):**
> He sits down heavily in his chair — not deliberately, the way a person sits when their legs decide before they do. He looks at the scorched edges of the mechanism.
> "Eleven years. I could have just—"
> *(He does not finish. The sentence ends because he does not have an ending for it that he is willing to say. Players who ask what he was about to say: he shakes his head. He means it.)*

**If asked directly "Why did you never go to the authorities?":**
> "I tried once. Before Ostenveld. The result was two weeks in a holding room and my documents returned damaged. The city did not want to know. I decided to wait for someone who did."

**If asked "Why did you wait for us specifically?":**
> *(He looks at them. He considers his answer.)*
> "I didn't wait for you specifically. I waited for whoever came with enough purpose to be here at all. You came. That is what I was waiting for."

### Body Language During the Puzzle

**Before any attempt:** Theron stands behind the desk, to the side, giving the players access to the box without placing himself between them and it. He is not presiding. He is watching. His arms are at his sides.

**When a player picks up the box:** He watches them notice the weight. He watches them notice the warmth. He does not say "yes" or "you feel it." He waits to see if they mention it.

**When players are searching the documents for the names:** He moves to the corner of the room, near the window. He keeps his back mostly to them. This is not theatrical. He does not want to watch them find the names the way he should have found them, and name them the way he could not.

**When the first correct name is spoken and the box responds:** He turns back. He is facing the room again. Both hands are loose at his sides.

**When the box opens:** He stands. He is still. He breathes. He watches the players take out the documents. Something shifts in his posture — not relief exactly, not grief exactly. The look of a man who has been holding a door closed for a very long time and has just let someone else take the handle.
