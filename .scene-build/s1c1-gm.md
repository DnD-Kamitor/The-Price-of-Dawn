# Session 1, Scene 1 — The Restorer Ambush: GM Build File

<!-- SCENE HEADER BLOCK — place above <details> tabs -->

---

**PURPOSE STATEMENT:** This scene exists to establish that someone already knows what the players are doing — and has decided to stop it before it starts. The ambush is not about violence; it is about the weight of organised opposition, and about planting the first seed of doubt: the people trying to stop this investigation may not be wrong.

**EMOTIONAL TARGET:** Players should feel *watched* — not just in this scene, but retroactively. The sense that they were already being tracked before they even started should linger past the fight and into the Archive.

**DRAMATIC QUESTION:** Will you take the thing that tells you who sent them, or will you let it burn?

**SURPRISE:** The Enforcer does not want to hurt anyone. He's good at his job, which is intimidation, and intimidation only works if the subject stays alive. Players expecting a murder attempt find instead a coordinated warning delivered by professionals — which is more frightening, not less, because it implies resources, advance knowledge, and restraint. The Restorers had the players' route before the players left the Chancellor's office.

**DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT:** The note-burning attempt.

→ Round 2 of combat has begun. The players are mid-fight, the crowd is scattering, and the zealots are pressing hard.

→ GM narration (delivered flat, mid-round, before the zealot's turn): *"The zealot nearest the market crate crouches without breaking eye contact with you — and holds the folded paper to the lantern flame."*

→ The players must choose, right now, between the fight in front of them and the intelligence burning in the corner. They cannot do both at full effectiveness. Whatever they sacrifice to save the note — or fail to save — defines how much they learn about Edoran this session.

This is what players quote after the session: *"The one with the note just crouched down and held it to the flame like he had all the time in the world."*

**PREP vs IMPROVISE:**

Must prep:
- The note's physical description (sealed, belt pouch, visibly present before round 1 — plant it in the scene-opening narration)
- The Enforcer's two-line yield speech (silence, then hands up — he has been instructed to say nothing, so he says nothing; this is more unnerving than a speech)
- Edoran's exact position in the alley and his sightlines (30 ft, south alley mouth, can see the whole junction — confirm this on your map before play)

Safe to improvise:
- How many civilians catch a stray elbow and react (crowd chaos is texture, not plot)
- The zealots' individual fighting styles and banter (they're cultist stat blocks — make them feel fervent, not tactical)
- Whether the queue disperses into the Archive side-street or the Lowmark market (either works for Scene 2 transition)

## 1. Guy Sanders Techniques — Applied to This Scene

---

### SECONDARY OBJECTIVE: How to Make the Note Feel Urgent Without Explaining It

Do not tell players the note is important. Show them the zealot is protecting it.

**Delivery technique — pre-initiative, in the scene-opening narration:**

*"One of them — the shortest, nearest the crates — has a sealed packet tucked visibly into his belt. He clocks your eyes on it. His hand moves across it once, a small deliberate gesture, then drops back to his side."*

That gesture is everything. It tells players: he knows you noticed. It tells them: he is prepared to do something about it. It creates a question — *what is in that packet, and why does he care whether you see it?* — without answering it.

Then, in round 2, the burning attempt answers the question by threatening to remove it permanently. The urgency is retrospective: the moment the flame appears, the pre-initiative gesture snaps into meaning.

**Do not name the note. Do not say "clue." Say only what a character in the scene would see.**

---

### FIVE SENSES — Timing

| Sense | Content | When to Deliver |
|---|---|---|
| **Sight** | Amber lanternlight on cobblestones, food queue stretching the block, bundled figures, the zealots cutting through the crowd with deliberate speed | **Pre-initiative** — first paragraph of scene narration, before any roll is called |
| **Sound** | The queue's low murmur, a vendor calling quantities, then boots on stone — fast, deliberate, wrong for the context | **Pre-initiative** — second sentence; the sound shift is what signals threat before sight confirms it |
| **Smell** | Lamp oil and old bread from the distribution cart; the sharper sweat of bodies moving to cut off exits | **Round 1, after initiative** — give this as the crowd compresses. It arrives with the physical press of the fight |
| **Touch** | The crowd closing behind players, no clean retreat route | **Round 1** — narrate after first enemy move, when players feel the tactical trap. "The queue has thickened behind you — there's nowhere to back into." |
| **Taste** | Dust and cold air, the dryness of a city that hasn't had open sky in fifty years | **Round 2 or transition out** — this one is atmospheric and elegiac, not tactical. Use it as the fight peaks or as the scene closes. It reminds players what is at stake beneath the brawl |

**Rule:** Sight and sound belong to the approach. Smell and touch belong to the fight. Taste belongs to the pause after.

---

### NPC QUICK REF — The Enforcer

**WANTS:** To complete this job without injury to his people or the targets — a clean warning, nothing that brings the City Guard down on the Restorers tonight.

**FEARS:** That one of the zealots loses nerve and actually hurts someone, escalating this into something Edoran can't contain.

**LIES:** That he is simply a hired enforcer following instructions he doesn't understand. He has read the note. He knows what Edoran is asking. He believes in it. He tells himself he's a professional keeping distance from the cause; he is not.

**At the table:** When the Enforcer speaks (pre-yield), he uses job language: *"Stand down. Walk away. This doesn't need to go further."* When he yields, he says nothing. His silence is not sullenness — it is instruction. He has been told that anything he says can be used. He is protecting Edoran by giving the players nothing.

---

### ENCOUNTER BEAT — The Pivot

**The pivot is not the note. The pivot is Edoran.**

Midway through the fight (round 2 or 3, whenever a player has a high Perception or specifically scans the perimeter), the GM delivers:

*"There is a figure in the south alley mouth. Thirty feet away, plain coat, hood up, very still. He is not moving toward the fight. He is watching it."*

This changes the scene's direction. The fight was about survival and the note. Now there is a third thing in the scene: a man who sent these people and is watching to see what happens next. Players who notice him have a choice — engage the combat fully, or break off and cross 30 feet to reach him.

This pivot reframes everything that has already happened. The zealots aren't random opposition; they're a test being observed in real time. The Enforcer isn't the leader; he's a proxy. The scene's actual stakes expand the moment that still figure becomes visible.

**When to deliver the pivot:** After the note-burning attempt has been resolved (or failed), so players are not tracking three things at once. The note is the micro-crisis; Edoran's presence in the alley is the macro-revelation.

---

### STAKES MAP

**What players can GAIN:**
- The note (Edoran's handwriting, the scripture quote — first evidence he is not simply a fanatic)
- Edoran's words if they break off and reach him: *"You have no idea what you are looking for. When you find it, you will understand why I did not want you to."*
- The implicit knowledge that their route from the Chancellor's office was known before they left it

**What players can LOSE:**
- The note, if no one breaks from the fight in time (the zealot gets it to the flame)
- The chance to reach Edoran in the alley — if they spend every action on the Enforcer, he steps back into the dark before they think to cross to him (he watches through round 3, then goes)
- Civilian goodwill — if the fight sprawls into the food queue, the Lowmark residents remember it

**The CHOICE the scene actually asks:**
*Do you finish the fight properly, or do you accept a worse tactical position to grab the intelligence?*

This is the campaign's first version of its central question in miniature: do you prioritise the immediate, visible problem, or the harder-to-reach thing that tells you what's actually happening?

## 2. Scene Transitions

---

### IN — Closing the Chancellor Scene, Opening Scene 1

Use this when the players leave the Chancellor's offices:

*"Ostenveld's door closes behind you with the specific quietness of an expensive latch. The corridor outside is empty. Down the stair, through the public hall, and then you are back in Lowmark — amber lanterns in their brackets, the food registry queue already stretching to the corner, bundled figures shuffling forward with the patient exhaustion of people who have been doing this for a long time. The Archive is twenty minutes east on foot. You don't make it five."*

**Technique:** The Chancellor's door close is precise and slightly cold; Lowmark is immediate and human. The queue — people waiting, patient, exhausted — is the thematic context for everything that follows. The ambush interrupts a scene of ordinary suffering, which is why it feels wrong rather than just dangerous.

---

### OUT — Closing Scene 1, Opening Scene 2

Use this after the Enforcer yields and the zealots scatter:

*"The queue re-forms around you. It takes about ninety seconds — the particular resilience of people who have learned not to let disruptions cost them their place in line. Someone picks up a dropped basket. Someone else retrieves a child from behind a crate. The junction looks almost normal. The south alley is empty. You are still holding [the note / nothing — call whatever they retrieved or failed to retrieve]. The Archive is fifteen minutes east. Whatever just happened, someone knew you were coming."*

**Technique:** The queue reforming is the emotional punctuation — life in Varenhold continues regardless, which is quietly devastating. The final line drives the paranoia forward without melodrama. It opens the Archive scene on unease rather than triumph.

## 3. Improv Anchors

---

**Anchor 1: Players try to avoid the scene entirely — go around the junction**

The Enforcer anticipated this. Two zealots are positioned on the Lowmark side-street that bypasses the junction. Going around the food queue means hitting a smaller version of the same encounter — two cultists, same warning, no Enforcer. The note is not with these two; it stayed with the main group. Players who avoid the ambush avoid the note entirely. They find out someone tried to stop them; they don't find out who. Let this be a genuine loss — it costs them intelligence — without punishing them for tactical thinking.

**Anchor 2: Players de-escalate completely — no combat, total diplomatic resolution**

This is allowed. DC 14 Persuasion or Intimidation to make the Enforcer stand down without fighting; he will not name his employer regardless. If players succeed, the zealots back off — but the shortest zealot, with the note, moves to the alley and hands it to the figure in the hood before the players can reach him. Edoran pockets it and leaves. Players get nothing from the note but may get Edoran's words if they move immediately (DC 12 Athletics or Acrobatics to close the distance before he steps back into the dark). The Enforcer, if asked directly who sent him: silence, and then he walks away. This outcome is valid; the players demonstrated skill. The cost is that the intelligence route was the violence route, which is an interesting inversion of the campaign's usual logic.

**Anchor 3: Players are too lethal — killing a zealot or seriously harming civilians**

Stop the momentum briefly. Describe the Enforcer's face: *"He goes still in a way that is different from before. This was not what he was sent here for."* He calls the remaining zealots off immediately — the note burns, Edoran withdraws — and the Enforcer raises both hands and backs away without a word. The encounter ends faster than expected. Consequence: the next day, a City Guard officer (one of Sera's people) finds the players. Someone in the Lowmark queue made a report. Sera doesn't arrest them. She wants to understand what happened. This is not a punishment scene — it is a relationship scene with Sera, moved earlier than planned. Use it.

## 4. Pre-Scene Resource State

Assumed: full HP, full spell slots (fresh from Chancellor meeting).

| Resource Level | What to Reduce | By How Much |
|---|---|---|
| Party at 75% HP | Reduce Enforcer HP from 52 to 40 | Keeps encounter length consistent |
| Party missing one spell slot tier | Remove one zealot | 3 zealots + Enforcer scales to a depleted party |
| Party at 50% HP or worse | Remove one zealot; Enforcer yields at 75% HP instead of 50% | Scene still delivers its beats; don't turn prep scarcity into a TPK |
| Party has used significant resources | Consider inserting a short rest as a scene beat at the Chancellor's — players are briefed, then told to wait while Ostenveld sends a message; they have twenty minutes in a private parlour |

## 5. The "What They Remember" Test

**The sentence players are still quoting:**
*"He just crouched down and held the note to the flame. Like he had all the time in the world."*

**Is it designed in?** Yes. It lives in the round 2 GM narration for the zealot's secondary objective attempt. The specific quality that makes it quotable is the *calm* — the zealot does not panic or rush. He does it with the unhurried deliberateness of someone who expected to reach this moment, which implies planning, which implies the whole thing was anticipated. That calm is scarier than aggression.

**If it's not landing:** The line fails if the GM delivers it in the middle of tracking initiative and hit points. Reserve the narration for a beat where you have the table's attention — call it cleanly, make eye contact, pause after *"flame."* The visual only works if everyone is picturing the same thing at the same moment. If the table is scattered, say: *"Hold on — before the zealot's turn, this is what you see."* Pause. Then deliver it. The interruption to initiative is the point; it mirrors what the zealot is doing to the fight.

**What would make it stronger:** Have the GM physically set something down on the table at that moment — a pencil, a token, anything — and leave it there. A small physical action that mirrors the zealot's small physical action. Theatre of the mind anchored to something real. Players will remember the object as much as the line.
