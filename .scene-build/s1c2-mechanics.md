## 2. Corven's Private Notation System

### What It Looks Like

The carved inscription on the door is approximately 30 cm tall and 80 cm wide, centered on the door face at eye level. The notation is carved in shallow relief, the lines precise and deliberate — not decorative, not architectural. It reads left to right in four lines, each line representing one clause of the riddle.

**Physical characteristics:**
- Base symbols are geometric: triangles, partial circles, nested squares, directional marks (arrow-like strokes)
- No alphabetic characters from any common script
- Spacing and grouping indicate sentence structure — even without knowing the symbols, the four-clause structure is visually apparent
- A small circular mark at the top-right corner of the inscription is the "answer prompt" — the place where the spoken word activates the lock. It is slightly worn from handling; players with passive Perception 12+ notice the wear

### What Players Can Decode Without a Roll

Any player who examines the inscription:
- Can count four distinct "clauses" (the sentence groupings are visually clear)
- Can see that the final clause is shorter and more condensed than the others — it is asking for a single word, not a phrase
- Can identify that the answer slot is the worn circular mark (no roll; the physical evidence is visible)
- Can determine the inscription is intentional, permanent, and the work of someone with a developed system — not casual or decorative

### What Requires History DC 14 or Arcana DC 12

With a successful check:
- Players receive the full riddle text in plain language (as written in session1.md: "I am given only when no one compels it. Forced, I become something else entirely. I am the difference between a gift and a theft. What word names the thing that makes the difference?")
- Players learn this is Corven's private cipher — not a Spire-standard system, not published anywhere
- Players learn the system uses a base-symbol vocabulary that Corven described in his academic papers but never translated publicly

### Fragment 1 of the Notation Key

Inside the restricted stacks (if opened), shelf 4-17-3 contains Fragment 1 of Corven's Notation Key — a single sheet in his handwriting that translates 12 of the 40+ symbols in his private system. This fragment is required to begin decoding Corven's personal journal in Session 2. Players who have the Fragment can reduce all future Arcana/History checks related to Corven's notation by 2.

The Fragment does not translate enough symbols to read the door inscription retroactively — but it confirms, visually, that the door inscription and Corven's personal documents use identical notation. The two things were made by the same hand, with the same intent.

## 3. Magic Interactions

### Knock

**Effect:** Works. The door opens.

**Consequence:** The resonance tone sounds at full volume, continuously, for 1 minute. It is audible from the reading level two floors below. Junior clerks look up. Anyone on the stairwell hears it clearly.

**Theron's arrival time:** 2 minutes from the moment Knock is cast (he was in his office, one floor down). He arrives breathing harder than he should be. He sees the open door. He does not close it. He says, after a three-second pause: *"You found a way around the question. I suppose that was always possible."* His tone is not accusatory. He sounds like a man who has just had something confirmed that he suspected. He will stand in the doorway until players explain their purpose.

**What Knock cannot do:** It does not reveal the answer. Players who use Knock skip the riddle — which means they skip the first time they encounter the word CONSENT in this campaign. The story note carries forward: Theron will reference "the question on the door" in later conversations, and players who bypassed it will not have the resonance the answer carries. This is not a failure — it is a different road.

---

### Detect Magic

**What it shows:** Two aura types on the door.

- **Abjuration (strong):** The ward layer. This is what prevents forced entry, resists Dispel Magic, and creates the resonance tone on wrong answers. The abjuration is Corven's original work — 50 years old and still operating at full strength. Arcana DC 14 identifies this as unusual: most abjuration wards of this age would have degraded. This one has not. Something is sustaining it.

- **Conjuration (faint, centered in the lock mechanism):** This is the sympathetic component. The lock on this side is paired to a receiver on the other side of the door. When the correct word is spoken into the answer-mark, the conjuration component activates simultaneously on both sides — the lock opens and the receiver confirms the activation. This is a ritual lock, not a spell lock.

**What Arcana DC 14 adds (analysing the Detect Magic result):** The conjuration signature is sympathetic: two halves of a single ritual, separated by the door. The receiver on the other side is not in the restricted stacks — it is somewhere deeper in the Archive, possibly in the private reading space at the back of the restricted section that the junior clerk mentioned. Something on the other side knows when this door is opened. Theron Waide may or may not know about the receiver.

---

### Dispel Magic

**Effect:** Absorbed without result. The ward does not flicker. The notation does not change. The door does not react.

**What players learn:** Whatever this ward is, it is not a standard spell. Dispel Magic is the correct tool for most abjuration wards. Its failure here tells players something important: this was not cast the way spells are cast. It was built into the door as a ritual structure. A ritual cannot be dispelled with a spell slot.

**Theron's response (if he is present when this is attempted):** He says: *"Corven did not use spell slots. He built things."* He says nothing more. He turns a page in the book he is holding, though he does not appear to be reading it.

---

### Comprehend Languages

**Effect:** Partial success. The carved symbols translate into the riddle's text in plain common language. Players have the riddle in full.

**What it cannot do:** The cipher layer of Corven's notation is conceptual, not strictly linguistic. Comprehend Languages translates signs to language — it gives players the words of the riddle. It does not solve the riddle. The translation reads exactly as written: "I am given only when no one compels it. Forced, I become something else entirely. I am the difference between a gift and a theft. What word names the thing that makes the difference?"

**Value:** Getting the riddle in plain text removes the Arcana/History barrier. Players who use Comprehend Languages can immediately proceed to the Research checks to find the answer, without needing to decode the notation first.

## 4. Wrong Answers — Escalating Responses

### First Wrong Answer

A low resonance tone sounds through the door — not painful, not loud, but clear. It lasts approximately 4 seconds and fades. The notation on the door does not change.

**Audibility:** The tone carries to the floor below (reading level). Junior clerks look up toward the stairwell. One closes a book and listens. No one comes yet.

**Theron's response (if present):** He goes still. He already knew the answer was wrong before the tone sounded — something in how it was delivered, or the word itself. He says: *"That is not it."* Not unkindly. Flatly. He waits.

**Theron's response (if absent):** The tone draws his attention. He will appear at the landing within 3–4 minutes, moving without urgency. He arrives before a second wrong answer if players are deliberating.

---

### Second Wrong Answer

The resonance tone sounds again — the same pitch, slightly longer (6–7 seconds). A thin line of amber light traces the door's outline, visible for 2 seconds, then fades. The notation is unchanged.

**Audibility:** Clearly audible one floor down. Two clerks exchange a look. A third closes their section of the archive and moves toward the stairs — not rushing, but moving.

**Theron's response (if not yet present):** He arrives at the landing during or immediately after the second tone. He looks at the door, then at the players. He says: *"You have one more attempt before it closes for an hour. Tell me what you have tried."* His tone is still controlled but something in him has shifted — he is not angry, he is deciding something about these specific people.

**Theron's response (if already present):** *"You are not guessing. You are working. That is different. Tell me what you think the question is about."* He will not give the answer. He will confirm or deny the analytical direction they are taking.

---

### Third Wrong Answer — Door Lock

The tone sounds a third time, sharpest yet. A thin crack of light runs along the full door frame from top to bottom, holds for 3 seconds, then fades. The notation dims perceptibly — the carved lines seem to pull back slightly into the stone, as if the door has withdrawn attention.

**Effect:** The door will not respond to any word or action for **one hour** (real time or GM discretion). The ward is still active. The notation is still there. The door has simply gone quiet.

**Audibility:** The tone and the light crack together make the most significant sound and visual event yet. All clerks on the floor below are aware. Theron — if not already present — is on his way.

**Theron's arrival (if third attempt made without him present):** He comes up the stairs at pace. He does not run. He arrives at the landing and looks at the door. He closes his eyes briefly. Then, when he opens them, he looks at the players and says:

> *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."*

This moves the scene forward. Theron becomes the guide. See Section 5 for his disclosure ladder.

## 5. The One-Hour Lock

### What Changes During the Wait

The door is quiet. The notation remains visible but does not glow or respond to touch. A Detect Magic check during this period shows the abjuration is still active — it has not weakened. It is waiting.

**Ambient detail:** A faint low hum is audible in the corridor, below conversation level. Players with passive Perception 14+ can hear it without being told. It is the locked ward resonating in its resting state. It has been doing this for eleven years.

### What Players Can Do

The one-hour lock is not wasted time. The following are productive uses:

| Activity | Skill | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Research the riddle in non-restricted stacks | Arcana / History / Religion / Investigation | 12–14 (see Section 1) | Full riddle solution (see above) |
| Talk to Theron — extended conversation (if he is present) | Persuasion DC 13, or Insight DC 12 to know what angle to take | 13 | Theron's first disclosure tier (see Section 5) |
| Read Corven's published works in the reference room | Investigation DC 11 | 11 | The footnote about "consenting anchor problem" |
| Search Theron's desk (he is away fetching reference materials) | Investigation DC 15 | 15 | Draft letter, margin system (see Section 1) |
| Ask the junior clerk additional questions | Persuasion DC 10 | 10 | Clerk's personal observations about Theron's visits to the door |
| Examine the door notation more carefully | Arcana DC 12 | 12 | Read the riddle in full (Comprehend Languages alternative, no spell slot) |
| Rest and recover | Short rest mechanics | — | Each character may spend hit dice as normal |

### Whether Theron Stays or Leaves

If the door locks on a third failure **and** Theron is already present: he stays. He does not leave players alone at a sealed door they have triggered three times. His goal is to assess whether these are the people he has been waiting for — and a third failed attempt without his guidance is information about how they approach problems.

If the door locks **before** Theron arrives: he arrives within 2–3 minutes of the third tone. He stays once he has confirmed the situation. He will offer to show players the non-restricted reference room to research while they wait, and he will accompany them. He does not want them searching the Archive unsupervised.

If players **insist on waiting alone at the door**: Theron goes back to his desk but leaves the connecting door to his office open. He reappears every 15 minutes with a cup of something hot and the manner of a man checking on something he is trying not to appear to be checking on.

## 6. Theron as Guide — Disclosure Ladder

Theron Waide knows the answer to the riddle. He has known it for eleven years. He has not told anyone because telling someone meant the door would open, the documents would be read, and the decision he has been deferring would arrive.

He is not going to volunteer the answer at first contact. But he can be drawn out. The following describes what he gives at each stage.

---

### What He Volunteers Unprompted

**On first meeting players (before any wrong answers):**
He describes the door as "Corven's notation — a private cipher he used for certain documents. The Archive has had several scholars attempt to decode it over the years. None have published a key." He gestures at it. He does not dwell.

He mentions, almost as an aside, that the door responds to a spoken word rather than a physical key. He says this as if it is simply a factual observation about the door's mechanism.

**After the first wrong answer (if he is present):**
He says: *"The riddle is philosophical in nature. The answer is a single word. I would focus on the question of what the four lines have in common, rather than on the symbolism of any individual line."*

This is the most direct guidance he offers unprompted. It confirms the answer is a single word and directs players toward analytical reading rather than knowledge checks.

**After the third wrong answer (door lock):**
He says: *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."* This opens his first active disclosure tier.

---

### What He Gives at Each Point (Skill Checks Required)

**First mention of the door — Insight DC 13:**
He is not confused by the door. He is withholding. The hesitation and qualifications are a performance of uncertainty from a man who knows exactly what he knows.

**Persuasion DC 13 — "Tell us what you know about the door":**
> *"Corven's notation system was his private vocabulary. He developed it over thirty years of research. I have — I have spent time with it. The answer to the riddle is not a technical term. It is a moral concept. I believe he chose to make the door answer a philosophical question because he wanted to ensure that whoever opened it had thought carefully about what that concept meant."*

He does not say the word. He has confirmed the answer is a moral concept.

**Persuasion DC 15 (after first disclosure) — "What is the concept?":**
> *"I have struggled with this, you understand. There is something about naming it — specifically, to someone standing in front of this door — that feels like a crossing I have been waiting eleven years to make. What I can tell you is that the four lines of the riddle all describe the same distinction. The distinction between something given and something taken. When you name that distinction correctly — when you use the word Corven would have used — the door will open."*

He is this close to saying the word. A player who says "CONSENT" at this moment, before he finishes — or immediately after — opens the door.

**Insight DC 15 (the deeper read) — "Why hasn't he told anyone for eleven years?":**
> He is looking for permission. He decoded the ritual's true cost eleven years ago. He told no one. He has been slowly destroying his own notes. He cannot destroy the original text. He cannot bring himself to open the door — not alone, not without someone who will be there on the other side of what is inside. These players, right now, are the first people who have stood at this door with any real chance of being the right people to open it. He is deciding, in real time, whether they are.

---

### What He NEVER Says Unprompted

These are things Theron will not volunteer under any circumstances. They require specific player approaches to unlock (see npcs.md for Skill Gates):

- The word CONSENT (not until Persuasion DC 15 has been cleared, and even then only through implication — he does not say it; he describes the distinction until a player says it first)
- That he decoded the ritual's cost eleven years ago
- That the "cost" means the Dawnborn die
- That he has been destroying his own notes
- That he has a reading space in the restricted stacks
- That there is a receiver on the other side of the door linked to the sympathetic seal
- Anything about Corven's sealed letter on shelf 4-17-3

**What happens if players press on these topics:**
He deflects first. He qualifies second. On a third direct press — or a successful Persuasion DC 15 — he does not break. He says: *"I am not the right person to answer that. Not yet. Find what is inside first."* The guilt is visible (Insight DC 13 reads it clearly) but he holds it.

## 7. Cross-Scene Mechanic Notes

### The Note's Connection (Scene 1 Payoff)

If players recovered the sealed note from Scene 1, the phrase "the willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot" (Auris, *Litany of the Open Hand*) directly maps to the riddle's third line: "I am the difference between a gift and a theft."

Players who make this connection in play — either by checking the note and reading the riddle, or by speaking the connection aloud — receive:
- Religion DC drops to DC 10 for the research check (already primed)
- GM option: allow a free attempt with advantage on the spoken answer
- Narrative reward: the campaign's first instance of a planted clue paying off at the scene designed to receive it

### The Dawnborn-Adjacent (Sera Voss) Angle

A player with the Dawnborn-Adjacent archetype who has spoken to Sera Voss (Scene 4 or any prior encounter) may make a DC 12 Insight check when reading the riddle:
- **On success:** The connection lands without prompting. Sera runs warmer than she should. She is, in some sense, something that was given rather than taken. The word CONSENT arrives through the emotional register of her presence, not through research.
- Use this if the player has clearly drawn the connection and is roleplay-describing it. This is a characterisation reward, not a mechanical shortcut.

### The Scholar's Edge

A Scholar archetype player (or any player with Arcana or History proficiency and the Sage background) who has interacted with Theron's published correspondence (from npcs.md: Theron mentions Corven in any extended conversation) may make a History DC 13 when examining the notation:
- **On success:** Recalls that Theron, in his only published paper about Corven (20 years ago), wrote: "Corven's work was defined by a question he called the anchor problem — the question of whether energy transfer from a willing source differs in kind from energy drawn by force. He believed the difference was fundamental. He built that belief into his notation system."
- This gives players the riddle's intellectual context without the answer — but "differs in kind" maps directly to the riddle's second line.

## 8. Rulings Cheat Sheet

**"We try every single-word moral concept we can think of."**
The door responds to wrong answers with the escalating tone sequence. Players have three attempts before the one-hour lock. If they are systematically guessing: SACRIFICE (wrong), TRUST (wrong), FREEDOM (wrong), CHOICE (wrong), GIFT (wrong) — allow each as a tonal response, but the third wrong answer locks the door regardless of which three they tried. Do not extend the attempts. The lock is the scene's pressure mechanic.

**"We try to force the door physically (crowbar, battering ram, Strength check)."**
The door does not respond to physical force. It is stone, set in stone, with an active abjuration ward. Treat it as an immovable object for mechanical purposes. DC 25 Athletics to move it physically — and even then, the ward resists; the door does not budge. The lock cannot be broken, only answered.

**"We try to climb around the door / find another entrance to the restricted stacks."**
The restricted stacks are a sealed section of the Archive — the door is the only entrance on this floor. A DC 15 Investigation check reveals that the exterior wall of the restricted section is flush with the Archive's outer wall; there is no second entrance from the outside. If players pursue this seriously, Theron confirms it: "There is only the door. Corven designed it that way. He wanted whoever opened it to have answered the question."

**"We ask Theron to open it himself."**
He cannot. He has tried. He knows the answer — but he cannot bring himself to say it here, in this context, alone. The door requires the word to be spoken by someone actively seeking what is inside. Eleven years of standing at this door and not opening it has produced in Theron a specific inability. He can describe the distinction until players say the word. He cannot say it first.

**"We use Suggestion / Charm Person to make Theron tell us the answer."**
Wisdom saving throw (Theron: +1 Wisdom modifier, Gestalt L3 spell save DC typically 13–14). On failure, he is charmed. He will tell them: *"The answer is consent."* The door opens. He is not angry — he cannot be, while charmed. When the charm fades, he is quiet for a long time. Then: *"You didn't need to do that. I would have told you eventually."* He is not certain this is true. Whether this damages the relationship is GM discretion — but it affects his first disclosure in Session 2, which will start at a colder baseline.

**"What if a player just says 'CONSENT' during casual conversation near the door without meaning to answer the riddle?"**
