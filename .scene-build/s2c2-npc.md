## Theron Waide — Master Archivist

### Physical Description

Fifties. Tall, angular, slightly stooped as if decades of concentration have bent him forward over desks and lecterns. The stoop is permanent now — it does not correct when he stands still. Grey hair, cut short on the sides and longer on top in the manner of someone who once cared about it and now cuts it himself. Wire spectacles, round lenses, slightly smudged on the left side. He never cleans the left side — he reads from the right.

Ink stains on the first two fingers of his right hand. Not fresh — these have been absorbed into the skin over years, a permanent secondary coloration. New scholars' ink stains wash off. Theron's do not.

When nervous, he straightens papers that do not need straightening. In this scene, he has only his pen case to straighten. He will straighten it.

His voice is precise in the way of someone who has spent decades choosing words carefully, who knows that the wrong word in the wrong record can create a decade of misunderstanding. In this scene, his voice wavers slightly. He does not try to hide this. Hiding it would be a performance. He is done performing.

He has never spoken of this to anyone. He did not acknowledge to himself, at the time, why he was doing it. He knows now. He was making sure it could be found. |

### Want / Fear / Lie

**Want:** To be believed. Not thanked, not praised — believed. To watch someone read "THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM" aloud and have them understand what they've just said. Eleven years of that sentence living only in his chest. He wants it to exist outside him.

**Fear:** That once the players know, they go immediately to the Chancellor — and the Chancellor acts without consulting the Dawnborn. That the ritual gets completed as an administrative decision. That ten people die in an orderly, documented, officially-sanctioned process.

**Lie:** "I'm giving you this because you've earned it." He is giving it because the Reckoning just broke into his Archive and he now understands that waiting until the right moment was a strategy that no longer exists. He is giving it because eleven more years is not a possibility he can hold. The players did not earn this. They arrived at the right time and did not run away.

### Dialogue — Complete Sequence

**Opening** (sets the slips on the table, sits down, looks at them for a moment, then at the players):
> "I encoded them myself. The cipher is simple enough that someone who wanted to find the answer could find it. That was intentional."

*He does not explain further. He waits for them to begin.*

---

**If asked why he didn't simply tell someone:**
> "I told the previous Chancellor. Indirectly. I described the mechanism without naming the people involved. He thanked me for my work and had my filing access reviewed for six months. I learned to be indirect."

---

**If asked why he encoded it instead of destroying it:**
> "Because destroying it felt like choosing. Encoding it felt like waiting for someone braver than me."

*Beat. He adjusts his pen case.*
> "I'm aware of the distinction."

---

**If asked how long he has known:**
> "Eleven years. Since the winter after the first Dawnborn child turned three. That was when I found the midwife records and understood what I was looking at."

---

**If players are working on the puzzle and frustrated:**
> "Keep trying. The pattern is simpler than it looks."

*He means this. He is genuinely rooting for them. His posture during the puzzle is attentive, not impatient — the posture of a teacher watching a student who is close.*

---

**If players try to charm or pressure him into just telling them:**
> "I could tell you. I've been going to tell someone for eleven years. But I want you to read it. It matters that you read it."

*He will not be moved on this — not yet. The third-attempt recovery is the exception, not the negotiation.*

---

**Wrong approach response (counting sentences):**
> "Different unit." (That is all.)

**Wrong approach response (reading initials):**
> "Not letters. Something else."

**Wrong approach response (acrostic — first word of each line):**
> "Broader than that. The three slips aren't separate."

---

**When the answer is decoded and read aloud:**
> *He looks at them when they read it. He does not look away.*
> "Yes."
> *(pause — let it sit)*
> "I've been saying that word in here for eleven years."
> *(touches his chest, not his head)*

---

**Third-attempt recovery (if players fail three times and he reaches for the slips):**

If players hold onto the slips:
> *(He stops reaching. His hands settle flat on the table.)*
> "All right," he says. "All right."
> *(Long pause. He takes his spectacles off, sets them on the table beside the pen case.)*
> "The Dawnborn are the vessel. The ritual energy lives within them. Completing the Founding Ritual — as it was designed, as Corven designed it — requires that energy to be drawn out."
> *(He puts his spectacles back on.)*
> "That is what the slips say. That is what I have known since the year the eldest Dawnborn child turned three."

*Move directly to Scene 4 RP.*
