## Per-Archetype Resonance — Scene 5

---

### Soldier / Martial PC

**Discipline is the tell.**

He didn't attack you when you left the Dawnhall, even though he had position and numbers. You came out of a warm building onto a cold lane with your attention on the door closing behind you, and four figures in the alley shadow, and a man fifty feet south who has been waiting long enough for his breathing to settle. That is a favorable situation for an ambush. He did not ambush you.

The zealots this morning were told to delay, not harm. You probably noticed that even in the moment — their formation was wrong for people trying to hurt you, and their exit was faster than it needed to be if they'd wanted to press. They were a clock, not a threat. Someone told them to be a clock.

Someone spent twenty years building a movement that controls its own use of force. That is harder than it looks. Gangs don't achieve this. Armies achieve it imperfectly, with command structure and supply chains and JAG officers and courts martial. A theological movement of forty lay adherents in the lower districts of a city that has been in twilight for fifty years, operating on doctrine rather than fear — and they held the line at "delay, not harm."

What kind of threat is disciplined enough to choose its battles? That is a threat that knows what it's doing. The man in front of you has been doing it for twenty years and has not yet done the thing he could do. That is information. Weigh it.

**What to ask this player:** *"He had you cold this morning and chose not to take it. He's got position and numbers tonight and he's stepped aside. What does that profile look like to you — is this a man who is restrained, or a man who has found a more effective kind of force?"*

### Scholar / Investigator PC

**He knows what you haven't found yet.**

When he says *"you have no idea what you are looking for"* — he is not dismissing you. He is not performing superiority. He is making a specific, bounded, accurate claim about your current information state. You found documents. You found Corven's summary and a fragment of the Notation Key. You found a woman who has consented to die so the sun returns.

He has been in the Archive for twenty years. He has read what you have read and more. When he says the Archive has more than you've found, he is telling you where to look without saying what is there. That is a deliberate choice. He is not withholding it as leverage — Insight at DC 15 will tell you he believes it is not his to give. That belief is functional, not strategic.

Whatever is in the Archive that you haven't found yet: it is the thing Edoran found twelve or more years ago and has been sitting with since. He built his movement before he found it. The movement did not change after he found it. That is the detail that should concern you. If the information changed the picture, the movement would have changed. It didn't. What does that mean?

Your job after tonight is not Edoran. Your job is Fragment 2 of the Notation Key, and whatever it says, and why a man who has been conducting a twenty-year theological campaign for the Dawnborn's deaths did not change course when he found information that might have changed the question.

**What to ask this player:** *"He told you there is more in the Archive and declined to say what. He has had it for twelve or more years. What does someone do with a piece of information that might resolve the central question — and why would they keep it to themselves?"*

### Healer / Cleric PC

**His sacrifice theology is not careless.**

Your tradition has language for meaningful death. The willing offering, the gift freely given, the light returning to its source. You know the shapes theology builds around sacrifice, and you know how those shapes are used: sometimes as comfort, sometimes as pressure, sometimes as both simultaneously by the same speaker.

Edoran is not using the shape carelessly. He has done the grief work. Not metaphorically — he has spent fourteen years with a specific, personal grief organized into a theological position, and the organization is rigorous. He knows the distinction between genuine consent and coerced consent better than most clergy you have met, because he knows what the engineering requires: Corven built the ritual to fail under coercion. Edoran's theology tracks the engineering because he has read the engineering. The faith and the mechanics are, in his case, aligned.

His answer to the sacrifice question is different from yours, probably. He has arrived at: the Dawnborn are called; consent is the form the calling takes; delay costs more lives than completion. You may arrive at: the Dawnborn deserve more time; alternatives should be exhausted; no calling can compel a choice. These are not the same answer.

But he got there the same way you would. The question asked. The materials examined. The position reached. The position held.

The question for your character is not whether he is wrong. The question is whether a position reached by your process, held for twenty years, built on grief — whether that position can be wrong. And if it can be, what would you need to see to know that yours can be too?

**What to ask this player:** *"He has your methodology and a different conclusion. He has your grief, organized differently. He has the same engineering documents and a different reading of what they require. Where is the argument, for your character, that his position has gone wrong?"*

### Thief / Rogue PC

**He waited until after Sera Voss.**

He could have intercepted you at the Archive door this morning — he was already positioned in the east alley when the zealots hit you at Lowmark Junction. He could have been at the Dawnhall entrance, or on the road between the Archive and Lowmark. He could have been at any point in your day. He chose this specific point: after you'd spent time with a Dawnborn who has consented. He wanted you to have that context before he said his piece.

That is two things simultaneously: patience and strategy. Patience, because he has been building toward this conversation for twenty years and he can wait one more afternoon. Strategy, because he knows that his argument about consent lands differently if you've met someone who has consented. He needed you to have that reference point.

He gave it to you. He waited for you to acquire it. Then he stepped in front of your path — not blocking it, but present — and waited for you to come to him.

You read rooms for a living. Read this one. This is not a man who is afraid. This is not a man who is posturing. This is a man who has been running a long operation with specific information advantages, and tonight he chose to spend one of them — the most direct conversation he has had with anyone outside the Restorers in years — on you. Why? What does he know about what you're about to find that made this investment worth making?

**What to ask this player:** *"He built toward this conversation. It was not opportunistic — he positioned for it all day. Why are you specifically worth that investment? What do you have, or what are you about to find, that he needed to influence before you found it?"*

### Noble / Diplomat PC

**He built something without money, title, or institutional support.**

Forty people. Twenty years. No patron, no charter, no city backing, no Auris institutional endorsement — he left the priesthood; they are not going to endorse a splinter movement run by a man who walked out on them. The Restorers exist because Edoran convinced forty people that his theological position was correct and worth organizing around. He did this through argument, presence, and twenty years of consistent purpose.

He speaks in complete sentences. He did not threaten you once. He made a specific argument with specific precision and he stood in a cold lane for two hours to make it. He did not apologize for the morning's intercept but he acknowledged it was a means, not a doctrine, which is the distinction that matters.

He is going to be in your life for the rest of this campaign. He is going to be right about several things. He is going to be wrong about at least one thing in a way that costs people, and the wrongness will be deeply connected to the rightness, and you will not be able to separate them neatly. That is an adversary worth understanding.

The question is not whether his movement is a threat. The question is what kind of force forty disciplined, theologically motivated people with twenty years of organizational development represent — and what they will do if Harran Lecht's approach produces the outcome Edoran believes it will produce.

**What to ask this player:** *"He's been running this movement for twenty years without institutional support and he hasn't made a significant mistake yet. What does he want from this conversation — what is the move he was trying to make tonight — and did he make it?"*

### Restorer-Adjacent / Religious PC

**You could be looking at yourself.**

Not now — now you are still working out what you believe, or you believe something adjacent to what he believes, or you believe something that will be tested in the course of this campaign. But he is what you could become if you organized your faith into a position and held it for twenty years without external validation, without institutional support, without anything except the theology and the grief.

He is also what you can see clearly from outside: the theology is a container for something that had no other place to go. You can see the construction. You can see, in the precision he brings to every word about consent, the effort of a man who is making sure the container holds. The container is real. The grief is also real. Both things are true simultaneously. He would agree with you — not that it changes anything, but he would agree.

The question your tradition asks about meaningful death, he has asked. His answer is different from whatever yours is, probably. But the path to his answer is the same path yours is on. That is the uncomfortable thing. You did not reach his answer yet. Maybe you won't. But you went through the same door, and you are somewhere in the building, and he is in a room in that building where you can see how the furniture is arranged, and you are not there yet.

What does it mean to you that the rigorous answer, held for twenty years, held with precision and grief and genuine examination, arrives here? Not that he is right. But that the method that would be yours produced this.

**What to ask this player:** *"He is what twenty years of organized faith looks like when the faith was built on loss. Your character's faith is younger. Is this a direction or a warning — and can you tell the difference from where you are standing?"*
