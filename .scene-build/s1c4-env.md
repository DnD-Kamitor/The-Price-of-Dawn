## The Dawnhall — Crestwick Lane, Lowmark

The Dawnhall is not what the name suggests. There is no hall, exactly — no formal chamber, no dais, no ceremony. There is a long room with long tables and forty people in it, and a smell that gets into clothing, and a woman with her back to the door who knows you're there before you've said a word.

---

## Arrival Read-Aloud

*Read this as players cross the threshold:*

> The door opens inward and the warmth hits before your eyes have adjusted. Not the warmth of a fire exactly — the warmth of forty people in an enclosed space, of broth in a cauldron, of woodsmoke from a kitchen you can hear but not see through a half-open door in the north wall.
>
> Long tables run south to north. Mismatched chairs — benches, stools, two that look like they came from different buildings entirely. Forty people, perhaps more: some eating in silence, some in quiet conversation, a child leaning against her mother's arm with her eyes half-closed. Most of them look grey. Not uniformly — there is a spectrum to it, the first-week pallor and the third-year hollow both visible in the same room. A man coughs once, softly, from the far corner. No one reacts. The cough is not an event here.
>
> On a hook by the door, level with your eye: a yellow scarf. It is the only yellow thing in the room.
>
> At the northeast corner, her back to you, a woman in an apron is serving soup from a cauldron that looks older than the building. She knows you're there. You can tell by the particular stillness of her shoulders — not stiffness, not tension, but the quality of a person who has registered something and is finishing the task in front of them before turning around.
>
> She fills one more bowl. Hands it to a teenager standing beside her — the teenager takes it without being asked, turning toward the tables with the ease of someone who has done this before. Then she removes her cooking gloves, tucks them in her apron pocket, and turns.
>
> She is not surprised to see you.

## Five Senses

**Sight**

The common room is fifty feet deep, thirty wide. Three rows of tables, south-to-north. Mismatched chairs and benches fill the gaps. The walls are bare stone — Lowmark construction, old granite, the kind of wall that sweats in winter. No tapestries, no decorations. There are hooks along the south wall, mostly coats and scarves, and on one of those hooks: the yellow scarf, alone in its color among the grey-and-brown of the rest.

Sera is medium height, late 40s, dark hair going grey and pinned efficiently for kitchen work. The apron is functional, well-worn. She moves with the economy of someone who never makes an unnecessary motion. A small pendant is visible at her collarbone — warm amber stone, not lit from within, simply present.

The kitchen door in the north wall is half-open. Light and steam from it. A second person in there, working.

**Sound**

The room is not quiet — it is *low*. Spoon against clay bowl. The scrape of a bench being shifted. Someone in the far corner coughing — controlled, the practiced cough of someone who has learned not to alarm people around them. A child's question to her mother, too quiet to make out the words. The mother's answer, also quiet. The ongoing muffled activity from the kitchen.

Above all of this, nothing alarming. This is the specific sound of a room where the worst thing has already happened and people have learned to live inside it.

**Smell**

Onions, cooked long. Broth, something with roots in it — parsnip, possibly turnip. Woodsmoke, the particular residue of a fire that has been burning all day and is banked low now. Beneath that, the particular smell of grey sickness: not rotting, not rank — clean, carefully maintained, but present. The smell of people whose bodies are slowly losing something. Someone has been using vinegar on the tables. The vinegar is almost gone.

**Touch**

The air is warm from the kitchen and from bodies. The table nearest the door, when a player rests a hand on it: worn smooth from years of use. Not polished — worn. The grain of the wood has been sanded back to a smoothness by palms and bowls and elbows over a long time.

The benches have no backs. This is not a room designed for comfort. It is designed to seat as many people as possible.

**Taste**

The air is thick enough to taste. Broth, mostly. Warmth. That particular density of a soup kitchen in a cold city at the end of an evening service — the food has been cooking all day and the air has absorbed it. A player who has eaten nothing since morning will notice a specific pang standing in this room. The soup smells sufficient. It is made to be sufficient.

## What the Room Tells Players (Before Sera Speaks)

- This building is functioning. Someone maintains it — the tables are clean, the sick are fed, the system works.
- Forty people depend on it tonight. More will come tomorrow.
- The yellow scarf on the hook does not belong to anyone in the room currently. It belongs to Sera. She put it there when she arrived. She will touch it when she leaves.
- The teenager with the ladle has done this before. This is not a one-person operation.
- Sera knew you were coming before you knocked.

---

## Scene Duration Note

This scene has a clock: the Dawnhall bell rings lights-out, and Sera has work. Let the scene run 15–30 minutes of real time. It is not a slow scene — it breathes. The breathing is the point.
