# S1C5 — Scene Function in Campaign Arc

<summary>📖 Story Function</summary>

## What This Scene Does

Session 1 ends with two voices in the players' heads. Not a revelation. Not a fight. Two voices.

**Sera Voss:** I have known for years what I am. I chose. I keep choosing.

**Brother Edoran:** You have no idea what you are looking for. The mechanism is ten people. The question is consent, not mechanics.

These are not the same voice. They are coming from different starting places and moving toward different purposes. Sera's consent is hers — private, sustained, arrived at through fifteen years of serving soup in a grey city. Edoran's argument is categorical — the Dawnborn are called, the consent is the form the calling takes, and the delay is killing people. Sera would recognize the argument. Edoran respects her answer. They have not met in years and each considers the other's position from a distance with the specific quality of people who agree on the conclusion and disagree on the context that makes the conclusion matter.

Players will carry both voices into Session 2. Those voices will be in the room when Theron Waide tells them what the ritual actually costs. They will measure Theron's revelation against Sera's settled peace and Edoran's organized grief. They will not be hearing it into a vacuum. The scene tonight ensured that.

---

## Edoran as the Necessary Antagonist

He is not the villain. This is important to maintain throughout the campaign.

He is the person who has thought about this the longest — twenty years in the same question — and arrived at the wrong conclusion by the right process. Or: the right conclusion with the wrong frame for what it costs the people it is about. Or: a conclusion that is partly right about the engineering and partly wrong about what "consent" means when it is organized into a theological position by someone who has personal reasons to want the position to hold.

Players will learn to hate agreeing with him. They will find, in Sessions 2 through 4, that when he is right about something — when his prediction about Harran Lecht's approach proves accurate, when his reading of the Archive documents proves to have been correct about the engineering requirements — agreeing with him feels like conceding something about the Dawnborn that they do not want to concede. They will be right to feel this tension. The tension is the point.

He is the campaign's mirror for a specific question: can you be right about the outcome and wrong about the people? Can the engineering requirements and the human costs be simultaneously true in a way that no single position fully accounts for? He answers yes — and he keeps building his movement around the yes. Players will spend five sessions working out whether there is a third position that is not his and not the Chancellor's and not Sera's quiet peace, and that third position is what the Inversion represents.

---

## The Structural Function of "You Have No Idea What You Are Looking For"

At this moment — the end of Session 1, leaving the Dawnhall, fifty feet of cold lane — he is right. Players do not know what they are looking for. They have documents and phrases and a Dawnborn who has consented and a fragment of an encoding system. They do not yet know that the living conduit dies. They do not know about the Inversion. They do not know what Theron has been carrying for eleven years. They have the words and not the meaning.

He is right now. He is wrong — this specific assertion, *"you have no idea"* — by the end of Session 2. Players will have an idea by then. They will know what the ritual costs and who bears the cost and what Corven built into the design that the subsequent fifty years of political management have failed to use.

The phrase is a timestamp. It marks the information state of Session 1's end precisely. Players who return to it in Session 2, when they know more, will feel the difference. That is the line's job: not to be true forever, but to be exactly true at the moment it is said, in a way that becomes exactly untrue as the campaign proceeds.

## The Daughter Beat as Emotional Depth

Players who find the crack — who ask the question that leads to *"She didn't"* — leave Session 1 with something they cannot immediately use.

They have an observation about Brother Edoran. A man who spent twenty years on a theological position about meaningful sacrifice and willing hearts and the consent that is the form the calling takes — and whose daughter died of grey sickness before the ritual could fire, without a consent question, without a theological frame that made the death meaningful. She just died. Of the sickness. At seventeen.

Players will not know what to do with this. That is correct. They are not supposed to know what to do with it tonight.

What they carry: the asymmetry between Mira's death and what Edoran is asking of the Dawnborn. The Dawnborn are being invited to choose meaningfully — to have the death that has a form and a purpose and is, in Edoran's theology, the highest expression of what they carry. Mira did not get that. She got grey sickness and three years and a winter. She never saw the sun.

That asymmetry does not make Edoran wrong about the engineering. It does not change what the ritual requires. It sits underneath everything he argues and has sat there for fourteen years and he has not found a way to resolve it, only to contain it, and players who found the crack have seen the containment from the outside and know something about him that he has not told anyone.

In Session 4, when the Inversion appears — when players discover that all ten genuine consents produce no deaths, that the sun can return without anyone dying if the consent is real — players who found the crack will know two things simultaneously:

One: there was always a door.

Two: Edoran has had Fragment 2 for twelve years. He knows about the door. He has been sitting with whether the door is real for twelve years, because if it is real, then there was never a reason for any of it. Not Mira's death specifically — the grey sickness would still have taken her regardless of the ritual. But the framing. The theology. The willing sacrifice he has been building toward for twenty years. If the door is real, the sacrifice was never necessary.

He does not know what to do with this. He has had twelve years to find out and he has not yet.

Players who found the crack will understand why. Players who did not find it will have to be told. Either way, the scene lands. But it lands differently.

---

## Tone: The Session Ends Quietly

Not with a fight. Not with a revelation that reframes everything. With a man stepping aside in a cold lane, and the words that have been accumulating all session, and the open south lane.

The session ends with players carrying weight they cannot yet set down. Two documents, encoded. A man who built a movement on grief and is partly right. A woman who serves soup and has chosen to die. The words: *distributed sympathetic anchors, living conduit mechanism, the completion requires their release.* They know what the words mean now. They don't know what the words mean.

The lane is empty. The Dawnhall door is behind them. The city of Varenhold extends south: forty thousand people in permanent twilight, grey sickness spreading slowly, the sun down for fifty years. The Ashring is two districts east, the stones that Sera Voss stood next to and felt recognized by.

They have been hired to investigate a fifty-year ritual failure. They have investigated. What they found is not a failure with a cause they can name. It is a question that has been waiting fifty years for an answer, and the answer has to come from ten people who have been carrying the question inside them their whole lives, and tonight they met one of those people, and she was serving soup.

That is the session. That is the weight. Let them go home with it.

---

## What Players Are Now Ready For

Session 2 begins with everything players found in Session 1 already in the room. They have the frame. They do not have the picture. Session 2 gives them Theron Waide and the picture, and it changes everything they thought they knew about the frame.

Players who enter Session 2 having only information — who stayed transactional through S1C4 and S1C5 — will experience Theron's revelation as new information. They will update their information state.

Players who enter Session 2 having felt something — who sat with Sera's clarity and Edoran's grief and understood that the thing they are investigating is made of people — will experience Theron's revelation as confirmation of something they were already afraid of. They will experience it as a cost landing, not a fact arriving.

The difference in those two experiences is what Session 1 was for.
