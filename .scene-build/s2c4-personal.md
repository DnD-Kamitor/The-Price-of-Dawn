## Per-Archetype Resonance — Scene 4

---

### The Investigator

**What the note-burning in Session 1 now means.**

In Session 1, the Investigator archetype found traces of deliberately destroyed documentation — partial pages, gaps in the filing sequence, the specific kind of absence that only appears when someone with access has been removing things carefully over time. At the time, it read as suppression by a hostile actor.

It was Theron. Burning his own notes. Twenty-three pages, over eleven years, trying to make himself unable to decode the rest of the Notation Key.

The Investigator has been following a trail of evidence that led to the Archive. The Archive is run by the person who was both the evidence's keeper and its partial destroyer. The person the Investigator trusted to help them navigate the documentation is the person who created the most significant gap in it.

**The beat:** When Theron mentions the destroyed notes (if asked, or voluntarily — see s2c4-npc.md), the Investigator archetype player has the specific contextual knowledge to understand what he means immediately. Their character spent Session 1 mapping what was missing from the Archive. They now know who made it missing, and why.

**What to ask this player:** *"You've been reading the gaps in the record for two sessions. What does your character make of someone who created the gap they found?"*

---

### The Cleric

**What EXTINGUISHED does to a theology of sacrifice and mercy.**

The Cleric archetype serves a faith tradition that has its own language for death, sacrifice, and the release of what is held. The word EXTINGUISHED lands differently for a person whose professional vocabulary includes concepts like "the willing offering" and "the soul's departure" and "the light that returns to its source."

There is a version of this revelation that a cleric's theology can absorb: ten people, willingly releasing what they hold, to restore the sun that feeds a dying city. That is legible to a theology of sacrifice. It is tragic and beautiful and has a shape that religious frameworks are built to hold.

The problem is the word. Not *died.* Not *passed.* Not *gave.* *Extinguished.* Like a lantern. Theron chose this word because it is accurate. The mechanism does not care about the willing heart — it functions because the energy releases, and the releasing kills. The beauty of sacrifice requires the consent to have meaning. The word *extinguished* is what happens after the meaning, in the body, when the energy has gone.

**The beat:** The Cleric archetype carries a theological toolkit that is suddenly inadequate. The framework for sacrifice exists. The framework for willing death exists. The framework for what happens to a person's body when fifty years of redirected solar energy releases out of it simultaneously, without ceremony, without ritual space, without the comfort of dying in one's own time — that framework has not been built yet.

**What to ask this player:** *"Your character has language for meaningful death. Does that language reach this far?"*

### The Martial

**The tactical implications — there is no version of this that doesn't cost someone.**

The Martial archetype processes threats operationally. Scene 4 delivers a revelation that has no clean operational frame.

The math: ten people need to release the energy they carry. The city has three to five harvests left without the sun. Grey sickness spreads. The Reckoning has soldiers and a timeline. The Amber Compact has resources and an agenda. Isolde Maren has a possible transfer method that may or may not work, developed in a rented room with limited notes.

No version of this scenario has a zero-cost outcome. The Martial archetype player who has been calculating threat responses since Session 1 has now encountered the enemy they cannot fight: arithmetic.

**The beat:** The Martial archetype is trained to identify the option with the lowest casualties. In this room, the options are: forced completion (ten deaths, Reckoning wins, no consent), voluntary completion (ten willing deaths, consent preserved), transfer method (Dawnborn survive, 15-20% chance of partial failure anyway), inversion (all ten consent genuinely, no deaths, hardest path), or inaction (sun stays dark, grey sickness wins, thousands die slowly).

There is no clean option. The Martial player knows this immediately. The question is whether they say it out loud or carry it.

**What to ask this player:** *"You've been counting. What's your character's assessment of the least-bad path right now?"*

### The Dawnborn-Adjacent

**They just heard that people like the Dawnborn are the mechanism.**

The Dawnborn-Adjacent archetype has had more direct contact with individual Dawnborn than most other archetypes — they may have an existing relationship with Sera, Tomas, or another of the ten. They came into this session knowing the Dawnborn as people: as the person who runs the food hall, as the scholar who is quietly writing his will, as the healer with a three-year-old daughter.

EXTINGUISHED hits this archetype specifically hard because they are not processing an abstraction. They are processing a person. Probably a person they spoke to in Session 1. Possibly a person whose name they said in a different context that was warm.

**The beat:** The Dawnborn-Adjacent archetype has information that most players don't: what it feels like to be in the same room as one of these ten people. The revelation does not land as a philosophical problem. It lands as a specific face. The GM should give this player space after EXTINGUISHED — not by asking them directly, but by leaving a beat where their character's response can emerge naturally.

If this archetype names a specific Dawnborn after EXTINGUISHED: follow that thread. That name is the scene doing its job. Do not redirect away from it.

**What to ask this player (after the session, or at a natural break):** *"Which face did you see when Theron said the word?"*

### The Player Who Retrieved Edoran's Note (Session 1)

**The note's quote now has full context.**

In Session 1, Scene 1, the Restorer Enforcer carried a sealed note from Brother Edoran. The note quoted: *"The willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot."*

At the time, this read as Restorer theology — provocative, slightly threatening, possibly meaningful.

It now has full context. Edoran was not describing an abstract spiritual principle. He was describing an engineering specification. Corven built willingness into the ritual's design. The mechanism fails with coercion. The willing heart gives freely — and the release kills — because that is what the ritual requires.

Edoran knew. He has known for years — possibly longer than Theron. The Restorers' theological position is not independent from the ritual's mechanics. It is the ritual's mechanics, reframed as faith.

**The beat:** The player who retrieved Edoran's note has been carrying a quote that seemed ominous and slightly abstract since Session 1. They now understand that Edoran's letter was not a threat. It was a description. The Restorers believe the Dawnborn's deaths are meaningful because the deaths are *necessary* — and the necessity is engineering, not philosophy.

This re-casts everything Edoran has done or said. He is not wrong about the mechanics. He may be wrong about consent. He is grieving his daughter. All of these things are simultaneously true.

**What to ask this player:** *"Edoran told you this in a note before you knew what it meant. Now you know. What does your character think he wanted you to understand?"*
