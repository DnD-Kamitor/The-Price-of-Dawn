## The Resonance Lock

The box lid has three circular indentations arranged left-to-right at even intervals. Each indentation must be pressed while a specific name is spoken aloud. The lock is auditory and tactile — the mechanism responds to the combination of physical pressure and spoken sound. Speaking a name without pressing, or pressing without speaking, does nothing.

The mechanism is old but functional. It was built to last and it has. The faint warmth players feel is the lock holding its charge — a low continuous resonance that has been running for eleven years without interruption.

### The Three Names

| Position | Name | Where Found |
|---|---|---|
| LEFT indentation | CORVEN | Bundle 3 colophon (witness list) |
| CENTRE indentation | SALI | Bundle 3 colophon (witness list) |
| RIGHT indentation | WREN | Bundle 1 colophon (single witness annotation) |

The order matches the signature order on the colophon — left to right as written on the document, which is the convention for witness signatures on formal Archive notations. The three scholars witnessed Corven's final notation on the night of the ritual redirection. Their names are the last things written on those pages.

### How Players Find the Names

**No roll required to read the colophons.** The colophons appear on the last page of each bundle — the final section of formal notation, where witnesses signed. Examining any bundle physically gives access to its colophon. Players who took bundles from Scene 1 and have not read them will discover the colophons now.

- **Bundle 1 colophon:** One witness name — WREN. Annotated in a different ink from the main text, slightly rushed, as if added after the formal notation was complete. There is a small mark next to the name — not a word, but a shape, a deliberate short stroke that looks like it might mean something and might mean nothing.
- **Bundle 2 colophon:** If Bundle 2 survived — no witness signatures. The formal notation is complete but unsigned. This was the bundle the Reckoning got closest to destroying; the colophon page was singed at the corner if it went near the fire. The absence of signatures here is itself a data point: these notations were sealed before the witnesses could sign. Burned or not, Bundle 2 does not provide names.
- **Bundle 3 colophon:** Two witness names — CORVEN and SALI, in that order, left to right. Signed in full, with dates that match the night of the ritual.

**DC 10 Arcana:** Connects the colophon names to the box mechanism. A character who makes this check understands that resonance locks of this construction period used name-anchoring — the lock was tuned to specific spoken sounds during its construction, and the colophon names were the convention for recording who was present. The names and the lock are linked by the same event: the night of the redirection.

**Without the Arcana check:** Players can still solve the puzzle by simply trying the names they found in the documents. When a correct name is spoken while pressing its indentation, the indentation glows faintly amber — this is confirmation without requiring a roll. The puzzle is designed to be solved through investigation of the documents, not through arcane knowledge.

**Theron's behavior:** He will not name the names. But if a player holds up the colophon page and says "Are these the names we need?" he will look at it for a long moment and then say: "The documents have what you need. They always did." This is as close to confirmation as he will give without speaking the names himself.

### Press Mechanics

**Pressing an indentation:** Requires placing a fingertip in the circular depression and applying light pressure — no more force than pressing a door latch. The indentation is body-temperature-neutral: it does not warm or cool to the touch until a name is being spoken.

**Speaking the name:** Must be spoken aloud, at a normal conversational volume or above. Whispering does not work — the mechanism requires sufficient sound resonance. A player who whispers the name will find nothing happens. If they ask Theron why, he says: "You have to mean it."

**Correct name spoken while pressing:**
- The indentation warms noticeably.
- It emits a faint amber glow, visible in the dim study.
- It holds — stays warm and lit — until the sequence is complete or abandoned.
- The box does not open on one correct name alone.

**Wrong name spoken while pressing:**
- A low tone sounds — not alarming, not a blare, but definite and resonant, the sound of something that knows it heard the wrong answer.
- The indentation goes dark and cold.
- That specific indentation is locked for 30 seconds. The other two indentations remain active.
- Theron closes his eyes. He does not comment. He waits out the thirty seconds in silence.

**Three correct names, in order:** The seam that was invisible becomes visible — a hairline of amber light running around the lid. The lid lifts. Not dramatically — it simply becomes openable, the way a door that was stuck finally gives. Someone has to take hold of it and lift.

### Partial State (If Bundle Was Burned in Scene 1)

If players have only two of the three names — most likely they have CORVEN and SALI from Bundle 3, but lack WREN from Bundle 1 if that bundle was destroyed — the box accommodates partial input.

Two correct names spoken correctly: both indentations glow and hold. The box enters a partial-state hold. This lasts **10 minutes** from the moment the second correct name was spoken. After 10 minutes without the third name, the mechanism resets: both lit indentations go dark, and the sequence must begin again.

**Finding the third name from surviving documents:**

| Method | DC | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Investigation of Bundle 3 colophon (cross-reference, annotation) | DC 14 | Sali's signature on Bundle 3 has a marginal note in her handwriting that reads "W— also witnessed, notation page 7" — the name is abbreviated but traceable |
| Asking Theron directly | No roll | He will tell them the third name if asked directly, but only the name — he will not speak it toward the box himself. "The third witness was Wren." |
| Examining Bundle 1 colophon (if that bundle survived) | No roll | Wren's name is clearly present |

### Force Option

**DC 20 Thieves' Tools:** The lock can be physically defeated. The mechanism is sophisticated but not magical — it is precision-machined and designed to resist force, but not indefinitely against a skilled hand.

Success: The lid opens. The seal fractures — not explosively, but audibly, a sharp crack, and the indentations go dark and remain dark. The documents inside are intact and fully readable, but the edges of the top document show scorching from the broken mechanism's discharge. The box is permanently open after this: the resonance lock cannot be repaired in the field.

**DC 22 Strength (Athletics) to simply break it open:** Possible but violent. If players are brute-forcing the box open, the mechanism discharges on a failed break attempt — **2d6 fire damage** to anyone within 5 feet. On a successful Strength check, the box opens and the mechanism fully shatters. Documents still legible.

**If players attack the box directly (weapons, spells):** Treat as an object with 15 HP, AC 13 (hardwood). At 0 HP the box is destroyed. Documents may survive (DM call based on nature of attack — fire damage destroys them; bludgeoning leaves them intact). Theron's expression in this case: he watches the box being destroyed with the same look as a man watching his house burn and knowing it is already done. He does not try to stop the players. He does not say anything.

### What Is Inside

The box contains three items:

**Item 1 — Corven's Original Ritual Redirection Notes**
Twelve pages of closely written notation in a distinctive cramped hand — Corven's handwriting, which will be recognizable to any character who has handled the Archive's Corven-attributed documents. The pages are folded in thirds, preserved in good condition by the box's sealed environment. They are crisp, not yellowed. They smell faintly of the cedar lining inside the box.

The first two pages are technical notation — ritual mechanics, energy distribution diagrams, the notation cipher partially visible in the margins. The third page is different. It is written in Corven's regular hand, not the cipher, addressed to no one and to whoever reads this, simultaneously. This is the key passage:

> *"The anchors are not lost. The light did not die. It redistributed into ten living vessels born at the moment of inversion. The ritual is incomplete, not failed. The completion requires their release."*

Players who have met any of the Dawnborn will feel this sentence differently than players who have not. The word "release" is doing a great deal of work. Do not rush past it.

**Item 2 — Fragment 2 of the Notation Key**
A separate folded sheet, smaller than the notation pages, written in Sali's handwriting (different from Corven's — rounder, more deliberate, organized into columns). This is a partial cipher for the notation system used in Corven's technical pages. It covers approximately a third of the symbols in the notation, cross-referenced with Plain-tongue equivalents.

Combined with Fragment 1 from Session 1, players can now decode a significant portion of Corven's technical notation. The combined fragments do not give complete decoding — a third fragment exists, and will appear in Session 3. But with Fragments 1+2, players can read enough to understand the energy distribution pattern, the Lux Anchor binding structure, and the approximate timeline.

**Item 3 — The Anonymous Sentence**
On the last page of Corven's notes, at the very bottom, written in a third hand — not Corven's, not Sali's. Someone else wrote this in the same ink, on the same page, at a time the GM cannot determine precisely and the players cannot either.

> *"He told me what it meant. I don't think he knew I would write it down."*

No signature. The handwriting is careful but not scholarly — someone who writes regularly for practical purposes but not academic ones. The sentence appears deliberately chosen: not a revelation, not a threat, just an observation written by someone who watched Corven explain something and thought, in that moment, that it should be recorded.

This sentence functions as a planted question. Who is "he"? Who is the speaker? The players cannot answer it in this scene. They should want to.

Theron, if asked: "I don't know whose hand that is. It was there when I found the notes. It was not there when Corven sealed the box — I believe he never saw it. Someone wrote that after the box was sealed and before I opened it the first time." A beat. "I have thought about that for eleven years."
