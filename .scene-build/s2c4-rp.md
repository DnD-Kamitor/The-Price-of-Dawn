## Full Scene Flow

---

### Beat 1 — The Opening (how Theron begins)

Players have just read the key passage from Corven's notes. The box is open. The room is quiet.

Someone looks up from the pages.

Theron has been waiting for that — not impatiently. The way you wait for something you've rehearsed for a long time and then stopped rehearsing because you finally accepted it was going to happen on its own terms.

He says:

> "I need to tell you something about how long I have known."

*(He does not sit yet. He is standing near the gap in the shelves. His arms are at his sides. He is looking at the players, not at the box, not at the documents.)*

He does not wait for them to invite him to continue. He has spent eleven years waiting. He is done waiting.

---

### Beat 2 — The Confession Proper

Delivered steadily. Not rushed. Not performed. Each sentence complete before the next.

> "I decoded enough of the Notation Key eleven years ago to understand what those documents would say. I could not be the one who condemned ten people. I have been waiting for someone with enough courage — or ignorance — to investigate seriously."

*(Pause here. Let the sentence sit. He is not apologizing. He is not explaining. He is stating the fact of what he did and what it cost, and leaving the players with it.)*

If a player speaks now — responds to this with any of the four response paths — follow that thread. The scene does not have a rigid sequence. Theron will arrive at EXTINGUISHED regardless of the path. How he arrives depends on the players.

If players are quiet: he continues.

> "I want to tell you what the document says. Specifically. The word the notation uses."

---

### Beat 3 — The Escalation to EXTINGUISHED

This is the scene's emotional peak. The path to it:

Theron explains that Corven used precise language. He draws the players' attention back to the third page — to the phrase *the completion requires their release.* He does not immediately define *release.* He walks toward it deliberately:

> "The notation says the completion requires their release. I want to be exact about what that word means. Corven used precise language. He did not write *death*. He wrote *release* — the way you release light from a lantern. You open the lantern. The light goes. The vessel—"

*(He stops. He looks at the desk for a moment. Then back at them.)*

> "The technical term Corven used in the secondary notation, the pages not in that box, is *extinguished*."

---

### Beat 4 — The Silence After EXTINGUISHED

Do not move immediately.

Theron does not say anything else for a beat. He does not elaborate, clarify, or soften. He said the word because it is the correct word and he has owed it to the room for eleven years, and now it is in the room, and everyone in the room is holding it.

The silence after this word is not empty. Let it stand for a count of three — longer if the table has gone quiet and that quiet is working. Do not rush to the next sentence to fill player discomfort. The discomfort is the scene.

When he continues, he sits. The chair sound is too loud.

---

### Beat 5 — The Full Explanation

After the silence. Theron explains, sentence by sentence:

1. The Dawnborn carry the ritual energy as living anchors.
2. The mechanism requires the anchors to release what they hold.
3. Release means the energy leaves the body. The body cannot survive.
4. Corven knew this. He built consent in as an engineering requirement — the ritual will not function with coercion. Willing vessels only.
5. The reason the ritual did not complete fifty years ago: it redirected. Energy went into ten children born at that moment. The ritual is unfinished, not failed. It is still present. It is waiting.

He delivers these as facts, not as persuasion. He is not arguing a position. He is reporting what the documents say.

---

### The Four Player Response Paths

#### Path 1 — Anger

*"You sat on this while people died of grey sickness."*

Theron's response:

> "Yes."

That is the whole response. He does not qualify it, explain it, or defend against it. He receives it. If the player continues — if they enumerate the dead, the seasons of grey sickness, the children who didn't grow up — he listens. He does not argue. He does not weep. He is quiet in the way of someone who has run those numbers himself, privately, every year for eleven years, and has arrived at the same conclusion the player is arriving at now.

If a player pushes further — *"Say you're sorry"* — he looks at them for a moment and then says:

> "I don't think sorry is the right word for what I did. I think it is too small."

He is not being cold. He is trying to be accurate. He has thought about this.

---

#### Path 2 — Practical / Cold

*"What do we do now?"*

Theron:

> "That is no longer my question to answer."

*(Pause.)*

> "It never was. That is the thing I got wrong."

If they press for a practical next step: this is where he introduces Isolde Maren (Beat 6 below). He has something to give them. He knows what the next move might be. He is offering it not to solve their problem but because he has been protecting the information about Isolde and can protect it no longer.

---

#### Path 3 — Grief or Horror

Silence from one or more players. Or a player whose character is visibly affected — by the revelation, by the implications, by the specific people they have met.

Theron waits in the silence. He does not prompt. He does not say "are you all right." He has learned to sit with the thing that was just said in the room. He has been doing it for eleven years.

If a player names a specific Dawnborn they have met — Sera, or Tomas, or Lira — Theron's expression shifts slightly. He was watching to see if they would do that. If they do:

> "Yes. That is who I mean."

He has always known who he means. He has known their names since they were three years old. He has watched them become adults. He has watched Sera build the Dawnhall. He has watched Tomas write his journal. He does not say this. But if a player asks *"you know them, don't you"* — he says yes.

---

#### Path 4 — Looking for a Third Option

*"There has to be another way."* Or: *"Corven built in consent — maybe there's a path that doesn't end here."*

> "I have spent eleven years looking."
> *(Pause.)*
> "I found one researcher who thought there might be. Her name is Isolde Maren."

Move to Beat 6.

---

### Beat 6 — Introducing Isolde Maren

This beat activates whenever:
- Players ask for options
- Players go cold and practical and need a next move
- Players ask if Theron did anything in eleven years besides wait

Theron explains:

> "Isolde has been working on a method to transfer the anchor energy into constructed vessels — objects, not people. The theory is that the ritual could fire using the constructed vessels as the release point. The Dawnborn would survive."
>
> "I gave her access to the lower Archive before Keseph Vyne revoked it. Her lab was reassigned. She is now working from personal notes in a rented room. I have her location. I have been tracking her work for three years, at a distance, because I did not want my involvement to attract attention to her."

*(He pauses.)*

> "I feel responsible for not protecting her access better. Keseph moved faster than I anticipated. She lost three months of notes in the reassignment. I should have warned her earlier."

He gives players Isolde's location if they ask: she is in the Ashring district, Dusk Parishes side, in a building called the Weavers' Terrace. Third floor, the room with the east window.

He has been to her building once. He did not go in. He watched from the street to confirm she was still there. He has not told her about this scene.

---

### Beat 7 — The Secondary Objective (what he would have done if no one came)

If a player asks — directly, or by implication — what Theron would have done if investigators had never arrived:

*(Pause. Longer than any other pause in the scene.)*

> "I had a date. Three years from now. I was going to go to Sera directly and tell her what I knew. I could not decide what to do with it before then."

*(He looks at the desk.)*

> "I did not know if I could do it then either. But I had the date."

This is the most honest thing he says in the scene. He had constructed a future version of himself who was braver. He did not know if that version would exist when the date arrived. He made the date anyway. It was the closest thing to a plan he could manage.

---

### How the Scene Ends

There is no formal closing line. The scene ends when the players are ready to move.

Theron sits where he sat when he sat down. He does not get up. He does not close the box. He says, at whatever point feels right:

> "You should go. You have more to do than I have."

He means this as a fact, not dismissal. He is done. He has said everything he knows. The players have somewhere to be and something to carry. He does not.

If a player stays to talk more — about Isolde, about the Dawnborn, about what comes next — he will answer what he can. He is not closed. He has simply transferred the weight, and without the weight, he is a tired man in a chair, and there is nothing more he can do from this room tonight.
