## Theron Waide — Master Archivist

*No combat stats. Theron does not fight. If violence reaches the study he steps in front of the box — not because he intends to stop anything but because it is the only object in the room he is still responsible for, and he knows it.*

---

### Physical Description (Scene 4 State)

Theron in Scene 3 was controlled. His hands were still. His voice was precise. He was managing how he appeared.

Theron in Scene 4 is not managing how he appears.

The coat is still on — he has not removed it since the soldiers came. He will not remove it. The pen case is still in the breast pocket. His glasses are on his face, not in his hand. He has stopped the habit of removing them to read and replacing them to look at distance — he is not reading anything, and he is looking at the players, and he does not need the glasses for that, and he has not noticed he is wearing them anyway.

He is standing when the scene opens. He sits at some point — in the plain chair, not behind his desk, on the same side of the desk as the players. The sit is not deliberate. His legs make the decision before he does. He does not comment on it. Neither does anyone else.

His voice in this scene: quieter than Scene 3. Not broken. Not trembling. Quieter in the way of someone who has stopped projecting into a formal register and is simply speaking in the room where he is standing. He sounds like himself, unperformed, for the first time.

### OGAS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Occupation** | Master Archivist, Varenhold Civic Archive. Thirty years. This building is his professional life in its entirety. He knows every document in it, every location, every suppression. He knows the smell of the restricted stacks in different seasons. He knows which shelves creak at what temperature. He built a career on the preservation of information — and then, eleven years ago, sat with the most important information the Archive contained and did nothing with it. He is aware of the irony. He has been aware of it every day for eleven years. |
| **Goal** | To be the person who finally said it out loud. Not the person who decided anything. Not the person who fixed anything. The person who handed the weight to someone who could carry it forward. He wants to complete the transfer: from his chest, where it has lived, to the room, where it can be handled by people who are not him. |
| **Attitude** | Past the point of performing composure. He performed composure in Scene 2 (when he handed over the slips). He performed it in Scene 3 (when the players worked the box). He cannot perform it anymore. He is not a man who breaks down — he is a man who has simply run out of the energy that performance requires. What is left is the actual thing: tired, honest, present. |
| **Secret** | He has been slowly destroying his own notes about the Notation Key for eleven years. Pages at a time — never in sequence, never all at once, always with a reason he could construct afterward. He told himself he was managing the Archive's information security. He knows he was trying to make himself unable to decode the rest. It has not worked. He remembers every page he destroyed. The act of destroying them fixed them more firmly in his memory than if he had simply filed them. He has never admitted this to anyone. If a player asks, he tells them flatly, as if reporting someone else's behaviour: *"I burned twenty-three pages of my own notes over eleven years. I still know what they said."* |

### Want / Fear / Lie

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | Absolution through honesty. Not "forgive me" — he will not ask for forgiveness. What he wants is for the knowledge to leave him. For the sentence *the completion requires their release* to exist in someone else's mind, someone who can decide what to do with it. He wants to be a person who told the truth after waiting too long, rather than a person who waited too long and told no one. The distinction matters to him. He is not certain it should. |
| **Fear** | That the players will leave this room and use the truth the way the Reckoning would — as a number, not as ten people. He did not spend eleven years sitting with this so that someone could hear it and immediately begin calculating. He wants them to understand that the ten people who carry this are running the food hall and tending the sick and teaching children. He is terrified that what he is about to say will become, in the wrong hands, operational. |
| **Lie** | "I was protecting people." He was. He was protecting the Dawnborn from being used as a mechanism, and he was protecting the city from a decision made in ignorance, and he was protecting the investigation from being suppressed before it could reach this room. All of that is true. He was also protecting himself from being the person who said the word *extinguished*. He has known what the word was for eleven years. He has never said it. He is about to say it now, and the reason he is saying it now is that he cannot carry it any further. That is not protection. That is weight transfer. He has not admitted this to himself fully. If pressed hard by a player — if someone says *"you were protecting yourself"* — he is quiet for a moment and then says: *"Yes. That also."* |

### Voice in This Scene

**Different from Scene 3.** In Scene 3 he was precise, controlled, patient. He waited for players to work the puzzle. He chose every word.

In Scene 4 he is direct. He has been building to this for eleven years. The directness is not abrupt — it carries weight. He stops after significant sentences. He does not fill the silence. When he pauses, wait with him.

He does not perform grief. He does not perform strength. He is a man in his late fifties who has been carrying something alone for a very long time and has arrived at the moment when he can put it down. There is relief in that, and there is cost in that, and both are visible simultaneously.

---

### Key Dialogue Lines

**Opening the scene:**
> "I need to tell you something about how long I have known."

*(He pauses after this. He is not waiting for them to respond. He is deciding where to begin.)*

---

**The core confession:**
> "I decoded enough of the Notation Key eleven years ago to understand what those documents would say. I could not be the one who condemned ten people. I have been waiting for someone with enough courage — or ignorance — to investigate seriously."

*(This is the statement. He does not dress it up. He does not apologize before it. It stands as a fact about what he did, stated plainly, with its full weight.)*

---

**When asked why he didn't go to the Council:**
> "I tried once. Before Ostenveld. The result was two weeks in a holding room and my documents returned damaged. The city did not want to know. I decided to wait for someone who did."

---

**When asked why he didn't destroy the documents:**
> "Because destroying it felt like choosing. Waiting felt like — I told myself it felt like preserving the option. I have had eleven years to examine that reasoning."
> *(He does not finish the examination out loud.)*

---

**When he explains what "release" means — the word EXTINGUISHED:**
> "The notation says the completion requires their release. I want to be exact about what that word means. Corven used precise language. He did not write *death*. He wrote *release* — the way you release light from a lantern. You open the lantern. The light goes. The vessel—"
> *(He stops.)*
> "The technical term Corven used in the secondary notation, the pages not in that box, is *extinguished*."

*(The room should go quiet after this word. Do not move immediately. Sit with it.)*

---

**After EXTINGUISHED — the full explanation, delivered slowly:**
> "The Dawnborn carry the ritual's energy as living anchors. The mechanism requires the anchors to release what they hold. Release means the energy leaves. The body cannot survive the release. Corven knew this. He built consent into the design as an engineering specification — the notation fragment you found in Session 1 says it plainly. The mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed. He meant: it requires genuine willingness. The ritual is engineered to fail if the people involved are coerced."
>
> "The reason it did not complete fifty years ago is not that it failed. It redirected. The energy went into ten children born at the moment of inversion. The ritual is unfinished, not broken. It is waiting."

---

**Response to anger:**
Player: *"You sat on this while people died of grey sickness."*
> *(He does not defend himself. He looks at them.)*
> "Yes."
> *(He does not add anything to it. If they keep going, he keeps listening. He does not argue. The anger is not wrong.)*

---

**Response to practical / cold:**
Player: *"What do we do now?"*
> "That is no longer my question to answer."
> *(Pause.)*
> "It never was. That is the thing I got wrong."

---

**Response to grief or silence:**
*(He waits. He does not prompt. He does not say "are you all right." He sits with the silence. He has been sitting with something like it for eleven years. He knows how to be in a room where the thing that was said is still filling the air.)*

---

**Response to "is there another way?":**
> "I have spent eleven years looking. I found one researcher who thought there might be. Her name is Isolde Maren. She has been working on a method to transfer the anchor energy into constructed vessels — objects, not people. The ritual fires. The Dawnborn survive. I gave her access to the lower Archive before Keseph Vyne revoked it."
> *(He pauses.)*
> "Her lab was reassigned. She is working from personal notes in a rented room. I have her location. I have been tracking her work for three years. I feel — I feel responsible for not protecting her access better. I should have."

---

**The secondary objective answer (if asked: "what would you have done if no one came?"):**
> *(Pause. Longer than usual.)*
> "I had a date. Three years from now. I was going to go to Sera directly and tell her what I knew. I could not decide what to do with it before then."
> *(He looks at the desk.)*
> "I did not know if I could do it then either. But I had the date."

|---|---|

| During the confession | Deliberate. Each sentence placed. Not fast. |

| Responding to anger | Still. No defence. Receives it. |

| Introducing Isolde | Something shifts — not hope exactly, but the slight re-engagement of a man who has one more thing to give |
