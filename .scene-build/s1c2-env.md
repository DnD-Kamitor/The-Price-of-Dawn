## Scene 2 — The Riddle: The Archive Door

---

## 1. Spatial Description

### The Route: Lowmark to Spire Quarter (~15-minute walk)

Players leave the Lowmark junction and climb northeast. The transition from Lowmark to Spire Quarter is immediate and legible: the cobblestones become wider-set and cleaner underfoot, the washing lines disappear, the second-story lean of older buildings straightens into newer stone facades. The crowd thins within two blocks. By the time the Civic Archive's tower is visible, they have passed through the invisible boundary between a district that is visibly struggling and one that is performing normalcy.

**What the route looks like (heading north on the Archive road):**

| Waypoint | What Players See | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Lowmark side-street (first 5 min) | Amber lanterns, residential lean, the food queue sound fading behind | Still loud enough to hear if anyone listens back |
| Transition zone (~8 min in) | Chalk-marked administrative buildings, a small Auris devotional plaque on a corner pillar — faded sun, cleaned recently | The Spire Quarter begins caring about appearances here |
| Archive approach road (final 3 min) | Quieter foot traffic, scribes and scholars in plain robes, no children, no cart vendors | The silence after Lowmark feels conspicuous |
| Archive exterior (~12 min) | A four-sided stone tower, grey-blue Reaches limestone, eight floors visible, no decorative additions — the austerity of an institution that considers itself above ornament | The tower's age is visible in the weathering at the base |

---

### The Archive Exterior — First View

The Varenhold Civic Archive occupies a corner of the Spire Quarter two blocks from the main Spire tower itself. It is not grand. It is old and serious: grey-blue stone, narrow windows on the upper floors to protect documents from light degradation, a heavy iron gate at street level standing open during operating hours. A carved lintel over the entrance reads, in the old city script: *THAT WHICH IS RECORDED CANNOT BE UNDONE.* No one in the Archive ever discusses the irony.

**Physical features:**

- Gate: wrought iron, always open during waking hours, secured by a junior clerk's desk just inside — a ledger records entrants. The clerk nods players through on Theron's name without checking ID.
- Entrance hall: low-ceilinged, three public reading tables, four shelves of pamphlets and city-notice archives available to any Varenholder. Smells of binding glue and old paper. Two scholars working at separate tables do not look up.
- Staircase: stone, well-worn at center, iron rail bolted into the wall. Four flights. Each floor landing has a small amber lamp in a glass box — the Archive runs on its own lamp-oil supply, more generous than most public buildings. The quality of light improves as players climb.

---

### The Staircase — Four Floors

The Archive is orderly by floor:

| Floor | Contents | Sound |
|---|---|---|
| Ground | Public reading room, reference pamphlets | Scratching quills, low cough, occasional page-turn |
| Second | General collection, city records going back 200 years | Quieter — one scholar moving between shelves |
| Third (landing) | **The restricted stacks door. This is where the players arrive.** | Near-silence. The building's ambient hum disappears here. |
| Fourth+ | Theron Waide's private study and the indexing rooms — accessible only from the restricted stacks via an interior stair | Not visible to players until the door opens |

**The junior clerk** (see Pre-Scene Setup, Section 5) intercepts the players at the ground floor, leads them up, or directs them. By the time players reach the third-floor landing, they are alone — or almost alone. The clerk stays on the second floor unless something goes wrong.

---

### The Third-Floor Landing

A stone landing approximately 15 ft × 12 ft. Three features dominate:

1. **The sealed door** — set into the north wall, wood reinforced with iron banding, carved notation covering most of the face. The carving is not decorative; it is dense and deliberate.
2. **A reading bench** — built into the south wall under the window. Worn velvet cushion, a small lamp bracket above it (unlit; Theron uses this bench, not visitors).
3. **A notation index card** — pinned to the wall beside the door in a small brass frame. The card is in common script and reads: *"Restricted Collection. Authorized access: Theron Waide, Master Archivist, by appointment. All materials remain property of the Varenhold Civic Repository."* It is the most ordinary-looking thing about the landing.

**What players notice immediately (no roll):**
- The door is solid. No handle, no keyhole visible. The iron banding is decorative rather than structural — the door does not need bolts because the notation is the lock.
- The carving covers the entire face of the door except a narrow band at top and bottom. The symbols are precise, repeated, layered.
- The carved symbols closest to the center of the door are slightly different in character from those at the edges — more angular, less flowing. As if added later, or by a different hand.
- The air on the landing is cooler than the staircase. Not cold, just noticeably stilled. The kind of temperature that belongs to a room that has been closed for a long time.

## 2. Physical Description of the Door

### The Door Itself

Approximately 7 ft tall, 3.5 ft wide. Old oak, darkened with age, reinforced with four horizontal iron straps across the face. The iron straps are plain and functional. Everything expressive about the door is in the carving.

**The carving — Corven's private notation system:**

Corven spent forty years developing a mathematical-philosophical cipher he used exclusively for his most significant work. The door's notation was carved by a craftsman working from Corven's prepared templates — the lines are too precise for freehand — but the system is entirely Corven's.

The notation appears as:

- **Concentric arc-patterns** in the upper third of the door — these are structural markers, equivalent to headings or section breaks. Each arc terminates in one of seven symbols that a DC 12 Arcana check identifies as notation markers related to ritual practice (not a specific tradition — Corven's own system).
- **A central panel** roughly 18 inches square at the door's midpoint — the lock mechanism proper. The symbols here are more deeply carved than the surrounding notation, approximately 2mm deeper, as if whoever carved them was instructed to make them legible from a distance. They form a question in notation-script. The underlying structure is a riddle, but it reads first as mathematical formula to any character with Arcana proficiency.
- **Border notation** covering the door's edges — flowing, continuous, the style of someone in a rhythm. This is Corven's marginal annotation style: running commentary rather than primary text. Most of it cross-references material in the restricted stacks. It is not necessary to solve the door, but it rewards attention (see Mechanics, Section 6).

**The old wax seal:**

At the door's center, approximately at chest height, a remnant of a wax seal is pressed directly into the wood — not into a document but into the door face itself. The wax is dark amber, cracked and old. It is not structurally part of the lock mechanism; it was applied separately, over the carved notation, as an additional signal: *do not open this without authorization.*

The seal's impression is a concentric circle design — the same pattern as the note retrieved from the zealot in Scene 1 (if the players saved it). A DC 14 Arcana check makes this connection explicit: the same ring was used. Corven made that seal. Edoran's letter used Corven's seal matrix. That seal has been in circulation, somewhere, since Corven's death.

---

### The Crack of Amber Light — Wrong Attempt Response

When players give a wrong answer, the door does not fail silently. On the first wrong attempt:

A resonance tone sounds from within the door — not from behind it but from the wood itself, as if the carved notation is vibrating. The tone is low, not painful, similar to a struck bell at the end of its ring: present, then fading over approximately three seconds. Simultaneously, a hairline crack of amber-orange light traces along the inner boundary of the central panel, following the carved lines. It runs the full circuit of the central panel — approximately one second per side — and then goes dark.

**What this communicates without words:**
- The door is active, not inert. Something is listening to what the players say.
- Wrong answers produce a response, not a silence. There is logic operating here.
- The light follows the carved lines, which makes the carved lines feel purposeful rather than decorative — the mechanism is visible when triggered.

**On the second wrong attempt:** The resonance tone is a half-step higher. The amber light traces the same path but slightly brighter. Dust shifts on the reading bench.

**On the third wrong attempt:** The tone sharpens, almost bell-like. The light traces the panel rapidly — too fast to follow with the eye — and then holds for a full two seconds before fading. A thin crack of light appears at the base of the door frame, along the floor, fading from the inside out. The door will not respond for one hour. This is when Theron, if not already present, speaks.

## 3. Three Read-Aloud Variants

Deliver one variant depending on the party's condition and approach on arrival. Variants are written for direct delivery — read aloud as written, or paraphrase for your table's tone.

---

### A — Arriving in Haste After Scene 1 Fight

*Use when: players are still in a heightened state from the ambush — moving fast, someone may be hurt, they may be carrying the sealed note still warm from the lantern.*

> The staircase ends. The landing is quiet in a way that feels wrong after the last twenty minutes — no one shouting, no crowd, no cold air from the streets. Just stone and amber lamplight and a door at the end of the landing that looks like it has not moved in a very long time.
>
> The clerk who met you at the entrance is standing slightly to one side, watching you with the expression of someone who was told to expect important visitors and is now revising what important looks like. She is young — mid-twenties, ink on two fingers, hair pinned without ceremony. She says: *"Master Waide said to bring you directly up. He's — he isn't here yet, actually. He's been delayed below."* She looks at the door. *"It's been closed since before my posting. I've never seen it open."*
>
> The door itself is carved from edge to edge with notation you half-recognize and half-don't — a formal cipher system pressed deeply into old oak. At its center, a wax seal the color of old honey, cracked and permanent. At the very middle of the carving: a panel of different symbols, more deeply cut, more deliberate. They are asking you something.

---

### B — Arriving Cautiously, Scanning for More Ambushes

*Use when: players are methodical, checking sight lines, covering each other coming up the stairs, expecting a second intercept.*

> The Archive is the kind of quiet that takes practice. The scholars on the lower floors produce noise without producing volume — quills, page-turns, the very occasional murmured question. By the third floor it has reduced further still, until the landing is simply still: four walls, a window with its shutters half-drawn, a bench, and a door.
>
> You came up watching the shadows. There are no shadows here worth watching. The landing smells of lamp oil and the particular dry cold of rooms that have been kept closed through many winters. No one waiting. Nothing watching back. Just a door that takes up the entire north wall of the landing, covered in notation carved with a precision that is slightly unsettling to look at directly — the symbols are too even, too dense, as if someone spent months making sure every line was exactly right.
>
> A junior clerk appears behind you from a lower step — she must have been running to catch up. *"I'm sorry — I was meant to come first. Master Waide said to bring you straight here. He's been detained. He'll be up directly."* She stops when she sees you looking at the door. *"It hasn't opened since Year 39. I checked the records when I was posted here. Eleven years. I don't know if that's significant."* A pause. *"It probably is, isn't it."*

---

### C — Arriving to Find Theron Already at the Landing Waiting

*Use when: players were efficient, negotiated Scene 1 quickly, or the GM has reason to have Theron already present — he has been waiting in the Archive all day, increasingly certain the players would arrive, increasingly certain he is not ready.*

> The staircase opens onto the landing and Theron Waide is already there.
>
> He is older than his correspondence implied — mid-sixties, a scholar's build (rounded shoulders, ink permanently in the creases of his fingers), standing at the reading bench with his coat still on and a lantern in his hand he does not appear to need. He has been here for some time. He looks at you in the way of someone who has been rehearsing the beginning of a conversation and has now forgotten it.
>
> *"You arrived,"* he says. Then, immediately: *"That's not — I knew you would arrive. I mean — the note. Did you get the note?"*
>
> He is referring to a different note than the one in your pocket. He doesn't elaborate. His eyes move to the door behind him — he stepped slightly away from it when you appeared at the top of the stairs, as if he had been standing very close — and then back to you.
>
> The door is covered in carved notation, dense and deliberate, with a central panel that reads as a question. The wax seal at its center is cracked and old. It has been eleven years. Theron has been in this Archive the whole time and he looks like the door costs him something just to stand near.

## 4. Five Senses — Expanded

### A. The Passive Study Atmosphere (Before the Door Engages)

**Sight**
- The amber-orange quality of Archive lamplight — not the sourceless civic light of the streets but actual lamp oil, burning warm and yellowish, in glass-boxed brackets on each landing. The restricted stacks door is fully lit by this; there is no atmospheric gloom obscuring it. Corven designed visibility. He wanted it seen.
- The notation on the door face: not just symbols but a *texture* — the carving catches the lamplight differently at different angles. Symbols that look flat from straight-on reveal shallow depth when a player tilts their head or brings their own light source closer.
- The wax seal at the center, older than anything else on the door, the amber wax now nearly brown with age. The impression is still sharp: concentric circles with a cross-hatched center. It looks nothing like official Varenhold seals, which are heraldic.
- The bench under the south window: the velvet cushion has a worn patch exactly where someone has been sitting for decades. Theron's reading spot. The lamp bracket above it is the only one with a fresh oil supply — it has been filled recently.

**Sound**
- Near-silence. The Archive absorbs sound differently from outdoor spaces: there is none of the ambient city hum, no wind, no distant cart noise. What remains: the faint creak of the building settling, the tick of cooling stone, someone on a lower floor turning a page.
- Breathing — the players' own, louder than expected in the stillness.
- The junior clerk's quill, if she is writing in the entry ledger she's carried up. Small, quick, precise strokes.
- No sound from behind the door.

**Smell**
- Binding glue and paper dust — the smell of the Archive is distinct and pervasive, closer to an old library than to a public building. It has been accumulating for over a century.
- Old wax: the seal on the door has a faint, faded smell of beeswax beneath the dust. Not unpleasant. Archival.
- Cold stone from the landing walls — the third floor is noticeably cooler than the street, and the restricted stacks door is the coolest surface in the room.
- If a player has been in a fight recently (Scene 1 blood, sweat, burned paper from a saved note): these smells are conspicuous here. The Archive does not smell of anything that has happened in the last fifty years. The players bring the present into it.

**Touch**
- The door surface, if touched: cool, smooth at the iron banding, slightly rough where the notation carving runs. The carving is precise but not polished — the channels of each symbol have raised edges from the carving tool. Running a finger along them traces the notation in a tactile way.
- The bench cushion: if sat on, it is denser than expected — the kind of worn-in firmness that takes decades to develop.
- Stone floor underfoot: colder than the staircase. The landing is not heated.
- The wax seal: if touched, it is solid and unyielding. It does not shift or flake. Whatever sealed this door eleven years ago sealed it without movement since.

**Taste**
- Paper dust, caught at the back of the throat with every breath. Dry. Different from street dust: this is the dust of preserved things, not weathered ones.
- For any character with lingering combat residue: the iron taste of adrenaline still metabolizing — the contrast between this and the Archive's academic stillness is itself a sensory note. The body has not entirely left the junction yet. The Archive is already asking it to.

---

### B. The Moment of a Wrong Attempt

**Sight**
- The central panel notation shimmers — not magically, but visually, in the way that heat makes air move. It is brief, a half-second optical disturbance. Then the amber light traces the carved boundary of the panel, running along the channels of the notation like liquid following a groove. One circuit, then gone.
- The notation on the rest of the door does not respond. Only the central panel.
- If Theron is present: his jaw sets. His eyes close for a moment. He doesn't look at the players.
- If not present: the sound that follows brings footsteps.

**Sound**
- The resonance tone: a sustained low frequency, like a struck iron bell at the far end of its decay. The sound is in the wood of the door and in the stone of the walls — it does not come from behind the door. It is the door's own voice.
- The tone lasts approximately three seconds, fading evenly to silence. There is a brief moment after it fades where the Archive's ambient quiet feels deeper than before — the wrong-answer tone has redefined the silence around it.
- The first wrong attempt: footsteps on the staircase from below begin shortly after the tone fades. Junior clerks on the reading level, looking up. A chair scraped back somewhere beneath.

**Smell**
- A faint ozone trace, brief and gone — the smell of something electrically or magically active discharging. Not dramatic. Subtle enough that only a character with proficiency in Arcana or a DC 13 Perception check catches it.

**Touch**
- If a player's hand is on the door when the wrong-answer response fires: a faint vibration in the wood, matching the resonance tone's frequency. Not painful. Like pressing a palm to a struck table.
- The air on the landing moves slightly — not a draft, but a pressure change. A single pulse, outward.

---

### C. The Moment the Door Opens (Correct Answer)

**Sight**
- The central panel notation stills completely — no shimmer, no light trace. Then, slowly, from the center outward: the notation carving fills with amber light, not running along the channels this time but flooding them, the way a lamp illuminates a paper from behind. The light reaches the outer boundary of the central panel and holds.
- The surrounding notation on the door face responds: the concentric arcs in the upper third each light at their terminal symbol, one by one, in sequence. They hold, too. The door is not opening yet — it is confirming.
- The wax seal at the center: the light does not touch it. It stays dark. It is the one thing on this door that is not part of the mechanism.
- Then: the door swings inward, slowly, of its own accord. No sound of a latch. No mechanical click. Just the door, moving, on a hinge that has waited eleven years.
- The restricted stacks beyond: rows of bound folios on dark shelving, stretching back approximately 30 ft. The same amber lamplight as the landing, but from smaller fixtures, spaced along the ceiling in a line. The shelves are floor to ceiling, densely packed. The organization is not alphabetical or by subject — the sections are labeled with constellation symbols. At the far end of the central aisle, a brass mechanism on a column catches the light: the star-wheel.

**Sound**
- Silence from the door itself. No creak, no mechanical sound.
- From within the restricted stacks: the quiet exhalation of air that has been sealed in a room for eleven years releasing. It is not dramatic. It is barely there. A player who is paying attention notices the sound of the Archive's ambient quiet changing texture slightly — the stacks have their own stillness, distinct from the landing's.
- If Theron is present: his breath audibly catches. He does not speak. He presses his hand to his mouth briefly and looks away.

**Smell**
- Old paper, but different in character from the landing — denser, more concentrated, the smell of documents stored in limited air circulation for a decade. Also: the faint cold of preserved space, a clean dryness without the street's mineral edge.
- Something underneath, barely: old wax, different from the door seal. Candle wax, from whatever Theron used when he last worked in here eleven years ago. A very faint ghost of it in the back of the throat.

**Touch**
- The air coming out of the restricted stacks is cooler than the landing by several degrees. Stepping through the doorway: a physical transition, as if the interior space has its own climate.
- The light within the restricted stacks is slightly weaker than the landing — players may want their own light source to examine documents in detail, though movement through the aisle is possible without it.

**Sight (restricted stacks interior):**
- Shelf labels in constellation notation — players who have been told about the star-wheel puzzle (Scene 3) will recognize these as the navigation system immediately.
- One shelf section slightly misaligned from the others — the only visible sign that Theron has been in here since the door was sealed. He checked something, once, and then stopped.
- At the end of the aisle, the brass star-wheel mechanism: three concentric rings on a central column, each engraved with constellation symbols. It is unset — rings in a neutral position, no combination established.

## 5. Pre-Scene Setup

### What Players Know Coming In

Coming from Scene 1 and the Chancellor's briefing, players carry the following:

| What They Know | Source |
|---|---|
| The Archive holds Corven's documents on the Ritual of Eternal Dawn | Chancellor Ostenveld's briefing |
| Theron Waide is their contact and has authorized access to the restricted stacks | Ostenveld's briefing |
| Someone already knew their route and sent people to stop them | Scene 1 ambush |
| The Restorers are organized, theologically motivated, and specifically opposed to this investigation | Scene 1 |
| (If note recovered) Someone with ritual knowledge and Auris theology wrote the interception order | Sealed note from Scene 1 |
| (If Ostenveld's drawer was examined) The phrase "willing participants only" exists in Corven's documentation | Session 0 / Chancellor scene |

**Players do NOT know (coming into Scene 2):**
- The door is sealed by a riddle, not a key
- The riddle answers with the word CONSENT
- The door has not been opened in eleven years
- Theron decoded the ritual's true cost eleven years ago and chose silence
- Theron has been destroying his own notes while being unable to destroy the original documents
- The note's phrase "the anchor holds" refers to a ritual term Corven coined

---

### What the Junior Clerk Says

The junior clerk (no name given in the text; GMs may name her freely — she is a recurring minor NPC if desired) meets players at the ground-floor entrance and escorts or directs them upstairs. She is young, efficient, and visibly uncomfortable with the restricted stacks landing in a way she cannot explain.

**On Theron's whereabouts:**
> *"Master Waide said to bring you straight to the third floor. He's below still — the Compact scholars were asking him something and he couldn't leave mid-sentence. He'll be up directly. He said the door would — he said you'd know what to do with the door."*

**On the eleven years:**
> *"I checked the log when I was posted here two years ago. Last entry for the restricted stacks is Year 39. That's eleven years. Nothing in or out. I asked one of the senior clerks once and she said 'that's Master Waide's department.' So I stopped asking."*

**On the carved notation:**
> *"It's been like that as long as anyone here can remember. I was told it was Archmagister Corven's private cipher. Some of the scholars have copied sections of it — there's a paper in the Spire's archive about the notation system, if you want to look. It doesn't include the solution to the door."* A pause. *"I've tried several things. Over two years. It's embarrassing."*

**On the wax seal:**
> *"That's not a city seal. I checked the register. It's not registered anywhere in the civic seal catalog. Someone made it privately."*

**What the junior clerk does NOT say** (does not know, or does not feel authorized to say):
- That Theron's private reading space is inside the restricted stacks
- That Theron has been visiting the public side of the third floor, specifically the bench, without opening the door — just sitting near it
- That two scholars from the Arveth Compact visited last month and spent an hour in front of the door and left frustrated
- That she has twice caught Theron weeping at that bench. She has never mentioned it to anyone.

---

### What Theron Wants on Arrival (If Present)

**Before his guard is up (first 30 seconds of meeting):** Relief that someone has finally come who might actually read what is in there. He has been carrying this alone, and the arrival of authorized investigators is almost overwhelming.

**After his guard reassembles (immediately after):** The familiar performance of scholarly hedging. He becomes helpful in the way that falls just short of actually helpful. He will not say what he knows until he trusts the players, or until the door opens and forces the issue.

**What he monitors during the door scene:**
- Whether players approach the door with curiosity or impatience
- Whether they examine the notation before attempting an answer
- Whether they communicate with each other or work individually
- Whether anyone mentions the sealed note from Scene 1 — if so, his attention sharpens and something in his posture changes

## 6. What NOT to Describe Yet

The following must be withheld until the scene earns their reveal. Do not mention or imply these during the environment description or the door scene:

| Element | Why to Withhold | When It Emerges |
|---|---|---|
| **The contents of the restricted stacks** | Players are solving a door, not shopping — the stacks are a reward, not a backdrop | When the door opens (correct answer or Theron's guidance) |
| **Theron's knowledge of the ritual cost** | His guilt and his secret are the RP scene's payload, not the door scene's | After the door opens, when Theron's defenses lower; fully in Session 2 |
| **What "the anchor holds" means** | This phrase from the recovered note (if saved) is a breadcrumb players may carry in; do not interpret it for them in the environment description | When a player asks, or when Theron sees the note |
| **The wax seal's connection to Edoran** | If players make the Arcana check that connects the door seal to the note seal, let that be a player-driven reveal in the moment | When a player examines the seal and has the note for comparison |
| **Who is listening from below** | There is someone in the Archive who is watching the players' arrival: a Reckoning observer who does not act this session. Do not place them in the scene or suggest surveillance beyond the Archive's normal foot traffic. | Not this session — this thread opens in Session 2 |
| **Theron's private reading space inside the stacks** | The worn bench cushion outside the door is a clue Theron sits *outside* the restricted stacks, not inside. The shape of his avoidance is more revealing than the fact of access. | When a player examines the bench or asks Theron directly |
| **Fragment 1 of the Notation Key location** | Shelf 4-17-3 is Scene 3's puzzle, not Scene 2's. Do not let the door scene collapse into the stacks scene before the riddle has its moment. | When the star-wheel is solved (Scene 3) |
| **The Reckoning's interest in the Archive** | They are not yet visible. The players have not yet done anything that triggers active Reckoning response — their first probe is the following morning. | Opening of Session 2 |

## 7. Estimated Real-Time Duration Per Phase

| Phase | What Happens | Estimated Time |
|---|---|---|
| **Scene transition narration** | GM delivers the Scene 1 → Scene 2 bridge (walk from Lowmark, approach, exterior), players may ask questions or take actions during the walk | 2–3 minutes |
| **Junior clerk interaction** | Players meet the clerk, receive the basic setup, hear about the eleven years | 3–5 minutes |
| **First approach to the door** | Players examine the notation, interact with the door's physical details, make initial checks | 5–8 minutes |
| **First wrong attempt** | Players offer an incorrect answer; door responds; tone sounds; footsteps begin | 1–2 minutes |
| **Theron arrival** (if first wrong attempt) | Theron appears at the landing — his entrance, his look at the door, his silence | 2–3 minutes |
| **Player attempts 2–3** (with or without Theron) | Additional wrong answers, rising stakes; Theron becomes progressively more available as guide | 5–10 minutes |
| **Correct answer** | Door opens; five-senses delivery of the opening; restricted stacks revealed | 2–3 minutes |
| **Theron's first RP beat** | His reaction to the door opening; his first honest sentence; transition to Scene 3 | 3–5 minutes |
| **Total (standard run)** | | **23–39 minutes** |
| **Total (heavy RP, many attempts)** | If players ask Theron many questions before solving the door, or if significant player discussion around the answer word | **35–50 minutes** |

**Pacing note:** If this scene is running long and the table is engaged with Theron, let it. The door puzzle is the mechanism; the relationship with Theron is the scene. If players are frustrated by wrong attempts and losing engagement, have Theron arrive earlier (after the first wrong attempt rather than being present from the start) and give him the line: *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."* This shifts the scene from puzzle to RP without abandoning the dramatic structure.

## 8. GM Opening Sentence (Before Read-Aloud)

Deliver this aloud before choosing a read-aloud variant. It closes Scene 1 and opens Scene 2 in a single breath:

> *"The queue re-forms around you. It takes about ninety seconds. Someone picks up a dropped basket. Someone else retrieves a child from behind a crate. The junction looks almost normal. You are still holding [the note / nothing]. The Archive is fifteen minutes east. Whatever just happened, someone knew you were coming. Let's see if they managed to get here first."*

Then pause. Ask the table if anyone does anything during the walk. If not, deliver the appropriate read-aloud variant.

## 9. Scene Transition In — Closing Scene 1, Opening Scene 2

**If players debrief during the walk** (discuss the ambush, examine the note, check for tails):

Let this happen. The walk is dead time narratively but alive time relationally. Players processing Scene 1 during the transition means they arrive at the Archive with more integrated thinking. Do not rush them. The Archive will wait.

If a player reads the note during the walk, deliver the note text naturally: *"The willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot."* Let it sit without comment. They will understand it better on the other side of the door scene.

**If players want to return to the Chancellor first:**

They can. It costs approximately one hour and means the Archive visit begins later in the day — which creates mild time pressure for Scenes 3–5. Theron will still be there. The door will still be sealed. The Chancellor's reaction if players report the ambush is detailed in the Outcomes file (s1c1-outcomes.md, Section 3).

**If players arrive at the Archive immediately, without discussion:**

Begin with the B variant (cautious) or A variant (haste) depending on their pace. The transition narration above handles the emotional register. Do not assume they have processed Scene 1 — they may bring that processing into the door scene itself, which is fine. The door scene's enforced pause (wrong answers, Theron's arrival) creates space for decompression that the walk might not have.
