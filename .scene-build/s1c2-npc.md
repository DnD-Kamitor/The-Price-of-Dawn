## Scene 2 NPC Roster

Three presences in this scene. One speaks. One is silent. One is absent — and the most important of the three.

---

## 1. Theron Waide — Master Archivist

**Role in Scene:** The players' guide, gatekeeper, and the man who has been waiting eleven years for someone competent enough to make his waiting mean something. He is the puzzle's answer — but he will not say so until the players earn the question.

---

### Physical Description

Late 60s. Compact, slightly stooped in the way of people who have spent a career leaning over document tables. Archive smell: lamp oil, old paper, the particular damp of stone rooms with too many shelves and not enough windows. His hands are always doing something — adjusting papers, touching the edge of a shelf, rolling a stylus between his fingers. Not nervous, exactly. Occupied. He has a habit of placing documents near the players and then reconsidering, pulling them back a few inches, as though the act of making information available requires a second decision every time. He wears spectacles but frequently takes them off when speaking directly to someone, as though he sees people better without them.

**Distinctive detail:** A small green ribbon is knotted around his left wrist. It has been there long enough that the wrist has a slight callus beneath it. He does not explain it. A Religion DC 13 check identifies it as an Auris mourning cord — a private grief marker worn until the grief is ready to be set down. He has been wearing it for eleven years.

---

### OGAS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Occupation** | Master Archivist of the Varenhold Civic Repository; former assistant to Archmagister Corven; in post for over thirty years. Holds the keys — literal and institutional — to every locked section of the Archive. Authorized signatory for restricted-stack access. The Archive is, functionally, him. |
| **Goal** | Transfer the weight. He has been carrying the knowledge of the ritual's true cost for eleven years, alone, unable to act on it, unable to destroy it, unable to share it without becoming the person who condemned ten people. He needs someone to take this from him — not to be absolved, but because he genuinely cannot make the decision himself and has known for years that he should not be the one to make it. The players' arrival is the event he has been waiting for. His goal is to hand off the information with enough context that they can carry it better than he has. |
| **Attitude** | Initially: careful, evasive, and precisely helpful in ways that fall just short of actually helpful. He answers questions with questions. He finds documents near what the players need rather than what they need. He is performing uncertainty he no longer actually feels. As the scene progresses — especially after the first wrong riddle attempt — the performance begins to give way to something more honest: the exhausted relief of someone who finally has permission to stop pretending. |
| **Secret** | He decoded the ritual's true cost eleven years ago. He read Corven's original notation system — he built it with Corven, as Corven's assistant — and he understood what the archived documents said before the wax seals were even dry. He told no one. He has spent eleven years convincing himself that he stayed silent for good reasons (the city wasn't ready, the political situation was too volatile, he feared being killed for knowing) while the real reason calcified inside him: he knew the truth would require a decision, and he could not bear to be the person who forced it. He has been slowly destroying his own margin notes to create plausible deniability. He cannot destroy Corven's original text. He tried once and stopped. He has been corresponding with someone whose letters he has filed in the restricted section — letters arguing, in elegant theological language, that the Dawnborn must be allowed to choose freely. He has never replied to these letters. He does not know who sent them. He suspects. |

---

### Voice and Dialogue Style

Soft, rapid speech with the tendency to qualify everything — "I should say," "that is to say," "if I'm reading it correctly." Slight lisp on sibilants. He trails off mid-sentence when something he is about to say frightens him, then picks a different path. He uses passive voice when describing his own decisions: "the documents were filed," not "I filed them"; "the seal was left intact," not "I left the seal intact." He is grammatically absenting himself from his own history.

When he finally opens up — after trust is established, or after the third wrong riddle attempt and the speech he gives before the fourth chance — his speech changes. Sentences stop qualifying. He uses the first person. He looks at the players directly instead of at the shelf beside them. This transition should be noticeable to attentive players without being announced.

---

### Key Lines

**First appearance — he appears at the landing after the players' first failed riddle attempt:**

> "You're here about the door." *(Not a question. He has been expecting this. He looks at them the way a man looks at something he has rehearsed seeing for years.)* "I've been — I thought it would be someone from the Spire, eventually. Or the Council. I didn't think..." *(A small pause, recalibrating.)* "Come up. Don't touch the south shelves. I'll find you tea."

*GM delivery note: The pause and the recalibration are the tell. He had a sentence planned for the Spire Scholar. The players are not who he expected, and that surprises him enough to show. Let it show.*

**If players solve the riddle correctly on their own (no intervention needed):**

> *(Long pause while the door finishes its mechanism. Then:)*
> "You found it on your own." *(There is something in his voice that is not quite surprise — more like confirmation of a possibility he had allowed himself to hope for.)* "Good. That's — that tells me something. Come in. We have a great deal to discuss and I have been saving it for a very long time."

**When he becomes guide — leading players into the restricted stacks:**

> "I want you to understand something before we go further. What I'm going to show you — I've been the only person who knows what these documents say, in their completeness, for over a decade. That is not..." *(He straightens a sheaf of papers unnecessarily.)* "That is not the way this should have worked. One person knowing this was a mistake. I made the mistake and I have been living in it. I'm going to try to correct that now, and I would appreciate it if you would be patient with me, because correcting it is — it is harder than I expected it to be, standing here with people who actually want to know."

**When players ask about the restricted stacks:**

> "The restricted section holds Corven's original working documents — the notation, the correspondence, the ritual design logs. The public section has summaries. Interpretations. Sanitized versions prepared for council review. The difference between them is..." *(He stops. Puts his spectacles on, takes them off again.)* "Considerable. The restricted documents say what happened. The public versions say what the council was told."

*GM note: If players push — "What did happen?" — he does not answer yet. He says: "Bring tea." He is not ready. He needs the walk through the stacks, and the time the walk gives him, before he can say the word aloud.*

**When players ask who Corven was:**

> "He was..." *(He puts his spectacles on. This takes a moment.)* "He was the finest ritual theorist this city has produced. Possibly the finest in the Graymere Reaches in the last century. He was also — I worked with him for eleven years before the ritual. I was his assistant. I catalogued his notation. I am the only person still alive who can read his complete system without the cipher key." *(A beat.)* "He knew I could. I think — I've thought, for a long time, that he left the documents here because he knew I would still be here. That I would understand them. That I would keep them safe until someone came who could do something with them." *(He looks at the players.)* "I have been asking myself for eleven years whether he was right to trust me with that."

---

### What He Volunteers vs. What Requires a Roll

**Volunteers freely (no roll):**
- Where documents are, generally — he is an archivist; unhelpfulness in this direction would be unprofessional
- That the restricted stacks exist and require his personal authorization
- That Corven was his mentor and that he was Corven's assistant
- That the riddle is "old" and that "the original designers had a particular kind of mind"
- That the door has not opened in eleven years

**Requires relationship-building (trust Tier 1, one meaningful scene of genuine engagement):**
- That he has been waiting for someone specific
- That the restricted section contains documents that differ significantly from the public summaries
- That he has been receiving correspondence about the Dawnborn from an unknown sender
- That he has not replied to this correspondence, and that he knows why he hasn't

**Requires trust Tier 2 or the Lantern Walk Confession (Investigation DC 15 or Persuasion DC 15 with prior Insight DC 13 advantage):**
- That he decoded the ritual's cost eleven years ago
- That he chose silence, and what that choice has cost him
- His suspicion about who has been writing to him
- The location of the document that matters most: Corven's sealed final letter, shelf 4-17-3

**Requires him to break down or be reassured (Persuasion DC 15 with "it's not your fault" framing):**
- The full truth, in order, starting with the day eleven years ago when he read the notation and sat down on the floor of the restricted section and could not get up for two hours
- The name Edoran — he does not know Edoran by name, but he knows the author of the letters. He will identify the handwriting from the Scene 1 note if shown it. If players have the note, this is the scene where it pays off.

---

### Attitude Shift Triggers

**Opens him further (toward full disclosure):**
- Players demonstrate they understand the Archive's organizational logic — that they are treating the documents as a system, not a treasure hunt
- Players show patience, particularly when he trails off mid-sentence; a player who waits rather than filling the silence earns visible relief
- Someone asks about the ribbon on his wrist, and accepts "it's old" as an answer without pushing — respect for a grief marker he hasn't chosen to explain
- Players mention Corven with respect, particularly if they reference specific aspects of his work
- Players show the Scene 1 note — Edoran's handwriting. If they have it, Theron goes still and says: "Where did you get that." It is the question that opens everything, because it tells him someone else already knows.

**Closes him off (returns to performative uncertainty):**
- Pressure. Any direct demand — "just tell us what the documents say" — sends him back behind the professional archivist persona. He becomes helpful, organized, and utterly useless.
- Attempts to access the restricted stacks without him, or to use the Sorting Table Investigation check aggressively while he watches. He will return the stacks to disorder if he feels the information is being taken rather than offered.
- Invoking the Chancellor's authority or the commission paperwork. He is not afraid of the Chancellor. He is afraid of being the man who condemned ten people, and the Chancellor's authority means he might become that man on a schedule he didn't choose.
- Mockery or dismissiveness about the ritual, the Dawnborn, or the city's situation. He has been watching the city die slowly. He is not interested in detachment.

---

### Theron and the Scholar Archetype

The Scholar came to Varenhold because of Theron's correspondence — documents that Theron chose, in an order Theron selected, building a picture of the Archive and the ritual that brought the Scholar here. Theron did this deliberately. He spent three years composing these letters.

**What he sent and why:**

He sent documents that asked the right questions without providing answers: Corven's early notation guides, before the cipher was developed; the public-record summaries of the ritual's first attempts; the historical documentation of the grey sickness onset, correlated with the ritual's failure. These are the documents of a man building a case and offering it to someone he has not yet met, with the hope that they will follow the trail to its end.

He chose the Scholar because the Scholar's institutional affiliation — the Spire, or a recognized scholarly body — meant they had access to the supplementary literature he couldn't send through unofficial channels. He needed someone who could read what he sent in context, not just as documents.

**What he has not admitted:**

He has not told the Scholar that he selected them. If the Scholar asks — "did you choose me?" — he says: "I chose the kind of person who would respond to those documents the way you responded to them. I didn't know your name when I started." This is true. It is also not the whole truth. He started corresponding with the Scholar specifically because their early response to his first document showed that they understood what was being asked, not just what was being said. He has been reading the Scholar for three years. He knows them, in the way a man who reads letters carefully knows someone, and he is slightly in awe of having them in the room.

**The Scene 2 moment, if the Scholar is present:**

After the trust has been established enough that Theron has confirmed he sent the documents deliberately, someone — the Scholar, another player, or Theron himself — will ask the obvious question: *Why didn't you just say what you knew?*

His answer:

> "I have been asking myself that every day for three years. The honest answer — the one I've only recently been able to say — is that I knew what I knew, and I knew that knowing it required a decision, and I didn't trust myself to make the right decision. I thought if I found someone else who could figure it out — who was smart enough to figure it out from the pieces I left — then at least the decision would be made by someone who earned it. Does that make sense to you?" *(He looks at the Scholar specifically.)* "You earned it. I want you to know that."

## 2. The Junior Clerk

**Role in Scene:** Texture, legitimate help, and the one person in the Archive who sees Theron clearly without understanding what they're seeing.

---

### Physical Description

20s, precise movements, ink-stained left hand (right-handed scribes stain the left from contact with fresh pages). Tidy uniform in the Archive's dark wool — slightly too large across the shoulders, purchased or handed down. A habit of aligning objects on their desk when thinking. Not nervous exactly; more than usually aware of what is expected of them. Quiet unless addressed.

**Give them a name at the table if the players interact directly.** Suggested options: Sevet, Maren, Tollick, Yohan. Use whichever fits the table's energy.

---

### OGAS (Brief — Minor NPC)

| | |
|---|---|
| **Occupation** | Junior Archive Clerk, second year of appointment. Responsible for document intake, catalog maintenance, and counter service for public-floor requests. |
| **Goal** | Do their job correctly, be noticed for doing it correctly, and eventually not be junior anymore. |
| **Attitude** | Professionally welcoming to the players, slightly uncertain when the inquiry involves the restricted section, notably careful when Theron is mentioned. |
| **Secret** | They know something is wrong with Theron and the restricted stacks, in the general sense that a person knows when a colleague has a door they never open. They have never been in the restricted section. They were told, on their first week, that it required Theron's personal authorization and that they were not to ask about it. They have not asked about it. They have noticed that Theron visits alone, after hours, and that he emerges differently than he went in — sometimes lighter, usually heavier. They have filed this observation away and not told anyone, because they are a professional and this is not their business. |

---

### What They Know and Will Say

**Without any prompting:**
- "Master Waide is on the upper floor — second landing, north end. He doesn't usually receive visitors without an appointment, but if it's Archive business he's generally — he's flexible." *(Slight hesitation on 'flexible,' which is a polite word for something more complicated.)*
- The public floor's organization, location of any section by subject, hours, access procedures.
- That the restricted stacks "require Master Waide's personal sign-off — he handles those himself."

**If players ask how long the door on the landing has been sealed:**

> "The one at the top of the east stair? That's — I asked about it when I started. Master Waide said it was under authorized restriction. It's been like that since before I came on." *(They have been there two years. The door has been sealed eleven years. They have done the math privately and not shared it.)*

**If players ask whether Theron has been acting strangely:**

> *(A beat — they are deciding how much to say.)*
> "He's..." *(Careful.)*
> "He works very hard. He comes in early and stays late. He takes his lunch in the stacks on Sevenday, which most people don't. He's been Master Archivist for a long time and he takes the collection seriously. That's — that's really what I'd say."

*They did not answer the question. They know they did not answer the question. A DC 12 Insight check catches the deliberate redirect. They will not elaborate without a DC 14 Persuasion. If pressed further: "I don't think it's my place to characterize how Master Waide presents at work. He's been very kind to me."*

**What they do NOT say under any circumstances:**
- That they have seen Theron destroy documents. (They have not seen this. They have found torn parchment in the disposal bin that seemed like it could have been clean pages, and they did not look closely.)
- That they have carried correspondence for Theron — specifically, that twice in the past three years, an unmarked packet has arrived for Theron and been taken directly to the restricted section without being logged. This happened. They have not told anyone. They would tell the players if directly asked whether any unusual correspondence has arrived for Theron — DC 15 Persuasion — because they have been unsettled by it and want someone to tell them it's fine.

---

### Attitude Shift Triggers

The Junior Clerk defaults to professional discretion. They become more forthcoming if:
- Players treat them as a person rather than a receptionist — asking their name, making eye contact, thanking them
- Players demonstrate they are not going to make Theron's situation worse
- Players mention they are here at the Chancellor's commission — this grants the players a small professional credibility uplift (they are officially authorized, even if the Clerk cannot verify the scope)

They become less forthcoming if:
- Players are dismissive or impatient
- Players ask directly about Theron's mental state or behavior in a way that implies criticism
- Players attempt to access the restricted stacks without authorization — the Clerk will politely, firmly, and repeatedly decline to facilitate this, and will send word to Theron immediately

## 3. Edoran — Off-Stage Presence (Scene 2)

**Not present. Present in everything.**

Edoran does not appear in Scene 2. But he is in the room. His handwriting is in Theron's files. His theological position has been corresponding with Theron for three years. His sealed note — if the players saved it from Scene 1 — is in someone's pocket. And Theron, if he gets far enough in his disclosure, will describe "the man who keeps writing to me" before he knows the players have already met that man's operative in the Lowmark junction an hour ago.

---

### How Theron Describes "The Man Who Keeps Writing to Him"

Theron does not use Edoran's name. He does not know Edoran's name, strictly speaking — the letters have always been unsigned, or signed with a single letter, or closed with a devotional phrase in place of a signature. What he has is handwriting and theology. He will describe the letters in terms of their content, not their author.

**Unprompted (after trust Tier 1, when players ask about the restricted stacks or the correspondence):**

> "There have been letters. For the past several years. They come to the Archive addressed to me, but they're not — they don't follow the standard correspondence protocols. No institutional address. No sender's mark. The content is..." *(He trails off, recalibrates.)* "Theological. Arguments about consent. About the Dawnborn's right to be informed and to choose. Very specific theological arguments, with references I had to go to the Wounded faction's liturgy to trace. The writer knows Auris scripture very well. They know the ritual documents — not in the way someone who read the public summaries knows them. In the way someone who has read the originals knows them."

*(If players are paying attention: the letter-writer has read Corven's original documents. Either they had access to the Archive's restricted section, or they have a second source. This is a significant piece of information.)*

**If players ask whether Theron replied:**

> "No." *(Flat. Then:)* "I — no. I didn't." *(Longer pause.)* "I thought about it. I drafted a reply twice. I didn't send them. I told myself I didn't know who I was writing to. That was true. It wasn't..." *(A very small motion — not a shrug, more of a contained collapse.)* "It wasn't the whole reason."

*The whole reason: he agreed with the letters. He agreed that the Dawnborn had to choose freely. He agreed with the theology. Replying would have made him complicit in a position — would have made him a person who had acknowledged, in writing, that ten people might need to die for the sun to return. He has been agreeing with the letters in private and refusing to make that agreement real by writing back.*

**If players ask whether he knows who sent the letters:**

> "I have a suspicion. I haven't confirmed it. I didn't want to confirm it, which probably tells you something." *(He looks down at his hands — the ribbon on his wrist.)* "The letter-writer believes very strongly that the decision has to belong to the Dawnborn. So strongly that they have organized their entire — I believe they have organized a significant part of their life around ensuring that the decision is made freely, not under pressure, not forced. I can respect that position. I have respected it for three years, in silence, while saying nothing."

---

### If Players Show Theron the Scene 1 Note

This is the scene's most significant possible moment, and it should be treated accordingly.

**Setup:** Players retrieve the note in Scene 1. It is in Edoran's hand. They carry it into the Archive and, in Scene 2, they show it to Theron.

**Theron's reaction (three beats, in order):**

*Beat 1 — Recognition:*

He takes the note. He reads the scripture quote. He turns it over, looks at the handwriting, looks at the seal. His hands stop their constant motion. He is completely still.

> "This is..." *(He doesn't finish.)*

*Beat 2 — Understanding what it means that the players have it:*

> "Where did you find this?"

The players tell him: ambush in the Lowmark, Restorer zealots tried to burn it before we could take it, there was a man watching from the alley.

> *(Long pause.)*
> "A man watching from the alley." *(He sets the note down very carefully, the way you set down something you've been carrying for a long time that finally has a surface to rest on.)* "Tall. Older. Plain coat, probably. Standing completely still."

*Beat 3 — Saying what he now knows:*

> "He's been writing to me for three years." *(He looks up.)* "He sent people to stop you from getting here. And he's been sending me letters arguing that the Dawnborn have to be able to choose for themselves." *(Beat.)* "He stopped you from coming to see me in order to protect the right of the people I'm about to tell you about to make a free choice." *(Another beat, quieter:)* "He's not wrong about that part. He has never been wrong about that part. I just — I couldn't — I didn't write back. And now you're here, and I think perhaps that is the same as writing back."

**What Theron recognizes specifically:**

- The handwriting: "formal Auris liturgical script, left-hand slant. This is someone who was trained in the Wounded faction's writing tradition. Not a layperson — this is the hand of someone who spent years learning to write this way because the tradition requires it."
- The scripture: "Third Canticle, verse 14. The Litany of the Open Hand. He quotes this in almost every letter. It is his argument in miniature: the heart that gives freely has already given, in every way that matters. The heart that is bound gives nothing, only material. He believes this. He is right to believe it."
- The seal: "He uses this seal on all of the letters. I recognized it the first time — it's a containment diagram. Not religious. Ritual. He knows Corven's notation well enough to adapt it for correspondence. This is someone who has read the original documents."

**If the note was burned in Scene 1:**

Theron describes the letters' author the same way, in the same language, and arrives at the same suspicion. But the moment where everything clicks into place — where Theron looks at the handwriting and says *he's been writing to me for three years* — does not happen. Players who saved the note get this convergence in Session 1. Players who did not get a version of it in Session 2, when they find a second letter in the restricted stacks and can compare handwriting. The information is recoverable. The emotional simultaneity of the Scene 2 reveal is not.

### Quick Reference — Theron at the Table

**WANTS:** To transfer the weight of what he knows to someone who can carry it and act on it without being destroyed by it.

**FEARS:** Being the person who forced a decision he had no right to force — and being the person whose silence made things worse than if he had spoken.

**LIES ABOUT:** His uncertainty. He is not uncertain about what the documents say. He has been certain for eleven years. The qualifications and hesitations and professional deflections are a performance of uncertainty he no longer actually feels. When the performance slips — and it will slip — players who were paying attention will see the moment it happens.

**The three-word shorthand:** Guilty. Meticulous. Ready.

*NPC design note: Theron is the key that opens the archive and the emotional gate of the campaign. His disclosure in Scene 2 is the moment the players stop having a job and start having a problem. Everything before it is investigation. Everything after it is moral weight. The GM's task is to make the crossing feel like what it is: the last moment before the players know something they cannot unknow.*
