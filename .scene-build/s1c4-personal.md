## Per-Archetype Resonance — Scene 4

---

### Soldier / Martial PC

**The difference between service and conscription.**

You have protected civilians before. You have probably also watched commanders send civilians toward outcomes they didn't choose — not with cruelty, but with the arithmetic logic of military necessity. You have learned to tell the difference between someone who is serving willingly and someone whose willingness is being assumed on their behalf.

Sera Voss is the former. She chose. She keeps choosing. Her version of service is as legible to you as anything you have encountered in a command structure — except that her commanding officer is herself, and her orders are her own, and there is no hierarchy above her that made this decision without consulting her.

The thing to watch for: Harran Lecht, when players encounter him, will treat the Dawnborn as a mechanism. He will speak about them with the same reasoned efficiency that a good quartermaster speaks about supply chains. He will not be wrong about the outcomes. He will be wrong about the people. You will recognize that error. You have made versions of it yourself, or watched it made above you. You know what it costs.

**The beat:** Sera's clarity about her own consent — *"I didn't make it once. I keep making it"* — is the answer to the question your character has probably been turning over since you took this investigation: *who consented to what?* This is what genuine consent looks like in a person who has had time to make it real. Hold this. You will need it as a reference point.

**What to ask this player:** *"You know what it looks like when someone has chosen to serve and what it looks like when someone has been pressed into it. What does this look like to you?"*

### Scholar / Investigator PC

**The processing gap.**

She has known for years. You have known for hours, or minutes, or since you walked in the door if this is your first hearing of it. Notice the difference.

She is calmer than you are. Not because she is incurious or unserious about what she is carrying — she is a rigorous person who researched this herself over eight months of careful work. She is calmer because she has already done the processing you are still in the middle of. She has been through the shock, the reckoning, the re-orientation of your entire sense of what your life means. She came out the other side. You are still somewhere in the middle of that terrain.

What this tells you about the timeline for her consent: it was not made in crisis. She did not say yes in the moment of discovery. She made her decision from a settled place, after the settling had finished.

This has methodological implications. If you are going to assess the validity of any of the Dawnborn's consent — not to override it, but to understand it — you need to know how much time passed between the discovery and the answer. Sera's answer came after years of ordinary daily work running the Dawnhall, which means her version of "settling" involved serving forty people dinner every night. That is a specific kind of processing.

**The beat:** The Ashring inscription she cannot read. She is a person who researched her own condition from Archive materials, who understands what she is with precision, and she has been to the site and traced the inscription with her fingers and cannot decode it. This is a gap in the information. It is your job to close it.

**What to ask this player:** *"She found out the same thing you're finding out, from the same sources. She had eight months and came out the other side settled. What do you think the eight months felt like?"*

### Healer / Cleric PC

**Two kinds of sacrifice.**

The Dawnhall is a feat of sustained care. Someone built this from a grain storage room over fifteen years, funded it without institutional support, staffed it, maintains it, and knows the name of every person who has eaten here in the last month. That person also carries the sun inside her and has known for years what that eventually requires.

Your tradition — whatever it is — almost certainly has something to say about sacrifice. The language varies: the willing offering, the gift freely given, the light that returns to its source. There is a shape in religious thinking for what Sera is describing. It is a recognizable shape. It has been given names.

The problem is the specificity. Sera's version of sacrifice is very personal and very exact. It is not the generalized abstraction your tradition probably uses. It is: she will release what she is carrying, at some point, and when the energy leaves, the body cannot sustain itself, and she will be gone. Not transcended. Not transformed. Gone, the way a lantern is gone when the light has been released and the flame extinguishes.

Your theological vocabulary may or may not reach this far. The question is not whether your tradition is wrong — it is whether the frameworks you were given for thinking about meaningful death were built to contain this specific shape.

**The beat:** She serves Auris. The sun god. She is carrying the sun, and she serves the god who represents the sun, and she finds this *complicated*. Not damaging. Not contradictory. Complicated. That is a very specific theological position that she arrived at through fifteen years of daily practice. It is worth asking about.

**What to ask this player:** *"Your character has language for this. Does it fit? And if it doesn't — what does that mean for the language?"*

### Thief / Rogue PC

**The same good faith.**

Everyone in this room trusts her. Watch how she moves: she knows everyone's name. The teenager with the ladle has been here three weeks and she handed him that ladle without a word, without a signal, because she knew he would take it. The man coughing in the far corner came in yesterday — he came to her when he had nowhere else. The child by the south table is in early stage and probably has a chance if the situation changes.

She looked at you the same way she looks at them. She is extending you the same good faith she extends everyone who walks through this door.

You know what it costs to extend good faith. You know what happens when people take it and use it wrong. You have probably been on both sides of that transaction. What you are looking at right now is a person who has extended that good faith forty times tonight plus however many times you represent, and who has apparently sustained this without becoming suspicious or guarded or performatively open in the way of someone who has learned to pretend trust without feeling it.

She actually trusts you. She trusts you the way she trusts everyone who walks in here, which is: she assumes you mean well until you prove otherwise. That assumption is not naive. She has been wrong about it before. She has learned the cost and continued making the same assumption because the alternative is a Dawnhall where nobody feels welcome enough to come in.

**The beat:** She will not be a mark. She is not performing openness. There is nothing under the openness to exploit. People who do this kind of work long enough learn to distinguish between people who are manipulating them and people who are simply receiving care, and she has made that distinction forty times tonight already. You are being read the same way she reads everyone else. This is, in the specific sense of your skillset, humbling.

**What to ask this player:** *"She extended you the same trust she extends everyone in this room. What does your character do with that?"*

### Noble / Diplomat PC

**How she built an institution without one.**

She built this from a grain storage room over fifteen years. No patron, no institutional funding, no formal charter. The building is listed in the district records as a "community nutrition program" on a renewable occupancy permit. She has been renewing it every three years for fifteen years. The district warden has never denied the renewal, possibly because no one has formally objected, possibly because everyone in Lowmark knows who she is and what she does, and the political cost of denying her is higher than the political cost of looking the other way.

She is the institution. There is no board, no governance structure, no mechanism for her succession. If she were gone, the Dawnhall would fold within a month. This is either an extraordinary achievement or an extraordinary vulnerability, and she knows both, and she has built it this way deliberately — because the alternative was a formalized structure that could be captured, redirected, or used by the city as evidence that the Dawnborn issue was "being handled."

Notice how she speaks about the other Dawnborn: careful, precise, utterly respectful of their autonomy, never claiming to know their minds. *"My yes is mine. Theirs is theirs."* That is a particular skill. It is the skill of a person who has been in rooms where someone claimed to speak for many and made a mess of it. She has learned not to do that. It is also, and not coincidentally, the thing that makes her harder to manipulate than she looks — because she will not speak for others, she cannot be used as a lever on them.

**The beat:** How was this funded? The answer is: imperfectly, across multiple small sources, with no single point of failure. She describes it if asked without pride or complaint. She built the funding structure the same way she built the building — because she needed it to work and the existing structures were not going to do it for her.

**What to ask this player:** *"What does it take to build something like this without institutional backing? What has she had to give up to keep it independent?"*

### Restorer-Adjacent / Religious PC

**Two answers to the same question.**

Edoran found out what the Dawnborn must do. He built a theology around it. His faith positions the Dawnborn's deaths as meaningful, necessary, redemptive — the willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot. He has made their deaths into doctrine.

Sera found out the same thing. She kept serving soup.

Both are faith responses to the same revelation. Edoran's version externalizes the meaning — the Dawnborn's deaths are meaningful because the doctrine says they are. Sera's version internalizes it — her life is meaningful because she has built something that matters to forty people tonight, and whatever happens to her will happen alongside all of that, not instead of it.

The distinction is subtle and important. Edoran found an answer and built a movement. Sera found an answer and built a kitchen. Both answers are sincere. They produce very different things in the world.

Notice also: Edoran's theology speaks about the Dawnborn in aggregate — the willing heart, the ten anchors, the restoration of the sun. Sera speaks about herself. Only herself. She will not speak about the other Dawnborn. She will not claim their consent or predict their answers. Edoran speaks about "the Dawnborn" as a category. She refuses the category for anything personal.

Which approach is more honest? Which is more respectful of the ten people involved? These questions do not have answers right now. They will have consequences later.

**The beat:** She finds it complicated that the sun is inside her, and she serves Auris, and she doesn't mind. *"I don't have a complete answer. I keep serving soup."* That is its own kind of theology. It is harder to name than Edoran's. It may be more durable.

**What to ask this player:** *"Edoran built a movement around this truth. Sera built a kitchen. Which version of faith is harder to maintain?"*
