## Opening Read-Aloud — Harran's Column Appears

*Use this text when the column becomes visible on the east road. Deliver it before initiative. After the last sentence, ask: "What do you do?"*

---

You hear them before you see them.

Formation march — not a group of people walking together, something more deliberate than that, a sound with weight behind it. Four beats, evenly spaced, coordinated. Then the torchlight: four lanterns carried at the front, spreading amber light across the east road's flagstones, and behind the light, shapes resolving out of the dark. Grey tabards. Silver-thread badge at the breast — the Reckoning's running sun, catching the torchlight at an angle. Eight soldiers in two columns, two abreast.

At the front, one pace ahead of the rest: a man in his fifties, studded leather, no helmet, one hand loose at his side and the other near the pommel of his longsword in the way that means habit, not threat. He is not hurrying. He walks with the specific economy of someone who has already decided how this goes.

Behind you, at the west arc: Aldric Stone moves one step closer to his Primer Stone. His hand settles on its face. Stone 9's amber core brightens, almost imperceptibly, and then — as the column comes closer — dims again, just slightly, as if the stone noticed something and drew back.

The man at the front stops twenty feet short of the circle's edge. He looks at each of you once, quickly, assessing. Then he looks at Aldric. Then he looks back at you.

> *"The Reckoning holds jurisdiction over the Ashring site under the Emergency Powers charter. Stand aside."*

## The Drag Moment Read-Aloud

*Use this when the Drag Pair breaks off toward Aldric — either Round 1 before they reach him, or at the moment they make their first Drag attempt. Deliver it to create the choice point explicitly.*

---

Two of Harran's Veterans break away from the column.

They do not run. They move with the same economy as their commander, cutting across the circle's interior toward the west arc — toward Stone 9, toward Aldric. One of them has already loosened his sword arm. The other is watching Aldric's hands.

Aldric sees them. He sets his feet. He is looking at them the way a man looks at something he has been expecting and has decided he is not going to step away from.

The rest of Harran's soldiers are moving toward you.

You have seconds, not minutes, and the two Veterans heading for Aldric are going to reach him.

*What do you do?*

## Exit Read-Aloud — Harran's Tactical Retreat

*Use this when Harran's exit condition triggers — half HP or surrounded on three sides. Deliver it slowly. The exit line is the last thing you say before he goes.*

---

Harran takes one step back.

Not a stumble. Not a retreat. A measured step — the foot placed, the weight shifted, the kind of movement that looks like a decision being made in real time. He lowers his longsword to his side without sheathing it. He looks at the player who last hit him, directly, for exactly two seconds.

The Veterans around him — those still standing — read the step immediately. They stop engaging. They pull back two paces, weapons down, watching their commander.

He does not look at Aldric. He does not look at the stone. He looks at you.

> *"When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it."*

*He turns. His Veterans fall in behind him, and they walk back down the east road in the same formation they arrived in. No one runs. The sound of their boots in formation fades steadily.*

*The Ashring is quiet.*

## Chancellor's Letter Read-Aloud — Sera Moth Arrives

*Use this after one beat of quiet following Harran's exit. Let players have a moment first — time enough for one exchange or one silence. Then:*

---

A figure appears on the east road — coming from the city, moving quickly but not running. She wears a plain coat with the Chancellor's administrative badge on the lapel: a pressed silver circle, the civic seal. She is young — mid-twenties, perhaps — and she takes in the state of the Ashring without comment. Whatever she expected to find here, she has decided not to make it her business.

She stops near the circle's edge and looks at the group of you. She holds a sealed letter in both hands.

> *"From Chancellor Ostenveld. She asked me to wait for your response, if you have one."*

*She holds it out.*

---

*When a player opens and reads it:*

The letter is sealed with the Chancellor's personal seal — not the official city mark, the small wax disc she uses for private correspondence. The paper is good quality. The handwriting is compressed, neat, the kind of hand that has written thousands of official documents and carries the economy of that practice into personal letters.

Read aloud:

> *To the investigators I hired:*
>
> *Rationing begins in seven days. The Granary Council has agreed on a structure that should hold through the third month. It will not hold through the sixth. I have done the arithmetic you have not seen — the numbers do not improve.*
>
> *I am sorry it has come to this. I hired you because I hoped you would find something I could use to make it come to something else. I am told you have been thorough.*
>
> *Tell me what you have found. I am ready to hear it.*
>
> *— Chancellor Esveth Ostenveld*

---

*After the letter is read:*

The Ashring is still around you. The Primer Stones glow amber in their circle — eight of them at full brightness, Stone 9 at whatever level the encounter has left it. The torches at the perimeter burn steadily. The east road is empty.

Sera Moth is watching you. She is not impatient. She has been waiting for things that mattered all her adult life. She can wait a little longer.

## Player Engagement Matrix

---

### Action Players

**What they do:** Combat. Positioning. Protecting Aldric by putting themselves between the Drag Pair and Stone 9. Engaging Harran directly.

**What this scene gives them:** A tactically rich encounter with a secondary objective that rewards aggressive positioning rather than just damage output. The player who intercepts the Drag Pair is doing more for the scene than the player who takes 20 damage out of Harran.

**The moment that matters:** When a player physically stands between the Drag Pair and Aldric's stone. That is the scene's action beat. Make it clear this is working — describe the Veterans stopping, shifting, recalculating their approach. Make the player feel the weight they're carrying by being in that lane.

**Risk:** They may focus entirely on Harran and miss the secondary objective. Let this happen if it's going to happen — don't redirect them. The consequence is real and trackable. They will understand in Session 4.

---

### Investigation Players

**What they do:** Watch Harran's approach, read his preparation, notice that he knew about the Inversion inscription.

**What this scene gives them:** A confirmed intelligence leak in their investigation. The knowledge that Varren Aldiss (the Spire administrator who tipped off Harran) exists and has been watching. The possible opportunity to pocket the Emergency Powers charter if the complication fires.

**The moment that matters:** The realisation — not from a roll, from observation — that Harran arrived knowing exactly which stone mattered and which Dawnborn to target. That is not luck. That is intelligence. Someone told him. Ask the investigation player what their character notices and when.

**What to offer:** *"He walked directly to this position, targeting exactly this stone, naming exactly this type of oversight. He was briefed."* DC 13 Insight to confirm the certainty.

---

### Roleplay Players

**What they do:** Engage with Harran directly — challenge his framing, push on his reasoning, look for the crack.

**What this scene gives them:** A well-realised antagonist who will not be broken by roleplay (this is combat, not a negotiation scene) but who will respond to direct engagement with genuine dialogue. The daughter crack. The moment when he goes quiet instead of louder. The exit line as the last word in a conversation that isn't over.

**The moment that matters:** Any exchange with Harran before Tactical Retreat fires. He is not a talker — he will not stop fighting to monologue — but he will respond to one direct question with one honest answer if asked in the right way. *"Why?"* gets: *"Because the clock doesn't care about consent. The city will starve before you finish asking nicely."*

**After the exit line:** Give roleplay players the space to respond to the empty road. They will want to say something. Let them say it to the place where he was standing.

---

### Problem-Solvers

**What they do:** Identify the Drag mechanic as a resource management problem and solve it efficiently.

**What this scene gives them:** A constraint optimisation puzzle: 8 Veterans, 4 players, 1 Aldric, 1 stone. The Drag Pair has a specific trajectory. Intercepting it is a geometry problem. Help action costs an action. Grapple costs more but removes the threat for multiple rounds. Killing the Drag Pair eliminates the mechanic entirely but costs action economy elsewhere.

**The moment that matters:** When they explain their positioning plan to the rest of the table. This is the scene functioning as intended — a player who has understood the secondary objective coordinating others toward it. Honor this. If their plan works, the stone stays lit. If it partially fails, something interesting happened.

**After combat:** They will want to know what dim Stone 9 costs them. Give them s2c5-outcomes.md's Variant C summary — not all of it, just: *"Stone 9 is dim. You'll feel that in Session 4."* Then let them figure out what they need to do about it.
