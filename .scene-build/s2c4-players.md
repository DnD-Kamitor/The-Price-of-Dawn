## Read-Aloud — The EXTINGUISHED Moment

Use this text or adapt it to your voice. Do not summarize it. Deliver it as Theron's direct speech.

---

*Theron has been standing. At some point in this speech he moves to the plain chair and sits. The chair is too loud. Neither of you acknowledges it.*

> "The notation says the completion requires their release. I want to be exact about what that word means. Corven used precise language. He did not write *death*. He wrote *release* — the way you release light from a lantern. You open the lantern. The light goes. The vessel—"

*He stops. He looks at the desk. A moment. Then he looks at you.*

> "The technical term Corven used in the secondary notation, the pages not in that box, is *extinguished*."

---

*Stop here. Let it sit. Do not add anything immediately.*

---

## The Room Description When Theron Finishes Speaking

After the full explanation — after EXTINGUISHED, after the mechanism, after Corven's consent engineering, after the redirection fifty years ago — the room is as it was when the scene began. Nothing physical has changed. The box is still open. The pages are still in players' hands or on the desk. The lamp is still where it was.

What has changed is the weight of those objects. The pages in their hands are the same pages they were holding when Theron started speaking. They now know what the pages say. The box on the desk is the box they opened. It is now the box that contained the evidence of what ten people are carrying.

The room description, for a GM who wants to offer it:

*The study is still. Theron is in the chair across from the desk — not behind it, not presiding, just present. The box sits open, its lid tilted back, the silk lining catching the lamp's light. Corven's pages are in your hands — cool paper, dense ink, a dead man's handwriting that says everything Theron has said and more, in notation you are still only partially able to read. Fragment 2 is on the desk beside the box. The building is empty below you. The Archive holds everything it has ever held. You are holding the thing it was never supposed to release.*

## Engagement Matrix by Player Type

### The Talker

Engages by speaking. Will respond immediately after EXTINGUISHED with either anger, questions, or practical next steps. Let them. Respond as Theron. Then look for the Watcher.

**What they need from this scene:** To be heard. Theron gives them that. He receives whatever they bring.

**Risk:** They fill the silence before it can land for other players. If this happens: when there is a natural pause in their response, slow down. Offer a beat. "Theron waits." The beat creates space for other players to enter.

---

### The Watcher

Engages by processing visibly but not speaking. Will be watching Theron, watching other players, sitting with what was just said. May not speak at all in the scene.

**What they need from this scene:** The silence to be honored. To not be prompted or asked to respond before they're ready.

**What you can do:** When Theron is in his "waiting in silence" mode (response to grief, Path 3), describe him specifically waiting. Not anxiously — calmly. He has been sitting with this for eleven years. He is comfortable in the space where something has just been said. The Watcher player who sees Theron comfortable with silence may find it easier to be in silence themselves.

---

### The Problem-Solver

Goes immediately to "what do we do." This is a valid and useful response that the scene honors.

**What they need from this scene:** A practical next step. Isolde Maren provides this. The transfer method gives them a research thread that goes somewhere.

**Risk:** They move past the emotional weight to the operational response before other players have had time with the moment. To manage this: honor the practical question fully and then check in with Theron's posture. *"Theron gives you the location. He doesn't immediately stand up. He stays in the chair."* That detail invites the other players to notice that something in the room is not ready to move yet, even if the Problem-Solver is.

---

### The Roleplay Driver

Wants to engage with Theron as a person — will ask the harder questions: why didn't you tell someone, what does it feel like to have known this, did you know Sera, do you think she'll say yes. These are the questions this scene is built for.

**What they need from this scene:** Theron to be fully present and honest. He is. He answers every direct question. He does not deflect personal questions — he may give shorter answers than the Roleplay Driver wants, but he gives honest ones.

**The most resonant moments for this player type:** The note-burning revelation. The date (three years from now, going to Sera directly). The line about Sera: *"She will take this better than she should."* The admission that *"I was also protecting myself."*

---

### The Skeptic

Will question Theron's motives. Why did he wait? Why did he arrange the evidence to be findable rather than simply handing it over? Is he using the players?

**What they need from this scene:** Theron not to be defensive. He isn't. He answers skeptical questions with the same directness he answers any other question. The Skeptic who pushes on "you were protecting yourself" gets *"Yes. That also."* The Skeptic who asks "why should we trust you now" gets *"You shouldn't trust me because I asked you to. You should trust what the documents say."*

---

## What Players Do with the Pages in Their Hands

The physical gesture of holding Corven's pages is a mechanic. Players who are holding the pages during Theron's speech are physically connected to the evidence as the revelation lands.

At the end of the scene, no one will have told them to put the pages down. They may still be holding them. They may have set them on the desk. Either is correct.

If a player asks about the pages before leaving: they can take them. Theron does not need to keep them. They have already done what they were sealed to do. If a player asks if they *should* take them: that is a good question that belongs to them, not to Theron.

---

## Handling Silence from Players

If the table goes quiet after EXTINGUISHED — genuinely quiet, not "waiting for the GM to say the next thing" but the quiet of a room that just heard something — do not fill it.

Say, in Theron's voice:

> "Take your time."

He means it. He is the least impatient person in this scene. He has had eleven years to develop patience.

Then wait. You are playing a man who is comfortable in the silence after a hard thing has been said. The players will come to you when they're ready.

That silence is not awkward. It is the scene working.
