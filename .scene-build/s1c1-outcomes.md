## 1. Loot Table

### The Enforcer (if searched or captured)

**Equipment** (well-maintained, non-magical, no markings):
- Leather lamellar vest with reinforced shoulder plates — patrol-grade, the kind worn by former city guardsmen who kept their gear when they left service. The straps have been re-sewn at least twice.
- A short sword in a worn wooden scabbard, edge recently oiled. The pommel has been filed smooth — something that was engraved there has been deliberately removed.
- A pair of iron-link manacles on his belt, key attached. Players notice: these are for taking someone, not fighting someone.

**Personal effects** (2–3 items; select or use all):
- A folded paper with a hand-drawn map of the food queue area, marked with the players' entry point in charcoal. The mark is not labeled. There is a second mark at the Archive's front gate, one block north, also unlabeled.
- A small tin with a damp cloth and dried wax inside — the kind used to waterproof boot seams. The tin is warm from being in his coat pocket. He took care of his feet.
- A cheap sun-disk pin — pressed tin, not cast silver — tucked into his inside breast pocket rather than worn visibly. New. The back is unscratched. He received this recently and hasn't decided whether to display it.

**Coin:** 2d6 sp + 1d4 gp (carried in a belt pouch separate from the tin).

*Nothing names him. Nothing names the Restorers. That is deliberate — he was briefed to carry nothing that could be traced back.*

### Zealots (if searched; 2d6 cp each)

Each zealot carries a sun-disk symbol cut from cloth — a roughly circular scrap with an inward-ray pattern stitched in yellow thread. A DC 12 Religion check identifies this as Auris iconography; the inward-ray variant specifically indicates a sub-sect that emphasizes voluntary suffering over worship.

**Personal effects (one per zealot — all different):**

| Zealot | Personal Effect |
|---|---|
| 1 | A folded letter, unsealed, beginning "Mother — I know you said not to write but I am well and I am doing something right." The letter is unfinished. It was never sent. |
| 2 | A wooden toggle carved in the shape of a fish. One side has been sanded smooth and the letters "M.V." scratched into it with a knife point. Not a Restorer symbol — a person's initials. |
| 3 | A small bundle of dried grey-stem herbs wrapped in oilcloth. The herbs are used to reduce grey sickness fever symptoms. Someone in his family is sick. |
| 4 | Nothing except a receipt from a Lowmark bread registry, folded four times and softened with handling. He has been in this queue before. He came back to stop it, not to rob it. |

### The Sealed Note — Complete Prop

**Physical description:** A single sheet of thin paper, folded in thirds and sealed with dark amber wax. The seal is not decorative — it is a containment diagram, a small ritual circle pressed with a stamp or ring. A DC 14 Arcana check identifies it as a binding-type circle, used for ritual correspondence requiring privacy; not common, not trivial. The paper itself is good quality, linen-fiber stock — not cheap. Whoever wrote this did not improvise it.

**Text of the note (as players read it):**

> *"The willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot."*
>
> *You know this. I have said it since before you came to us.*
>
> *The investigators are moving. They will reach the Archive by nightfall and they will find what Corven left there — not everything, but enough to ask the right questions. After that, the questions become the danger, not the documents.*
>
> *Do not let them reach the second floor. A warning is sufficient. Harm is not required and will not be sanctioned. If they are the kind of people I think they are, being warned will only make them more certain. That is acceptable. Certainty will slow them.*
>
> *The work continues. The anchor holds.*
>
> *Do not write back.*"

**What players learn from the note at each check level:**

| Check | Information |
|---|---|
| No check (obvious) | The note was written to warn someone off the investigation — specifically this investigation, by someone who knew the players were coming and roughly when. It was not improvised. |
| Religion DC 12 | The opening line is a direct quote from the *Litany of the Open Hand*, a lesser-known Auris devotional text concerned with the ethics of sacrifice. Using it as a salutation is a marker of deep familiarity — this person doesn't quote scripture; they think in it. |
| Investigation DC 14 | The handwriting is controlled but shows small inconsistencies in letter pressure — someone writing quickly while managing strong feeling. The phrase "the kind of people I think they are" has been crossed out once and rewritten identically. The writer hesitated there. The phrase "harm is not required and will not be sanctioned" is underlined with a single stroke — the writer wanted to be sure that line was read. |
| History DC 14 | The phrase "the anchor holds" does not appear in standard Auris devotional literature. It appears once, in a 40-year-old academic paper on Corven's ritual notation, as a technical term — an "anchor" is a sympathetic ritual component that remains stable after the primary rite concludes. The author of this note knows ritual theory, not just theology. |

## 2. Information Gained Per Success Level

### Full Success — note retrieved + Edoran reached + Enforcer examined

- Someone with ritual knowledge and theological depth organized this ambush specifically around the players' investigation, not the investigation in general.
- The Enforcer is not a true believer. He is a professional who received his Auris pin recently and carries it hidden. He was hired, not recruited.
- Edoran is present, watching, and chose not to fight. The words he leaves them — *"You have no idea what you are looking for. When you find it, you will understand why I did not want you to"* — are not a threat. They are a warning from someone who already found it.
- The note's author knows about "the second floor" of the Archive and what Corven left there. This means the author has either been to the Archive or has sources inside it.
- "The anchor holds" — a ritual term. Whatever the Ritual of Eternal Dawn created, some component of it is still active. Forty years later.
- The Restorers had the players' route before they left the Chancellor's office. Either the Chancellor's office has a leak, or someone was watching them from the moment they entered the city.

### Partial Success — fought well, note retrieved, Edoran not reached

- Someone with theological and ritual knowledge organized this, and they were briefed on the players' movements.
- The Enforcer is hired muscle, not a convert.
- "The anchor holds" — a technical phrase the players cannot yet place. It will matter later.
- Edoran was in the alley. They know someone was watching. They do not have his words.
- The ambush was a warning, not an assassination attempt. Whoever sent it wants them stopped, not dead — which implies stopping them is sufficient, which implies the situation can still be stopped.

### Minimal Success — fought, no note, no Edoran contact

- Someone organized this ambush specifically against this investigation. It was not opportunistic.
- The Enforcer is former city guard, professional, says nothing. The Restorers have resources and discipline.
- The sun-disk symbols on the zealots identify them as Auris-aligned. That's all.
- Edoran was there. If any player scanned the alley mouth, they saw him — a still figure who did not move during the fight and was gone when it ended.

### Failure — party forced to retreat or yield

The zealots do not pursue beyond the junction. The Enforcer watches them go.

What the players observe from retreat: the group does not loot the abandoned area, does not disperse immediately, and does not celebrate. The Enforcer says something brief to the remaining zealots, and they move off in two directions, not one. Organized withdrawal, not victory.

**Information they still gain from the experience itself:**
- This was a coordinated warning by a group with advance intelligence on the players' movements. The people who sent them are not improvising.
- The zealots were not trying to kill anyone. They were trying to stop, not destroy.
- The Enforcer looked at the players once — directly, assessing — before the group moved. He was measuring them. For a future encounter.

### Sera Voss: How She Hears About It

Information moves through Lowmark on three channels: the food queue (people talk while they wait), the amber-workshop runners who carry messages between district hubs, and the Dawnhall volunteers who live throughout the district and compare notes at shift change. The ambush at the junction happened in front of fifty people in the food queue. By the time the players reach the Dawnhall three hours later, Sera has heard two accounts — one from a queue volunteer, one from a runner — that agree on the broad shape of it.

**What she says when the players walk in (Scene 4 opening, variant lines by outcome):**

- *Full success / note retrieved:* She looks at them for a moment, then: "Someone told me the group at the junction grabbed a piece of paper out of a lantern flame before they finished the fight. I've been trying to figure out what's worth that kind of multitasking." She does not ask what was in the note. Yet.
- *Partial success / fought well:* "I heard there was trouble at the junction this afternoon. Queue's still half-scattered." A pause. "You're all here, so I assume it went your way." She wipes her hands on her apron and gives them her full attention.
- *Minimal success / survived:* She looks at them longer than is comfortable. "You look like Lowmark tried to teach you something. Did it?" Not unkind. Diagnostic.

---

### Chancellor Ostenveld: What Her Composure Slipping Looks Like

She does not raise her voice. She does not move from her chair. What changes: she stops completing sentences at their normal pace. She begins one sentence — "If they knew your route before you left my —" — and stops it. She laces her fingers on the desk surface and looks at them, not through them, for approximately three seconds. Then she says: "Tell me everything. In sequence. Leave nothing out."

The moment of stillness is the tell. She has been in this office for eleven years and she calculates before she speaks. The three seconds in which she did not calculate is the thing players remember.

### The Enforcer (If Captured): Edoran Learns by Session 3

Edoran has other contacts in Lowmark. He knows the Enforcer was taken within 24 hours. By Session 3, he has arranged for the Enforcer's release through intermediaries — not through direct action — and has received a full debrief.

**Consequence note:** If the players treated the Enforcer with any form of dignity — offered water, didn't intimidate him, asked his name — Edoran's approach in Session 3 shifts from "obstacle to be managed" toward "people who might listen." If they were harsh: Edoran sends a second intermediary rather than making contact himself. He is not unwilling to talk. He is unwilling to be hurt again.

## 4. Failure Track — Interesting Failures

### A. Enforcer Escapes Clean + Note Burns

**What this means for Sessions 3–5:**

Edoran receives a complete operational picture: how the players fight, what they sacrificed (nothing) to save information (nothing), and that they are effective combatants who do not distinguish between intelligence and targets. He recalibrates.

In Session 3, he does not send the Enforcer again. He changes the approach entirely:

- He stops using intermediaries who can be identified and searched. Future warnings arrive as written messages slipped under a door or left on a table — unsigned, in the Auris-quoting style the players have now lost the key to recognizing.
- He makes direct contact in Session 4 instead of Session 3, in a setting where combat is not possible (a crowded location, a space with a civilian hostage dynamic already in place from the scene structure). The delay costs the players one session of potential dialogue.
- In Session 5, if the players have not yet spoken to him: he is not present at the ritual location. He has already made his choice, alone, without the conversation that might have changed it.

**GM note:** This is not a punishment path. It is a lonelier one. The campaign's most interesting version of Edoran is the one who is still reachable. Each failure in these early scenes reduces the window.

---

### B. A Player Is Grappled and Used as Brief Hostage

**What the Enforcer demands:** He doesn't make a speech. He says two things: "Everyone stops moving," and, after a pause, "Walk away." He is not asking for information, payment, or surrender — just for the investigation to pause long enough for his people to withdraw. He will not hurt the hostage; that was not in his brief, and he knows it. But he is good at making the situation feel like he might.

**How it resolves:** The Enforcer releases the hostage when the players de-escalate or when one of the other zealots signals clear. If players push the confrontation anyway, he releases the hostage and retreats — he was never going to follow through, and a professional knows when the leverage has failed.

**What players remember:** Being on that side of the leverage is different from being the party that applies it. The scene is designed so players feel the weight of the gap between "I could hurt you" and "I am choosing not to." That gap is the emotional center of the campaign. Edoran is living in it. Now one player knows what it feels like.

**Optional carry-forward:** In Session 3 or 4, if that player faces a scene where they could use leverage against someone weaker, ask them privately: "You remember the junction. What does your character do?" Do not frame it as a mechanical choice. Let it be a character choice.

---

### C. Players Kill a Zealot (Who Is a Civilian)

**City Watch inquiry:** A junior Watch sergeant arrives at the Archive gate the following morning — not at the Dawnhall, not at the Chancellor's office. At the Archive, where the players are most likely to be identified as investigators on official business. He is not hostile. He is methodical.

**Brief scene: the Watch interview**

The sergeant is mid-thirties, ink-stained cuff, the quiet manner of someone who has been doing this long enough to stop being angry about it. He asks, in order: were you present at the junction on the afternoon of [date]; were you in contact with the deceased; what was the nature of the altercation; did you have authorization to use force in Lowmark district. He takes notes. He does not editorialize.

He mentions, before leaving: "The deceased was in the food queue registry. Eleven years. Gave three times a month at the distribution point." A pause. "That doesn't change what happened. It's just what I know about him."

The inquiry does not result in charges unless the players were egregiously violent or lied to the Watch. It does result in a file. That file exists.

**How Theron Waide reacts when players mention it at the Archive:**

He is quiet for a moment. Then: "Eleven years in the registry. That's someone who came here because the city was in trouble and thought showing up was worth something." Another pause. "I'm not telling you what to feel about it. I'm telling you what I know: the people who follow Edoran are not soldiers. Most of them have someone sick at home."

He does not say the players were wrong. He says it clearly enough that they feel the weight without him having to say it. This is intentional. The campaign does not adjudicate moral choices; it witnesses them.

---

### D. The Party Retreats from the Ambush Entirely

**Where they go:** The most immediate refuge in Lowmark is the Dawnhall — it is a public space, staffed, and the Restorers will not force entry there. The players arrive early and in disorder rather than on their own terms.

**What they learn later:** By the time they reach the Archive (Scene 2), they have no note, no contact with Edoran, and no read on the Enforcer. What they do have: the shape of what it felt like to be the targets of organized opposition that knew their route. That sensation — of being anticipated — is itself information about what they are dealing with.

**What they miss:** The note. Edoran's words. The window to examine the Enforcer. The Religion and Investigation checks that would have told them they are dealing with someone who thinks in ritual theory, not just theology. They arrive at the Archive slightly more in the dark than the scene intended.

**How the GM recovers the story:**

The note's content can surface through a second path. When players reach the Archive and speak with Theron Waide (Scene 2, following the door puzzle), he has been tracking the Restorers' activity around the Archive for months. He has intercepted a previous note — same handwriting, same style, same ritual-circle seal. He did not know what to do with it and filed it. If players describe the ambush, he retrieves the file. The content is not identical — earlier correspondence — but the voice is the same, and the phrase "the anchor holds" appears there too.

The story does not close. It reroutes.

Gestalt Level 3 (two full class progressions, ~35 HP, AC 15–18) vs. 4 Cultists + 1 Veteran in an urban crowd environment:

- Light engagement (Enforcer yields early, note retrieved without scramble): 15–35 HP total across party (3–7 HP average per character).
- Moderate engagement (full fight to half-HP threshold, note burn contested): 40–70 HP total (8–14 HP average per character).

- Light: 0 (party waits for a rest opportunity)
- Moderate: 1–2 across party
- Heavy: 3–4 across party; one character may need to use a hit die before reaching Scene 2

Standard engagement leaves the party at roughly 70–85% HP, 1–2 level-1 slots spent, no level-2 or higher slots expended, and carrying one piece of significant intelligence (the note) or the memory of a deliberate intelligence loss (if the note burned). They are alert and slightly wrong-footed — they expected a city investigation, not a coordinated ambush fifteen minutes into it. The Archive door puzzle will feel harder because they are not fully settled. That dissonance is intentional.

## 6. Post-Scene GM Checklist

Eight items to confirm before moving to Scene 2:

- [ ] **Note outcome recorded.** Did the players retrieve it? Did it burn? Did it burn and they smothered it (partial text)? Write it on your session sheet now — this changes Theron's opening and Edoran's Session 3 approach.
- [ ] **Edoran contact noted.** Did a player reach the alley and receive his words? If yes: which player, and how did they react? That player gets the Session 3 direct-contact window.
- [ ] **Enforcer status confirmed.** Escaped / captured / still at scene. If captured: where is he now? Who is holding him? The Watch will ask.
- [ ] **Civilian casualty check.** Were any civilians injured or killed by player actions (AoE, stray attacks, crowd panic)? If two or more: flag the Watch inquiry scene for Session 2 opening.
- [ ] **Relationship table updated.** Mark Edoran's calibration level, note what Sera will have heard, note whether the Enforcer received dignity or harsh treatment.
- [ ] **Chancellor notification decision.** Did players report back to her before heading to the Archive? If yes: did her composure slip visibly? Did any player push on that moment? Note what they asked.
- [ ] **Secondary objective XP awarded.** Add the relevant bonuses to your XP tracker per character. Do not announce the numbers at the table — tell them total XP at session end.
- [ ] **"What they remember" check.** Before Scene 2 begins: pause. Ask yourself — did the peak moment land? Was there a beat where a player stopped talking and just reacted? If not, plant one seed in the Scene 2 transition narration: reference something specific from the junction (a detail from the note, the Enforcer's hands going up, the smell of singed paper) to carry the scene's emotional register forward.

## 7. VTT Setup Notes

**Recommended map:** Lowmark district junction — a T-intersection or four-way cross with a market stall cluster at the east end and a distribution cart at the north. Minimum usable dimensions: 30 ft × 40 ft playable area, with an additional 10 ft alley mouth visible at the east edge for Edoran's position. The map does not need to be large. The scene is about compression — exits closing, crowd pressure from the north, alley too far to reach without commitment.

**Key terrain features required:**
- Food queue crowd zone (north edge, 20 ft × 40 ft, marked as difficult terrain)
- Market crates (east end, half-cover objects, one adjacent to a light source)
- Lantern flame (east crate cluster — the note-burning hazard; mark as a designated hazard zone if your VTT supports interactive lighting or token-linked triggers)
- At least one alley mouth visible at frame edge (east or south — confirm against your map before play; Edoran's position is 30 ft from the nearest player starting position)

**Token setup:**
- 5 PC tokens (standard)
- 4 Zealot tokens (Cultist stat block, sun-disk iconography if your VTT supports custom art)
- 1 Enforcer token (Veteran stat block, visually distinct — heavier build, no faction markings on his token)
- 1 Edoran token: greyed out, semi-transparent, placed in the east alley mouth at 30 ft from the junction center. Do not label it "Edoran." Label it "Hooded Figure" or leave it unlabeled. It should be visible but ambiguous — players can see someone is there; they do not know who.

**Lighting:**
Amber ambient wash across the full map — not radial torchlight from point sources. The sourceless quality of Varenhold's light is a campaign visual signature; use your VTT's global ambient tint set to warm amber (hex approximately #C8860A at 40–50% opacity over the base map). Avoid sharp shadow cones from the lantern. The lantern flame itself can be marked as a hazard object without generating a radial light effect. Nothing in Varenhold casts a sharp shadow.

**Ambient audio:**
Queue the "Lowmark" track before the scene opens: market murmur, slow water (drain or fountain nearby), occasional cough from the queue crowd, distant low drums from the Ashring district three blocks east. This track runs until the ambush triggers (Round 1 initiative). At Round 1, shift to a lower-volume version or a "tension" variant — the crowd sounds continue but the market murmur drops out. Silence the track entirely when the Enforcer goes still.

**Camera moment:**
When the Enforcer reaches half HP (or is grappled and restrained) and the scene ends — do not narrate immediately. He goes still. His hands come up, slowly, palms out. Hold for two to three seconds of table silence before you describe what the players see. The silence is the scene's last beat. Do not rush past it. Players will remember the pause longer than they remember what you said next.

**Dynamic lighting note:**
If your VTT supports it: mark the lantern-adjacent crate as a hazard zone linked to the note-burning action. When the zealot crouches toward it in Round 2, briefly expand the "lit" zone around the crate by 5 ft to make the flame visible on the map. Collapse it again once the action resolves. This is texture, not a mechanical trigger — but it focuses player attention on exactly the right square at exactly the right moment.

## 8. "What They Remember" Test

**The sentence players are still quoting after the session:**

*"The one with the note just crouched down and held it to the flame like he had all the time in the world."*

**Where it lives:** Round 2, Initiative Count 20 (start of round), before the zealot's turn resolves. The GM delivers it flat, without rising inflection, mid-combat — not as a dramatic announcement but as a factual update. The player who was about to attack the Enforcer now has to decide, in the next two seconds of real time, whether to change their action.

**If it is not landing strong enough:**

The failure mode is narrating it too dramatically — giving it weight with your voice before the players have decided what it means. Deliver it dry. "The zealot nearest the market crate crouches without breaking eye contact with you — and holds the folded paper to the lantern flame." Then wait. The drama comes from the players' reaction, not the narration's tone.

If the table is not engaging with the note as significant (it wasn't planted clearly enough in the scene-opening description), insert this line before initiative is rolled: "You notice, before anything moves, that the lead figure has a sealed paper cylinder tucked visibly in his belt pouch. It catches the lantern light — the wax seal is deep amber, not the standard brown." Plant the object as a question before it becomes a crisis. The burning is only a crisis if the players already care what the note says.

The moment works when a player says "wait, I'm doing that instead" and the table goes slightly quiet. That pause is the scene working correctly.
