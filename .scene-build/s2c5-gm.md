## Pacing

This scene runs 3-5 combat rounds. Do not drag it.

The session has already done its emotional heavy lifting in Scene 4. Players are carrying Theron's confession, the word EXTINGUISHED, and the weight of knowing what the Dawnborn don't know. Scene 5 does not need to compound that weight with a long, grinding combat. It needs to land one clear statement: the Reckoning is here, it has force and jurisdiction and will, and it is going to be a problem for every decision players make going forward.

Three rounds of tight, tense combat with a clean exit achieves this. Five rounds of attrition does not add anything the scene needs.

**Your pacing anchors:**

- Round 1: Harran arrives, negotiation fails, combat begins. The Drag Pair moves.
- Round 2: First Drag attempt on Aldric. Players feel the secondary objective.
- Round 3: Inspiring Shout if triggered. Tipping point visible. Players know which way this is going.
- Round 4-5: Exit conditions fire. Harran retreats. Exit line lands.

If you're past Round 5 and Harran hasn't triggered his exit, check: is he at half HP? Are three sides of his position covered? Force the trigger if the math is there. He exits at the right time, not when it becomes mechanically convenient.

---

## The Drag Mechanic Is the Engine

If players ignore Aldric and focus exclusively on Harran: the Drag Pair succeeds. Aldric gets pulled. Stone 9 dims.

This is not a failure state for the GM. This is the scene teaching players that fighting Harran is not the point. They will learn it by losing the secondary objective once — and that loss will mean something in Session 4 when Stone 9 is still dim.

**Make the choice visible every round.** Do not let players make the decision to focus on Harran without knowing the consequence of that choice in real time.

How to do this:

*When the Drag Pair moves, say:* "Two of Harran's Veterans break away from the group and angle toward the west arc — toward Aldric's stone."

*When the Drag Pair reaches Aldric, say:* "One of them grabs Aldric's shoulder. You can see what's about to happen."

*When Drag succeeds, say:* "Aldric is pulled backward — one, two steps, clear of the stone. Stone 9 dims. Not slowly. Immediately."

*When players block the Drag Pair, say:* "The Veterans stop. They look at you. One of them has a hand on Aldric's shoulder but can't complete the pull while you're standing there."

The secondary objective tracker in s2c5-combat.md gives you the round-by-round state. Keep it in front of you.

## Harran's Exit Line Is Mandatory

Do not let Harran go down fighting.

Harran exiting in good order with his exit line intact is structurally necessary. He is not the final antagonist. He is the first face of the institutional force that will be present across Sessions 3-5. A Harran who dies here cannot appear in Session 3. A Harran who exits with his line intact is someone players will remember — and dread — the next time they hear his name.

*"When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it."*

He says this at normal speaking volume. Not dramatic. Not sneering. He is actually asking. He does not know the answer yet. He will be asking this question again in different words for the rest of the campaign.

After the line: he turns. His Veterans fall in behind him. They walk back down the east road in the same formation they arrived in. Let the sound of their boots fade.

**What to do if players chase them:** The Perimeter Pair at the east road entrance is still fresh. Harran's Tactical Retreat means the whole group disengages — players cannot simply follow without choosing to open a new engagement. If players pursue, the Veterans hold the road entrance and the pursuit becomes a chase, not a combat extension. Harran himself does not look back.

## After Combat: One Beat of Quiet Before Sera Moth

After Harran's column clears the perimeter: stop.

Do not immediately introduce Sera Moth. Do not summarize the encounter. Do not move to outcomes.

Ask: *"What do you do?"*

Let players respond. Let Aldric sit back against his stone. Let someone check if anyone is hurt. Let the silence of the Ashring come back in.

The adrenaline of combat needs exactly one beat — ten seconds at the table, thirty seconds in the fiction — before the Chancellor's letter arrives. If you bring Sera in too quickly, the letter lands in the combat adrenaline and bounces off. If you give it one beat of quiet first, it lands in the space after, which is where gut-punches work.

*"While you are still standing in the circle, a figure appears on the east road — coming from the city, not following Harran's group."*

That is enough. Then Sera Moth arrives.

## The Chancellor's Letter Is the Session's Gut-Punch

Players have spent Session 2 building knowledge. Scene 1: the documents. Scene 2: the riddle. Scene 3: the resonance box. Scene 4: Theron's confession. They know more than anyone in Varenhold now.

The Chancellor's letter says: the clock is real. Seven days to rationing. Six months to collapse. This is not new information — Harran just said six months in his exit line. But a formal letter from the person who hired them, saying *I am sorry it has come to this*, is different from a tactical antagonist making the same claim.

The Chancellor trusted them. She hired them to find the truth. She is now telling them she needed that truth because there is no time left for anything else.

**What players are now holding at session's end:**

1. The truth (the Dawnborn carry the sun, the ritual requires their death or free consent)
2. Theron's eleven years of silence
3. Harran's threat and his exit question
4. A seven-day clock to rationing, six months to collapse
5. The knowledge that the Chancellor doesn't yet know what they know

They leave Session 2 with more weight than they started with. That is the correct ending state.

## Secondary Objective Scoring

Track this. Players will feel the consequences in Session 4, and the tracking should be clean.

**Aldric stayed on Stone 9 all combat (Full Success):**
Stone 9 is fully preserved. The Inversion option remains at 10/10. Players know there is a Spire leak (Harran knew about the Inversion inscription). Full details in s2c5-outcomes.md, Variant A.

**Aldric pulled off but players recovered him (Partial Success):**
Stone 9 flickered. Aldric returned. The stone re-lit. But Aldric was physically removed from the thing he has been guarding, and that is in his body now. In Session 4, before he will stand at his stone without hesitation, he needs acknowledgment. He won't ask for it. He'll be waiting for it. Players who pay attention will notice something is different about him. Full details in s2c5-outcomes.md, Variant B.

**Aldric off stone, not recovered (Failure):**
Stone 9 is dim at session end. Inversion now requires 9/10 minimum. This shifts the campaign toward Ending A territory unless addressed directly. Full details in s2c5-outcomes.md, Variant C.

## Reading the Room After Scene 4

Players arriving at Scene 5 have just heard Theron's confession. Depending on how Scene 4 landed:

**If players are carrying grief:** The combat may feel jarring — they were in an emotional space and now someone is trying to drag an old man off a stone. Use this. Grief and adrenaline together create a specific kind of anger. Let it fuel the encounter.

**If players are cold and practical:** They will immediately understand the strategic geometry of the Drag mechanic. They will position to defend Aldric without being told. Honor this — a practical party that executes the secondary objective cleanly still gets the scene's emotional payload in the Chancellor's letter afterward.

**If players are numb:** Some tables will arrive at Scene 5 still processing Scene 4 and not fully present for the combat. Read the room. If the combat is going through the motions because players are emotionally somewhere else, compress it. Two rounds, Harran exits, letter arrives. The letter is the thing anyway.

---

## What Harran Does After He Leaves

He walks back to the Reckoning's administrative headquarters in the Spire Quarter. He files a formal report on the encounter — that jurisdiction was contested by investigators, that the Ashring remains under unauthorized occupation, that Stone 9 (if not secured) requires follow-up action. He does not sleep unusually. He thinks about the players' faces. He does not know yet what to do with what he saw. He writes a note to Aldiss: *"They are faster than I expected. Accelerate the secondary timeline."*

Players will not know this. They will find it later.
