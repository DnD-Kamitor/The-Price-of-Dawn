## Theron Relationship States (Three Variants)

### Variant A — Damaged but Operative

**Conditions:** Players expressed anger and did not release it during the scene. They left with the information but without resolution.

**State:** Theron understands this is the correct response to what he did. He does not pursue reconciliation. He remains available at the Archive. If players return with questions, he answers them. If players need Archive access in Sessions 3-5, he provides it, because he owes them the resources he has. He does not expect warmth and does not perform remorse to earn it.

**Downstream:** Players may find it harder to ask follow-up questions — not because Theron withholds but because the relationship has friction. This is trackable. If a player makes a point of repairing the relationship in a later session, it repairs.

---

### Variant B — Honest but Distant

**Conditions:** Players were cold and practical. They received the information, took Isolde's location, and left with purpose. No strong emotional response either way.

**State:** Theron respects this. He reads it as people who know how to carry hard information and keep moving. He is available. He is not close. If players come back to the Archive in later sessions, he is already working on something they will find useful, because he has been thinking about what they might need next.

**Downstream:** Theron is a resource, not a relationship. That is fine. He functions as a resource very well.

---

### Variant C — Genuine Connection

**Conditions:** Players engaged with Theron as a person. They asked the harder questions. They named specific Dawnborn. They received his honesty without immediately trying to solve or dismiss it. Some version of the silence after EXTINGUISHED was honored.

**State:** Theron's regard for this party is at its highest. He is fully cooperative in all future scenes. In Session 3, without being asked, he has pulled the documentation on Keseph Vyne's Archive access revocations and left it where players will find it. In Session 4, if players are at a decision point with the Dawnborn, he sends word: *"There is something in Corven's journal you have not yet decoded. Come to the Archive."* He means the consent passages. He found them with Fragment 1+2 after the players left.

**Downstream:** Theron becomes a quiet supporting force across Sessions 3-5. Not active in combat or confrontation — but always having pulled the relevant file before players needed to ask.

## Forward Hooks to Session 3

The scene opens these threads, each of which becomes active in Session 3:

**Thread 1 — Isolde Maren**
If players obtained her location, they can go to Weavers' Terrace at any point in Session 3. She has been expecting someone — Theron warned her, indirectly, that investigators were in the Archive. She does not know who. She knows someone is coming.

**Thread 2 — The Dawnborn Conversations**
Every conversation players have with a Dawnborn in Session 3 is now different. They know what these people are. They know what the truth is. They have not been told to tell the Dawnborn. That is their decision. Session 3 is built around what it means to have a conversation with someone while holding a thing about them you have not said.

**Thread 3 — Fragment 2 + Fragment 1**
With both fragments, players can decode 60% of Corven's journal. Session 3's archive scene should surface the remaining 40% (or access to Fragment 3) as a meaningful research beat.

**Thread 4 — Keseph Vyne**
Players now have his name as the person who revoked Isolde's access. In Session 3, his interference becomes directly felt. Having his name is a small advantage that compounds.

**Thread 5 — The Decision Not Yet Made**
Players leave Session 2 knowing everything except what to do about it. That is the correct state. The question — *do you have the right to decide someone else's fate, even to save thousands?* — is now personal, because they know whose fate is being decided. Session 3 begins with that question already in the room.
