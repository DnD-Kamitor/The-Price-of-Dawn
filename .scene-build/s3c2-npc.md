# S3C2 — Isolde Maren: NPC Profile

<details><summary>👤 Isolde Maren — Full OGAS Profile</summary>

**Occupation:** Independent researcher. Former Spire-affiliated research scientist, Amber Compact institutional review board (suspended three years ago). Currently: self-funded, rented workroom, Ashring district.
**Age:** Forty-one
**Appearance:** Medium height, angular features, hair pulled back with the absent-minded practicality of someone who stopped thinking about hair years ago. Her hands are stained at the fingertips — not ink, not soot, something faintly amber-tinted that doesn't wash off completely. She is wearing a work apron over her clothes because she has been at the apparatus since before dawn and she is always at the apparatus.

**O — Objective:** Get her Transfer Method tested under controlled conditions with institutional support. She has the design. She has the apparatus. She has one empirical result. She does not have access to a Dawnborn, a proper lab, or anyone in the Amber Compact who will take her calls.

**G — Goal (scene-level):** Determine whether the people at her door are worth the risk of letting them in. She has already solved this by the time the lock fires — she was watching through the door slit — but she wants to see how they handle the first minute after the door opens.

**A — Attitude:** Precise, dry, measuring. She has been alone with her work for three years. She has had no professional peers in that time — no colleagues to validate findings, no co-author to check her reasoning, no institutional review to force her to articulate her assumptions out loud. She is very good at her work. She is slightly strange from the isolation. She does not notice the strangeness.

**S — Secret:** She tested the Transfer Method on a mouse. The mouse survived. It is not the same mouse. She has been looking at it for six months trying to decide what "not the same mouse" means for the Dawnborn, and she has not arrived at an answer she can defend.

---

**Want:** For someone with institutional authority — or the credibility to compel someone with institutional authority — to look at her data instead of her credentials. Her credentials were revoked by the same institutional review process that buried her research. She cannot prove to the Spire that she is a legitimate researcher using the Spire's credentialing system, because the Spire's credentialing system is controlled by the people who revoked her credentials. She needs an end-run. She does not know how to ask for that without sounding like she knows she needs an end-run.

**Fear:** That the Transfer Method works — but with a casualty rate she cannot predict empirically from one data point. Her theoretical models put mortality risk at fifteen to twenty percent. The mouse survived. The mouse is not the same mouse. She has been running the numbers on what "not the same" means and the numbers do not reassure her. If the method works with a fifteen-percent mortality rate and some form of permanent alteration, she needs to know that before anyone offers it to the Dawnborn as a solution.

**Lie:** *"The mortality risk is theoretical."* It is theoretical. The mouse is also real evidence and she has been staring at the mouse for six months trying to decide what it means. She says "theoretical" because she does not yet know how to weight empirical data with a sample size of one. This is methodologically correct. It is also how she avoids saying: *"I don't know. I am frightened. I have been alone with this for six months and I cannot tell you if it's safe."*

---

**Her Tell:** When she is uncertain about something, she puts the apparatus in the passive voice.

She says: *"The apparatus was calibrated at 0.4 microstave intervals"* — not *"I calibrated it."* She says: *"The resonance field was stabilized"* — not *"I stabilized it."* When she is confident, she uses the first person without thinking. When she is uncertain, the passive voice appears and she does not notice that it does.

The mouse: specimen jar on the second shelf from the left, end position. Clear glass, wire-mesh lid, appropriate substrate. The mouse is alive. It is grey, medium-sized, unremarkable in appearance. The way it moves is not unremarkable. It does not move like a mouse moves. It stands at the edge of the jar and looks at things with both eyes at once, deliberately, instead of the lateral, scanning movement of normal mouse vision. It is not aggressive. It is not distressed. It is something that has been somewhere and come back, and it knows it.

She has not named it. The jar is positioned at the left edge of her desk, angled slightly toward her work surface. She can see it from anywhere in the room.

---

**Dialogue — Isolde's Lines:**

*Opening — door swings open, she is already there:*
> *"You took longer on the stairs than I expected."*
> *(She steps back. Not welcoming — assessing. She has been watching through the door slit.)*

*If players look surprised that she was watching:*
> *"There's a quarter-inch gap at the base. I can see feet. Yours were slow on the second flight."*

*On why she didn't open the door herself:*
> *"The lock isn't decorative. I've had three people try to reach this room in the last six months. Two of them were from the Compact. The third one I'm still not sure about."*

*On the riddle:*
> *"I've tested three scientists before you. None of them sang. Two picked at the lock. One tried to argue that 'a tone' was too simple and that I must mean something else."* *(dry)* *"He was standing there for forty minutes."*

*If players ask about the Transfer Method:*
> *"Sit somewhere. Not on the apparatus. The blue stool. I'll show you the sequence."*

*On the mouse — first mention (when players notice the jar):*
> *"That's the Transfer Method's only empirical result."*

*On the mouse — what happened to it:*
> *"It survived. It's not the same mouse."*

*If pressed on what "not the same" means:*
> *"It moves differently. It looks differently — at things, I mean, not its appearance. I've been trying to tell you that for three years. I can't tell you what it means yet. I have one data point."*

*On Keseph Vyne's suppression of her research:*
> *"He pulled my institutional access eighteen months into the project. Called it a resource allocation decision. The review board filed it as an inconclusive methodology dispute."* *(pause)* *"The methodology wasn't in dispute. The conclusion was. There's a difference."*

*If players ask what the conclusion was:*
> *"That the Transfer Method is viable. That it carries risk I cannot yet quantify. That it should be tested under controlled conditions before it is offered to the Dawnborn as an option."* *(flat)* *"Keseph decided that 'risk I cannot yet quantify' meant 'no.'"*

*On whether the method is ready:*
> *"The apparatus was calibrated last week."* *(passive voice — she notices, almost corrects herself, doesn't)* *"I've been running resonance stability checks for three months. The theoretical framework is complete. What it needs is a subject with lux-bearing capacity and an institutional framework that won't shut it down after the first test."*

*If players offer to help get her access to a Dawnborn:*
> *"I need to know you understand what you'd be offering them. This is not a cure. This is a method. The method has a theoretical mortality rate of fifteen to twenty percent. The mouse survived. I don't know if that number adjusts upward or downward from the data."* *(pause)* *"I need you to be able to say that to someone and have them still agree."*

*If players ask why she stayed in Ashring:*
> *"Where would I go? My credentials were suspended by the same board that has the only facility capable of testing this at scale. I'm not going to the Spire directly — they'll suppress it again and this time there won't be a copy."* *(she glances at the apparatus)* *"There's a copy."*

*On what she needs from players:*
> *"A Dawnborn who will consent to a preliminary resonance test. Not a transfer — a test. One hour. No risk to lux-bearing capacity. I need to confirm the apparatus can interface with active lux before I ask anyone to do anything that matters."*

*If players have been hostile or impatient:*
> *"I've been alone with this for three years. I've had two Compact agents at my door, one anonymous threat, and thirty people in the alley every other week. You can be impatient at me when you've had that."*

*If players ask if she's afraid:*
> *(long pause)*
> *"I'm a scientist working without peer review on something that might kill people at a rate I can't predict, in a room I've been hiding in for three years, with one piece of empirical evidence that I don't fully understand."* *(she looks at the mouse jar)* *"Yes."*

---

**History:** Born Varenhold, lower Ashring. Educated at the Spire's technical institute on merit scholarship. Joined the Amber Compact's research division at twenty-three. Assigned to the Dawnborn Lux Continuity Project at twenty-eight — the institutional effort to find a non-fatal resolution to the Dawnborn's situation. Identified the Transfer Method's theoretical basis at thirty-six. Began apparatus construction at thirty-seven using her own savings after initial institutional funding was approved. Institutional access suspended at thirty-eight, research classified as inconclusive, apparatus confiscated — except for the component she'd already moved to the rented workroom. She rebuilt the apparatus from the salvaged component. The mouse test was at thirty-nine. She has been looking at the mouse for two years.
