## Session 1, Scene 3 — The Star-Wheel Navigation (Archive Restricted Stack 4-17)

**Design intent:** This is a lock, not a test. The star-wheel existed before Corven — it was installed when the Archive was built, two centuries before his birth. Corven spent fifty years memorizing it until he didn't need the chart anymore. The players are arriving at a mechanism that works perfectly, has always worked perfectly, and which nobody living has used in years. The puzzle is not clever. It is archaeology.

**Emotional target:** Players should feel the Archive's depth — that they are operating inside a machine built for people who are long dead, and that the machine does not care about them, only about the configuration. The first time the shelves move, it should feel like the building inhaled.

**Dramatic question:** Do you have the patience to read the sky inside a room?

**No combat. This tab covers puzzle mechanics, interaction tracking, failure consequences, and the Notation Key Fragment.**

---

## 1. The Puzzle System — Step by Step

### Physical Setup

The central column of Stack 4-17 is a cylindrical structure roughly 6 ft in diameter, floor-to-ceiling, built from dark ironwood and brass fittings. Three brass rings encircle it at shoulder height, each about 4 inches wide, each etched with eighteen constellation symbols spaced evenly around the circumference. The rings rotate independently. Each clicks into position with a firm, audible *snap* — there is no ambiguity about whether a ring is set. Set means set.

The shelving in Stack 4-17 is not fixed to the walls. It is mounted on brass rails set into the floor and ceiling, arranged in six curved sections that can pivot around the central column. When the rings are configured correctly, a locking mechanism inside the column disengages, the rails unlock, and the sections can be slid to open or close pathways between them.

At rest (no configuration entered): all six sections are locked in a default arrangement that leaves the room navigable but blocks access to the outer alcoves. Shelves 4-17-1 through 4-17-3 are in those outer alcoves.

### What Players See First

When the players arrive in Stack 4-17, read or paraphrase:

> The column is the first thing you see — dark wood and brass in the center of the room, taller than any of you, ringed at chest height with three bands of metal etched with symbols. Stars, you realize after a moment. Or not stars exactly: arrangements of them, each etched pattern showing a different constellation in miniature, with a name inscribed beneath it in the old liturgical script. The rings look like they turn. Around the room, curved shelving runs floor to ceiling in sections, the kind that pivot — you can see the brass rails at the floor's edge, can see the gap between the outermost section and the wall where a mechanism of some kind is waiting to engage. The room smells of fifty years of closed air and cedar oil. Everything in here has been waiting to be used again.

A DC 10 Investigation check (no roll needed if the party spends 1 full minute examining the column) establishes:
- The three rings turn independently
- Each ring has eighteen positions
- The symbols are constellation names in Old Varenish script
- There are three shelf sections that are clearly not in their normal position — they've been pushed to block something behind them

### The Star Chart

On the reading shelf built into the west wall of Stack 4-17, Corven has left — not deliberately for visitors, but because this was his working copy — a celestial chart for the Dawnmark constellation. It is a large piece of folded vellum, well-used, with small handwritten notations at the margins. The chart shows the six stars of the Dawnmark constellation with their names, their compass orientations (west to east, as they rise), and their relative brightness rankings.

**Reading the chart requires no roll.** The chart is a functional navigational document, not a cipher. Any literate character who picks it up and spends 1 minute reading it can extract the relevant information:

| Star | Position in Dawnmark | Rising Order | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vael's Crown | Leftmost / westernmost | Rises first (anchor) | Dim, fixed |
| Shepherd's Eye | Middle | Rises second (pivot) | Mid-brightness |
| Trailing Light | Rightmost / easternmost | Rises last | Brightest of the three |

The chart also shows three other stars in the Dawnmark that are not relevant to the mechanism (the shelving only uses three of the six stars). **A DC 12 Arcana or Investigation check** narrows this down: the player identifies that the three secondary stars represent the viewer's position, not the navigation target, and can be discarded. Without this check, the player must try all combinations including the secondary stars — which still works, but takes longer and risks triggering failure states.

**With the check:** Players know the exact three symbols to find and their order before they touch the rings. Proceed directly to configuration.

**Without the check:** Players must infer from context. The correct stars' names appear on the rings; trial-and-error is possible but increases failure risk (see Section 4).

## 2. The Star Chart — What's Obvious vs. What Requires a Check

| Information | Access |
|---|---|
| The chart exists and is on the reading shelf | Automatic — it is visible when entering the room |
| The chart shows the Dawnmark constellation | Automatic — the title is in large print at the top |
| The three stars of rising order: Vael's Crown, Shepherd's Eye, Trailing Light | Automatic for any literate character who reads the chart for 1 minute |
| Which stars to use (not all six) | DC 12 Arcana or Investigation |
| The mechanism was designed around this specific constellation | DC 14 History (reveals the Archive was founded under the Dawnmark astronomical calendar — the constellation appears in the Archive's foundation charter) |
| Corven is the last person to have used this | DC 13 Investigation, examining the dust patterns around the rings — only the three correct symbols show recent brass wear. The others are uniformly dusty. |

The dust wear check is worth calling out to players who specifically examine the rings before touching them. It effectively gives them the three correct symbols without any other roll.

## 3. Configuration Tracking

### The Solution

```
Ring 1 (leftmost / top ring): VAEL'S CROWN
Ring 2 (middle ring):         SHEPHERD'S EYE
Ring 3 (rightmost / bottom ring): TRAILING LIGHT
```

Each ring clicks into position. GM: track these on a scrap of paper or your notes using a simple three-line format:

```
R1: [ __________ ]
R2: [ __________ ]
R3: [ __________ ]
```

Fill in the symbol players set each ring to. This avoids any ambiguity mid-session about what configuration is currently entered.

### Entering a Configuration

Setting all three rings takes **1 action per ring** (or the equivalent — a player spending a full turn working the column can set all three as a single deliberate action if there is no time pressure). Once all three are set and the player pushes the small brass test-pin at the base of the column, the mechanism evaluates. The pin push requires no roll — it is a physical button, obviously present, labeled in Old Varenish as *"engage"* (DC 10 History to translate; DC 8 Intelligence if the player guesses from context).

### Partial Success State (Two of Three Correct)

If the players set Ring 1 and Ring 2 correctly but Ring 3 incorrectly (or any two-of-three combination), the column's mechanism fires but incompletely:

- Sections 4-17-1 and 4-17-2 unlock and slide open — their outer alcoves are now accessible.
- Section 4-17-3 remains locked. Its alcove is now **visible** through the gap left by 4-17-2 sliding aside — the players can see the shelf face and can read spine labels if they get close, but cannot enter the alcove or retrieve anything from it.
- The third ring produces a single short click — different from the full-engagement sound — and stops. Nothing rotates back. The partial configuration holds.

**What 4-17-1 and 4-17-2 contain:** Reference materials, survey maps, older Corven correspondence that is useful background but not the primary target. Players can retrieve these without completing the puzzle. Tell them clearly that they can see 4-17-3's shelves from where they stand but there is a brass catch still holding the section closed.

**Correcting from partial success:** Players only need to change Ring 3. The other two rings hold position. One more action to reset Ring 3, then re-engage the pin.

### Full Correct Configuration

All three rings correct, pin engaged: a low mechanical sound builds inside the column — gears, or something that functions like gears — and then the six shelf sections unlock simultaneously. Each one is free to slide. The players must physically push section 4-17-3's outer panel to open it (no roll, 1 action), revealing the alcove and everything inside.

## 4. Failure Mechanics

### What Counts as Failure

A failed configuration is one where **two or more rings are wrong** and the pin is engaged. One wrong ring in a two-of-three scenario is a partial success (see above), not a failure. The mechanism is smart enough to tell the difference — it was built by people who understood that astronomical navigation involves approximation.

### The Shelf Rotation

When a failed configuration fires, the mechanism does not simply refuse and lock up. It resets — and the reset is dramatic:

> The column vibrates once, a deep resonance you feel in the floor more than hear. Then the shelving begins to move.
>
> All six sections. At once. Slowly — one rotation per minute — but they are *moving*, sliding on rails you couldn't fully see before, and the pathways between them are opening and closing and opening again in a pattern that has nothing to do with you. Folios shift against each other. Somewhere in the rotation, a book falls from a high shelf with a sound like a flat crack in the dark. The room that was navigable thirty seconds ago now has its exits in different places.
>
> The column is resetting itself. This takes thirty minutes.

**The mechanical effects:**

- The room is **fully shuffled** — the six sections are now in their default rest configuration (not the partial-success configuration). All three alcoves are locked.
- Any player standing between sections when the rotation begins must make a DC 13 Dexterity saving throw or take 1d6 bludgeoning damage from being shunted by a moving shelf face. On a success, they step back in time.
- The rotation lasts **30 real-world minutes** (campaign time). The column locks during rotation — no new configurations can be entered until it stops.
- The **operator** (the player who pushed the pin) takes **one level of exhaustion**. The star-wheel is not punishing them — this is the ancient mechanic's safety system. The mechanism was designed for trained archivists who knew what they were entering. A wrong configuration implies the operator misread the stars. The exhaustion is a feature, not a flaw. Corven's marginalia on the chart, readable with DC 11 Investigation, confirms this: *"Trust the mechanism. Do not fight the reset. It is not angry with you."*

### The 30-Minute Clock

The clock starts the moment the rotation begins. During this time:

- Players can move through the rotating room but cannot access any alcove.
- Players can read materials already in their hands (anything retrieved from 4-17-1 or 4-17-2 in a partial-success state, for example).
- Players can search the parts of Stack 4-17 that do not require the alcoves.
- Players who sit down and do nothing will hear the Archive breathe around them — the only sound the slow grind of the column resetting, and then silence.

**After two failed configurations**, the footsteps mechanic triggers (see Section 5).

## 5. The Footsteps Mechanic

### What Triggers It

After **two failed configuration attempts** — meaning two full wrong-configuration pin pushes, not counting partial successes — at the start of the second reset rotation, a player with passive Perception 12 or higher hears:

> Footsteps. Below you. The lower stacks — whoever is down there is moving with purpose, not browsing. The sound is getting marginally clearer, which means they are moving toward a staircase.

This is not announced as a danger. It is presented as information. The players must decide what it means.

### Who Is It?

**Default:** Theron. He patrols the upper stacks periodically and the column's reset vibration carries through the building's ironwood framing in a way that an experienced archivist would recognize — the frequency is distinctive, different from normal use. Two resets in one session means someone is struggling, or testing configurations they don't know, or trying to break in. He will come to check.

**GM discretion modifier based on paranoia level:**

| Table Paranoia | Who it is | What they do on arrival |
|---|---|---|
| Low | Theron | Curious, not hostile. "Are you finding your way all right? The mechanism can be tricky without Corven's notation." Will help if asked — he knows the constellation but has been told not to offer without prompting. |
| Medium | Theron, suspicious | He arrives looking at the column, not the players. He sees which symbols are set. He does not immediately confront, but makes a note. His demeanor shifts to watchful. |
| High | A junior archivist Theron sent ahead | Does not know the mechanism. Just came to look. Will report back to Theron whatever they saw. Players can send them away easily, but the report still happens. |
| Very high (if players have already alarmed Theron) | Theron with the senior archivist | Two witnesses. Much harder to explain. The senior archivist has authority to revoke the Chancellor's access pass. |

### What Happens If Players Stop and Listen

A player who says they are actively listening (rather than relying on passive Perception) can make a DC 10 Perception check to:
- Determine the footsteps are coming from one floor down, not two
- Determine it is one person, not more (by spacing of steps)
- Determine the pace — purposeful, not searching

A DC 15 Perception check additionally establishes: the footsteps stopped at a point that corresponds to the base of the Stack 4-17 staircase. Whoever it is, they paused before climbing.

**The pause is Theron checking something before he comes up — probably his schedule or his lantern.** This gives the players approximately one minute of real-world time to finish what they are doing or look like they were doing something else. Use this as a beat, not a countdown — the pause creates tension, the arrival delivers it.

## 6. The Notation Key Fragment

### What It Is

On the lower reading shelf of Stack 4-17 — not in an alcove, not locked away, simply resting in the open as though Corven set it down mid-thought and forgot to take it — is a single folded sheet of heavy paper, densely covered on both sides in small handwriting. It is clearly not a shelf entry form or a catalog record.

**Physical description:**

> A single sheet, folded twice, the paper heavier than correspondence stock — the kind used for permanent records. The handwriting on the exposed half is dense and irregular, mixing what looks like Common script with symbols you don't immediately recognize: small geometric shapes, numerical subscripts, and at least two alphabets that appear to be invented rather than derived. Where a word should be, there is sometimes a shape. Where a number should be, there is sometimes a letter. The ink is old but not ancient — ten, fifteen years at most.

This is **Fragment 1 of Corven's private notation key** — specifically, the key that maps his personal cipher symbols to their plaintext equivalents for categories A through F (the cipher has nine categories; this fragment covers the first six).

### When It Can Be Retrieved

The fragment is on an open shelf. Players can pick it up **at any time** — before, during, or after solving the star-wheel. It requires no check to take it. It requires no check to notice it if any player specifically examines the reading shelves (which is a reasonable thing to do in a restricted archive alcove). A player with passive Perception 14 or higher notices it automatically when they first enter the area.

**It is not in an alcove.** It does not require solving the puzzle to retrieve. This is intentional — Corven used this shelf as a working surface, not a storage system. He was sloppy about his working notes in his private spaces.

### What Players Can Do With It

| Action | Time Required | What They Get |
|---|---|---|
| Read it in place | 5 minutes | Confirms it is some kind of cipher key; cannot decode it without a sample of ciphered text to compare |
| Copy it by hand | 10 minutes | A working copy; the original stays in the Archive |
| Take it | 1 action | The original; Theron may eventually notice it is gone (not this session, but later) |
| Attempt to decode it | Requires Corven's ciphered journal (not present this session) | The fragment becomes fully useful in Session 2 |
| DC 14 Arcana check while reading it | Immediate | Player identifies the symbol-system as a hybrid between liturgical notation and personal shorthand — this is the work of someone who expected no reader but themselves |

**GM note:** If players take the original, do not flag this as theft in-scene. It is a loose sheet on a working shelf. Taking it is the obvious choice and should feel like a discovery, not a crime. Whether it becomes a complication depends on how carefully Theron tracks the working materials in the restricted stacks — and that depends on what the GM wants for Session 2.

### What the Fragment Contains

The fragment is a key, not content. Players looking at it this session see:

- **Column A:** Symbol → Common letter equivalents (standard substitution, 26 symbols)
- **Column B:** Number encoding (Corven replaced digits with names of historical figures; a number sequence becomes a list of names, which encodes a date or quantity)
- **Column C–F:** Category-specific substitution tables covering topics that the players cannot identify without the ciphered text as context (one column appears to relate to faction names, one to locations, two to unknown categories)

The fragment will allow players to decode approximately 70% of Corven's journal in Session 2. The remaining 30% requires Fragments 2 and 3 (scattered across the Archive in later sessions).

## 7. Creative Approaches

### What If Players Try to Navigate Without the Mechanism?

The shelves are physically substantial — each section weighs several hundred pounds and sits on precision-machined brass rails. A player who tries to force a section open (Str DC 20) succeeds in moving it 6 inches before the rail catch bites and holds. The section does not break. The catch does not break. The shelf was designed by people who anticipated brute force.

A player who tries to find another way around the sections — climbing over, squeezing through gaps — can make a DC 14 Acrobatics check to thread the gap between two closed sections and reach the alcove face. They can read spine labels from this position but cannot retrieve anything larger than a loose sheet of paper without the mechanism opening the section properly.

**Award this approach:** It shows initiative and should work for small retrieval tasks (a loose sheet, a small object). It does not substitute for the mechanism if the primary target (Corven's restricted file) is bound into a folio.

### What If Players Use Arcana to Understand the Mechanism Without the Chart?

A DC 16 Arcana check, taking 10 minutes of examining the column, allows a player to deduce:

- The mechanism responds to astronomical configurations (the symbols are constellations in a rotational order)
- The correct configuration is encoded in the mechanism's wear patterns (this effectively replicates the dust-check result from Section 2 without the chart — they identify the three worn symbols)
- The mechanism is tuned to a specific constellation, not random stars — the three worn symbols cluster in a way consistent with a constellation that rises west to east

This does not give them the order (west-to-east vs. east-to-west), but narrows valid configurations from 18³ to 3! = 6. From there, trial-and-error has a reasonable failure budget before the footsteps mechanic triggers.

**If they have both the chart and the Arcana check:** They know everything. Configuration on the first try. The mechanism fires cleanly. They have earned the clean solve.

### What If Players Ask Theron to Just Take Them to 4-17-3 Directly?

This is the cleanest version of the creative bypass, and it works — with costs.

Theron can open 4-17-3 manually. He has an override key (a physical brass rod that disengages the mechanism's lock from the underside of the column). He has not offered it because his instructions from the Senior Archivist are to allow Chancellor-sanctioned access but not to facilitate it actively — there is a distinction, politically, between visitors finding their own way and staff escorting them into restricted alcoves.

If asked:
- He says he can open it, but must log the access with his name as the facilitating archivist.
- His name then appears in the access log for 4-17-3, which is reviewed weekly by the Senior Archivist.
- He is willing to accept this if the players are honest with him about what they are looking for and why.
- **If the players lie convincingly (DC 13 Deception vs. Theron's Insight +4):** He opens it and logs it without noting their deception. This comes back in Session 2 when the Senior Archivist asks Theron directly what the Chancellor's people wanted.
- **If they don't bother to lie and are simply honest:** Theron opens it, logs it, and tells them: "Corven's materials are in the third section from the left once you're inside. The bound journal is on the second shelf, spine-out. You'll know it — it's the only one with a lock-clasp."

**The cost of the bypass:** They solve the scene cleanly but lose the star-wheel experience — and they leave a paper trail with Theron's name on it. Whether that matters depends on how closely the Senior Archivist is watching.

## 8. The Designed Peak Moment

### The Shelves Moving for the First Time

Whether this is a success state or a failure state, the first time the six sections of Stack 4-17 move, it should be described in full:

> You hear the mechanism engage before you feel it — a sound deep inside the column, something between gears and breath, like the room clearing its throat after fifty years of silence.
>
> Then the shelves move.
>
> All six of them. Simultaneously. The brass rails at the floor take the weight without protest — they were built for this — and the sections swing outward and inward in a pattern that is clearly intentional, clearly practiced, clearly not afraid of the dark it has been sitting in. You can feel the air moving as they displace it. Folios shift against each other with the dry whisper of paper against paper. Somewhere near the ceiling, a small cloud of dust shakes loose from a high shelf and drifts down through the lantern light like the Archive shaking off a long sleep.
>
> The mechanism is fifty years old. It works perfectly.
>
> When everything stops, the room is different. The paths between sections are different. And in the far wall, where there was a closed wooden face before, there is now an opening — dark, narrow, exactly wide enough to step through.

This narration is appropriate for both a successful full configuration and the start of a failure rotation (with minor adjustments in the final paragraph — for failure, the opening does not appear; instead the sections continue moving and the rotation clock begins). The key element in both cases is the same: the physicality of hundreds of folios shifting in the dark, moved by a mechanism no living person built.

**Hold the silence after this description.** Let the players sit in it for a moment before anyone speaks. This is the peak moment — not because it is dramatic in the combat sense, but because it is the Archive revealing that it still has weight, that Corven built something that outlasted him, that the past is more solid than it first appeared.

| State | Rings Set | Result |
|---|---|---|
| No configuration | — | Default lock; all alcoves sealed |

| Failed configuration | 2+ rings wrong | Shelf rotation; 30-min clock; operator gains 1 exhaustion level |
| After 2 failures | — | Footsteps from below; Theron inbound |
