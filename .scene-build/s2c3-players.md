## Engagement Matrix

Different player types engage with this scene at different points. The scene is structured to reward all of them.

| Player Type | Entry Point | Peak Engagement | What They Get |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Puzzle-first** | The box immediately — "what are the three indentations for?" | The moment the first correct name lights | The satisfaction of mechanism; Fragment 2 |
| **Story-first** | Theron's body language, the shelf gap, the waiting | When the key passage is read aloud | The weight of "release"; the anonymous sentence |
| **Character-first** | How their specific character relates to the Dawnborn or to Theron | The Dawnborn connection in the key passage | Personal investment in the stakes going forward |
| **Tactile / hands-on** | Picking up the box first, noting the weight and warmth | Pressing the indentation and feeling it respond | Embodied puzzle mechanics; sensory confirmation |
| **Observer / quiet** | Watching the others work; noting room details | When Theron says nothing at the box opening | Reading Theron's silence; understanding the scene without dialogue |
| **Roleplayer who asks questions** | "Why did you never open it yourself?" | Theron's full answer | The secondary objective; depth on Theron's character |

## What Different Players Do With the Documents

**The player who reads everything:** They will find both colophons, read the full content of all three bundles' surviving pages, and potentially notice the ink discrepancy in Bundle 1's annotation. They will also be the one who reads the anonymous sentence. Give them time. Don't interrupt their reading by having something urgent happen. This is the quiet before Scene 5.

**The player who scans for keywords:** They will find "CORVEN, SALI, WREN" in the colophons within a minute of looking. They will see "witnesses" or similar heading. They will understand the connection to the box. This is fine. The puzzle is designed to be solved. Their fast solution is not a problem.

**The player who asks Theron instead of reading:** Theron will direct them back to the documents for the names, but will answer almost any other question. This player is getting character information rather than mechanical information, and that is a valid trade.

**The player who tries things:** They pick up the box, turn it over, try pressing an indentation without speaking, try pressing and making a sound that isn't a name, try pressing all three at once. Let them. Each attempt gets a description — pressing without speaking produces nothing, pressing all three simultaneously produces a low sustained tone and then nothing, pressing and speaking a non-name produces the wrong-name tone. The box is responsive to engagement. Players who engage with it physically before they have the names are learning its vocabulary.

## Read-Aloud: The Key Passage

When the box opens and players take out the documents, they will eventually reach page three of Corven's notes. This is the moment for a read-aloud. Deliver it slowly, with a pause after "vessels" and a longer pause after "release."

---

> *"The anchors are not lost. The light did not die. It redistributed into ten living vessels born at the moment of inversion. The ritual is incomplete, not failed. The completion requires their release."*

---

After reading: do not speak immediately. The table should have a moment with that sentence.

Then, very quietly, if Theron is still in the room: he says nothing. He already knows what it says. He has known for eleven years. He does not need to hear it again. But he is watching the players' faces as they read it for the first time. He has been waiting, in some form, to see what other people look like when they understand.

If a player looks up from the page at Theron: he meets their eyes. He nods, once, very slightly. He says: *"Yes."*

Just that. It is enough.

## Read-Aloud: The Anonymous Sentence

This one does not need staging — it appears at the bottom of the last page, when players have already processed the key passage and may be reviewing the final notes. It can be read out quietly, almost to oneself:

---

> *"He told me what it meant. I don't think he knew I would write it down."*

---

No signature. No date. Just those two sentences, in a practical hand, at the bottom of the page.

If this generates immediate discussion: good. The questions it raises — who is "he," who is the writer, why "I don't think he knew" — are deliberate. None of them are answerable in this scene. They are seeds.

If players show it to Theron: he looks at it for a long moment. *"I've looked at that many times. I still don't know."* He means it.

## What Happens in the Room When the Box Opens

The box opening is the scene's physical climax. Describe it carefully.

**The amber light:** the hairline seam around the lid fills with amber light — the same quality of light as the Archive lanterns below, as the amber workshops in the setting, as the light that fills the building. It is not a surprise color. It fits the world. But it fills the seam around the lid for a moment before the lid becomes openable, and that moment is worth noting.

**The sound:** a low harmonic tone, not a click or a thunk — the lock disengaging sounds like a chord resolving. It is, technically, beautiful. Given what is in the box, the beauty of the mechanism is worth noting.

**The warmth:** when the lid opens, the warmth inside the box is slightly greater than the exterior warmth. The air inside has been sealed for eleven years. It carries the particular smell of cedar and old parchment and, very faintly, something burnt — the last traces of whatever energy the resonance lock discharged on its last operational test, which was Theron's second failed attempt to open it three years ago.

**Theron:** he stands when the lid opens. He does not approach. He is at the back of the room, near the shelf gap. He watches.

What he looks like when the box opens: a man who just heard a piece of music he never thought he would hear. That specific kind of stillness.

## After the Box: Transition to Scene 4

Scene 3 ends when players have:
1. Retrieved the documents and Fragment 2
2. Read (or been read) the key passage
3. Encountered the anonymous sentence

The transition to Scene 4 (Theron's confession) can happen here, in this room, with the box open on the desk between them. There is no required location change. If Theron starts talking — if the box opening broke something loose in him and he begins to explain what he has known and why he did not act — that is Scene 4 beginning in Scene 3's space.

The document says Scene 4 involves Theron sitting in the plain chair on the visitor's side of the desk. He may do that now, after the box opens. He may come around the desk and sit with the players rather than behind the authority of the desk surface. If he does, Scene 4 has begun.

Let it happen.
