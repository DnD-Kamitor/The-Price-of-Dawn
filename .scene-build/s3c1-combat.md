# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: Combat as Failure Path

<details><summary>⚔ Combat Overview: What Forced Passage Costs</summary>

The crowd is not a combat encounter. There are no enemy stat blocks. There are thirty people who are tired and angry and in the way, and there is a door at the far end that players need to reach.

Forced passage means treating thirty exhausted dock workers and parents as an obstacle to push through. The crowd responds the way people respond when someone starts pushing: they get louder, they get tighter, and the people at the center — who came here to be heard — feel confirmed in their worst assumption about how the city treats them.

**This path reaches the door. It costs something.**

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Do not prevent players from choosing this path. Do not add hidden consequences that punish them in ways they can't anticipate. Be honest: this is a viable route with a known cost (Isolde is shaken; Senna is still outside in S3C5 and carries a different energy toward players). The cost is narrative, not mechanical. Let players make the choice with clear information.

The goal is to reach Isolde's door. This path accomplishes that. It does not accomplish anything else.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>⚔ Encounter Structure: Movement Through the Crowd</summary>

**Initiative:** Not called. This is not a combat encounter. There are no turns in the traditional sense.

Instead, run it as a series of movement checks across three phases, each representing approximately one "round" of fiction:

---

**Phase 1 — Entry (0 to 10ft into crowd)**

Difficult terrain begins immediately. Movement costs 2ft per 1ft.

The crowd tightens when players push. Nobody swings first. People lean away, stumble, shout.

> *The moment you move against the crowd rather than with it, the crowd knows. Thirty people shift at once — not organized, not planned, just the physics of packed bodies and the instinct to close around whatever is disrupting the center.*

**Crowd Response Check — Phase 1:**
DC 10 Persuasion (can be called, or players can elect to keep pushing)
- Success: the crowd parts enough to allow movement at difficult terrain speed. No escalation.
- Failure: the crowd tightens further. Difficult terrain becomes impassable for this phase (crowd walls off, players must spend a full action pressing through — no other actions).

Senna watches from her position 20ft in. She does not approach. She says, loudly enough to be heard:
> "I told them this was going to happen."

---

**Phase 2 — Center (10 to 20ft — Senna's position)**

Players reach Senna's position in the crowd's center. She is here. She is not a combatant. She does not raise her hands, does not block with her body, does not threaten.

If a player moves into her space, she steps back — once, then again — keeping exactly two feet between herself and whoever is pushing.

**What she says:**
> "You're pushing through thirty people to reach one woman in a rented room." *(flat, no judgment in the delivery)* "I hope she has something worth hearing."

She does not stop players. She does not fight. But she is watching every one of them.

**Crowd Response Check — Phase 2:**
DC 10 Persuasion — but this check is harder to describe as "persuasion." It's closer to: is anyone in this party doing anything that looks like it has a human reason behind it?

- Success: the crowd parts. Ugly, but passable.
- Failure: someone in the crowd throws something — not a weapon, a bit of rubble, a handful of dust. Nobody is targeted. It's frustration finding a direction. The noise doubles.

> *If players are visibly armed and moving with intent, describe the crowd's energy shifting from exhaustion into something tighter and faster. These are people who have spent their whole lives being moved out of the way.*

---

**Phase 3 — Door (20 to 30ft — the building's entrance)**

Players reach the door. It is unlocked. The stairwell goes up.

Behind them, the crowd has backed away — not dispersed, backed away. They are watching.

Senna is at the crowd's rear now. She says nothing. She looks up at the second-floor window. It has gone dark.

> *The window above was lit when you arrived. It is not lit now.*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The window going dark is Isolde's reaction. She saw the crowd scatter. She heard the noise. She has turned off her lamp and moved away from the window — not fled, but retreated from visibility. She is still in the room. She is now frightened in a way she wasn't before.

Players can still reach her. The door is open. The stairwell works. But the first thing she says when they enter is going to reflect what she heard.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>⚔ Senna's Behavior Through Each Phase</summary>

**Before forced passage begins:**
Senna is at crowd center, holding her placard at her side. She is watching the south approach. She noticed players when they arrived. She has been deciding whether to speak to them.

**Phase 1 — when players push:**
She does not move toward them. She says:
> "I told them this was going to happen."
*(To the crowd. Not to players.)*

**Phase 2 — when players reach her position:**
She steps back. Maintains two feet of distance. Makes eye contact with each player who passes her. Says:
> "You're pushing through thirty people to reach one woman in a rented room. I hope she has something worth hearing."
*(She does not say it accusingly. She says it as a statement of what is happening.)*

**Phase 3 — when players reach the door:**
She has moved to the crowd's far edge. She is looking up at the building. The window has gone dark. She says nothing.

**After players enter:**
She stays. The crowd fragments slowly — people at the edges peel off first, then the center. Within fifteen minutes, Lowmark Alley is clear. Senna is the last to leave. She picks up her placard from where she set it against the wall, holds it for a moment, then carries it away.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Senna does not call players murderers or monsters. She does not curse at them or threaten them. She watches them and she says the true thing and she lets them go. This is worse than anger would be. She has already decided who they are in this moment, and she is deciding quietly.

If players survive to Session 5 and Senna appears again, she will remember this. Not with malice — with the specific attention of someone who watched something happen and is still trying to understand it.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>⚔ Crowd Escalation Table</summary>

| Phase | DC | Success | Failure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phase 1 (entry) | DC 10 Persuasion | Crowd parts, difficult terrain | Crowd walls off; full action to press through, no other actions this round |
| Phase 2 (center) | DC 10 Persuasion | Crowd clears a path | Debris thrown (no damage, noise doubles, Isolde's window goes dark immediately) |
| Phase 3 (door) | No check required | Players reach door | — |

**Difficult Terrain Rules (crowd):**
- 2ft of movement spent per 1ft traveled
- Crowd occupies 30ft of depth, full alley width (15ft)
- No flanking edges — the crowd is wall-to-wall at Chandler's Block
- Side lane (north) bypasses crowd entirely; only accessible before entering crowd

**If combat (actual violence) breaks out:**
- No member of the crowd is armed
- If players attack anyone: DC 15 Persuasion required immediately, or crowd becomes impassable for remainder of scene
- Senna backs away to crowd's far edge; does not engage; watches
- Davan Crel, across the alley: makes a note. Stands. Leaves the café. His report will now include this detail.

</details>

<details><summary>⚔ Aftermath: Isolde's Reaction</summary>

When players reach the second floor and enter Isolde's workroom, she is at the far wall, her back to the door, reorganizing papers she has already reorganized.

She turns when they enter.

> *"I heard the noise."*
> *(She looks at each of them in turn.)*
> *"I thought it was the Reckoning."*

She does not ask what happened. She does not need to. She saw it from the window before she pulled back.

**Mechanical consequence:**
If the crowd was forced through (Phases 1-3 without de-escalation), Isolde begins the Transfer Apparatus scene (S3C3) at +1 difficulty on the first check. She is not refusing to help — she is rattled. Her hands are not steady. Her usual precision is slightly off.

This is not punitive. It is what happens to a person who has been hiding in a room for three years when the hiding is suddenly over and it ends with people pushing through a crowd to reach her door.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** If players de-escalated the crowd before reaching Isolde, she is still watching from the window when they arrive — she saw the conversation, she saw Senna step back, she saw the crowd part. Her first line in that case is different:

*(Unlocking the door before they knock):*
*"I saw what you did. With the woman outside."*
*(pause)*
*"Come in."*

Her tone is not warmth exactly — it's recognition. Someone finally handled the thing she's been watching happen outside her window since morning. She is still cautious. But she opens the door.
:::

</details>
