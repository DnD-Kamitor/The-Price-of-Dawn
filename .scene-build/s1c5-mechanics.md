### Are There Rolls?

The scene is not gated by dice. Edoran has decided to be present with these players before the Dawnhall door opens. No Persuasion check extracts his knowledge. No Intimidation makes him flinch. He is a man who has spent twenty years with this theology and this grief and no player at the table tonight has an argument he has not heard.

What does exist: **Insight** checks to understand what is underneath his precision, and **Perception** checks to notice specific physical tells. These do not gate the scene — they deepen it.

**Persuasion:** Not applicable. He has been thinking about this for twenty years. A Persuasion check against Edoran is like a Persuasion check against a load-bearing wall — the wall does not move because of the argument; it is doing what it is doing independently of the argument. Players who try to argue him into a different theological position will find that he listens carefully, responds with precision, and does not change. This is not stubbornness; his position is the result of twenty years of examination. The examination is done.

**Intimidation:** He is a man who chose to stand in this lane and wait. The threat spectrum available to players does not operate on him. If players make an Intimidation attempt, he looks at them — patiently, specifically — and says: *"I've waited here for two hours. What exactly are you threatening me with?"*

**Deception:** If players try to bluff or mislead him about what they found — claiming ignorance of the Archive findings, for instance — he receives the deception without comment, says what he came to say regardless, and departs. The bluff gains players nothing except his awareness that they are playing a different game than he is. He notes this and files it.

### Insight DC Table

| DC | What a player notices |
|---|---|
| **10** | He is grieving. Not actively — the grief is old and organized — but it is present in the specific quality of his precision. He is not precise because he is a precise man; he is precise because precision is what the grief became. |
| **13** | His position was built on personal loss. Something happened to him. The theological certainty and the personal loss are the same thing — he built one from the other, and the construction is visible in how he talks about the grey sickness. He does not use abstract examples. He speaks about grey sickness the way a person speaks about something they have been inside. |
| **15** | He knows more than he is saying about the Inversion. When he says *"the Archive has more than you've found,"* he is not guessing. He has been in the Archive for twenty years. There is something specific he is declining to share, and the declining is deliberate — not because he wants to withhold it strategically but because he does not believe it is his to give. |
| **18** | The theology is a container. Without it, he would have nothing left. The movement, the precision, the twenty years — all of it is in service of the container holding. If a player could remove the theological certainty from Edoran, they would find a man who has not known what to do with himself since his daughter died, and who built the most rigorous structure available to him around that void. The container is real. The void is also real. He does not examine the seam between them. |

### Persuasion Against Edoran: Special Case

There is one situation where something like Persuasion applies: if players have Insight at DC 13 or higher — they understand his position was built on personal loss — and they specifically engage with that loss rather than his theology, the conversation changes quality.

This is not a Persuasion check. It is a role-play beat. If players engage with the grief directly, with genuine intent (not as manipulation), Edoran becomes less precise and more present. He does not change his theological position. But the delivery softens into the grief underneath it, and players learn things he was not going to deliver in precision-mode.

This is the scene's hidden mode. It does not have a DC. It has a requirement: genuine engagement.

### Combat: The Failure State

Combat is a failure state for this scene. Players who fight through Edoran reach the end of Session 1 without understanding the consent question. The scene's information — the living conduit beat, the consent argument, the oblique confirmation of the Inversion's existence in the Archive — is unavailable to players who chose violence.

**Encounter Setup:**

| Combatant | AC | HP | Speed | Attack | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Brother Edoran | 12 | 28 | 30 ft | Quarterstaff +3, 1d6+1 bludgeoning | Does not attack first; defends; stops at 14 HP |
| Restorer Zealot (×4) | 12 | 16 | 30 ft | Shortsword +3, 1d6+1 piercing | Defensive fighting; goal is delay, not harm |

**Edoran's Combat Behavior:**

He will not attack first. If a player makes an attack against him, he parries rather than strikes — Dodge action, plus a single staff strike if they continue. He is not trying to win a fight. He is trying to not be incapacitated before he can say what he came to say.

When he reaches 14 HP (half):

> "You're looking for a fight. You'll find a harder one."

He stops fighting. He stands. He does not flee. If players continue attacking him, he takes it — he will not run and he will not beg off and he will not produce more force in response. The zealots stop when he stops.

**After combat:**

If players defeat or bypass Edoran by force, the scene ends without the living conduit beat, the consent argument, or the crack. The only information players have is what they carried out of the Dawnhall. The zealots regroup. Edoran's movement becomes more active in Sessions 2-3. He is harder to reach, and when they reach him, he does not extend the same patience.
