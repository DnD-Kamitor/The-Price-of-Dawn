### Fragment 1 of the Notation Key

**Physical description:** A single sheet of heavy paper, folded once and creased firmly — the fold is old, the crease is a deliberate storage fold, not a hasty one. Corven's name appears at the top in plain common script, followed by a date (11 years pre-session, 3 years after the Ritual of Eternal Dawn). Cipher fills both sides, dense and consistent — a trained notation hand, not shorthand. The bottom line of the reverse face is in plain common: *"When you have all three, begin at the first mark."*

**Where found:** The open working shelf inside the restricted stacks, visible before the star-wheel puzzle is engaged. Players do not need to solve anything to take this. It was placed here to be found.

**What players can learn from it now:**
- Corven's name and date confirm he created this himself, deliberately, and filed it in an accessible location.
- The bottom line in plain common establishes that this is one of three matching fragments.
- The cipher is unreadable without the Notation Key journal — every check players make on the cipher text fails or yields nothing useful this session.

**What it does in future sessions:**
- Reduces all Arcana and History checks related to Corven's notation by 2, once all three fragments are assembled and the assembly is begun with the Notation Key journal present.
- Theron knows where the second and third fragments are. He will say so under the right conditions (see Section 2).
- The Archive has acquisition records that trace the other two fragments, if players choose the institutional route over the Theron route.

**Cannot be used this session.** The plain-language bottom line tells players exactly why.

Seven tubes of varying age — the oldest is cracked at one end and has been re-sealed with a different wax color. **Future sessions:** Background reference only. Can be used to establish Corven's pre-Ritual character in contrast to his post-Ritual documentation. No mechanical effect.

### "Public Summary — Ritual Architecture and Sympathetic Mechanism — For Archive Record"

**Physical description:** A bound report, 14 pages, stitched at the spine with grey thread. Corven's hand throughout — the handwriting players have been learning to recognize. The title is written on the exterior in large, deliberate letters. This was meant to be found. Tone is clinical, confident, and impersonal. Not a private document.

**Content summary:**
Corven's public-layer account of the Ritual of Eternal Dawn's mechanism. He uses precise technical vocabulary throughout: "distributed sympathetic anchors," "living conduit mechanism," "structural resonance," "active binding substrate." He does not define these terms — they are written for an audience assumed to share his notation training. The tone is admiring: he believed the mechanism was elegant. He does not express doubt. He does not express fear.

The word "consent" appears twice, in passing, as a component parameter. It is not emphasized. It is treated as one specification among several.

**What players can learn from it:**
- *No check required:* The Ritual used "distributed sympathetic anchors" — plural, positioned across the city — and a "living conduit mechanism." Both are currently undefined. Players have the vocabulary now; they lack the lexicon.
- *Arcana DC 12:* "Sympathetic anchors" are ritual components that remain structurally active after the primary rite concludes. Distributed anchors are unusual — most sympathetic rituals use a single anchor point. Distributing them across a city would mean the ritual's effects are structurally embedded in the city itself, not contained at a central site.
- *Arcana DC 16:* "Living conduit mechanism" is not standard notation terminology. "Living" in ritual theory refers specifically to a biological substrate — a living thing used as a structural component. The term does not appear in any academic paper Corven published in his lifetime. He used it here, in a document marked "For Archive Record," and did not explain it.
- *History DC 14:* The Ritual of Eternal Dawn occurred 40 years ago. The amber light that has illuminated Varenhold every night since — without a single source, without decay, without fuel — is a physical phenomenon that has never been explained by the Chancellor's office or the city's academic institutions. Players who make this check understand, for the first time, that the light itself may be the mechanism still operating.

**Future sessions:** The phrase "living conduit mechanism" is the Session 2 investigation anchor. Players have it now. They cannot decode it until they have the Notation Key and the personal case gloss (see below).

### Small Personal Case (Shelf 4-17-3)

**Physical description:** A flat case slightly smaller than a book, brass clasp, worn at the corners. The clasp is engraved: *"Notation. Personal. Do not file separately."* The instruction is addressed to an archivist — someone who might find this and attempt to cross-reference it into the general collection. Corven did not want it separated from the shelf. He filed it here deliberately.

**Contents:** A single folded sheet. One side is dense cipher. On the same page, in the margins and at the bottom, are two annotations in plain common — handwritten, slightly smaller than the cipher text, in a different ink (the cipher is Archive-black; the glosses are a warmer brown, an older formulation).

**First gloss (verbatim):**

> *"The open hand — not 'offering given' but the particular quality of a thing that is freely decided. This symbol marks what cannot be compelled — not the gift, but the freedom of the giving."*

**Second gloss (verbatim):**

> *"The weight borne willingly is not the same weight as the weight imposed. This is not a philosophical observation. It is an engineering specification. The mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed."*

**What players can learn from it:**
- *No check required:* Corven drew a functional distinction — explicitly, in his own hand, on an engineering document — between willing participation and compelled participation. He called it a specification, not a principle.
- *Arcana DC 10:* The phrase "the mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed" means the ritual was designed to break if consent was violated. Not incidentally — structurally. This was an intended design constraint.
- *Arcana DC 14:* The word "collapsed" in ritual theory refers to a structural failure of a sympathetic mechanism under conditions it was not designed to handle. The ritual did not collapse. The amber light is still active. One of two things is true: the distinction was not collapsed, or "as designed" means something other than complete failure.
- *History DC 14:* The Ritual of Eternal Dawn was performed 40 years ago. Players now know what the engineering required. They do not yet know whether those requirements were met.

**Future sessions:** This gloss is the campaign's central moral question made concrete. It connects backward to the Scene 1 note ("the willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot") and forward to the Session 2 revelation about what "living conduit" means. The second gloss in particular carries the weight of the entire campaign in 42 words. Read it aloud to the players, slowly.

## 3. Campaign Hooks Activated

- **Fragment 1 of the Notation Key** — one of three; the other two require Theron's guidance or Archive records to locate. The assembled key reduces all future Corven notation checks by 2. Opens into Sessions 2–3.

- **"Living conduit mechanism"** — players have the phrase and cannot decode it without the cipher journal. The question "what is a living conduit" is the engine of Session 2. Opens into Session 2 (primary investigation thread).

- **Consent as engineering specification** — the second gloss in the personal case states explicitly that the ritual's mechanism will not function if the distinction between willing and compelled participation is collapsed. Players now know this. They do not yet know whether it was violated. Opens into Sessions 2–4 (campaign moral spine).

- **The connection backward to the Scene 1 note** — "the willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot" (Scene 1) and "the weight borne willingly is not the same weight as the weight imposed" (personal case) are the same idea in two different voices. One is theology. One is an engineering specification. They were written by different people about the same ritual. Opens into Session 2 when players encounter the author of the Scene 1 note.

- **Someone was on the stairs** (if Option C footsteps were used) — who? What did they hear? The Archive's access log records a second entry after hours this evening, regardless of whether players investigated the footsteps. Someone else was here tonight. Opens into Session 2 (Archive thread).

- **The access log** — even without Option C, the players' own late-hours entry was logged. The Archive is not a building that receives investigators in the restricted stacks at night without that fact being known. Opens into Session 2 (background pressure; the Chancellor receives the log summary).

- **Theron has been destroying his notes** (if players triggered this) — what did they say? He cannot tell players what he destroyed because he destroyed most of it. One page survives. Opens into Session 2 (Theron's personal arc; the surviving page is the Session 2 revelation catalyst).

## 5. The Final Image

Session 1 ends when the players leave the Archive.

The building's front door closes behind them. The amber light is overhead — sourceless, unchanged, the same quality it has been since they arrived in Varenhold. It does not flicker. It does not dim. It will not change by morning.

The street is quiet. Lowmark at this hour: distant water, a slow cart somewhere two blocks west, the low note of the Archive's door settling back into its frame.

The players are carrying a flat personal case with a brass clasp. A bound report in Corven's hand. Seven document tubes. A single cipher-filled sheet with two glosses in brown ink.

They have been in this city for one day.

What they know: Corven designed a ritual that required willing participation as a structural specification. The mechanism is still running. The light overhead is forty years old and has never been explained.

What they are carrying in their hands is the reason it has never been explained.

Give them a moment before you close the session. The five-senses beat, brief: the weight of the document case, the smell of old paper and the Archive still on their clothes, the amber light on the cobblestones, the particular quality of a city that does not go fully dark. Then one line to close:

> *Corven wrote that the mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed. The light has been on for forty years. Draw your own conclusions — but not tonight.*

Do not explain the line. Do not follow it with mechanics or XP announcements. Close there. Let them carry the question out the door. The question is the session's gift to Session 2.

- [ ] **Star-wheel outcome recorded.** Full solve (all three rings) / partial (two rings, shelves 1 and 2 only) / solved with Theron's hint / Option C footsteps triggered? Write it now — it controls Theron's Session 2 opening.

- [ ] **Second gloss read aloud.** Did you read the second gloss to the table verbatim and slowly? If the session moved fast and it was paraphrased or skimmed, read it aloud now before closing — not as recap, but as the document itself. Players should hear it at least once as language, not summary.

If no player made the comparison aloud, note it for the Session 2 opening beat — Theron can make it, or a player can reach it on a History DC 10 overnight reflection.

- [ ] **Access log flagged.** Mark whether players investigated the footsteps (Option C) or not. Either way, the access log records a second entry tonight. Flag this for the Session 2 Chancellor debrief — she receives the log summary.
