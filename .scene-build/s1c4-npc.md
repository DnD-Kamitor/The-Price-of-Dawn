## Sera Voss — The Dawnhall

*No combat stats. Sera does not fight. If violence enters this room she steps in front of whoever needs it, because that is what she does, and then she is very quiet and very cold until the violence has made a decision about what it actually wants.*

---

### Physical Description

Late 40s. Dark hair going grey at the temples, pinned up for kitchen work, a few strands escaped and ignored. The apron is canvas, worn at the front from years of use. Her hands are work-worn — calloused, efficient, the hands of someone who has been doing physical work for twenty years without a gap.

She does not perform warmth. She has it. The distinction is visible in the first thirty seconds.

The pendant at her collarbone is small — a smooth amber stone on a plain cord, not decorative, not jeweled. It is warm to the touch at close range. She does not call attention to it and does not hide it. It has been there so long that she no longer notices it.

When she is being careful about what she says next: she touches the pendant. Not obviously. A single fingertip, briefly. Then she answers.

When something surprises her: she goes very still. Not frozen — still. A bird-still, the kind of complete non-motion that is a form of control, not a reaction to losing control.

### History with the Dawnhall

She has been in Lowmark for fifteen years. Before the Dawnhall there was a grain storage room on Crestwick Lane, city-owned, empty because the merchant it was leased to had gone under in the second wave of grey sickness. She petitioned the district warden for a three-year occupancy permit, listed her intended use as "community nutrition program," and was granted it on the condition that she maintained the building's structural elements.

She has maintained them. She has also replaced the floor, the roof pitch, the kitchen fittings, and three of the original walls. The building that now stands on Crestwick Lane is the Dawnhall in function and the old grain store only in footprint.

She did not build it because she knew what she was. She built it because she could, and because people were hungry, and because the city's official relief provisions had a three-week processing delay that she found intolerable.

She found out what she was two years into running the Dawnhall. She deciphered it slowly, from fragments, over about eight months of careful Archive research. She did not stop running the Dawnhall. She ordered more root vegetables from the eastern supplier and kept the kitchen open.

---

### Relationship to Auris and Her Faith

She was a believer before she knew what she was. She grew up with Auris — the sun god, the giver of warmth and growth, the one who left and will return. Her faith is not performance; it was simply present in her household, practiced with the particular casualness of people who believe so steadily they have stopped noticing they believe.

She finds it complicated that the sun is inside her. She has thought about this for years. The theological position she has arrived at: she is not the sun. She is holding something the sun left behind when it could not complete what it started. The difference matters to her. She is not divine. She is a vessel carrying something divine, the way the Dawnhall carries the people it was built to hold.

She finds it more complicated that she doesn't mind. She expected the knowledge of what she carries to feel heavier than it does. It feels like a responsibility she is already meeting.

Her faith is what makes her answer to the question of consent simple. Not easy — simple. She did not choose to be a vessel. She did not choose what she carries. But she chooses what she does with it, and she has already answered that question, and the answer does not change from day to day.

---

### Relationship to the Other Dawnborn

She is the most public-facing of the ten. She did not plan this — the Dawnhall simply made her visible in a way that other Dawnborn, who have quieter lives, are not. They know who she is. She knows who they are. The relationships vary.

She is closest to Tomas Areth. She thinks he is braver than he knows — she has watched him carry the knowledge with a level of scholarly caution that she reads as self-protection against the fear of getting it wrong. She is not wrong about the self-protection. She is not wrong about the bravery either. She has never said this to him directly.

She respects the others and does not claim to speak for them. She says this with precision when players ask: *"My yes is mine. Theirs is theirs. I would not tell you their answer if I knew it."* She is not being evasive. This is a principle she holds with the same steadiness she holds everything else.

### Tells

**When she's being careful about what she says next:** She touches the pendant. One fingertip, briefly, at the collarbone. Then she answers. The answer that follows a pendant-touch is always honest and always considered.

**When something surprises her:** She goes very still. Not startled — still. This quality of stillness is distinct from her normal efficient composure. The stillness is what happens when her composure encounters something it didn't anticipate.

**When she is performing acceptance rather than feeling it:** She doesn't. She cannot perform it convincingly. If she is afraid or uncertain, those things are visible in specific ways — she is more formal, more precise, slightly slower than usual. She does not produce fake warmth when the real thing is occupied elsewhere.

---

### Her Crack

*Ask me how I am.*

If a player asks with genuine intent — not as an investigation probe, not with a hidden agenda, simply as a person asking another person how they are — she pauses. Long enough to be visible. Then:

> "I'm all right. Most days."

She does not elaborate unless pushed. If pushed:

> "Ask me again after."

She means: after whatever this is, after the ritual question resolves, after she knows what is going to happen and has processed it enough to have an answer. Not "later" as avoidance. "After" as a specific temporal reference to a moment she is already thinking about.

---

### What She Knows About the Ashring Stones

She has been to the Ashring once. She went alone, three years ago, on a quiet morning when the plaza was empty. She stood at the stone that corresponds to her position in the ritual — Stone 1, the southernmost of the outer ring.

She says, if asked:

> "I stood there for maybe ten minutes. It was — it was like being recognized. Not seen. Recognized. Like a door you've walked past a hundred times and never noticed had your name on it."

She has never been able to read the inscription carved into the base of the stones. She knows the inscription is there — she traced it with her fingers. She cannot decode it. This troubles her, mildly, the way an unresolved thing that may not matter continues to present itself at the edges of thought.

> "Ask someone about the inscription on the Ashring stones. I've never been able to read it. But I know it's there."

### On the Pendant

The amber pendant is warm to the touch at close range. If a player is within five feet of her and pays attention: the stone is warm above room temperature. It has been warm since she was three years old. She does not know if it is always this warm or if it is warm specifically in the presence of people who are looking for something.

She does not comment on it unless asked. If asked:

> "It's warm. It's been warm for as long as I can remember. I stopped thinking about it."
