## 1. Information Gained by Success Level

The riddle's answer is CONSENT. The door opening is not a reward — it is a threshold. What players carry *into* the restricted stacks depends on how they reached the door.

---

### Full Success — Solved Independently, Theron Not Needed

Players arrived at CONSENT without prompting. The notation carvings shifted, the door swung inward, and Theron — if he has now appeared — witnessed it.

**What they gain:**

- The word CONSENT now belongs to them, not to Theron. When he tells them what the Dawnborn actually are, they will remember that they named the campaign's central principle before they understood why it mattered. That retroactive weight is irreplaceable.
- Theron registers that these are not people who need managing. His trust level enters Scene 3 at **Engaged** rather than **Cautious** — the difference between a guide who volunteers information and one who waits to be asked.
- If players already showed Theron the Scene 1 note, he makes the connection aloud: *"Whoever wrote that note and whoever designed this door were thinking about the same thing."* He does not yet explain what that means. He is working something out.
- **Bonus information (Theron volunteers unprompted):** He tells players that Corven built CONSENT into the door eleven years after the ritual, not before. Which means Corven understood what had gone wrong before the city did — and built a door that named it, and then locked the evidence behind it. This is not in any document players will find on shelf 4-17-3. Theron knows it because he was here when Corven installed it.

**Relationship effect:** Theron's trust advances immediately. He walks into the restricted stacks with the players rather than waiting at the door. He is not yet their ally — but he is no longer a gatekeeper. He has become something closer to a collaborator.

---

### Partial Success — Needed Theron's Help, Asked Good Questions

Players got to CONSENT through dialogue with Theron rather than pure independent reasoning. They showed genuine engagement — pushed back on his hints, offered wrong answers that revealed their thinking, asked why the door was built this way rather than just what the answer is.

**What they gain:**

- CONSENT, the door open, access to the restricted stacks.
- A developing relationship with Theron. He has seen them think. He knows they are curious rather than merely procedural. His trust level is **Cautious-Positive** — he will not volunteer the bonus information about Corven building the door post-ritual, but if asked directly in Scene 3, he will confirm it.
- If players asked good questions during the riddle exchange ("What is Corven's door actually asking?" / "Why would a ritual archivist care about the difference between a gift and a theft?"), Theron's response plants a seed: *"I've been asking that for eleven years. The documents inside may not answer it cleanly."* This is honest. They will find confirmation of the what in Scene 3, not the why. The why is Session 2.

**What they do not get yet:** The unprompted detail about Corven's timing. Theron does not give it unless asked.

---

### Minimal Success — Theron Guided Completely, Players Passive

Players attempted the door, failed enough times to trigger the one-hour lockout, and Theron arrived and said: *"Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again."* He walked them to the answer through directed questions. Players followed his lead without pushing back or asking about the logic behind the riddle.

**What they gain:**

- CONSENT, the door open, access to the restricted stacks.
- Access granted, but the moment belongs to Theron rather than to the players. He is already managing the process when they step across the threshold. His trust level is **Neutral** — he does not distrust them, but he is not yet sure what they are here for. He remains a gatekeeper, functionally.
- In Scene 3, Theron does not volunteer information beyond what is mechanically required. He answers questions he is asked. He does not offer the Corven timing detail unless players specifically ask what Theron himself believes Corven was thinking when he built the door.

**Residual effect:** Players who moved passively through Scene 2 will feel Scene 3's information as denser and harder to parse. This is not punishment — it is the natural result of not having developed working assumptions during the riddle. Theron will need to carry more of the explanatory weight in the puzzle, which is a heavier lift for him and a flatter experience for the players.

---

### Non-Failure (Scene Always Completes)

**This scene does not close with the door locked.** Theron always opens it eventually — by the third wrong attempt, he has been drawn upstairs, and the one-hour lockout is also a structural guarantee that the GM walks Theron in before the players can simply exhaust all options and stall. The question is never whether the door opens. It is what shape the players are in when it does.

The failure modes in this scene are relational and informational, not mechanical. A player group that tries to force the door or bribe Theron for the answer does not get stuck — they get a different version of the next scene.

### Theron Trust — What Changes Between Levels

**Engaged (full success):** In Session 2, Theron volunteers Corven's personal journal before players ask for it. He has decided they are the people Corven would have wanted to find it.

**Cautious-Positive (partial success):** In Session 2, Theron shows the journal when asked. He is honest about what it contains and does not soften the worst sentence in it.

**Neutral (minimal success):** In Session 2, the journal exists and players can find it in the puzzle — but Theron does not direct them to it. They have to work for the discovery rather than being handed the moment.

All three paths reach the same Session 2 revelation. The difference is how much table time the revelation costs and how warm the scene around it is.

## 3. Theron's Intercepted Restorer Note

### What It Is

Theron has been monitoring unusual activity around the Archive for months — Restorer members visible near the entrance, a request for access to Corven's materials that was refused under standing policy, footsteps in the stacks late at night on one occasion he cannot explain. He filed a note he found slipped under the Archive's service entrance three months ago. He meant to report it to the Chancellor's office. He did not. He is not entirely sure why.

### What It Says

The note is not the Scene 1 note — it is earlier Restorer correspondence, same author, same hand. Players who already have the Scene 1 note will recognize the handwriting immediately (History DC 10 or automatic if a player makes the comparison aloud).

> *"The second floor holds what Corven chose not to publish. He chose correctly. What is there is not dangerous in itself — it is dangerous only if someone reads it who already knows how to ask the right question.*
>
> *The door will stop most people. It has stopped everyone for eleven years.*
>
> *If someone reaches the second floor, the anchor's position will become readable from the documents alone. We cannot allow the anchor to be located before the time is right. Keep watch. Tell me when someone new arrives with access.*
>
> *The work continues.*"

The note is unsigned. The seal is the same containment diagram as the Scene 1 note — DC 14 Arcana to identify it as the same stamp or ring.

### What Players Learn From It

| Check | Information |
|---|---|
| No check (obvious) | Someone was watching the Archive for access attempts specifically related to Corven's documents — before the players arrived. This was not reactive surveillance. It was preexisting. |
| History DC 10 (with Scene 1 note in hand) | Same handwriting as the Scene 1 note. Same author. The Restorer leadership has been monitoring the Archive for at least three months. |
| Investigation DC 12 | The phrase "the time is right" implies the author has a timeline — a moment when the anchor's location *would* be appropriate to reveal. They are not suppressing the information permanently. They are managing when it surfaces. |
| Arcana DC 14 | The seal is the same containment diagram — same stamp, same trained hand. Ritual correspondence prepared by someone with formal training in sympathetic notation. |
| Investigation DC 16 | The phrase "readable from the documents alone" means the restricted stacks contain enough to locate the anchor without additional decoding — but only for someone who already has partial context. Players who have Fragment 1 of the Notation Key from Scene 3 are in exactly that position. |

### When Theron Shows It

**Theron shows the note unprompted** if any of the following apply:
- Players described the Scene 1 ambush to him (he listens, goes quiet, retrieves the file)
- Players solved the door riddle independently (he trusts them enough to share what worries him)
- Players asked about recent unusual access attempts or Restorer presence near the Archive

**Theron shows the note if asked** about Archive security, Restorer monitoring, or whether he has seen correspondence from the same handwriting as the Scene 1 note. He does not lie. He is not comfortable with having failed to report it, and that discomfort is visible (Insight DC 10: he held onto this note because part of him wanted to understand it before it disappeared into an administrative report).

**Theron does not show the note** if players were passive through the riddle scene (minimal success path) and have not mentioned Scene 1. In this case, the note exists and players can find it in Scene 3 if they search his desk.

### This Note as a Recovery Path

If the Scene 1 note burned and players have no handwriting sample to connect Edoran to the Restorer leadership, Theron's intercepted note provides the same data. The content differs but the voice is identical — same liturgical scripture pattern, same ritual terminology, same author. Players who put both notes together (Scene 1 note + Theron's note) can begin constructing a profile of Edoran without yet having his name.

The phrase "the time is right" also opens a question players will not be able to answer until Session 3: what is Edoran's timeline, and what is he waiting for?

## 4. Scene Transition Into Scene 3

### What Theron Says As the Door Opens

The exact line depends on how the scene resolved:

**Full success (players solved it, Theron witnessed):**

> *"I've stood in front of that door more times than I can count. I never once tried that answer."*

A pause. He looks at the open door.

> *"I think I was afraid to."*

He steps back and gestures the players through. He follows.

**Partial success (worked with players to reach it):**

> *"There it is. Eleven years."*

He says nothing else for a moment. Then he moves through the door with the players, pulling a small lantern from a hook on the wall.

**Minimal success (Theron guided):**

> *"Well. Now you have it."*

He holds the door open. He does not step through yet. He is watching to see if they are ready.

---

### What Players See First

The sentence that ends Scene 2 and opens Scene 3, delivered immediately as the door swings inward:

> *The restricted stacks smell of old paper and something else — something the rest of the Archive does not have. Lamp oil, yes, the same amber fuel. But underneath it: a faint mineral dryness, like stone that has not seen air movement in a long time. The shelves run floor to ceiling and the ceiling is further than expected — the room extends upward into a space the Archive's outer walls should not contain, or perhaps you misjudged the building on your way in. The amber lanterns are placed every fifteen feet along the central column. Between them, it is almost dark. Whatever Corven put here, he put it in a room designed to feel like it costs something to enter.*
>
> *Theron lights the lantern in his hand. The flame catches amber and holds.*
>
> *"Shelf 4-17-3," he says, quietly, as though saying it too loud might change something. "It's been waiting eleven years."*
>
> *He starts moving. The shelves rotate slightly as he passes — the mechanism responds to footfall. You are already inside the puzzle.*

This narration accomplishes three things: it gives players the five-senses reset a new scene requires, it carries Theron's emotional register into Scene 3 (quiet, careful, something at stake for him personally), and it triggers the puzzle immediately without a scene break. Scene 2 ends in the last word of the door opening; Scene 3 begins on the first footfall inside the stacks.

## 5. Failure Tracks — Interesting Failures

### A. Players Try to Force the Door

**What happens:** The door has no lock mechanism players can manipulate physically. It is stone, sealed with notation carvings that respond only to the spoken correct answer. Attempting to force it — shoulder, crowbar, cutting tools, raw damage — produces the low resonance tone on the first attempt, a sharper tone that carries to the floor below on the second, and brings Theron running before a third attempt becomes physically possible.

**Theron's arrival after a forcing attempt:** He rounds the landing corner already speaking — *"The door is not structural, it —"* — and then stops. He looks at whatever damage the players have made (cosmetic only; the door will not actually yield to physical force). He looks at the players. His first words: *"What are you trying to do?"* Not accusatory. Genuinely asking. He needs to know whether these are people who came here to find something, or people who came here to take something.

**What it costs:** Nothing mechanical. But the junior clerk has already gone downstairs to report unusual sounds from the restricted floor. The Archive's standing protocol means Theron now has a duty to log the access attempt formally — which means the Chancellor's office receives a report that her investigators attempted to breach the Archive's restricted materials by force. The Chancellor's composure will slip slightly when she reads it. She will not comment to the players directly, but they will notice at the Session 2 debrief that the way she asks about the Archive is more precise than the way she asks about anything else.

**Recovery:** Theron does not refuse to help. He is actually relieved the door held — it means the mechanism works. He walks them through the riddle. Trust level arrives at Neutral regardless of prior session performance.

---

### B. Players Bribe Theron for the Answer

**Does he take it?** Theron does not take bribes. Not on principle — he does not frame it that way — but because the riddle's answer is not Theron's to sell. He did not design the door. He does not know what Corven intended the riddle to cost. Taking coin for the answer would feel, to Theron, like charging admission to something that belongs to whoever was meant to find it. He cannot articulate this clearly if pressed. It will come out as awkwardness rather than refusal.

**What he says:** *"I — no. Keep it. That's not how this works."* If pushed: *"I don't know what the answer is."* This is technically true. He has suspicions he has not allowed himself to confirm. He has never spoken the word aloud to the door.

**What players can read:** Insight DC 10 — Theron is telling the truth about not knowing, but there is something else in his discomfort. He is not protecting the information for the Archive's sake. He is protecting something about himself, about this room, about eleven years of not opening a door he could have opened.

**What happens if players offer something other than coin** — information, a favour, the contents of the Scene 1 note: Theron's posture changes. He is not taking a deal. But he is listening. Information about the Restorers, about someone monitoring the Archive — this moves him. Not as a transaction. He becomes more forthcoming because he now believes the players have skin in the same game he does.

---

### C. Players Solve the Riddle Immediately and Theron Has Not Yet Arrived

The Archive's setup has Theron notified of new access to the restricted floor, but not immediately present — he is two floors below, cataloguing, when the junior clerk mentions that the Chancellor's investigators have arrived. He is on his way.

**If players speak CONSENT to the door before Theron reaches them:** The door opens. The notation carvings shift and the stone swings inward. There is no one to witness this except the junior clerk, who goes very still.

**What Theron does when he finds the door open:** He rounds the landing and stops at the top of the stairs. The door is open. The restricted stacks are visible beyond it. He does not enter. He stands in the door frame for a moment — not blocking it, just standing there — and then he says, to no one in particular:

> *"Someone finally did."*

He looks at the players — wherever they are in the stacks — and asks: *"How long did it take you?"*

The question is not competitive. It is the question of someone who has been carrying an answer they could not reach for eleven years, and who wants to know whether it was as hard for someone else as it was for them. The answer changes how he talks to players in Scene 3: if they solved it fast (one or two attempts), he becomes slightly quieter, slightly more careful, as though he is recalibrating something about himself. If they tried wrong answers first and worked through the logic, he is warmer.

**If players have already begun exploring the stacks:** Theron enters, locates them, and begins from where they are. He is not upset. He is — this is the correct word — relieved. He says very little for the first few minutes. He pulls his small lantern and moves with the players without narrating what he is doing. He is watching them navigate the room Corven built. He wants to see how they move through it.

### Party Resource State Entering Scene 3

Scene 2 costs no HP, no spell slots, no hit dice. But it takes real time — fifteen to forty minutes of table time depending on how deeply players engage with Theron. The resource the players are spending is **attention**, and Scene 3 arrives immediately on the back of it.

**State entering Scene 3 by path:**

| Scene 2 Path | Entering Scene 3 |
|---|---|
| Full success | Alert, invested, CONSENT already named and understood; Theron is a collaborator; players are oriented forward |
| Partial success | Engaged but slightly unsettled — they got the answer but not why it was right; Scene 3's puzzle will feel like the continuation of an incomplete thought |
| Minimal success | Passive; Theron is managing; Scene 3 will feel like being walked through a museum |

**Combined resource state (after Scene 1 + Scene 2):**

From Scene 1, the party entered Scene 2 at approximately 70–85% HP, 1–2 level-1 spell slots spent, possibly 0–2 hit dice used. Scene 2 burns none of this. The party entering Scene 3 is:

- HP: same as end of Scene 1 (70–85% typically)
- Spell slots: same as end of Scene 1
- Mental/attention state: Scene 2 has either sharpened them (full success) or softened them (minimal success) — this is not mechanical but the GM should read the table before Scene 3 begins

**If the party is significantly below 70% HP entering Scene 3:** Theron offers them a seat in the small reading alcove at the stacks entrance while he retrieves a lantern. This is a natural pause that functions as a short rest opportunity without the GM having to announce one. Players who take five minutes here recover hit dice at GM discretion. Theron does not comment on it. He understands that people who have just been ambushed in the Lowmark may need a moment before engaging with a puzzle.

## 7. Post-Scene GM Checklist

Before moving to Scene 3, confirm the following:

- [ ] **Success level recorded.** Full, Partial, or Minimal? Write it on the session sheet. It gates Theron's behavior in Scene 3 and Session 2. One word is enough: Engaged / Cautious-Positive / Neutral.

- [ ] **Theron's intercepted note — shown or not shown.** If shown: did players make the handwriting connection to Scene 1? If they did, mark the Edoran intelligence track as Advanced (they are building a profile without yet having a name). If not shown: is the note filed somewhere players can find it in Scene 3 during a search?

- [ ] **Scene 1 note cross-reference.** Did any player show Theron the Scene 1 note? If yes: did Theron confirm the connection aloud ("Whoever wrote that and whoever built this door..."), or did players reach the conclusion themselves? Note which — the GM-confirmed version is less satisfying in Scene 3 than the player-reached version.

- [ ] **Junior clerk status.** Present and noted? If players spoke to him before or after the riddle, he may surface again in Session 2 (Edoran's Archive contact). If players ignored him completely, he remains background texture. No action required — just know which version of the clerk the players will recognize if he appears again.

- [ ] **Theron's timing detail.** Did Theron volunteer (or get asked about) the fact that Corven built the door eleven years after the ritual, not before? If yes: players are ahead of the Session 2 revelation curve by one step. If no: this lands harder in Session 2 when Theron reveals it in the context of the journal. Mark which.

- [ ] **CONSENT moment — delivery assessment.** Did the word land? See Section 8 below. If the moment felt procedural rather than resonant, seed one reference in Scene 3's opening narration — Theron touching the notation carvings on his way past the door, or the word still visible on the interior door frame. The players named it; the environment should confirm it.

- [ ] **XP tally updated.** Add Scene 2 XP bonuses to the tracker. Do not announce yet.

- [ ] **Transition narration delivered.** The five-senses Scene 3 opening (see Section 4) should flow without pause from the door opening. No break in narration, no "okay, so now we're in Scene 3." The door swings and the sentence begins. Prepare it before play.

## 8. "What They Remember" Test

### The Sentence Players Quote After the Session

*"The door just... opened. Because someone said the right word."*

Variants:
- *"We've been fighting Restorers and chasing notes and the whole time the answer was a single word carved into a door."*
- *"Theron said he'd been standing in front of that door for eleven years. He was afraid to try it."*

### The Moment CONSENT Unlocks the Door

The mechanical delivery is simple. One player speaks the word. The GM says the carvings shift — or does not say it yet, holds a half-second, lets the table be uncertain — and then describes the door swinging inward. The notation moves first, then the stone, then the air from the room beyond reaches the players' faces. The sequence matters: the mechanism responds before the door opens, which confirms the answer without removing the satisfaction of the door's actual movement.

**What to deliver, word for word:**

> *The notation carvings catch the lanternlight for a moment — they move, slightly, in a pattern that is not random — and then the door swings inward. Not fast. Just open. The room beyond is darker than this corridor and smells of old air and stone.*

Then stop. Hold the beat. Do not narrate what is inside yet. Let players look. The door has been closed for eleven years. What is on the other side deserves two or three seconds of table silence before the GM describes it.

**How to deliver it so it lands:**

The failure mode is delivering the door-opening as a mechanical confirmation: *"Okay, the door opens, you can go inside."* That removes all the weight from the word.

The correct delivery is to make the carvings move first — describe the notation responding, visibly, before the door does anything. This establishes that CONSENT was the correct name, not just a password. The door is not locked by a code. It is locked by a concept. When the carvings shift, players see the concept recognized.

Then the door opens.

**If a player says the word in a question — "Is it CONSENT?" — rather than speaking it to the door:**

Do not let it work. Gently redirect: *"You'd need to say it to the door."* The distinction matters. Players who are asking whether they're right are still asking Theron, asking the GM, asking each other. The riddle requires them to commit to the word — to say it to the door as a statement, not a query. A player who asks "is it consent?" and then turns to face the door and says "CONSENT" has taken a real step. A player who asks and waits for the GM to confirm is not there yet. The door makes this distinction for you.

**The moment works when:**

A player says the word and the table goes slightly quiet before the GM responds. That is the table holding its breath. That is the scene working. The GM should hold the pause slightly longer than feels comfortable before beginning the notation description. Two seconds of silence after the word is spoken is the scene's peak moment. Do not rush it.

**What Theron remembers about this moment (if asked in Session 2):**

> *"I've stood at that door probably thirty times. I always found a reason not to try. I told myself I hadn't worked out the riddle. I think the truth is I knew the answer and I wasn't sure I was ready for what was behind the door."*

He does not say this in Scene 2. If players ask him in Session 2 — after they know what was behind the door — this is what he says.

That is the "what they remember" test passing: players who ask that question are the ones who registered, in Scene 2, that the door cost Theron something. That registration only happens if the CONSENT moment was delivered as a threshold rather than a transaction.
