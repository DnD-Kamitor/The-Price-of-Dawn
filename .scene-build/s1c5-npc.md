## Brother Edoran — The Restorer

*Has combat stats. Will not use them unless attacked. If violence is brought to this lane, he will not run and he will not fold, but he will stop fighting before he is killed and tell players they are looking for a harder fight than him. The combat is a failure state for the scene, not a path through it.*

---

### Physical Description

Late 50s. Grey temples, grey in his beard, the particular weathering of a man who has spent decades outdoors at odd hours. His robes are dark wool — practical, not ceremonial — with a plain cord at the waist. No insignia. No overt symbols of the Auris faith he left, or the movement he built. He carries a quarterstaff, walking-staff length, that he has not raised.

He moves with the economy of a much younger man: not quickly, not with performance, just without wasted motion. He stands still when standing still is appropriate, and that is most of the time.

His eyes are grey. When he is choosing his words carefully — specifically about consent, specifically about the Dawnborn — there is a visible pause before he speaks. Not a hesitation. A deliberation. He chooses every word about consent with effort that is visible if watched for.

When he is hit by something he did not anticipate — a player who finds the crack — he goes still. Not frozen. Still. It is different from his default patient stillness: it is the stillness of a man who has been brought up short by something he has not thought about in the specific way it was just presented.

### Want / Fear / Lie

| | |
|---|---|
| **Want** | Someone to understand what the Dawnborn are being called to — not just what is being taken from them. He has been having the wrong argument for twenty years: people hear "the Dawnborn should complete the ritual" and respond with "but they'll die," as if he has not accounted for this. He has accounted for it in excruciating detail. He wants one conversation where the person on the other side starts from the same place he does — where the question is not whether the cost is real, but what the cost means — and reasons their way to a different answer if they can. He has never had that conversation. Players, tonight, are the closest he has come. |
| **Fear** | That the consent argument is being used to delay the ritual until the city collapses — and that he will have been right all along about the urgency, and it will not have mattered because the decision came too late. He has watched the grey sickness spread for twenty years while the theological and political arguments continued. Every year the sickness advances, he is more afraid. He does not show this fear. He has organized it into the precision of his argument, which means the precision sometimes tips into urgency in ways he does not catch. |
| **Lie** | "I'm presenting philosophy." It is grief. He has been presenting grief since his daughter died, in the most organized and rigorous form grief can take, and he has convinced himself — most days — that the organization has transformed the grief into something else. It has not. The philosophy is real. The grief is also still real. He built the movement on both. He has not examined the seam between them in years and is not sure he could stand to. |

### History: The Restorers

Edoran was an Auris priest for eighteen years before Mira fell sick. He believed in the sun's return as a matter of faith — the abstract faith of an institution that has been waiting for so long that the waiting has become its own practice, disconnected from the thing waited for.

Mira's illness changed this. He spent three years watching what the grey sickness cost in specific, daily, particular terms. He watched her learn the managed cough. He watched her stop making plans past the next season. He watched her have the particular dignity of a person who knows they are dying slowly and refuses to make it someone else's burden. She was seventeen. She had not gotten to do most of what she had planned.

When she died, he left the priesthood inside a month. Not in protest — he was not performing a break. He simply could not continue the institutional patience of the Auris faith, which had been waiting for the sun's return for fifty years with a composure that now struck him as indistinguishable from indifference.

He spent the following two years in the Archive. He found what there was to find. He read Corven's design documents, the fragmentary Notation Key, the records of the anchoring ritual that had misfired. He understood, with the rigor of a man who had nothing else to do, what the Dawnborn were and what completing the ritual required.

He then spent eighteen years building a movement around that knowledge.

The Restorers are forty people in Varenhold's lower districts who believe: the Dawnborn are called; their consent is the form the calling takes; and the theological and political arguments about how to "help" them that are conducted in chancelleries and archive reading rooms are a form of delay that kills people like Mira by the hundreds every year the sun stays down.

They are not violent by doctrine. The S1C1 intercept — the zealots at Lowmark Junction this morning — was unusual. Edoran ordered it to buy time to position himself for this conversation. He has not ordered direct action before. He will not order it again for the rest of Session 1.

### History: His Daughter Mira

Mira Edoran. Born twenty-four years before this campaign, in a quieter Varenhold, in the years when the grey sickness was thought to be a cyclical condition that would ease. It did not ease. She fell sick at fourteen.

She was, by every account Edoran has told people who did not know her name, a person of specific qualities: she read voraciously, she argued, she had opinions about theology that were better formed at fifteen than his had been at thirty, and she wanted to study the Archive. She had not gotten there.

She died at seventeen, in winter, in a room not unlike the Dawnhall, with the same grey pallor and the same carefully managed breath. She did not get to see the sun. The sun has been down for fifty years and she was born inside that fifty years and she never saw it once and she died for a condition that — Edoran believes — the Dawnborn's ritual would cure.

He has not spoken her name in public since the memorial service fourteen years ago. He has spoken about "the people the grey sickness takes" and "the cost of delay" and "what is owed to those who cannot wait for another decade of philosophical caution." All of those phrases, without exception, mean Mira.

### His Crack

The daughter question. Any version of it:

- *"Why didn't your daughter get to choose?"*
- *"Did Mira consent to dying of grey sickness?"*
- *"You talk about the Dawnborn's consent. Your daughter didn't get a consent question."*

He stops. This is not the stopping of someone struck by a new argument — he has thought about this. This is the stopping of someone for whom the argument has always been present, underneath everything, and who has never heard it said aloud by someone outside his own head.

He does not answer immediately. If the silence is held — if players do not fill it with follow-up — he says, after a beat long enough to be visible:

*"She didn't."*

Two words. He does not elaborate. He does not explain what that costs him or why it has not changed his theology or what he has done with the asymmetry between the death she did not choose and the consent he is asking the Dawnborn to exercise. He has done a great deal with that asymmetry, over fourteen years, in private, and none of it is in those two words.

He then returns to the scene. Not immediately — there is a beat. Then he returns.

**GM instruction:** Do not let Edoran explain. Do not add a follow-up line. The scene changes shape after "She didn't." Players feel the weight shift. Let it shift. Then let him return to the scene without resolution, because there is no resolution. That is the point.

### Relationship to Other Antagonists

**Harran Lecht (Chancellor's Reckoning):** Edoran thinks Lecht is a blunt instrument. He thinks Lecht's approach — political pressure, administrative coercion, the quiet removal of Dawnborn autonomy through procedural means — will produce the worst possible outcome: a ritual completed under coercion that collapses at the moment of release, because Corven built consent into the design as an engineering requirement, not an ethical nicety. Edoran has read the original documents. He knows the ritual will fail if the consent is not genuine. He is watching Lecht build a machine that will produce the wrong result. He has not found a way to stop it. They have not spoken directly in three years.

**Keseph (the Scholar's faction):** Edoran thinks Keseph is a coward who uses philosophical complexity as a way to avoid action. He is not entirely wrong. Keseph's position — that the ethics of consent in cases of world-historical sacrifice require further examination before any action is appropriate — strikes Edoran as indistinguishable from an ethics-washing of indefinite delay. He has no personal hatred for Keseph. He finds him mildly contemptible.

**Sera Voss:** He respects her completely. Her consent is genuine, freely arrived at, held with the quality he has spent twenty years arguing the consent should have. He cannot tell her that — any contact with him would compromise her position, and he is aware enough to know this. He finds her willingness privately important to him in a way that has nothing to do with the ritual. He also, in the part of himself he does not examine, finds it insufficient: one person's peace, privately arrived at, does not answer the question of the other nine or the city that will not survive without the sun. He needs the whole arc. He cannot have Sera's peace without the arc.

**Theron Waide:** He knows Waide. They were briefly in contact when Edoran was still a priest and Waide was beginning his archive work. The contact ended badly — not with hostility, but with a divergence so complete that neither has sought the other out since. Edoran thinks Waide destroyed his notes because he could not accept what the notes meant. He is right. He does not know what to do with being right about this.

### Dialogue Lines — Full Reference

**Opening line (mandatory):**
> "You have no idea what you are looking for."

*(Quiet. Not an insult. An observation made with precision by someone who has watched people search for a long time.)*

---

**If players do not approach and try to go around:**
> "I'm not stopping you."
*(He lets them pass. If they are clearly departing without engaging, he says, to their backs:)*
> "The woman you just met knows what she is. She chose. You don't know yet what she chose within."

---

**On what players found in the Archive:**
> "You found the phrase 'living conduit.' You think it's a mechanism. It's not a mechanism. It's ten people. You found Sera Voss tonight. Now you know two things simultaneously: what she is, and what she's chosen. Those two things together are the whole question."

---

**On whether the Dawnborn should decide for themselves:**
> "I'm not asking them to die. I'm asking them to complete what they carry. The distinction matters."

---

**On consent — his theological position:**
> "The consent is not the precondition. The consent is the form the act takes. Corven understood this. He built it that way. Without genuine consent the ritual does not complete — not because of ethics, because of engineering. Your opinion about what they should choose is irrelevant. Mine is irrelevant. What matters is that they understand what they are being asked."

---

**On the Restorers (if asked about this morning's intercept):**
> "I needed time to be in a position to speak with you. The intercept this morning was a means, not a doctrine. The Restorers do not use direct force as a matter of practice. This morning was unusual."

---

**If asked why not just tell the Dawnborn directly:**
> "Most of them know. They have known for some time. The question is not information. The question is will."

---

**On Harran Lecht:**
> "Harran Lecht is going to get people killed. Not the Dawnborn — by coercion the ritual fails; Corven was clear on this. The people who will die are the ones in the room you just left. Every year the ritual does not complete, more of them. Lecht does not understand what he's building."

---

**If players ask what he wants from them:**
> "I want you to understand what you are investigating before you report back. You have half the picture. The half you have is true. It is not sufficient."

---

**On the grey sickness:**
> "The sickness has been advancing for fifty years. You saw what it looks like tonight. I have been watching it for longer."
*(He does not add anything. He lets the weight land.)*

---

**On the Dawnborn's deaths specifically:**
> "If you have found evidence that the mechanism works without their deaths, bring it to me as well as to anyone else. I have not found that evidence. I have looked."

---

**On whether he knows about the Inversion:**
*(He does know. Fragment 2 of the Notation Key. He will not volunteer this.)*
> *(If asked directly about an alternative to the deaths:)* "The Archive has more than you've found. I am not the right person to show you where."

---

**On players' mission from the Chancellor:**
> "The Chancellor wants to understand what happened fifty years ago. That is a reasonable thing to want. The problem is that understanding it will tell you what has to happen next, and the Chancellor will have opinions about that, and his opinions may not align with the engineering requirements of the ritual."

---

**Exit line (mandatory):**
> "The Restorers will not trouble you again tonight."

---

**If players attempt to detain him after his exit line:**
> "I've said what I came to say. The lane is open. Go."

*(He steps around them. He does not look back.)*

### His Tells

**When being careful about consent:** Visible pause before answering. He chooses every word. This is noticeable after two or three instances — the precision is not the normal precision of a careful speaker; it is the precision of a man who knows that the line between facilitation and coercion runs through word choice and has been watching himself for twenty years.

**When he is near the grief:** He becomes more still than usual. His normal stillness is patient. The stillness when he is close to Mira is heavier — the particular density of a man carrying something that is not going anywhere.

**When he is genuinely surprised:** Very rare. If players say something he has not thought before — a genuine philosophical challenge, not a familiar objection he has answered a hundred times — he goes still, and then he considers it, and then he answers it on its merits. He is not closed. He has simply answered most things already.

### Combat Profile (Failure State)

**Brother Edoran:** AC 12 (robes), HP 28, Speed 30 ft. Quarterstaff: +3 to hit, 1d6+1 bludgeoning. He will not attack first. He defends if attacked. When reduced to 14 HP: *"You're looking for a fight. You'll find a harder one."* He stops fighting. He does not flee.

**Restorer Zealot (×4):** AC 12 (robes), HP 16, Speed 30 ft. Shortsword: +3 to hit, 1d6+1 piercing. They fight defensively — their goal is delay, not harm. They do not pursue if players withdraw.
