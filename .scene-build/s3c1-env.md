# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: Environment & Five Senses

<details><summary>👁 Sight: Chandler's Block from the South Approach</summary>

The alley opens ahead of you into Chandler's Block. Fifteen feet wide here — usually enough for a cart and two pedestrians. Today it is full.

Thirty people. Mostly standing. A few have shifted their weight enough times to find somewhere to lean — a wall, a neighbor's shoulder. Three placards are visible above the crowd, hand-lettered on rough board. One reads: **WE GAVE THE DAWNHALLS EVERYTHING / WHAT DID IT GIVE US BACK**.

At the center of the crowd is a woman. She is not standing on anything. She is not shouting. Everyone else in the crowd is oriented toward her the way water orients toward a drain — not consciously, but accurately. She has a placard too, though she's holding it at her side, the lettered face toward her hip. The reverse is visible if you get close enough: charcoal, smaller, a different hand.

Above the chandler's shop — second floor, left of center — a window is lit. The amber glass throws a faint warm stripe across the flagstone. Someone is up there. The window has not moved.

Across the alley, set back from the crowd's edge, a café with a cloth awning. One table inside, visible through the window. A man. A cup of tea. He has not moved it in longer than you have been watching.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Three sight elements to establish in the opening description: (1) the crowd as a unified body oriented toward Senna, (2) the lit window above — Isolde is watching, (3) Davan Crel at the café. Players do not need to notice Davan yet. But describe the café. Let them decide whether to look.

Senna's placard face is toward her hip — the crowd-facing side reads the political slogan. The charcoal reverse is personal. DC 10 Perception to read it from ten feet if players move closer. It does not require a roll if they walk up to her.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>👂 Sound: What a Crowd Sounds Like After Hours</summary>

Not shouting. That's the first thing.

You have heard crowds shout. This is not that. This is what a crowd sounds like after the shouting has been done and nothing happened and they are still here.

Someone coughs. A placard taps rhythmically against a shoulder — the holder not thinking about it. Two people speaking in low voices at the crowd's left edge, not arguing, just checking. The flagstones amplify a child's boot somewhere in the middle of the block.

Then a voice, flat and carrying: *"They're still in there."*

Not directed at you. Directed at no one in particular. The woman at the center said it and the crowd heard it and nothing changed. She said it the way you say a fact you have said many times — not for effect, but because it is still true.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** Senna's line — *"They're still in there"* — should be the first thing players hear her say. She is not performing. She is accounting. She is tracking whether the building's occupant is still present and she has been doing this for hours. The flat delivery is important: she is exhausted in the specific way that grief becomes exhaustion when you have been angry about it for long enough.

Do not have her shout. Do not have the crowd chant. The silence is more unsettling than noise would be.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>👃 Smell: Lowmark Alley at Mid-Morning</summary>

Cold flagstone and close-packed wool — the smell of people who have been standing in one place since before dawn. Lamp oil from the café across the alley, burning low. The dry, slightly chalky smell of the dust the crowd's shuffling has raised from the flagstone seams.

Under all of it: the city's ambient cold. Fifty years without direct sunlight means Varenhold never fully warms. The stone stays cold. The streets stay cold. The cold is not dramatic. It is simply permanent.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The cold detail is thematic, not decorative. Varenhold has been in twilight for fifty years. The cold is geological now — it has had time to sink into the buildings. Players who live here don't notice it consciously. Players from outside do. Either way, it is the city's most honest characteristic.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>✋ Touch: The Physical Approach</summary>

The crowd's warmth reaches you from twenty feet — not comfortable warmth, but the specific warmth of many bodies standing close together long enough that their heat has become collective. You feel it as a wall before you feel any individual person.

The flagstone underfoot is slightly slick. Morning damp that hasn't burned off — there's nothing to burn it off. The alley walls close in: Chandler's Block is fifteen feet wide at the south entrance and narrows slightly toward the building. The crowd fills it completely. There is no clean path through.

To the north, before the crowd, a lane angles off to the left — eight feet wide, stacked with crates along one wall. Dimly visible from the alley mouth if players look for it. It leads around to the building's north side.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The side lane (flanking route) is visible from the alley mouth on a DC 12 Perception or Investigation check. Players who actively describe scanning the alley get it at DC 10. Do not volunteer it — but do not hide it. It is physically there. The crates along one wall mean it requires DC 10 Acrobatics to move at full speed; normal movement is unimpeded, just slower.

The crowd warmth as a physical wall is intentional: this is the first moment players realize the crowd is not just a visual obstacle but a spatial one.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🌫 Atmosphere: What Is Actually Happening Here</summary>

Senna Kard is not performing anger.

She has been here since before the amber lanterns were trimmed for day-use. She organized thirty people — dock workers, parents, a few older residents from the Ashring — not through speechmaking but through word of mouth and the specific credibility of someone who has already lost something. Her husband died of grey sickness two years ago. Her daughters are nine and six. She is not here because she believes this will work. She is here because Nia is nine and Brek is six and she could not stay home.

The crowd is oriented toward her because she is the clearest thing in the space. She is not elevated. She is not louder than anyone else when she speaks. She simply knows exactly why she is standing there, and that certainty is visible.

The lit window above is Isolde's. She has been up there since the crowd arrived. She has not come down.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM:** The scene's emotional register is exhaustion, not rage. The crowd has moved past the productive part of anger into the part where you stay because leaving would mean admitting it didn't work. Senna is the only person in the crowd who knows, consciously, that it probably won't work — and she is still here.

This is important for how players approach her. If they treat her as a rabble-rouser to be managed, they will fail every social check. If they treat her as a person doing the only thing she could think of to do, they have a chance.
:::

</details>
