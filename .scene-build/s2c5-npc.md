## Harran Lecht — Commander, the Reckoning

### Physical Description

Harran is fifty-one and looks like someone who has been sleeping adequately and eating regularly despite everything, because he considers physical discipline part of the job. His hair is cut short, greying at the temples. He carries the slightly forward lean of a man who spent his formative military years in narrow corridors and never fully corrected it. He wears studded leather tonight rather than parade armour — he did not come here to make a ceremonial point. He came to assert jurisdiction and leave.

His longsword is on his hip, hilt visible, not drawn. This is also not a ceremonial choice. He keeps it visible so that his right hand near it reads as habit rather than threat. It is both.

When he is lying, he touches the hilt. Not dramatically — a slight shift of the right hand to the pommel, contact for a moment, then away. He does not know he does this.

When he is genuinely angry — not performatively stern, genuinely angry — he goes very quiet. Not colder. Just quieter. The voice drops to a conversational register. The eyes hold without blinking. Players who read people for a living will recognise this. Everyone else will simply feel something change in the room.

He looks at players the way a practised administrator looks at a form he is not sure is filled out correctly — assessing, clinical, not unkind. He is not unkind. This is important. He is not the cruel antagonist. He is the rational one, and the rational one is sometimes more frightening.

### OGAS

| | |
|---|---|
| **Occupation** | Commander, the Reckoning — the city's emergency enforcement arm, operating under a charter signed by the last three Chancellors. He has held this post for nine years. Before this, infantry officer, two campaigns. He knows how to run a contested space without collateral damage and without losing people unnecessarily. He is genuinely good at his job. |
| **Goal** | Control access to the Ashring so the Inversion ritual fires on the Reckoning's timeline, not the Dawnborn's. This means preventing the Dawnborn from reaching a freely-organised Inversion — which requires all ten stones occupied by consenting Dawnborn simultaneously — before the Reckoning has completed its own preparations. What those preparations are: coerced compliance, where the Dawnborn are persuaded that the alternative to the ritual is worse than the ritual. Not torture. He is not a torturer. The persuasion is rational, documented, and will work on seven of the ten. He knows the three it won't work on. He has not decided what to do about them yet. |
| **Attitude** | Professional contempt. This is not contempt born of cruelty — it is the contempt of a man who has been solving resource problems for nine years and believes the players are treating a logistics crisis as an ethics seminar. He does not hate them. He thinks they are naive. Naive in a specific way: they believe that the right answer arrived at correctly is better than the wrong answer arrived at in time. He has watched cities fail over this distinction. He believes cities fail because good people get so focused on the right process that they miss the deadline for any outcome at all. |
| **Secret** | He knows about Isolde Maren's transfer method. He has known for eight months — the Reckoning has been watching her since Keseph Vyne's people flagged her access revocation as a potential source of delay. He has not disclosed this knowledge publicly because it gives the Dawnborn leverage: *"Wait — we don't have to die, there is another way, we need more time."* The city cannot afford the time that option creates while it is being developed. He believes this. He may be right. He is also wrong in a way he cannot yet see: Isolde's method may be closer to completion than he knows, because he has only had access to the reports on her work, not the work itself. |
| **Want** | The ritual completed before food stores collapse — at latest eight months from now, ideally three. Ten deaths or ten consents, in that order of achievability. He would prefer consent because a coerced Inversion risks partial failure, and partial failure means some Dawnborn survive with reduced anchoring, which means the energy release is incomplete, which means the ritual has to be attempted again. He does not want to do this twice. He is, despite himself, invested in doing it correctly. He is not invested in the Dawnborn's perspective on what *correctly* means. |
| **Fear** | That he will have traded his integrity for nothing. That the Reckoning pushes the ritual through, the city holds for a year, and then the grain routes collapse anyway, or the eastern power moves in the power vacuum, or something else the players were right about happens, and he was the person who made the hard choice that turned out to be wrong. He does not show this. He has trained himself not to show it. It is the thing underneath everything else he does. |
| **Lie** | *"I'm protecting the city."* True. Provably true in the narrow sense — everything he is doing is oriented toward Varenhold's survival. Not true in the sense that matters: he is not protecting the people in the city who are Dawnborn. He has constructed a category difference between *city* and *the Dawnborn* that allows him to act for the former at the expense of the latter. He knows he has done this. He calls it triage. He does not examine it too closely at night, which is why he sleeps adequately rather than well. |

### His One Crack

*"My daughter lives in Lowmark."*

If a player invokes this directly — uses her existence against his position, points out that grey sickness spreads furthest in Lowmark, or asks what she thinks of the Emergency Powers charter — he pauses. Exactly two seconds. A real pause, not a performance.

He does not change his position.

He says: *"She knows what I do and why I do it."*

Then he continues.

What he does not say: she is eleven years old. He has not had a conversation with her about Emergency Powers or ritual sacrifice or the ethics of coerced consent. He has told her he keeps the city safe. She believes him. The pause is the space where that is true and not true simultaneously, and he does not have language for both at once.

Do not press the crack too hard. It is not a pressure point that collapses his resolve. It is a window into a person who is doing what he believes is necessary and lives with what that costs. Players who find the crack and treat it with care will see something true about Harran. Players who use it as a weapon will not.

### His Relationship to the Other Antagonists

**Edoran Maris (Restorer leader):** Harran respects Edoran's grief in the abstract. A man whose daughter is Dawnborn and who built a theology around her death rather than collapse — that is, in Harran's view, someone who has accepted the cost of the situation and organised his life around it. Harran thinks Edoran's theology is wrong in ways that matter (the emphasis on willing consent as an engineering requirement conflicts with Harran's timeline), but he respects the commitment. He would rather deal with Edoran than with players, because Edoran at least understands that someone is going to die.

**Keseph Vyne (Spire administrator):** Harran thinks Keseph is a coward. Not in combat terms — he has no information about Keseph's physical courage — but in the administrative sense: a man who creates institutional obstruction to avoid taking a clear position, who revokes access and restructures appointments rather than ever making a direct decision. Harran can work with Keseph because Keseph's obstructions are predictable. He does not admire him.

**Chancellor Ostenveld:** He has never met her personally. They communicate through staff. He is aware she hired investigators; he does not know whether this was to find the truth or to establish deniability. He suspects deniability. He is, in this, correct about her process and incorrect about her motives — she genuinely wanted to know, she was simply afraid of what she would learn.

**Varren Aldiss (Spire administrator who tipped him off):** His current intelligence source. Aldiss reported the Inversion inscription at the Ashring. Harran does not know Aldiss's motives for this — he treats it as a reliable source because it has been reliable. He has not asked why Aldiss is watching the Ashring.

### Key Dialogue — 12 Lines for Different Situations

**On arrival:**
> "The Reckoning holds jurisdiction over the Ashring site under the Emergency Powers charter. Stand aside."

**When players assert their own standing:**
> "You're investigators. You don't have standing here. I do."

**When asked what he wants:**
> "Access. And for this circle to not be occupied until the authorised ritual sequence begins. Both of those are within my jurisdiction. Neither of them requires your permission."

**When challenged on coercion:**
> "The charter authorises Reckoning oversight of ritual sites during emergency declarations. If the Dawnborn choose to proceed voluntarily under that oversight, they have every right to do so. I'm not preventing consent. I'm supervising it."

**When asked about the Inversion specifically:**
> "The inscription you found is centuries old and its parameters have not been validated by current Spire analysis. Until that analysis is complete, no ritual action at this site proceeds without Reckoning oversight."

**When pressed on why oversight means removing Aldric from his stone:**
> "Because an unsupervised occupant of a ritual site during an active investigation is an unsecured variable. We are securing variables."

**When asked why he's really here:**
> "Because the clock doesn't care about consent. The city will starve before you finish asking nicely."

**When players invoke the Dawnborn as people:**
> "I know who they are. I have their names, their addresses, their family members. I have not made this easier by knowing that. But it doesn't change the arithmetic."

**When losing ground — cornered or outflanked:**
> "You think you've won something tonight."

**His exit line (mandatory — delivered on Tactical Retreat):**
> "When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it."

**If any player asks why consent matters to him at all:**
> "It matters because a coerced inversion risks partial completion. I need this to work. Which means I need it done correctly. I define correctly differently than you do."

**If asked about Isolde Maren (he knows her name):**
*(He is still for a moment — this is his tell for someone who has just touched something he didn't expect.)*
> "That name is outside the scope of tonight's conversation." *(He does not deny knowing her.)*

## Aldric Stone — Retired Guard at Stone 9

Aldric is sixty-three. He was a city guardsman for thirty-one years, retired due to chronic knee problems two years ago. He has been drawn to his Primer Stone for as long as the Dawnborn have been aware of the Primer Stones — approximately four months, since the investigation began surfacing what the stones are. He does not know what makes him come here. He knows that when his hand rests on Stone 9 something in the stone changes — a warmth that isn't temperature, a brightness in the amber core. He calls this "the settling" because he does not have a better word.

He has been a guardsman. He is not afraid of Harran Lecht. He has served under commanders like Harran for thirty-one years and he knows what the Emergency Powers charter means when it is invoked against civilians. He is afraid of leaving the stone. He will not say this clearly. What he will say:

> "I know what happens if I leave it."

He does not know what happens mechanically if he leaves it — he does not have the technical language from Corven's notes. He knows it intuitively, the way people who carry something important know they're carrying something important. He has been standing at this stone for months. He is not going to leave it because Harran Lecht tells him to.

His stat block during combat: treat as Reckoning Veteran (same stats, same HP), but on the players' side. His tactical priority is remaining at his stone — he will use his action to stabilize himself against Drag before attacking. He says:

> "I'm not moving. You'll have to carry me."

And he means it.
