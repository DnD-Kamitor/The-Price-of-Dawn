# S3C1 — Lowmark Alley: Per-Archetype Personal Hooks

<details><summary>⚔ Soldier / Martial PC</summary>

You have protected civilians before. You have also been ordered to move through them.

You know what the correct tactical assessment of this situation is: the crowd is not armed, the crowd is not organized as a fighting force, and the fastest route to the objective is a controlled advance through the center. You have done this. You know how it goes. The crowd parts or it doesn't; if it doesn't, you apply more pressure until it does.

You also know what Senna becomes the moment the first person in your group puts a hand on her.

Not a combatant. She doesn't have the training for it and she's not trying to start a fight. She becomes something harder: a person who has been pushed by someone who should have known better. The crowd reads her face and not her words. If she goes from exhausted to something else, thirty people will make the same shift, and then you have a different problem.

The tactical read here isn't "can we get through?" — you can. The read is "what is this crowd in thirty seconds if we do it wrong?" You've seen that answer. It isn't good for anyone.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Martial PCs who describe reading the crowd tactically should get a free DC 10 Insight check: *they recognize the crowd is organized around one person, not a general sentiment; that means disrupting the center disrupts everything, for good or ill.*
:::

</details>

<details><summary>📚 Scholar / Investigator PC</summary>

Senna's question — *"What did it give us back?"* — is the campaign's central question from the outside.

She doesn't know about the Inversion. She doesn't know about the Transfer Method. She doesn't know that there might be an alternative path that preserves the Dawnborn. She knows that the Dawnhalls have taken forty years of her family's labor in the form of tithes, service obligations, and civic participation, and in return they have offered the grey sickness and a promise that the ritual is being studied.

That's not a political grievance. That's an accounting. She ran the numbers and the numbers don't balance.

You are an investigator. You understand ledgers. What Senna is doing in this alley is the equivalent of arriving in person when the letters aren't answered — she has exhausted the formal channels and she is now standing on the doorstep. If you had arrived at a research institution three times seeking answers and received the same non-answer each time, you might do the same thing.

The difference is that her daughters are nine and six and the grey sickness has a timeline, and you can see, looking at her face, that she knows the timeline.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Scholar PCs who ask about the political context of the protest should get: the Dawnhalls have been promising action for years; there's no formal accountability structure for that promise; Senna's question is technically unanswerable under current civic law. She's not wrong. There just isn't a mechanism.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🌿 Healer / Cleric PC</summary>

Grey sickness in two children. Ages nine and six. Early symptoms.

You know the progression. Fatigue first, then pallor, then joint involvement. Without sustained sunlight — real sunlight, not the amber-filtered half-light of the lanterns — the disease progresses in three to five years to full systemic involvement. Senna's daughters are in the early window. They have time. Not much, but time.

Senna has been watching their symptoms for months. She knows what she's looking at. She went to the Dawnhall healers three times this year and came away with the same answer — the situation is being addressed, continue current treatment, return in six weeks. She is a dock worker with two sick children and she cannot afford to wait for the situation to be addressed.

She has been here since before dawn.

You are looking at a parent who has done everything correct — brought her children to the healers, followed the guidance, participated in the civic structures — and arrived at the conclusion that the civic structures are not going to help her in time. And she is right. Not politically. Medically. You know the numbers. The timeline is real.

What she needs is not a healer. She needs sunlight or a Treatment Method that doesn't require sunlight. You are currently walking toward the woman who might have that second option. Senna doesn't know that. But you do.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Healer PCs who describe assessing the crowd for illness should notice: at least three or four people showing grey sickness pallor. This is not a political demographic — this is a medical one. Senna's crowd is disproportionately people whose families are sick.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🗝 Thief / Rogue PC</summary>

Davan Crel. Cold tea. Twenty minutes at a café table.

You notice things others don't — exits, watchers, the person who doesn't move right. Davan is the person who doesn't move right. He arrived before the crowd and he hasn't left. He is sitting at the window table with a cup of tea that went cold before you walked down this alley. He is not watching the crowd. He is watching the south approach — the direction you came from.

He was watching for someone coming down this alley. You came down this alley.

His coat is civic-quality — the kind of nondescript that gets made to order for people who need to be unremarkable. His boots are better than his coat. Someone is paying him enough to afford good boots and telling him to look like he can't. He has a folded piece of paper at his elbow that he hasn't consulted but hasn't put away.

He has already done whatever he came here to do.

The question isn't what he's watching. The question is what he's already sent.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Rogue/Thief PCs who specifically describe watching the perimeter rather than engaging with the crowd get DC 13 Perception to spot Davan — reduced to DC 10 because they're actively looking. If they succeed and describe approaching the café to confirm: the cold tea is visible from the doorway. They can make a DC 12 Sleight of Hand check to read the paper at his elbow without him noticing. The paper has Isolde's address and a time (this morning) written in a neat hand. It is not in code. He didn't expect to need code.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>👑 Noble / Diplomat PC</summary>

Thirty people with nowhere else to go, organized behind one clear voice, making one specific demand.

Senna Kard has done something that takes most political operatives years to learn: she has reduced a complicated grievance to a single question that cannot be answered with a bureaucratic deflection. *What did it give us back?* There is no good answer to that question from the Dawnhalls. The good answer doesn't exist yet. She knows that. She's not asking because she expects an answer. She's asking because an unanswerable question in a public space, repeated, is pressure.

She is better at this than most of the diplomats you have worked with. She has organized thirty people without a formal structure, kept them focused without a written agenda, and maintained the crowd's discipline through hours of waiting without a single visible incident. She did this while exhausted and grieving and wearing her dead husband's coat.

She is also completely wrong about one thing: she believes the Dawnhalls chose not to act. They chose. They had a reason. The reason is not good, but it exists. She doesn't know about the Amber Compact's suppression of the Transfer research. She is making her case against a set of facts that is missing its most important entry.

If you could give her that entry — Isolde's research, three years suppressed — you would change everything about what she is doing here.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Noble/Diplomat PCs who try to out-negotiate Senna in public will find she has already anticipated and pre-countered every formal rhetorical move. She has been thinking about this longer than they have. The correct move for a diplomat is to find what she doesn't know and offer it — not to win the argument she's already having.
:::

</details>

<details><summary>🕯 Restorer-Adjacent / Religious PC</summary>

The Restorers believe the Dawnborn should choose to die for the city. They have built a theology around it: the willing heart gives freely, the gift given under compulsion has no power, true sacrifice requires true choice.

Senna Kard believes the same thing. She arrived at it from a different direction.

She is not a Restorer. She has probably never heard Edoran's name in a theological context. But her line — *"If they die for this, I need to know they chose it"* — is the Restorer position without the doctrine. She got there through grief and logistics. She asked: *if someone is going to die for my daughters, I need to know they decided to.* That's not theology. That's accountability.

This is what Edoran's movement looks like from the outside when it hasn't been organized. Before the theology, before the structure, before the formal position — this is the question a parent asks when they understand that someone's death is being offered on their behalf.

Senna and Edoran are not allies. They don't know each other. But they would agree. And they would agree for reasons that have nothing to do with each other's reasons, which means the agreement runs deeper than either of them knows.

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
**GM hook:** Religious PCs who make the connection between Senna's position and Edoran's theology (DC 13 Insight or automatic if they quote Edoran to her) can use it as a bridge: *"There are people in this city who have been saying the same thing from inside the temple walls."* Senna's response: *"Then why is it taking so long?"* She is not won over by the comparison. But she is listening.
:::

</details>
