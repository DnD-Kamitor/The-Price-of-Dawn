Scene — Cube 2

Tomas Areth

Location: his study, surrounded by astronomical tables and dense notebooks  |  Type: The Puzzle
WANT An airtight, verified activation sequence    FEAR A calculation error that dooms the participants    LIE "I have the sequence under control."
Physical: early forties, gaunt and ink-stained.

He spots the puzzle box and goes still for a moment.

"Sit down. I need to show you something before we talk about the cube."

He opens the notebook to the sequence page. Eight columns of star designations, astronomical notation from fifty years ago.

"This is the activation order. Brightest to dimmest, as they appeared on the ritual night. This is how the cubes go into the box. I have checked it four times. I cannot check it a fifth — I have looked at it too long to see it anymore. I need someone else to go through it with me."

The Puzzle. He hands over the star charts and a rubbing of the Ashring dais's central star map carving. Each of the eight positions is marked with a constellation symbol; beneath each, in Corven's cramped hand, is the old pre-Twilight name.

Tomas has looked at these names for forty years and no longer sees them. A fresh eye will: the first letter of each name, read in order brightest to dimmest, spells FREEWILL. Corven did not choose the sequence. He chose the word and built the sequence to hide it.

But Tomas's copy has a transposition: the sixth and seventh names are swapped, so the acrostic doesn't read. Only a player pointing at the two swapped symbols and saying what the word is supposed to be breaks it open.

DC 14 Arcana to work through the astronomical order. Advantage if the party visited the Ashring dais earlier. Tomas can assist (also grants advantage) but cannot find the transposition himself.

On Success
The sequence confirms, the acrostic resolves. The moment someone says the word, Tomas catches the transposition: "There. I have been staring at that for three days and my eye kept sliding past it."

Then, very still: "FREEWILL. He left us a word, not a formula."
On Failure: 30 minutes of recalculation produces the correct order but the word is never found. The party gets the sequence without the confession. Tomas tolerates honest struggle but bristles at carelessness.

After verification, he pauses:

"Repeat that slowly. The part where everyone survives."

He confirms three things in order, and needs to hear each one before moving to the next:

  1. "Is Ellan Voss alive?" — Yes. He thought she was dead or captured.
  2. "All ten?" — Confirmed. Raises the mystery of the tenth vessel in his mind.
  3. "Freely willing, without coercion?" — He takes a full minute on this one.

He looks at the word on the page.

"Fifty years ago one of us said no. Ellan. And the whole thing stopped. He wrote the reason into the sequence, and none of us saw it until now. The ritual runs on people being free to refuse."
"Which means it can always stop again. Which means it was never going to be safe. I don't know whether that makes it easier to say yes or impossible."

He does not expect an answer.

"I will give you the sequence. But understand this: if this fails, if it is nine voices instead of ten, the sequence is now in the wrong hands. Not yours. Anyone who comes after you. I am trusting you with the mechanism. Do not use it like one."

Cube and verified sequence together. He does not separate them.

If pushed or pressured: "I know coercion when I hear it. Come back when you can speak plainly." He refuses until treated with respect.
Scene — Cube 3

Ysel Maren

Location: secluded Dawnhall courtyard, stone bench, amber sky  |  Type: The Overlooked
WANT Complete, unforced consent across all participants    FEAR That Cormac's hesitation will spread to the others    LIE "I have no doubts."
Physical: tall Ashfen woman, calm, imposing. Cube already in her coat pocket.
Sight: Secluded courtyard, stone bench, amber sky overhead.   Sound: Kitchen clatter and muffled conversation from inside.   Smell: Dried garden herbs, damp moss, kitchen smoke.   Touch: Chilled limestone bench.   Air: Crisp courtyard breeze.

She hears the party arrive but does not turn immediately. When she does:

"Sera sent you, or the stones did. Doesn't matter. You found me."

She reaches into her coat pocket. Does not hand the cube over.

"Ask me how I am."

Not a deflection. An earnest request from someone who has been carrying this alone while everyone around her managed Cormac.

Unlock: Ask how she is coping, and mean it
"I've been ready for seven months, and no one thought to ask. Everyone was too busy managing Cormac."

She surrenders the cube. Then:
"His stone remains dark. He's been spiraling at the Dawnhall for three weeks. I'm going to confront him, but I'll let you speak with him first."
If treated as a transaction: she pockets the cube. "Wrong approach. Speak to me like a person or come back later."
After the cube: Ysel heads toward the Dawnhall, setting up the Cormac scene. She will be watching when the party arrives.
Scene — Cube 4

Nin Fletch

Location: Ashring-Lowmark intersection, watching the crowd  |  Type: The Patience
WANT To break their habit of indecision    FEAR The finality of committing to a dangerous ritual    LIE "I haven't made up my mind." — they decided weeks ago.
Physical: forties, slight, unobtrusive. One hand in coat pocket around the cube at all times.

Finding them: DC 10 Perception or Investigation at the Ashring-Lowmark intersection.

They are standing at the junction watching the crowd move past them in both directions. They have been here for a while. The cube has worn a groove in the coat lining from being taken out and put back.

If players approach with urgency or apocalyptic stakes:

"I know. Give me a minute."

If players wait without pressing:

"It's easier with someone here. I couldn't decide which way to go by myself. Both ways felt the same and then neither way felt like anything at all."

Still not moving.

Unlock: One player asks, plainly — "Are you coming?"
No context needed. No stakes, no urgency. Just the question.

A pause. Long enough to be real.

"I've known for two weeks. I just needed someone to say it out loud."

They take the cube from their left pocket.

"I kept hoping the decision would feel different once I'd made it. It doesn't feel like anything. Maybe that means it's real."

Cube goes in the box. They do not watch where it sits. They are already looking back at the junction.
Hard-sell persuasion fails. Intimidation fails. Nin falls quiet and looks away. Patience only.
Scene — Cube 5

Orya Doss

Location: her Primer Stone, northern edge of the Ashring  |  Type: The Freebie
WANT To hand over her cube and proceed    FEAR Being overlooked or forgotten    LIE None. Remarkably frank.
Physical: late thirties, composed. Already at her stone when the party arrives.

She is already there. Sitting on the carved base of her Primer Stone, legs folded, hands in her lap, watching the amber overhead. She has been here since the fourth bell. She saw the box from thirty feet out.

"Good. I was starting to think I'd have to come find you."

She stands, brushes stone dust from her coat, walks toward them without hurry.

"I've been here since the fourth bell. Couldn't sleep. Came here instead."

She looks at the box.

"I decided a long time ago. I just hadn't told anyone, because no one had asked."

Cube already in her hand. She places it in the box herself. Her Primer Stone responds before she steps back — a low tone, brief, like something settling into place. She looks back at it once.

"There. Now it knows too."
"Cormac was at the Dawnhall two hours ago. Still sitting. You might want to get there before Ysel runs out of patience — in fairness, she has had enormous patience. But everyone has a limit."
No check required. Clean exchange after several harder negotiations.
Scene — Cube 6 (hardest)

Cormac Drell

Location: Dawnhall, communal table — then the alley behind  |  Type: The Wavering
WANT To not have to look at his own yes    FEAR That when he looks, he will find it was never courage    LIE "I stand by what I told Edoran." — he isn't sure there was ever a self behind that yes.
Physical: stocky dockworker, mid-thirties. Restless, visibly strained. Empty clay mug in both hands.
Sight: Amber lantern light. The long Dawnhall table. Cormac's hands around a clay cup. Ysel watching from across the bench.   Sound: Food sounds from the kitchen. The silence of a room waiting for someone to speak.   Smell: Warm bread. Lamp oil. Old wood.   Touch: The bench worn smooth by years of use. Clay cups warm from the kitchen.   Air: Old timber and smoke.

As the party steps in, Ysel breaks the silence:

"Do you still mean what you told Edoran?"

Cormac freezes. Sets the cup down — carefully, with both hands, the way you set down something you might need to pick up again. Then he leaves through the rear door into the alley.

In the alley. He is leaning against damp brick, eyes closed.

If a player follows and listens without demanding excuses:

"I said yes when I thought it would happen fast. Having weeks to think about it broke my nerve."

If they stay without pressing further, the darker thing — what he has not said to anyone:

"Do you want to know the part I can't look at? When I said yes to Edoran, I wasn't sure it was courage. I was never sure. A man can want to save his city, or he can want to be done with his own life, and from the inside those two things look exactly the same. I said yes nine weeks ago and I still don't know which one it was."
Unlock: Ask "why did you say yes in the first place?" then stay through the ugly answer without fixing it
This is not a dice roll. The wrong question is "will you still do it?" That only makes him set the cup down again.

The right question is why he said yes — and then the party must be able to hear an answer with no heroism in it. Cormac does not need to be told he is brave. He needs someone to sit with the possibility that he wasn't, and stay anyway.

If a player does that:

"Because I told nine people I would. And because they've been patient with me for three weeks. That's not a good reason. It's the only one I've got that's true."

Flag: CORMAC RENEWED.
If players do not follow up before Session 5 closes: his stone is dark at the Inversion Circle. The ritual cannot complete.
The RENEWED flag matters for the ritual ending. A Cormac who examined his consent and chose it again is the difference between Ending B (all survive) and Ending A (sacrifice).

Sample lines:

  • "I need you to stop asking me that." (if pressed before he's ready)
  • "I said yes when I thought it would be over before I had time to think about it again. It kept not being over."
  • "I don't know if I was saving the city or just done with myself. And I'm afraid to find out which, because if it's the second one, then saying yes again isn't sacrifice. It's just... giving up, with everyone watching."
Scene — Setup Only (cube in Session 5)

Lira Anwick — Healing House

Location: Lowmark Healing House ward  |  Cube NOT given today — setup visit only
WANT Her patients protected before she commits    FEAR That abandoning her clinic causes preventable deaths    LIE "I just need a few more days." — she has run out of time and knows it.
Physical: late forties, apron with amber medicine vials, hands always busy.
Sight: Lowmark Healing House ward. Sickbeds, three occupied. Lira at a grinding table.   Sound: Labored breathing, clinking vials, scraping pestle.   Smell: Crushed menthol, boiled bandages, pungent tinctures.   Air: Warm, sterile, and herbal.

She sizes the party up immediately.

"You're not patients. I know why you're here. There is someone under my care who needs me, and I will not abandon them without handover. Return tomorrow. I must arrange coverage first."

This is a pacing beat, not a failed check. She cannot be rushed today.

If pressed:

"You can push, or I can come to you tomorrow with a yes. Choose."

DC 14 Insight: She is not stalling. She is protecting someone, she knows exactly what her yes means, and she is not afraid of it. She needs to make the arrangement first.

Session 5: Lira arrives at the Ashring at dawn, cube in hand, alone. She made her arrangement. She will not explain it. If asked: "I found someone to stay." That is all.
Floating Encounter — Scene 3b

The Flowering Approach — Ixa

Triggers between any two cube scenes, or forced at end of Cormac scene if Ixa ignored the letter  |  Private: letter arrives for Ixa specifically  |  Ellan/Ixa recognition already played — Ellan in safe house
Sight: A pressed flower in a doorway crack. One petal too many. Center darker than it should be.   Sound: Nothing. The lane is empty. Someone was here a moment ago.   Smell: Faintly botanical. Not unpleasant. Not right.   Touch: The letter paper is slightly warm — not from the sun. From being held.   Air: The sensation of being watched from a direction that has no one in it.

The letter is addressed to "the one who should not exist, but does."

"We helped someone once. A woman in this city, many years ago, who loved someone the world said she was not permitted to love. We showed her it was possible. You are the outcome."
"We have been trying to make more outcomes like you. We have not always succeeded. The ones who did not succeed are still with us."
"Come to us. Or simply stop walking away. We will find each other."

This is for Ixa's player. Whether she shares the letter with the party is her choice.

If Ixa ignores the letter or the party moves to shut down the approach: The Flowering sends retrieval. Two Grasping Tendrils arrive. They are not violent in intent. They are extremely violent in body.

Secondary Objective — state before combat
Prevent Ixa from being carried off. The Tendrils grapple Ixa exclusively. If one gets Ixa grappled for 2+ rounds, a third Tendril emerges from a side alley to assist escape. Protecting Ixa matters more than defeating the creatures.

Grasping Tendril — quick stats (full block in encounter reference)

  • AC 14 | HP 91 | Speed 35
  • Resist: nonmagical B/P/S | Immune: charmed, frightened
  • Grasping Slam +7 | 2d6+4, grapple escape DC 16
  • Constrict (vs grappled): 2d8+4, DC 15 Str or restrained
  • Haul (BA): full move dragging grappled target
  • Target Lock: cannot be taunted off Ixa

At 0 HP: down but conscious. Still reaching for Ixa. Not dead. Eyes open. One wears a plain iron ring. One has a child's name tattooed on the inner forearm, cut through by whatever happened to the skin around it.

DC 12 Medicine: this is a person. Altered down to the bone. Still in there behind the eyes. Knows what was done to it. Cannot say.

After combat, a second pressed flower is tucked in the larger Tendril's clothing:

"We will try again. We always do. Please just stop walking away."

The party must decide what to do with the downed Tendrils. There is no clean answer.

If Ixa says she will go willingly: both Tendrils stop immediately and stand aside. Ixa going with the Flowering is a live Session 5 track. Do not resolve here.
