<summary>📄 What the Ritual Was Actually Trying to Do</summary>

**GM context - read before running this session.**

Corven's plan was simple in principle and catastrophic in execution. Varenhold was already famous for its amber light. Corven believed the city's geography (crossroads of three trade routes, the specific stone composition of the Ashring, the angle of the valley) made it possible to permanently anchor the sun's warmth to the city. Not magic. Engineering. The ritual would use ten freely-consenting people born in Varenhold as living conduits: they would channel the city's ambient solar energy into the Primer Stones and fix it there permanently. A second sun, rooted in the Ashring. The amber workshops forever. The city's golden age, preserved against entropy and politics and whatever came next.

When Ellan withdrew, nine consents had already begun to flow. The energy couldn't complete - but it couldn't return either. Nine had given everything. It stayed. Trapped in the stones, in the sky, in the blood of everyone born that night. Eternal twilight is Corven's ritual frozen mid-dawn: almost enough, not quite.

The Inversion is not an undoing. It is a completion. Fifty years late, with the element that was always missing.

**What Theron actually did:** Eleven years ago, Theron Waide found Ellan Voss's account of the stillbirth in a restricted Archive record. He located the preserved remains (the ritual's overflow energy had kept them intact in a way that shouldn't have been possible) and used Corven's technique to restart the child's heart. He told himself he was saving a life. He was also, without understanding it, borrowing a piece of the ritual's trapped light to do it.

The other Dawnborn carry the ritual energy the way water is carried in a vessel. The vessel and the water are separate things. When the Inversion completes, the water returns to the sky and the vessels remain.

Ixa is not a vessel. When Theron used the ritual energy to revive her, the energy did not pass through her - it became her. Her heartbeat is the ritual's heartbeat. Her warmth is the trapped dawn. When the Inversion completes and the light returns to the sky, she returns with it.

Theron knows this. He has known for three months. He told Edoran. He has not told anyone else. This is his guilt, not the revival, but what he understands now about what the revival was.

**What Ixa knows:** Edoran told her. Three months ago. She asked him directly, and he couldn't lie to her. She has had three months to make her choice. She is at the plaza this morning not because she was summoned. She decided to come.

She is the only person in the Ashring who has had time to fully examine what she is willing to do. She is not performing courage. She is not rushing toward anything. She is simply present, watching, waiting for the right moment - which she has understood, since Edoran told her, is not hers to create. It belongs to someone else.

**What players know:** Whatever they've figured out. If they ask Edoran directly, he tells them - quietly, precisely, once. If they ask Ixa directly, she tells them too. She does not soften it. She does not ask them to make it easier. If they don't ask: they may not know until after.

**GM note on player agency:** If players know and still complete the ritual (if they extend a hand knowing what it costs) that is one kind of story. If players don't know and the dawn rises and then they understand - that is another. Both are valid. This is the last session. Let the table choose the version they're in.

<summary>📄 The 11th Dawnborn - Ixa</summary>

**The count comes up wrong.**

Ten Primer Stones are assigned - each stone warming when its Dawnborn approaches. But there is an eleventh stone: no name, no warmth, standing slightly apart from the circle's geometry. And there are eleven people carrying something from the original ritual.

No one raises it directly. Players who count will find it.

If players raise it: Edoran, at the plaza edge, quietly: *"We found the eleventh three months ago. We didn't know what to do with it."*

**The eleventh is Ixa.** The original ritual was designed for ten. It failed with nine. What no one knew (what Corven couldn't have known) was that the tenth's refusal was not defiance but love: Ellan withdrew because she was carrying a child, and the child was the ritual's missing piece made flesh before she had a name for it. Ixa was always the eleventh. She wasn't born in time. That's all. Fifty years late. Still here.

*Physical:* The party knows her. She is tabaxi, adult, carrying the Crucible's ash-tattoos on her neck and shoulder - the scars of her birth and everything the Crucible made her survive afterward. Her hands are always warmer than the room. Not fever-warm. Consistently, inexplicably warmer. People who shake her hand sometimes look down at it afterward. She notices. She has always noticed.

She was dead at birth. The ritual's overflow energy kept something intact. Theron used it to restart her heart. The energy that revived her did not pass through her - it became her. Her warmth is the trapped dawn. Her heartbeat is the ritual's heartbeat. She has been alive her entire life powered by a piece of unfinished daybreak, and the Crucible spent years studying why their asset ran warm and never understood what they were measuring.

The stone warms when she approaches it. Edoran found out three months ago. He told Theron. Theron told him he had already known for longer. That conversation is visible in Edoran's face, the specific expression of someone who has been carrying something they cannot put down.

**GM note:** Ixa knows. She has known for three months. She is here because she decided to come. Give her player a breath before explaining anything to the table. This is not a combat reveal.

<summary>📄 Who Steps Back</summary>

With eleven Dawnborn and eleven stones, the original question of "who yields" transforms. No one steps back. But the reason no one has to die (the reason Ending B exists at all) is because Ixa does.

This is the arithmetic players have been doing wrong. They thought the choice was: which nine of ten survive. The real choice is: all ten survive, and the eleventh, who was not meant to be here at all, returns to where she came from. She was borrowed. The light borrowed her. The ritual's completion is the settling of that debt - not with grief, but with consent. Hers.

**GM note:** This is the campaign's final reversal. Five sessions about consent, sacrifice, and the arithmetic of who gets left out. The last answer is: no one gets left out. Everyone who was supposed to be here is here. Ixa included, who was never supposed to be here, and who is here anyway, and who gets to choose what that means.

The love that stopped the ritual is the same love that completes it. Ellan refused because she was carrying Ixa. Ixa completes the circle because she chooses to. She is not a victim of the ritual. She is not a sacrifice demanded by the city or by Corven or by anyone. She is a person who has had three months and made a decision. That is all. That is everything.

If Ellan is present: she steps onto her stone without speaking. She has been waiting fifty years to do this freely. She does not make it large. If a player is watching her face when she sees Ixa standing at the 11th stone (the stone that could only exist because she once refused) it is the expression of someone for whom everything finally makes a terrible, perfect sense.

*"I stepped away once. I am done stepping away."*

She steps on.

<summary>📄 Petra Vane - The Missing One in Greenhollow (Stone 6)</summary>

*Want:* To find peace without being coerced into martyrdom by either the city or the Reckoning.  
*Fear:* Being cornered and forced to make a permanent choice before she has found her footing.  
*Lie:* *"I'm just an herbalist now. The stones forgot about me thirty years ago."*

*Physical:* Early fifties, lean, silver-streaked hair bound in leather cord. Soil-stained fingertips, sharp herbalist's smock over travel clothes. She holds herself like someone who has spent weeks listening for the sound of boots on her gravel path.

**Location:** Greenhollow Village, Dusk Parishes (a half-hour ride through the dense twilight mist outside the Ashring wall). A damp settlement of slate-roofed cottages clustered around a dying, overgrown wayside shrine.

**Five Senses & Setup:**
- *Sight:* A low timber apothecary cottage behind rows of drying glass bell-jars. Thick grey ground-fog curling around elderberry bushes. In the road behind the party: fresh horse tracks and boot prints—Reckoning scouts have been asking questions here this morning.
- *Sound:* Rhythmic drip of condensation from slate eaves; the dry rustle of hanging lavender and marsh-mint bundles inside; distant crow calls.
- *Smell:* Pungent dried rue, boiled wintergreen, sharp turpentine, damp peat.
- *Touch:* Clammy, freezing parish mist clinging to skin; the cold iron latch of the apothecary door.
- *Air:* Still, heavy with moisture and old incense from the nearby shrine.

**The Approach:**
When players push open the creaking door of the cottage, the front counter is abandoned. A mortar and pestle sits half-ground on the counter, still moist.  
- **DC 12 Perception or Survival:** Notice scuff marks on the floorboards leading behind a heavy hanging drying rack of silver-thistle.  
- Behind the rack is a concealed cellar trapdoor, cracked open a fingernail's width. Petra is sitting on the steps below with a pruning sickle in her hand, listening to the room above.

When she sees the party (and sees they are not wearing Reckoning armbands):
She exhales slowly, lowering the sickle.

> *"You aren't Harran's hounds. They were at the inn twenty minutes ago asking for 'the woman who survived the Year of Ash.' I went out the back window."*

She climbs back into the apothecary room, brushing cobwebs from her shoulder. Her eyes fix on the party's gear, then on Aldric's iron tokens or the Concordance Stone if visible.

> *"You came all the way out into the dark for me. Why? The city has nine other names. Varenhold has always been willing to round down."*

**The Encounter Beat:**
Petra does not respond to civic guilt (*"Varenhold is dying"*)—she watched Varenhold abandon the Dusk Parishes decades ago.  
What unlocks her is **personal truth and unforced choice**:

1. **Aldric's Token:** If players present the forged iron token Aldric gave them:  
   She takes the cold iron in both hands, testing the weight with her thumb.  
   > *"Aldric made this. He tests every weld three times. If he gave you this, he believed you were asking, not drafting."*
2. **The Secret of Lira's Child:** If players mention Lira Anwick or the Healing House:  
   Petra's composure breaks slightly. DC 13 Insight reveals she has been sending anonymous coin pouches to Lira for two years via parish couriers.  
   > *"How is Lira? Did she... did she stay at the clinic, or did they take her to the circle already?"*  
   When the party explains that Lira is waiting for her own moment of honest consent:  
   > *"She was always stronger than me. I ran because I thought if I stayed in the city, someone would make the choice for me while I was sleeping."*
3. **The Inversion Pathway:** If players reveal the Inversion (that unanimous consent allows everyone to survive):  
   She stares at them in absolute, stunned silence.  
   > *"Say that again. You're telling me Corven didn't build an altar... he built a bridge?"*

**The Resolution:**
She reaches into a hollowed-out timber beam above the hearth and pulls down a small velvet pouch. Inside is **Cube 6 (Stone 6)**. It is cold when she draws it, but as her fingers wrap around it, a faint green-gold luminescence pulses through the crystal facets.

> *"I've kept this buried for twelve years. I thought if I forgot where I put it, the stones would forget me too. Take me back with you. I'm not letting Lira stand in that circle alone."*

**Outcome:** Petra gathers her cloak and travels back to the Ashring with the party. Flag: **"Petra: Willing & Present"**.

<summary>📄 Davin Shore - The Veteran's Vigil on the Bastion Wall (Stone 10)</summary>

*Want:* To make one final, meaningful choice as a free man before the choice is stripped from him by age or force.  
*Fear:* That his death will be wasted on political theater or another failed compromise.  
*Lie:* *"I'm just an old watchman pulling a cold shift. It doesn't matter what I think."*

*Physical:* Mid-sixties, grizzled, broad-chested soldier's posture weathered by three decades in chain and boiled leather. Deep laugh lines carved into a face that has seen too many sieges. An unlit clay pipe clenched between his teeth.

**Location:** The East Rampart of the Ashring Quarter—the high stone battlement overlooking the twilight boundary where the city's cobblestones give way to the desolate grey plains.

**Five Senses & Setup:**
- *Sight:* A long stretch of ancient granite wall. Lanterns hanging every sixty feet, glowing dull amber through thick cloud. Davin leaning against a crenel, his heavy wool cloak pulled tight against the northern draft.
- *Sound:* The steady scrape of his boot heel against the wall stone; the low, mournful moan of the wind blowing across the outer plains; a faint, persistent high-frequency hum vibrating in the air around him.
- *Smell:* Bitter pipe leaf, wet wool, gun-oil, cold river mist.
- *Touch:* Freezing granite parapet; the slight, physical vibration passing through the stone into your boots.
- *Air:* Biting cold, thinner and cleaner than the smoky lower districts.

**The Scene:**
Davin doesn't turn when the party climbs the watchtower steps. He taps his cold pipe against the battlement stone.

> *"Hear that?"*

He points his pipe stem toward his own sternum, then toward the distant, silhouetted monolithic rings of the Ashring below.

> *"Been hummin' like a struck tuning fork inside my ribs for fourteen months. First I thought it was lung rot from the damp. Then the stones started waking up three days ago, and the hum turned into a drumbeat. I know why you're here. You've got Corven's box."*

He turns to face them. His eyes are clear, steady, and utterly without fear.

> *"I've had sixty-four years. Seen three wars, buried two regiments, and watched this city chew through good people like cordwood. If the sun needs ten coals to light the hearth, my coal's ready. I'm not afraid of the fire. But I need to know one thing: is Harran running this, or are you?"*

**The Encounter Beat:**
Davin is an old soldier. Speeches about "hope" and "destiny" make him spit over the wall. He respects **tactical reality, command accountability, and the truth**.

- **If players explain the Reckoning's plan (forced blood sacrifice):**  
  > *"Harran's a fool who thinks discipline can substitute for honor. You force a man onto that stone, the stone will choke on him. Corven's notes were clear on that much."*
- **The Inversion Reveal:** If players explain the Inversion pathway (Ending B: all 11 willing voices invert the loop, returning the sun without death):  
  Davin freezes. The pipe slips from his calloused fingers, clattering onto the flagstones. For ten seconds, the old soldier does not breathe.  
  > *"Say that one more time. Slowly. You're telling me... nobody has to die? None of the kids? Not Lira? Not young Tomas?"*  
  He looks down at his weathered hands. A rough, trembling chuckle breaks out of his chest, growing into genuine, raspy laughter that echoes off the parapet.  
  > *"Gods above. A soldier spends his whole life learning how to die well, and you come up here at the fifth bell to tell me I have to learn how to live in the sunlight. That's a damn sight harder."*

**The Tactical Advice (GM Boon):**
He bends down, retrieves his pipe, and points toward the plaza below.
> *"Listen to me. If Harran's moving, he'll hit from the East Gate where the road widens. He'll send veterans to pin the dais and skirmishers to drag Dawnborn off the perimeter stones. Don't let them split the circle. Keep someone at the dais to anchor the resonance, and put your heaviest fighter between Stone 3 and Stone 5—that's the blind spot where the market stalls bottleneck."*

**The Cube:**
He unbuckles a brass-bound leather soldier's cartridge case from his belt and opens it. Nestled inside oiled sheepskin is **Cube 10 (Stone 10)**. It hums with deep, resonant vibrations that match the rhythm in his chest.  
He hands it to the player who spoke to him:

> *"Take it. Put it where it belongs. I'll finish my watch, wash my face, and walk down to Stone 10 before the sixth bell rings. If we're bringing the sun back, I want to be looking right at it when it comes up."*

**Outcome:** Davin surrenders Cube 10. The party gains tactical advantage (+2 to initiative or positioning in Scene 1 Combat). Flag: **"Davin: Committed & Ready"**.

<summary>📄 Cormac Drell - The Dawnhall Back Room (Stone 4)</summary>

*Want:* To be certain his consent is honest and not just momentum, fear, or civic guilt.  
*Fear:* That if he examines his reasons, they will break, and he will prove to be a coward.  
*Lie:* *"I've been ready for nine weeks. I just needed some tea before heading down."*

**Location:** Dawnhall back room, Ashring Quarter (five minutes' walk from the circle).

**Five Senses & Setup:**
- *Sight:* The rarely-used Dawnhall back room. One amber oil lamp guttering low on the table. Cormac sitting motionless, staring at his palms.
- *Sound:* The distant clatter of pots in the kitchen two rooms over; inside the room, dead silence.
- *Smell:* Cold bergamot tea, seasoned oak shavings, damp wool.
- *Touch:* The door latch is freezing iron; the room itself is close and warm.
- *Air:* Thick, heavy with the quiet of a man who has been sitting alone since before dawn.

**The Approach:**
Before the ritual assembly, nine stones are responsive, but Stone 4 remains cold. Ysel Maren points toward the Dawnhall: *"I know where he is. I was waiting to see if someone else would go first."*

When players enter, Cormac looks up from the table. He doesn't apologize:

> *"I know. I told Edoran yes nine weeks ago and I meant it. And then it kept not being over, and I kept not examining it because examining it might break it. And then it was last night, and I examined it. The answer is still yes. I think. I'm not sure I know how to trust that it's yes for the right reason anymore."*

**The Encounter Beat:**
Cormac does not need pressure, stakes, or speeches about the city.
- **If players push urgency, civic duty, or logistics:** He lays his hands flat back on the wood.  
  > *"I've been sitting here since before dawn. Urgency isn't the thing I'm missing."*

  *"Why do you want to?"*  
  He sits in long silence. Then:  
  > *"Because I said I would. And because the people who need me to have been patient long enough."*  
  He stands. *"I need you to walk to the stones with me. If I go alone, I'll stop."*

**The Resolution & Cube:**
He unbuttons his vest and draws out **Cube 4 (Stone 4)**. As his thumb touches the face, a warm gold pulse runs through the crystal facets.  
He hands it over:

> *"Take it. Put it in the box. I'm coming with you."*

**Outcome:** Cube 4 collected. Cormac walks to the Ashring with the party. Flag: **"Cormac: Reaffirmed & Grounded"**.

<summary>📄 Seating the Cubes - The Projection of the Dawn Bird</summary>

*Once all required cubes are gathered (Aldric, Tomas, Sera, Ysel, Cormac, Orya, Nin, Petra, Davin, Lira), the party holds the complete set of resonance vessels for Corven's Concordance Stone.*

**The Assembly on the Dais:**
The party brings the octagonal stone vessel to the central dais of the Ashring. Eight open sockets ring the rim. Each socket is carved with an archaic constellation glyph from the night of the First Ritual fifty years ago.

**The Mechanical Order (Brightest to Dimmest / FREEWILL Acrostic):**
As decoded with Tomas Areth in Session 4:

**Secondary Objective:** Hold the center dais for three rounds while the Dawnborn take their positions. Any player who uses their action on the dais (Dodge, Shield, Help action for a moving Dawnborn) grants that Dawnborn advantage on saving throws and immunity to forced movement until the player's next turn.

**Combat End:** Three rounds elapse and all eleven positions are occupied, OR Commander Harran Lecht is reduced to 0 HP. The moment Harran falls, his remaining soldiers drop their weapons.

<summary>📁 The Ritual Moment</summary>

The Ashring. Whatever stones are lit, whatever stones are dark. Players have the activation word. The Dawnborn are at their positions or they aren't.

Give the table a moment of actual silence (ten to fifteen seconds) before asking: "Who speaks?"

---

**Ixa's stone - the 11th element:**

Ten stones glow when their Dawnborn steps on them. Ixa's stone is waiting for something different. Not consent - she consented three months ago, alone, alone, three months before she chose to walk to the Ashring. What the stone is waiting for is the thing she decided she needed to see first.

She has watched everything. Five sessions of people making choices under pressure. She has watched Harran become less than he was. She has watched Cormac set down a cup very carefully and walk out a door and come back. She has watched Ellan Voss step onto a stone fifty years after the moment she couldn't. She has been watching to understand if people are worth what she carries. Whether returning it to the sky is a fair trade for what happens to the city. Whether the exchange is actually just.

She is standing at the 11th stone. Not on it. Her hands are at her sides. Her hands are warm.

She has already decided. She is waiting for something to confirm it. Not something about the ritual. Something about people.

**What her stone responds to:** A genuine human moment - not an argument, not an explanation of the stakes, not a speech about what she means to the mission. The stone responds when someone sees her fully (knowing what she is, knowing what stepping on means) and does something human anyway. Something small. Something that has nothing to do with saving the city.

It is not a skill check. There is no wrong answer that isn't an argument. There is no right answer that isn't just a person.

| What a player does | What happens |
|---|---|
| Offers their hand, not to lead her to the stone, just because | She looks at the hand for a long moment. Then she takes it. She steps on. |
| Says something true, not about the ritual, just something true about her or about themselves | Her stone warms before she moves. She notices. She exhales. She steps on. |
| Says nothing, simply stands beside her and waits | She looks at them for a long time. Then, quietly: *"Okay."* She steps on. |
| Tells her what she means to the ritual, what her stepping on will accomplish | Her stone stays cold. She has heard this. She has known this for three months. This is not what she was waiting for. |

**If players know and act anyway:** This is the most devastating version. They know what stepping on costs her. They extend a hand knowing. She knows they know. This is not deception and it is not cowardice. It is two people acknowledging a true thing and choosing to do it together. The stone goes white.

**If players don't know:** The stone goes white. The dawn rises. And then someone looks at where Ixa was standing, and understands. This is a different kind of devastating. Both are honest.

Before she steps, she looks at whoever asked her. Then at her own hands - the ash tattoos on her neck and shoulder, IV-A's ashes pressed into skin, briefly warm in a way that has nothing to do with the Primer Stone. She looks at them for a long moment.

> *"I've been warm my whole life. I always thought it was hers."*

She steps on.

When the stone lights, it does not glow gold like the others. It goes white (briefly, startlingly white, the color of something that has been waiting a very long time) and then settles as it joins the circle. Eleven stones. A complete ring of light, for the first time in fifty years. Maybe ever.

The activation word can be spoken now.

<summary>📄 Ending B - The Inversion (all eleven stones lit)</summary>

The word is spoken from the center of the dais. All eleven Dawnborn speak it simultaneously - the players hear their voices overlap across the circle. The amber light doesn't flash. It doesn't surge. It changes quality. Something in the air shifts - the particular heaviness that Varenhold has carried for fifty years lifts, the way a held breath releases. The stones go cold. The Dawnborn are still standing.

All of them.

And then someone notices.

Ixa's stone goes cold last. By the time the players look toward it, it is dark. She was standing there. Her hands were warm.

She is not there.

There is no dramatic exit. No body. No visible departure. The ritual energy returned to the sky and she went with it - the way the sun returns each morning, without announcing itself, simply happening. Her stone is the only one without a person standing beside it. Her stone is the only one already cold.

Sera looks at her hands. She starts laughing. She cannot explain why. She does not know why. She will not be able to explain it for days, the laughter that came before the understanding did.

Players who were beside Ixa (who offered their hand, who stood in silence, who said something true) have a moment to decide what they do with that. There is no correct response. There is only what they do.

**Ellan Voss, if present:** She is still standing at her stone. She has not moved. If a player looks at her face: she knows. She has known since the moment she stepped on. She chose to step on anyway. This is what it looks like when someone makes the same choice twice, fifty years apart, for the same reason - because you cannot give consent for someone who has no voice yet, and Ixa had a voice, and she used it.

Ellan does not cry. She has been crying, in various ways, for fifty years. She is done.

The sun does not rise immediately. It rises the next morning, for the first time in fifty years. The players have the night to wait with what they carry. They can spend it however they need to.

In the morning, the first light is not amber. It is gold. The real kind. The kind the city forgot.

<summary>📄 Ending A - Willing Sacrifice (8–10 stones lit, Ixa's stone cold)</summary>

Ixa did not step on her stone. She is standing beside it, watching.

The willing Dawnborn complete the ritual without the 11th element. Without Ixa, the energy has nowhere clean to return - it cannot invert, it can only release. The Dawnborn who consented give everything they carry. The stone positions of the willing go quiet - not dramatically, not loudly, just quiet. The sky changes. The players know who is gone before anyone says it.

The stones are cold. The Dawnborn who consented are not standing anymore.

Ixa's stone is the only one still slightly warm. She does not pull her hand back from it. She stands there for a long time. She understands, now, what her being here would have meant. She understands it clearly and completely and she has no one to be angry at.

The sun rises the next morning. For the first time in fifty years.

The remaining Dawnborn are alive. The city will recover. The sky is real again. These things are true.

The other things are also true. Both fit in the morning light.

<summary>📄 Ending D - Refusal</summary>

The players stand in the Ashring and do not speak. The Dawnborn leave, one by one. Sera is last.

She looks at the players for a long moment. She does not argue. She does not explain. Then, quietly, without anger: *"I was ready."* She picks up her crossbow and walks out of the Ashring without looking back.

Ixa watches her go. Then she looks at the players. She does not say anything either. She leaves.

The city will adapt, or it won't. This is a valid ending. It says some things are worth more than the sun.

<summary>📁 The Debrief</summary>

Not optional. The fifteen minutes after Session 5 are part of the experience.

Ask each player in turn, not rhetorically, with a pause between each question and each answer:

1. "What did your character do right? What are they sure of?"
2. "What are they not sure of?"
3. "When the right choice and the good choice were different - which did they make? Do they know the difference?"

Do not answer these for them. Do not wrap up the moral question. Let it sit.

If a player tries to resolve it: "The campaign doesn't answer that. That's yours to carry."

Corven spent six minutes writing a blank that took fifty years to fill. The word the table chose is the word this campaign carried. Write it down. It belongs to the record.
