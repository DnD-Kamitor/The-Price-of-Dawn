<details>
<summary>Edoran's Sealed Note — recovered from Z4 (Scene 1)</summary>

*The note Z4 tries to burn at the NE lantern post in round 2. Hand to players if recovered. If burned, describe the smell of singed wax and old paper. The content surfaces through other routes — Jaret knows it, Theron has seen the handwriting before.*

---

*[A single sheet of paper, rolled and sealed with dark red wax. The seal is a circle with a small sun-ring pressed into the center — the mark of the Auris Wounded faction. The paper inside is dry, precise, written with the care of someone who knows this document will be read by strangers.]*

> The willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot.
>
> You were hired to investigate. You will find the truth.
> When you do — ask yourself who benefits from your finding it,
> and whether those people were willing.
>
> This is not a threat. It is the only question that matters.

*[No signature. The handwriting is formal Auris liturgical script — the kind learned in a seminary, not a counting house.]*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
*Religion DC 10 or History DC 10: liturgical Auris script — formal, seminary-trained. DC 14: specifically the Wounded faction's grief liturgy, not standard Auris worship. The Wounded faction believes the Dawnborn's deaths would be meaningful. The author is clergy, or former clergy.*

*Theron Waide, if shown this note, goes very still. He has seen this handwriting before — earlier correspondence, same hand, same style. "Someone has been writing to me about this for years. Arguing that the anchor holders must choose freely." He did not know who. He filed it.*

*If the note burned: Jaret, at the Dawnhall approach, will mention that "Brother Edoran keeps that scripture close — says it to anyone who asks why the Restorers won't force the issue." That's the recovery path.*
:::

</details>

<summary>Archive Door Riddle — carved into the restricted stacks door (Scene 2)</summary>

*Carved directly into the door face in Corven's private notation — unreadable without the Notation Key. If players have assembled Fragments 1+2+3 (Sessions 1-2), they can decode it. Otherwise they must solve the riddle from the meaning-context alone, or wait for Theron.*

---

*[The restricted door of the Varenhold Civic Archive's third floor. Stone, solid, cold. Carved into its face: rows of small symbols in Corven's private cipher. Directly below the cipher, in plain script added by a different hand, at some later date:]*

> *"In case the cipher proves too much — the question is plain enough once you see it."*

*[Below that, in plain language, the same riddle the cipher encodes:]*

---

> I am given only when no one compels it.
>
> Forced, I become something else entirely.
>
> I am the difference between a gift and a theft.
>
> What word names the thing that makes the difference?

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
*Answer: CONSENT. Spoken aloud at the door, in any form. The door responds to the word and its synonyms.*

*Wrong (first attempt): low resonance tone, audible from the floor below. Theron appears at the landing, coat askew, ink on sleeve. He looks at the door. He looks at the players. He says nothing immediately.*

*Wrong (third attempt): tone sharpens, thin crack of light along the door frame. Door won't respond for one hour. Theron: "Perhaps I should show you what I know before you try again." He becomes the guide.*

*The riddle is the campaign's central word. Players who solve it before Theron intervenes are already asking the right question. The small script addition ("in case the cipher proves too much") is in Theron's handwriting. He wanted someone to open it. He could not open it himself.*
:::

</details>

<summary>Corven's Public Notes — found in shelf 4-17-3 (Scene 3)</summary>

*The outer folder on shelf 4-17-3. Hand to players when they open the correct shelf. This is Corven's formal record — the version he was willing to have found. Fragment 1 is tucked inside (see next card).*

---

*[Several pages of dense formal notation, bound with a dark cord. The cover page bears the Archive's filing stamp: "Ritual of Eternal Dawn — Technical Documentation — Archmagister A. Corven." Filed Year 48. Not opened since.]*

---

**RITUAL OF ETERNAL DAWN — TECHNICAL SUMMARY**
*Archmagister A. Corven — Filed for Archive Record*

The mechanism employed in the Year 48 ritual attempt draws on distributed sympathetic resonance — a configuration in which the anchor points for the ritual's energy are distributed across multiple sites rather than concentrated at a central point.

This approach was chosen specifically because concentrated application at a single anchor risks cascade failure. The distributed model is more stable under load, though it requires precise calibration across all points simultaneously.

The living conduit mechanism was selected as the most reliable method for maintaining the distributed sympathetic anchors over time. Theoretical alternatives were considered and rejected.

The ritual did not fail in the manner first reported.

Further details are encoded in the supplementary notation. The key to that notation is provided separately, in three portions.

*[The document ends here. The next several pages are dense notation — unreadable without the Notation Key.]*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
*"Distributed sympathetic anchors" and "living conduit mechanism" are the two phrases players cannot fully decode yet. They have the language. They do not have the referents. That lands in Session 2.*

*"The ritual did not fail in the manner first reported" — Corven knew what actually happened. He filed this document in plain language and then made the explanation unreadable without a key he split into three fragments. He was protecting something and creating a path to it at the same time.*

*"Living conduit mechanism" = the Dawnborn. Players will understand this when Theron says "extinguished" in Session 2. Until then, the phrase sits in their notes.*
:::

</details>

<summary>Restorer Pamphlet — handed out near the Dawnhall (Scene 4)</summary>

*Jaret hands one of these to whoever seems most receptive as players approach the Dawnhall. Jaret will answer questions honestly if asked directly.*

---

**ON THE NATURE OF THE LIGHT WE HAVE BORROWED**
*A Philosophical Address from the Society for Ritual Restoration*

The sun did not leave us by accident.

It was held — anchored against its nature, bound by mechanisms our predecessors understood imperfectly and executed without the consent of those most affected.

We do not blame the architects of Year 48. They acted in fear, in haste, for reasons that seemed sufficient at the time. We do not seek punishment. We seek resolution.

The amber light sustains us. We acknowledge this. But sustainability built on a foundation that was never chosen freely is not a gift. It is a debt. And debts, left unpaid, accumulate.

We are not Restorers because we wish destruction. We are Restorers because we understand that some things cannot hold forever — and it is more merciful to choose the moment than to wait until the moment chooses for us.

If you have questions, Jaret will answer them honestly.

*— The Society for Ritual Restoration, Year 98*

::: {.prop-alt-ink}
*What the pamphlet does not say: the mechanism requires the death of the ten Dawnborn.*

*Jaret knows this. The pamphlet doesn't say it. If players ask Jaret directly what "resolution" means, he pauses — then tells the truth. He is not a liar. He leads with the argument before the cost.*

*Players who came through session 0.5 already know Restorers exist and have operational capacity. What's new here: this is a different chapter's public-facing theology. The vault beneath the Dawnhall was the Restorers' practical work. This pamphlet is their argument. They are not the same.*
:::

## Player Moments

<details>
<summary>Pel — The Recognition [Frank's player only]</summary>

*Play when Sera Voss first looks at Pel directly — whether or not Pel has said anything.*

---

She's been talking to the group. Giving clear, complete answers the way she always gives them — which you noticed immediately, because you have never once in your professional life received a complete answer from someone in authority.

She turns to you specifically. Not because you said anything. Because she looked at the group and then looked at you twice.

Then she says your name.

Not "you" or "the investigator" or "your colleague." Your name. First name. The one you stopped using when you started working criminal investigation, because it was safer to be a title than a person.

She remembers your name. You were a stranger in a burning building. You were unconscious for part of it. She didn't know you then and she doesn't know who you are now.

She says: *"I thought it might be you. When the Chancellor mentioned investigators, I thought — the probability of it was low. But."*

She doesn't finish that sentence.

*What do you do?*

</details>

<details>
<summary>Serah — Divine Sense Reading [Kirsten's player only]</summary>

*Play when Serah uses Divine Sense in the Dawnhall.*

---

You've used Divine Sense in the Chancellor's study: nothing. In the Archive: nothing. Standard Spire Quarter. Bureaucratic and unglamorous and theologically unremarkable.

The Dawnhall is different.

No celestials. No fiends. No undead. Those are the categories your training gave you — the things it names, the presences it identifies. What you're feeling doesn't fit any of them.

Sera Voss is standing twelve feet away explaining the Dawnhall's intake procedures to the rest of the group. She is not radiating anything dangerous or holy or corrupt.

She is radiating *presence*. Like standing near a hearth where the fire hasn't been lit yet. The stone is warm. Something is about to burn. It isn't burning now.

You have felt this once before — standing at a wayshrine, very young, in the rain. The Wanderer's shrines feel like that. Like something absent that is still somehow there.

Sera Voss is not a wayshrine.

You don't know what she is. Write it down somewhere. You will need to remember this feeling later.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Keothi — The Seeds [Dennis's player only]</summary>

*Play when Keothi is in the Dawnhall kitchen or offers the seeds to anyone.*

---

The cook's name is Mira. Not the Chancellor — a different Mira. She's been in this kitchen for nine years, since the last head cook left and nobody qualified came to replace her, so she stayed.

She hands you a wooden spoon while you're standing near the prep area. Not a question. Not an invitation. Just: here, you look like you know what to do with this. You do.

She tells you, while you work, that they get two flour deliveries a month and one root vegetable shipment from a farm in the northern district that is on its last good season. She says this the way farmers say things — without drama, because the numbers are the drama.

You have the seeds in your pack.

You brought them for the city. For the Chancellor, or whoever represents the city officially. You brought them as a gift.

Looking at this kitchen — at Mira's hands, at the careful rationing on the prep table, at the single small window that looks out on amber light and no soil — you understand that the city isn't the Chancellor.

*What do you do with the seeds?*
