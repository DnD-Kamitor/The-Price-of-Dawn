# Session 3 — GM Review Document
## "The Watched City" — Critical Analysis

*Reviewer lens: Guy Sclanders (theGreatGM) + teacher. Covering story logic, riddle integrity, puzzle mechanics, combat design, and scene craft. Flagged items are graded: 🔴 BROKEN (will fail at table), 🟡 WEAK (will disappoint), 🟢 WORKS.*

---

## Part 1 — Story Logic

### 🔴 BROKEN: Necromancer faction vs. darkness faction — same or different?

Throughout the chapter, two names are used:
- "Necromancer faction" — Scene 1, Scene 2 consequence tracker
- "Darkness faction" — Scene 5 (Ellan), consequence tracker Ellan row

Are these the same faction? The chapter never says. A GM prepping this cold will not know. Players who ask will get a shrug. **Fix: Pick one name and use it consistently, or explicitly state they are the same group.**

---

### 🔴 BROKEN: Magical Darkness blinds the Cultists too

Round 1 tactical priority: Priest casts *Darkness* centred on the amber lamp.

**Rules problem:** Darkness is a 2nd-level spell that creates magical darkness. Per 5e rules, darkvision does NOT work inside magical darkness — only Devil's Sight (Warlock invocation) or equivalent pierces it. The Dusk Cultists have darkvision 30 ft. Darkvision ≠ Devil's Sight.

**Result:** The Priest casts Darkness and blinds his own team. Cultists inside the sphere cannot see. Shadow Step requires the destination to be a space the cultist "can see" — which they cannot, if they're inside or adjacent to the Darkness sphere.

**Fixes (pick one):**
1. Give the Priest and Cultists the Devil's Sight trait (add one line to each stat block).
2. Change Round 1 tactic: Cultists smash the lamp (action), creating natural darkness — darkvision works in natural darkness. Priest casts Darkness later only if needed as personal cover.
3. Change the spell to *Hunger of Hadar* or *Evard's Black Tentacles* if you want a darkness control zone that the Priest doesn't care if allies avoid.

---

### 🔴 BROKEN: Priest escapes via Dimension Door — but it's not on his spell list

Withdrawal text: *"The Priest dimension-doors if possible."*

Dimension Door is a 4th-level spell. The Priest's 4th-level slot is *Shadow of Moil*. Dimension Door is not on the Cleric spell list in standard 5e.

**Fix:** Replace the escape method. Options:
- He Shadow Steps to dim light outside the building (bonus action, free) then Dashes.
- Give him *Misty Step* (2nd-level) in place of one of his other 2nd-level slots.
- Just say he disengages and runs for the lane.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Reinforcement enters through a door described as already closed

Scene 3 setup: *"The door behind the players closes."*

Skill challenge failure consequence: *"1 additional Dusk Cultist enters via the lane door at the end of round 2."*

The lane door already closed. Either clarify this is a different entry point (window, side door, hatch) or note that the watcher outside simply forces it open — but if it was locked or barred from inside, explain that.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: No path to Ellan Voss if the puzzle box is sealed

The three-ring box is the ONLY stated route to Ellan's address. If players set all three rings wrong simultaneously, the box seals permanently.

Three-clue rule (Justin Alexander): every critical piece of information needs at least three independent routes. Currently there is one.

**Alternative routes not provided — add at least one:**
- Osindra's scrying pool can locate an old woman in Ashring who is under a ward (she notices the ward, not the person).
- Theron can be contacted directly — he knows Ellan's address. His number/sending contact is available.
- The second cryptic (library cryptic) may reference Ashring independently if fully decoded.
- A Lowmark street contact Sera trusts knows of an old woman in Ashring who the darkness faction visits.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: How did Sera end up in the Ironbell tannery?

Sera says the darkness faction "knew my name before the beacon." She's been hiding for some unspecified time. The tannery is a specific, real location — but the chapter gives no explanation for why the tannery, who owns it, or how Sera knows about it.

Players who ask *"Why this location?"* will get a blank. **Add one sentence:** Who runs the tannery? Is it a Dawnborn contact? A sympathiser? Sera's own family connection?

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Why is Lira going to a combat zone?

Sera's Sending response says: *"Lira's already coming."*

Lira has no weapons. She cannot fight. She carries a medical kit. She is going to a location she knows is dangerous (Sera is hiding from an armed faction). The chapter never explains why she would walk into this.

**Answer needed:** Did Sera call Lira before calling the players? Was Lira already with Sera and left to get supplies? Does Lira not know how bad it is? One sentence resolves this. Without it, her arrival feels like a GM contrivance for emotional effect.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Theron's letter at Ellan's door contradicts "she does not know I know"

Scene 4 box note (Theron's handwriting): *"She does not know I know."*

Scene 5 door panel: A wax-sealed letter from Theron, addressed to whoever finds it.

Theron placed a letter in Ellan's door panel — but she doesn't know he knows? If he could access her door panel, he was at her house. She would have either found the letter or known he visited. These two facts cannot coexist without explanation.

**Fix options:**
- The sealed letter was placed by a third party on Theron's behalf, after he died or disappeared.
- The door panel is not accessible from Ellan's side — it's an external compartment only reachable from the street.
- The letter was placed after Theron's disappearance by his apprentice following posthumous instructions.
- Acknowledge the tension in the text: *"Theron placed this before Ellan knew he knew — or he never came to her door himself. A courier. Someone who knew the mechanism."*

---

### 🟡 WEAK: "They knew my name before the beacon" — what does this mean?

Sera says this in the Sending message. This is a great line. But it implies the darkness faction had intelligence on the Dawnborn *before* the library room was opened.

Players will ask: how? The chapter doesn't answer. If this is intentional mystery for later sessions, say so in the GM notes. If it's a clue, provide enough for the GM to work with. Currently it's a dangling thread with no guidance.

**Minimum addition:** A GM note saying "This is a deliberate mystery — do not resolve it this session. If players press Sera, she doesn't know either."

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Ellan Voss has no "Lie"

The chapter uses Want/Fear/Lie for Osindra (well done). Sera has implied positions. Lira's stated clearly.

Ellan Voss — the most important NPC in the session — has no Lie listed.

She presents as completely transparent. No one is completely transparent. What is Ellan not admitting?

**Suggestion:** Her lie is her stated position. She says she will not consent "not because I am afraid." That is partially false. She is afraid. She has been afraid for fifty years. She is also — and she will not say this — relieved that someone finally came. She has been waiting for someone to ask the right question since Theron told her he had one. Her "I will not give it. Not before." is a door held open by someone who desperately wants someone to walk through it.

This makes her 10x more interesting and gives the players something to find.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: The Osindra intro for Sephandrius

Funny, specific, earned. The door-opens-before-they-knock mechanic is clean. The observation log noting Sephandrius adjusted his appearance is perfect. Don't change it.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Sera/Lira confrontation

No lie in the scene because neither is lying — and that's stated explicitly. This is correct and powerful. The beat is well-written. The silence is earned. Leave it exactly as is.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Consequence tracker

Clean, complete, useful at a glance. The skill challenge failure states feeding into combat is smart design.

## Part 2 — The Riddle

### Line-by-line integrity check

> *"I was present when the sun stopped."*
✓ Consent was present (10 Dawnborn agreed). The ritual required it.

> *"I am present now that it has not returned."*
🔴 **INVERTED LOGIC.** Consent is ABSENT — which is WHY the sun hasn't returned. Ellan's withdrawal removed consent, which broke the ritual, which left the world in darkness. This line says consent is *present* in a world where its absence caused the problem. Smart players will catch this and either think they have the wrong answer or that the riddle has an error.

**Rewrite:** *"I am absent now that it has not returned."* OR *"I remain possible — the only reason it might yet return."*

> *"I am not carried by the willing or the unwilling."*
✓ Consent cannot be imposed.

> *"I am not held by the living or the dead."*
✓ Works. Philosophical. Fine.

> *"I am not time, though I outlast it."*
✓ A single act of consent (Ellan's withdrawal, 50 years ago) outlasts lifetimes.

> *"I am not truth, though I contain it."*
✓ Consent requires truth to be real.

> *"I am not silence, though I am what remains when speaking ends."*
✓ Elegant. The agreement is what remains after the words.

> *"Ten held me fully."*
✓ Ten Dawnborn consented.

> *"Nine still hold me."*
✓ Nine still consent.

> *"The one who reclaimed me made me whole again — and broke everything else."*
🟡 **WORD CHOICE PROBLEM.** "Reclaimed" implies consent was taken from Ellan and she got it back. It wasn't taken — she voluntarily gave it and withdrew it. "Reclaimed" may mislead players into thinking someone's consent was coerced.

**Better word:** *"The one who withdrew me made herself whole again — and broke everything else."* OR *"The one who took me back..."*

> *"I cannot be divided. I can only be present or absent."*
✓ Consent is binary.

> *"When I am absent from even one, I am absent from all."*
✓ Unanimous requirement. Clean.

> *"The door does not open for the word."*
✓ Sets up the trap.

> *"The door opens for the act."*
✓ Clean.

> *"Perform me — do not name me — and enter."*
✓ Excellent. This is the best line.

---

### 🔴 BROKEN: What counts as "performing" consent?

The door mechanism requires each player to state their real reason. The GM guidance shows Lira saying: *"I am here because I have a daughter and I need to know if there is another way."* The panel clicks.

But there is no guidance for the GM on:
1. What counts as "real"? Sincerity? Specificity? Vulnerability?
2. What if a player says "I am here because the city will die"? Is that real or tactical?
3. What if a player says "I follow my companions"? Does obligation = consent?
4. What if a player gives a false reason — will the door know?

The riddle's mechanism hinges on a judgment call the GM must make with zero guidance. This WILL create table conflict when a player thinks they said a real reason and the GM says no.

**Required additions:**
- The ward tests specificity, not nobility. "Save the city" is not consent — it's a mission. "I am here because I need to know if my choice will kill someone I care about" is consent.
- A player who says "I follow my party" — the panel doesn't click for them. They must find their own reason.
- If a player genuinely cannot state a personal reason (rare but possible), the door holds them. The others can enter. The panel does not require unanimous entry, only genuine individual statement.
- The door cannot be fooled by a false reason that the player themselves believes. It does not detect lies — it detects whether the speaker has asked themselves the question. A character who says a real reason they've never admitted to themselves causes the panel to click loudest.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Sera giving the hint too easily

GM guidance: when players are stuck, Sera reads the last three lines again and says *"It's not asking you to say the word. It's asking you to do it."*

This comes after how long? Ten minutes? Thirty? The chapter gives no timing guidance. If Sera gives the hint immediately when players are confused, the riddle collapses. If she waits too long, players get frustrated.

**Add:** Sera gives the hint only after the group has made at least two failed attempts and has been at the door for ten real-world minutes. Not before. Let them work.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: The core trap

Saying "consent" does nothing. This is the correct design for smart players. They will solve the riddle in two minutes. The trap is that knowing the answer is not the answer. This will delight and then briefly infuriate them, which is the right sequence.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Lira as the unplanned key

Lira isn't trying to open the door. She's processing out loud. And the panel clicks. This is perfect dramatic irony. Don't remove it. It recontextualises her entire arc.

## Part 3 — The Puzzle (Three-Ring Box)

### 🔴 BROKEN: Ring 3 requires a fully decoded library cryptic — was it?

Ring 3: *"requires the players to cross-reference both cryptics... the library cryptic contains, if fully decoded, a single symbol that Theron embedded as a forward reference."*

The session 2 summary says players found both cryptics. It does not say they decoded either fully. If the library cryptic was not decoded in session 2, Ring 3 is impossible without the GM handwaving it.

**Required additions:**
- A GM note confirming what state the cryptics are in at the start of session 3.
- If the library cryptic is not decoded: the party can spend time decoding it here (estimated 1 hour in-world, Investigation DC 14 group check). Success reveals the forward reference symbol.
- If they fail the group check: the symbol is not found, and Ring 3 must be approached via the Arcana route (DC 14 for divination/encryption speciality, or Sephandrius with advantage).
- If Ring 3 is wrong and the box seals: see fallback section above.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: Ring 3 has no concrete physical description

"Cross-reference both cryptics" is a method. What does the GM actually show? What does the symbol look like? Players will ask to see the cryptics.

**The GM needs:**
- A visual or description of the forward reference symbol in the library cryptic.
- A visual or description of which symbol on Ring 3 matches.
- Even if the GM describes it verbally ("a spiral with a broken outer edge, different from the others — you've seen it before"), players need a tangible moment of recognition.

Without this, "you cross-reference and find the symbol" is just a skill check with no player agency or discovery moment.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: The puzzle has no sensory detail

Scene 4 is entirely mechanical. There's no five-senses opening. Where are they when they open the box? The tannery (smell of acid and blood)? Osindra's safe house? The Archive? The setting shapes how this feels.

**Minimum addition:** One-paragraph five-senses for the safe house / location where they work on the puzzle. What does the box feel like? Does it make a sound when a ring clicks into position?

---

### 🟢 WORKS: The single-wrong-attempt trap mechanism

Individual rings can be adjusted freely. Only submitting all three wrong simultaneously triggers the seal. This is fair, logical, and rewards careful players. Panicked simultaneous guessing is punished; methodical work is not.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Theron's letter content

"One withdrew. All broke." is a great line. The five-sentence format (short, heavy) is tonally correct. The reveal of Ellan's name lands cleanly.

## Part 4 — Combat

### 🔴 BROKEN: Darkness blinds own team (see Part 1)

Already flagged. This is the most critical combat issue. Fix before running.

---

### 🔴 BROKEN: No CR on Dusk Cultist stat block

The stat block has no Challenge Rating listed. The GM cannot verify XP or encounter difficulty without it.

**Add:** CR 1 (200 XP) for Dusk Cultist. CR 4 (1,100 XP) for Dusk Priest. This produces ~3,800 adjusted XP as stated.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: No Priest fallback if Round 1 plan is countered

Round 1: Priest casts Darkness. If a player uses Counterspell (very likely at level 6 — someone probably has it), Darkness fails. The chapter has no Round 1 fallback.

**Add:** If Darkness is countered, the Priest uses Toll the Dead on the highest-HP player (no spell slot, no concentration) and repositions. The cultists still move to flank — the plan shifts to aggression rather than control.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: No environmental detail for the tannery

Level 6 players will absolutely interact with the environment. The chapter mentions: amber lamp, shutters, cobbles. That's it. A working tannery has:
- Tanning vats (acid/chemical — fall-in causes 2d6 acid damage per turn)
- Hanging hides (soft cover, blocks line of sight)
- Wooden racks (difficult terrain, breakable)
- Rope lines (can be tripped with Athletics DC 12)
- Barrels of bark/urine (can be pushed, flammable in some variants)

**Add:** A brief environmental table or paragraph. Not a full map — just four bullet points of "here's what players can do if they get creative."

---

### 🟡 WEAK: "Sera is not prioritising self-preservation" — what does this mean tactically?

The chapter says: *"She does not prioritise self-preservation — this is a tactical liability the players should notice."*

But there's no mechanical guidance. Does she:
- Move into melee with the Priest immediately?
- Use her bonus action to grant attacks to players already endangered?
- Refuse to use cover?
- Stay at range shooting while cultists flank her?

The GM needs one sentence of actual tactical direction for Sera. Otherwise she'll be played as a passive ally by a GM who doesn't know what "tactical liability" looks like.

**Suggested directive:** Sera uses Captain's Eye to grant attacks to the most endangered player, even when doing so leaves her exposed. She does not move to safety. She holds ground. She fires at the Priest until she can't.

---

### 🟡 WEAK: What changes in Session 4 if Sera is captured?

*"If Sera drops to 0 HP (captured via Hold Person + drag), the Priest signals immediate withdrawal — and Session 4 changes significantly."*

There is no guidance on what changes. The GM who runs this and then reads Session 4 will find... nothing. This is a major consequence with no follow-through.

**Minimum addition:** A GM note: "If Sera is captured: she is held by the darkness faction as leverage. Session 4 opens with players needing to find where she is held before they can pursue Ellan. The darkness faction now has one Dawnborn secured and will use her to negotiate."

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Secondary objective (protect Sera)

Stated before initiative. Consequences are real and tracked. This is exactly right. This is what makes combat interesting beyond HP. Do not remove or soften it.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Lira as non-combatant problem

Her arrival mid-combat, going immediately to Sera's position, the opposed STR check to prevent dragging — this is clever design. It forces players to make decisions under pressure. Keep it.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Withdrawal trigger

Defined clearly. Not a death-fight. The darkness faction is professional, not fanatical. The cultists don't beg when downed (nice character note). The Priest gets away if possible — sets up future antagonism. This is good.

---

### 🟢 WORKS: Mid-combat line

*"They've had my name for months. This isn't about the cryptics. They've been hunting us one by one."*

Delivered during a lull, not explained. Then combat continues. This is Guy Sclanders practice: the emotional revelation drops in the middle of danger, not after it's safe. Excellent placement.

## Part 6 — What's Missing Entirely

### Missing: Scene 2 has no sensory opening

The skill challenge is mechanical. Where do players start? What does the Ironbell district feel like? What do they see/hear/smell as they move through it? This scene is a transition — it should feel alive, not like a stat block.

**Add:** A four-line sensory intro for the streets: what Ironbell looks, sounds, smells like. The moment they enter the copper-pipe zone, what does it feel like? (Osindra said it feels like silence. Describe it.)

---

### Missing: Scene 4 location and atmosphere

Where exactly does the puzzle happen? The chapter says "somewhere safe" with three options. This is fine for flexibility but means no sensory grounding. Even "wherever they choose" should have one sentence of atmospheric guidance for the GM.

---

### Missing: Any hint about the darkness faction's leadership

After three scenes, players know the faction's capabilities but not its face. There is no named antagonist, no specific threat, no personality behind the cult. This is acceptable for Session 3 IF the GM has Session 4 context. But if the cult leadership is not established by end of Session 3, Session 4 will feel like another generic encounter.

**Minimum:** The Dusk Priest should have one identifying feature — a signet ring, a specific phrase he says, an unusual physical detail. Something players can track. Something that pays off when they meet the faction's leader later.

---

### Missing: What does Osindra do after they leave?

The consequence tracker says "Osindra gave intelligence. Received an offer from darkness faction. Has not refused." But there's no trigger for when this resolves. Does she send a message if the offer is accepted? Does she contact the players? Does she feed information to both sides?

**Note for Session 4:** Osindra accepting the offer is a potential Session 4 betrayal hook. Flag it explicitly.
