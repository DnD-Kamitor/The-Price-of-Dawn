# The Concordance of the Withdrawn
## Full Faction Guide — GM Reference

---

## Origins

The Concordance did not form because of the Silence. It predates the Twilight by at least two generations.

The theology existed first. A small sect within orthodox Morthis worship began interpreting the Third Passage of Morthis — the scripture concerning willing death — in a direction the main temple rejected. They were not expelled. They were considered eccentric but harmless. The Morthis clergy has tolerated stranger readings than this.

When the sun went out, the sect did not grieve. They gathered. They said: *we told you.* The Silence was confirmation, not catastrophe. Within a decade they had a name, a structure, a mission, and a growing membership drawn from people who found the mainstream temples' grief unbearable.

They have operated continuously for fifty years. They are not improvised. They are not desperate. They have outlasted three city administrations and two attempts by the Spire Quarter to formally investigate them.

## Theology

### The Core Argument

Orthodox Morthis teaches: the soul that passes through the god's door does so once, willingly, and cannot be recalled without violation. Death is a passage, not a destination. The god of passage governs the threshold — not the territory beyond it.

The Concordance extends this principle to all things, not just souls.

**The Third Passage, Concordance reading:**
> *"All light that enters the door of darkness has passed. It cannot be called back. To call it back is to unmake the passage — to reach into the god's domain and drag what is gone back to where it was. This is not restoration. This is desecration."*

The sun passed through Morthis's door. The Silence is not a wound in the world — it is what follows the passage of light through death. The sun is gone the way the dead are gone. Mourning it is understandable. Attempting to reverse it is a violation of the god himself.

### Why They Are Not Simply Wrong

This is the most important thing the GM must understand about the Concordance: **their theology is internally consistent**.

Orthodox Morthis agrees that reversing death is a violation. Orthodox Morthis agrees that the god of passage governs all thresholds. The Concordance's error — if it is an error — is in classifying the Silence as a passage rather than a wound. If they are correct about that classification, everything else follows logically.

This is why the Concordance is genuinely dangerous: they cannot be defeated by argument. Their position is not incoherent. It requires a prior disagreement about the nature of the Silence itself, and that prior disagreement cannot be resolved by anyone currently living — because the only people who were present at the ritual are either dead, silent, or Ellan Voss.

### The Sealed Door

The **sealed door** symbol — a door sealed from the outside — was originally an orthodox Morthis symbol representing peaceful death: the threshold crossed, the door closed behind the soul, sealed against return. The Concordance inverted the symbolism without changing the image.

In orthodox use: the door is sealed because the soul has passed and is at rest.
In Concordance use: the door is sealed because the light has passed and must not be recalled.

Every legitimate Morthis temple considers this an act of desecration. The Concordance considers this irrelevant. They believe the main temple has forgotten the true meaning of its own symbol.

Members bear the sealed door as a tattoo on the wrist. The Returned bear it branded, glowing faint violet.

## The Schism

Approximately twenty-five years after the Silence, the Concordance fractured over a question of mission.

**The Preservation Faction** held that the Concordance's role was passive: protect the darkness, prevent restoration, ensure the passage is not violated. They are guardians. They watch and obstruct.

**The Guidance Faction** held that passive protection was insufficient. The darkness — the continuation after the sun's passage — is not an absence. It is a state. It has a direction. The Concordance's role is not merely to prevent restoration but to *guide the world through what comes after*. They are shepherds of the world's next state, whatever that state is.

The schism was not resolved. Both factions still exist. They cooperate against restoration attempts — they share that goal absolutely — but their long-term visions are incompatible.

**The brass ring with black glass** is the leadership token of the Guidance Faction. The Dusk Priest in Varenhold wears one. Varenhold's cell operates under Guidance Faction authority.

What "guiding the world through what comes after" means in practice, the Guidance Faction does not say publicly. Even within the Concordance, the long-term vision of the Guidance leadership is known only to the inner circle.

## Hierarchy

### Dusk Priests
Ordained operatives. They hold corrupted Morthis liturgy — recognisably derived from authentic temple ritual, systematically altered. They command in the field. They perform the animating rites that create The Returned. They are the cell's decision-makers.

They are not priests in any temple sense — no Morthis temple would recognise their ordination. They call themselves priests anyway. The word is deliberate: a claim to legitimate religious authority the main temple has denied them.

### The Unlit
Initiates and field operatives. Some are simply watchers and contractors — people who believe the theology and carry out operational tasks. Others are being prepared for The Return: they have volunteered, upon death, to be reanimated as Passage Witnesses.

### The Returned (Passage Witnesses)
Dead initiates reanimated as Ghasts by Dusk Priests. The official theological term is *Passage Witnesses* — they are proof that passing through Morthis's door is not the end. They witness passage. Their continued existence demonstrates the theology.

In practice: they are obedient undead soldiers who feel nothing and remember nothing. Whether they are actually *witnessing* anything, the theology does not say.

They bear the sealed-door brand, glowing violet. They do not speak. They respond only to Priest commands.

### The Watcher (Bound Revenants)
Not all Returned are Ghasts. The Concordance has, on rare occasions, produced Revenants — bound to specific tracking tasks rather than general service. WATCHER-1, active in Varenhold, is bound to the arcane trace of Sending spells. It was someone once. The Concordance does not consider this a tragedy.

### The Seer
Not a rank — a role. The Concordance has at least one active seer in Varenhold who reads arcane signatures in real time. Every spell cast in the open streets leaves a trace the seer can read within minutes. The seer's identity is not known. Osindra suspects the Concordance seer is more accurate than hers.

## Operations in Varenhold

The Concordance has operated in Varenhold continuously for over twenty years.

**Current objectives (as of Session 3):**
1. Keep Ellan Voss (ASSET IX) alive, isolated, and refusing consent
2. Prevent Sera Voss (SUNRISE-4) from reaching Ellan
3. Prevent any party from assembling all ten Dawnborn
4. Monitor and disrupt restoration attempts before they reach operational stage

**Intelligence infrastructure:**
- Street-level watchers at key intersections throughout Ashring and Ironbell
- Seer with real-time arcane signature reading
- WATCHER-1 as mobile tracking asset
- Copper-pipe dead zones mapped — they know where scrying fails

**Known assets:**
- Varenhold Archive: a contact the players have not yet identified
- Osindra (Divination Tower): an approach has been made; she has not refused
- Ellan Voss's street: under surveillance for three years

**The Ironbell cell (IRONBELL-7):**
Operational objective: hold Sera at Ironbell, away from Ellan. The tannery ambush is this plan in action. WATCHER-1 filed the field report.

## The Patron Saint Problem

Ellan Voss withdrew her consent from the ritual fifty years ago. In Concordance theology, she did the correct thing: she refused to participate in an act of cosmic desecration at the last moment. She is living proof that the moral choice is refusal.

She is venerated within the Concordance as the person who made the right call when no one else did. The other nine, who stayed, are considered the ones who failed.

Ellan despises this. She does not consider herself a saint. She does not consider herself correct. She considers herself a nineteen-year-old who was afraid, and she has spent fifty years living with that.

The Concordance keeps her alive because her living refusal prevents the ritual. She is their most valuable operational asset. She is also, to them, a sacred figure. They have never resolved the tension between these two facts.

She knows she is their patron saint. This is part of why she refuses contact with anyone: the Concordance, the Restorers, the players. Everyone who comes to her door is there because of what she did fifty years ago, and she is tired of being what she did.

## Using the Concordance at the Table

**They are not evil by their own theology.** Play them as people who are wrong about something factual (the nature of the Silence) rather than wrong about something moral. A Concordance operative who gets captured and talked to should be able to explain their position coherently. Players who listen carefully will find the argument disturbing not because it is incoherent but because it is not.

**The Guidance Faction's long-term vision** is a Session 4–5 revelation. Do not reveal it before the players have earned the question.

**Osindra's offer** — the Concordance approached her with something she does not yet have. What they offered her is connected to the Guidance Faction's knowledge of what the darkness actually is. She is deciding whether the information is worth the cost of the relationship. This is the Session 4 betrayal hook.

**Ellan Voss as patron saint** — this detail lands differently if players have met Ellan first. After they meet her, when they learn the Concordance venerates her, the horror is personalised. She is not a symbol to them. She is a specific old woman who makes tea and is tired. Use the sequence deliberately.
