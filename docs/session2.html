26  Session 2 — The Weight of Light (Level 5)

Dilemma: Do you have the right to decide someone else’s fate, even to save thousands?

26.1 Scene 1 — Opening Combat: The Reckoning at the Archive

PURPOSE STATEMENT: This scene exists to establish that the Reckoning is not reacting to the players — it is ahead of them. The soldiers arrived before the players did. The documents were already being moved toward the fire. The scene opens mid-action, not at the start of a confrontation, and that displacement should feel deliberate and disquieting. The secondary objective (saving the document bundles) reframes combat as a triage decision: players cannot simply fight their way through this scene without also managing the clock on the documents.

EMOTIONAL TARGET: Urgency first, then cold realisation. The soldiers are burning evidence — not hiding it, not securing it, burning it. That is different. Someone has already decided these documents should not exist.

DRAMATIC QUESTION: Can you stop the fire and learn who ordered it, or do you have to choose between the two?

DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT: Round 2. A soldier has a bundle in both hands and is walking steadily toward the fireplace, not looking at the players, not flinching when someone shouts at him. He is completing a task. He will walk into crossbow range. He will keep walking. The players must physically stop him or the bundle is gone.

🌍 Environment & Setup

26.2 Tactical Map Description

26.2.1 The Archive Ground Floor Reading Room — ~60×40 ft

The Varenhold Civic Archive’s ground floor reading room is the longest room in the building: a wide rectangular hall with the main entrance on the south face, shelves along three walls reaching nine feet high, and a large functional fireplace built into the east wall. The room is used for supervised research access — long oak tables run parallel to the shelves, with high-backed chairs and individual reading stands. The lighting is amber lantern sconces every eight feet along the upper shelves, warm and steady. In the permanent twilight of Varenhold, this room is always lit.

The players have been here before — Session 1, Scene 3. They know the layout: the main door south, the staircase to the upper floor behind the reference desk at the room’s north end, the librarian’s standing desk near the door, Theron’s reading alcove partition built flush into the north wall.

Tonight, the room has been altered in three specific ways: - The fireplace is lit and stoked high — it has been burning long enough to be hot, not just lit. - Three document bundles from the restricted archive are spread across the central reading table — they were pulled in haste, some still in their binding-twine, laid flat. - Reckoning soldiers are already in the room, moving with the particular efficiency of people who have done this before.

26.2.4 Three Read-Aloud Variants

A — Cautious Entry (Party scanning before entering):

The Archive doors are open. Not ajar — propped. Someone left them that way. Inside: the reading room, lit as always, amber sconces along the shelves, the long oak table down the center. And the fireplace at the far end burning higher than you have ever seen it in a building where the books are the point. You count four soldiers before you step through the threshold. One of them is walking toward the fire with something in both hands. He does not slow down when you appear.

B — Direct Entry (Party moving with purpose):

The Archive smells wrong before you reach the door — old paper and smoke, two things that should not share a room. Inside: Reckoning soldiers, the central reading table with bundles of documents laid across it, and a fireplace that has been burning long enough to be serious. A soldier with a bundle in his hands is twenty feet from the fire. In the middle of the room, a Lieutenant turns to face you with the specific unhurriedness of someone who was expecting to finish before you arrived.

C — Players Who Saw Signs of Entry (Forced door, footprints, etc.):

You were right. Inside the reading room, four soldiers and a Lieutenant who clearly arrived before you and clearly intended to be finished by now. Three document bundles from the restricted archive are on the central table — or were: one is in a soldier’s hands and moving steadily toward the fireplace on the east wall, which is burning high and hot and entirely ready. The soldier carrying the bundle does not look at you. He is completing a task. You have arrived in the middle of it.

26.2.5 Five Senses — Full

Sight: - The familiar amber reading room, shelves high on three walls, the long oak table with its reading stands — but the atmosphere is wrong. The fire is too big. Research rooms don’t need fires this large. - Three cloth-wrapped bundles on the central table, binding twine still on two of them, the third already loose where someone pulled documents out to check they had the right material. - A soldier walking with steady deliberate steps toward the fireplace, a bundle under both arms. No hesitation. No glance at the players. The door being open meant he heard them arrive; it hasn’t changed anything. - The Lieutenant’s face when he turns: not surprise. A scheduling adjustment. He was expecting to be done. He is not. - Four dark-coated soldiers in Reckoning livery — the grey coat with the closed-eye emblem on the left breast — positioned across the room with the particular dispersal of people who planned where they would stand.

Sound: - Crackling from the fireplace, louder than a reading room fire should be — someone has been feeding it. A low, persistent roar of a fire built for purpose. - The scrape of the soldier’s boots on the stone floor as he walks, unhurried, toward the east wall. That sound — steady, rhythmic, not stopping — is the combat clock made audible. - Paper rustling from S3’s hand on bundle T3. He is not lifting it yet. He is waiting to see what happens. - The Lieutenant’s voice when he speaks, or the silence if he does not. - Beneath everything: the building’s own quiet, which is the wrong kind of quiet for a library — no creak of a librarian’s footstep, no turn of a page. The Archive is empty of everything except the Reckoning and what they came to destroy.

Smell: - Old paper, leather bindings, archival dust — the room’s permanent base note. The smell of preserved knowledge. - Over it now: wood smoke from the fireplace, sharper and more present than it should be in a reading room. Not unpleasant, but wrong. - A faint scorched-cloth smell from the direction of the fireplace — S4 has already fed one document into it before the players arrived. One bundle is already gone.

Touch: - The door handle warm from recent contact — someone touched it minutes ago, coming in. - If players move to intercept S1: the close warmth radiating from the fireplace before they reach the east end, the air getting drier and hotter. - The reading room’s typical chill at the south entry — the doors being open have let the Varenhold night in.

Taste: - Woodsmoke at the back of the throat, the thin edge of it that reaches all the way to the doorway when the fire is this size. - The mineral dryness of the Archive itself — dust and old paper absorbed into the air.

26.2.6 Pre-Scene Clarity

What players see immediately (no roll): - Three document bundles on the central table. - S1 carrying one bundle toward the fireplace — he is 20 ft from the fire at the start of Round 1. - The fireplace burning at full height. - Four soldiers and one Lieutenant. - The doors propped open.

DC 12 Perception: - S4’s small hand torch near the fireplace — there to light the edges of bundles that are slow to catch. This tells a perceptive player that the soldiers expected to need time, that burning documents is harder than it sounds when they are old and dense.

DC 14 Insight (Lieutenant): - He is not angry at the interruption. He is recalculating. A player who reads this correctly understands: this is a professional setback to him, not a crisis. He will adapt.

DC 15 Perception (alcove, north wall): - The alcove partition sits slightly proud of the wall — the internal door is not flush because it is not fully latched. Something is behind it. Or someone.

⚔️ Combat

26.3 Scene 1 — The Reckoning at the Archive

Design intent: The Reckoning is not here to fight. They are here to complete a task. The secondary objective (saving the document bundles) exists in tension with the combat — players who try to fight their way through systematically will lose documents in the process. Players who ignore the soldiers to chase bundles will take hits without spending actions on defense. The correct play is task management under fire, not tactical combat. Reward split-attention.

Reckoning Soldiers are immune to the Frightened condition and do not

Hold the Line (1/round). When a player moves adjacent to the soldier's
bundle-carrying path, the soldier may make one Shortsword attack as a

26.5 Initiative & Surprise

26.5.1 Surprise?

The doors are open. The players can hear the fire. There is no surprise in either direction — this is a room-entry into an active situation, not an ambush. Roll initiative normally at the threshold.

Exception: If players spent time outside the building listening or observing through a window, they may have identified S1’s movement before entry. In this case, they get one free action before initiative — a player may declare one preparatory move (draw weapon, call a specific command) that happens before the first initiative roll resolves.

26.6 The Document Clock

26.6.1 Three Bundles — Status at Round Start

Bundle Position Soldier Assigned Round Reaches Fire
T1 Central table, south end None — closest to players’ entry Never, unless S2 or S3 reassigned
T2 In S1’s hands, 20 ft from fire S1 (actively carrying) End of Round 1 (movement + action = 20 ft + drop in fire)
T3 Table, north end — S3’s hand on it S3 (not yet lifted) End of Round 2 if S3 acts normally

Fireplace consumption: A bundle in the fire burns for 2 full rounds before the documents inside are unrecoverable. A player can reach into the fire (DC 12 Dex, action, takes 2d6 fire damage on failure or half on success) to retrieve a bundle that has been in the fire for 1 round. After 2 full rounds, the documents are ash.

26.6.2 Intercepting a Bundle-Carrier

A player who gets adjacent to a soldier carrying a bundle may: - Grab the bundle (contested Athletics: player vs. soldier +4). On success, player has the bundle and the soldier’s Document Priority action is spent. On failure, soldier keeps it and the player takes a Hold the Line reaction attack. - Tackle the soldier (Shove, Athletics vs. Athletics +4). On success, soldier is knocked prone; they cannot move the bundle that turn. Bundle is still in their hands. - Simply block the path (stand adjacent). The soldier must either attack the player (ending its bundle-carry movement this turn) or pass through the player’s space (requires Shove — Athletics DC matching player’s Str).

26.7 Round-by-Round Guide

26.7.1 Round 1 — “They Were Here First”

Initiative count 20 (top of round): Describe the room’s state plainly: “S1 has twenty feet to the fire. S3’s hand is on bundle T3. The Lieutenant is between you and the table.”

S1: Uses full movement (30 ft) toward fireplace. If he began 20 ft away, he reaches it and can use his action to place the bundle in the fire. This is the first crisis point. Any player who acts before S1 in initiative can intercept if they move immediately.

S2: Engages the most forward player — Multiattack. His job is to hold the south end of the room and slow the players’ movement into the space.

S3: Picks up bundle T3 (action) and begins moving toward the fireplace. With 30 ft movement, he ends Round 1 about 10 ft from the fire.

S4: Positions himself at the fireplace, hand torch ready. He does not attack players unless adjacent. His role is to receive the bundles. If S1 delivers T2 this round, S4 uses his action to push T2 deeper into the fire (ensuring 2-round consumption starts this round).

Lieutenant (Round 1): Does not attack immediately. He uses Command as his bonus action to direct S3 (move 15 ft extra toward fire). He spends his action on Longsword attacks against the first player to step past him toward the table. He is the gate. He expects to buy S1 the one round he needs.

26.7.2 Round 2 — The Second Bundle Decision

If T2 is in the fire at this point: The bundle has been burning for 1 round. One round left before it’s gone. A player can still retrieve it (2d6 fire damage attempt), but must weigh this against T3 still in motion.

S3: If not intercepted, reaches the fire and begins feeding T3. S4 assists.

S2: Continues engaging front-line players. Uses Multiattack each turn.

S1: Bundle delivered. He draws his weapon and engages the nearest player — Multiattack this round.

Lieutenant: Now switches to full offense. Two Longsword attacks against the player who most threatens the operation. Uses Shield Bash bonus action to push that player away from the fireplace side of the room.

26.7.3 Round 3+ — Endpoint Conditions

The combat ends when ONE of these is true: 1. The Lieutenant is incapacitated or captured (grappled and restrained; he does not yield at half HP the way the Session 1 Enforcer did — he is more committed than the Restorers). 2. Two or more bundles are in the players’ secure possession (not just grabbed — held away from the fireplace or removed from the room). At this point the soldiers withdraw on a sharp two-word command from the Lieutenant if he is still standing: “Out. Now.” They disengage and leave through the main entrance in good order. 3. All soldiers are incapacitated.

If the Lieutenant is captured: He gives information under pressure (see NPC dialogue below). He does not give Harran’s name — he uses “the Commander.” He is professional.

26.9 Tactical AI

26.9.1 Soldier Priority (in order)

  1. Move bundle toward fireplace.
  2. If adjacent to a player blocking path: Hold the Line reaction, then continue movement.
  3. If bundle is in the fire or in a player’s hands: draw weapon, Multiattack on the nearest player.
  4. On Lieutenant’s “Out. Now.”: Disengage and withdraw. Do not chase players outside.

26.9.2 Lieutenant Priority (in order)

  1. Command (bonus action) — keep soldiers moving toward the fire.
  2. Longsword attacks on the player most likely to intercept the next bundle.
  3. Shield Bash to push players away from the fireplace.
  4. If reduced to 0 HP: he drops. No yield — unlike the Enforcer, he is Reckoning, not a contractor. He takes his duty seriously enough to be unconscious for it.

26.9.3 What They Will NOT Do

  • Harm Theron if they find him (they don’t know he is present; their orders are about the documents, not the archivist).
  • Set fire to the building. Documents in the fireplace, not the shelves.
  • Fight to the death for the last bundle if the Lieutenant is down — soldiers at that point withdraw.
  • Give Harran’s name under any circumstances. They know it. They will not say it.

🎭 NPCs — Scene 1

26.11 Reckoning Lieutenant — Scene 1 Capture Profile

26.11.1 Physical Description

50s. Lean jaw, grey at the temples. Reckoning coat immaculate despite the work — he changed into fresh livery for this, which is the detail players who search him will notice. He does not perspire visibly. He fights with the economy of someone who has done it many times and has no need to perform it.

26.11.2 OGAS

Occupation Reckoning field Lieutenant, seven years. Before that, city guard, Spire Quarter patrol.
Goal Complete the assigned destruction order and leave without civilian witnesses. The players have complicated this. He is now in triage mode.
Attitude Professional. Not hostile in a personal sense. He has an order. The players are between him and the order’s completion. That is the situation.
Secret He does not know what the documents say. He was told to destroy them. He follows orders. He has never asked what is in the documents he destroys. He is beginning to wonder why.

26.11.3 Dialogue (Captured / Cornered)

If players capture him and demand information:

“We were told the city knows nothing about the ritual documents. Clearly that is no longer true.”

On “who sent you”:

“The Commander. That is what I call him. That is what I will continue to call him.”

If pressed further:

“The Commander has been watching this Archive for three months. He knew you would come back. He wanted the documents gone before you got to them. He was wrong about the timing.”

If asked whether he knows what he was burning:

“I know what I was ordered to burn. I was not ordered to know why.”

A DC 14 Insight check on this response reveals: he is not lying, and he is not entirely comfortable with the answer. Something in that sentence has been bothering him.

On releasing him:

He straightens his coat. He does not thank them. But before he leaves:

“The Commander is going to want to know what you found in those bundles. I will tell him I don’t know. That is the first time in seven years I have filed an incomplete report.”

He leaves. He does not look back.

🎬 GM Notes — Scene 1

26.11.4 Pacing

The scene should feel rushed — it opens mid-action and never fully slows down until the Lieutenant is dealt with. Do not let the players take a breath between entering and the first decision about S1 and the bundle. The urgency is the scene.

Do not describe the room fully before initiating. Give the read-aloud, call for initiative, and let the tactical picture develop through play. Players who want to know exactly where everything is can ask, and you answer, but do not front-load the spatial information — the confusion of entering mid-crisis is correct.

26.11.5 Secondary Objective Guidance

The bundle clock is your pacing tool. If the scene is running slow (players spending too many actions on careful positioning), have S3 pick up T3 and narrate him walking: “Two of them now. Moving.” This should prompt a response.

Do not remind players that T1 is still on the table and unattended — let them discover it or not. T1 is the safety net for a party that loses T2 and T3. If they save T1 and T2, they have enough for Scene 3. If they save only T1, Scene 3 becomes harder. Track the outcome; do not manage it.

26.11.6 What Not to Do

  • Do not let the Lieutenant be verbose. He is not a monologue villain. He is a manager doing a job. If he talks, it is brief.
  • Do not make the soldiers hesitate to burn documents because the players look intimidating. They have an order. They complete it unless physically prevented.
  • Do not have the soldiers fight to the death for the last bundle once the Lieutenant is down. At that point they withdraw — that is their training.

26.11.7 Theron’s Emergence

After the scene ends and the players have secured whatever bundles they have, pause for one beat of silence. Then:

“From behind the north wall — the section that looks like shelving — a sound. A latch. The panel swings open. Theron Waide steps out with ink-stained cuffs and the very specific composure of someone who has been standing completely still behind a locked door for fifteen minutes.”

Do not rush Theron. Let the players react to him before he speaks.

26.11.8 Connects Forward

Everything in this scene feeds Scene 2: - The bundles the players saved contain the colophon signatures for the resonance box in Scene 3. - The Lieutenant’s line about “the Commander watching for three months” confirms the Reckoning knew about Corven’s documents before the players did — which means someone in the administration told them, which Theron will corroborate in Scene 4. - Theron’s emergence from hiding is his first appearance this session. His survival of the raid is important to him — he waited behind that door not because he was afraid, but because he was protecting the alcove’s locked drawer. The parchment slips were in it the whole time.

26.12 Scene 2 — The Riddle: Theron’s Encoded Message

PURPOSE STATEMENT: This scene exists to give players the first clear statement of what the documents mean — not through an NPC monologue, but through a puzzle they solve themselves. Theron has been carrying this encoded message for eleven years. He wrote it for himself, not for them. The fact that it is solvable is the most honest thing about it. By the time players read the answer aloud, they have earned the horror of knowing.

EMOTIONAL TARGET: Slow dread, then the specific stillness of understanding something you cannot un-understand. The moment the answer is spoken aloud should be followed by silence at the table, not clarification.

DRAMATIC QUESTION: What kind of person writes down the most dangerous truth they know, in a code they leave solvable?

DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT: A player reads the decoded message aloud for the first time. Theron looks at them. He does not deny it. He says: “Yes.”

🌍 Environment & Setup

26.13 Location: Archive Ground Floor — Reading Room to Private Alcove

The scene begins in the reading room, which now has the aftermath quality of interrupted destruction: chairs shifted, the fireplace burning lower now that no one is feeding it, document bundles spread across the central table in whatever state the players managed. The soldiers are gone.

Theron is standing near the alcove doorway. He has not moved far from it — he can see the room, the state of the bundles, the bodies if there are any. He takes stock. He does not say anything immediately.

After thirty seconds of player action — gathering documents, checking the room, asking questions — Theron goes to the reference desk. He takes a key from his coat. He unlocks the top right drawer.

He removes three parchment slips. Not from a folder, not in a box — loose, slightly bent from being carried flat in the drawer for years. He sets them on the reading table in front of the players without speaking first. He straightens them once, aligning their edges. Then he steps back.

26.13.1 Physical Description — The Three Parchment Slips

Each slip is approximately 6 inches by 3 inches, cut from larger parchment with a clean edge. The writing is Theron’s hand — neat archival script, the handwriting of someone who has transcribed thousands of documents. No ornamentation. Each slip has a single sentence.

The slips are not labeled. They are not numbered. They are not in any indicated order. Theron places them left-to-right on the table: First Slip, Second Slip, Third Slip.

26.13.2 The Riddle — Three Parchment Slips

First slip: “The Dawnmark rises when the anchor holds. The vessel must be willing. The energy of the third circuit does not disperse; it dwells.”

Second slip: “Ask the archivist what lives within the amber light. The answer is not in the records. Within them is the truth of the ritual’s end.”

Third slip: “Every third scholar agreed: the Dawnborn are the mechanism. The energy lives in willing vessels. The ritual them waits still.”

26.13.3 The Pattern

Reading every third word across all three slips in sequence, starting from word 3 of the first slip and counting across slips continuously:

First slip word 3: rises — word 6: anchor — word 9: willing — word 12: third — word 15: not — word 18: dwells

Continue into second slip word 3: what — …

The cleaner reading is every third word from the beginning of each slip, taken in order across all three slips:

Slip 1: The Dawnmark rises when the anchor holds. The vessel must be willing. The energy of the third circuit does not disperse; it dwells.

Slip 2: Ask the archivist what lives within the amber light. The answer is not in the records. Within them is the truth of the ritual’s end.

Slip 3: Every third scholar agreed: the Dawnborn are the mechanism. The energy lives in willing vessels. The ritual them waits still.

Reading only the capitalised words (the pattern Theron used — every third word starting from the first word of each slip, in sequence across all three):

THE · ANCHOR · VESSEL · ENERGY · CIRCUIT · DWELLS · ASK · WHAT · THE · THE · NOT · RECORDS · WITHIN · THEM · TRUTH · RITUAL · EVERY · AGREED · ARE · THE · IN · THE · THEM · STILL

The relevant phrase — what Theron was encoding — is assembled from the word that begins every third grouping, starting at word 1 of the full sequence:

THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM

For GMs who prefer a clean puzzle mechanic: The simplest version is this: read only the words that occupy positions 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28 across the three slips read left to right as continuous text. Those words, in order, form the sentence. The slips were designed so a scholar would find it eventually. Theron is a scholar.

26.13.4 Five Senses — Scene 2

Sight: - Theron standing back from the table after placing the slips, hands at his sides. He does not fold his arms. He is not protecting himself. He is waiting. - The three small rectangles of parchment on the oak table, slightly yellowed at the edges from drawer storage. They look like nothing. They look like a list. - If the room is still lit by the fireplace: the fire has died to embers by now. The amber sconces are the only light. The slips catch it at an angle.

Sound: - The fire’s settling — occasional crack and shift of cooling wood. The fireplace no longer roaring; it is winding down. - Paper on oak as Theron straightens the slips. One small sound, very deliberate. - The building quiet around them. Whatever noise Varenhold makes at night — distant cart wheels, the bell sequence from the Spire — comes through the propped south door.

Smell: - Old paper — the slips have a faint dry smell of long drawer storage, different from the archive shelves. - Smoke from the fireplace, dissipating now. - Ink, faintly, from Theron’s cuffs. He has been writing today — or he always smells like this. Both things may be true.

Touch: - The slips themselves, if players pick them up: parchment, not paper. Heavier. The edges sharp and clean where they were cut. - If the table has been shifted in combat: slightly wrong geometry to the room, chairs at angles. The room is inhabited differently now.

Taste: - The smoke is almost gone. What’s left is the mineral dryness of the Archive at rest — the taste of still air in a building full of old things.

26.13.5 Transition from Scene 1

Theron does not emerge in a hurry. He takes in the room. He looks at the bundles. He does not ask about the soldiers — he heard everything. He goes directly to the desk. The parchment slips are his priority because they are what he has been protecting for eleven years, and the bundles are merely evidence of what the slips say.

If players ask “Were you in there the whole time?”, Theron’s answer:

“From when they arrived. I heard the fireplace being stoked. I thought I would wait and see whether they would find the drawer.”

“They didn’t. You did.”

🧩 Puzzle

26.14 The Riddle — Full Mechanics

26.14.1 Giving It to the Players

Set the slips in front of the players physically if possible — print or write them on separate pieces of paper, or read each aloud slowly and write them on an index card. The riddle must be visible and rereadable; players should be able to point at words. If running digitally, paste the text into the shared space where players can see it.

Do not tell players there is a pattern to find. Do not say “it is a cipher.” Simply: “Theron places three parchment slips on the table in front of you.” Then read the three texts. Then: silence. Theron says nothing.

26.14.2 What Theron Does During the Puzzle

Theron watches. He does not pace. He does not give hints unless prompted, and even then his hints are minimal — he is measuring whether they can arrive at the answer, because he has spent eleven years arriving at it himself and the fact that it can be found is the only thing about it he still finds comforting.

If players are working: Theron is silent. If asked “Is this a code?”, he says: “Yes.” If asked “What kind?”, he says: “The simplest kind I could devise.”

If players hit a wrong answer (first attempt): > “Keep trying. The pattern is simpler than it looks.”

He does not confirm the method. He will confirm when they have the right approach: “That direction. Keep going.”

If players are stuck after two wrong attempts: > “Every word has a position. Count.”

This is the most explicit he will be. Count positions. Every Nth word. He will not say which N.

If players fail three times or attempt to stop: Theron reaches for the slips. “Perhaps it’s better you don’t know yet.”

This is the moment. Players must choose whether to let him take them back.

  • If they let him: he pockets them. He goes quiet. The scene stalls for one beat. Then a player must either ask him directly — “Tell us what they say” — or he will say, after a full silence: “All right. I’ll say it plainly. But I want you to understand why I wrote it down instead.” This begins the scene 4 confession early.
  • If they hold the slips: he stops reaching. He sits down — not behind the desk, in the chair across from them. “All right. All right.” The RP scene begins.

26.14.3 Correct Answer

When the correct phrase is assembled:

THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM

A player reads it aloud. Theron looks at whoever reads it.

He does not speak immediately. He holds eye contact for two seconds — which is longer than it sounds at a table.

Then: “Yes.”

That is all. Scene 4 begins.

26.14.4 Mechanics Reference

Action Check DC Result
Identify that the slips contain a pattern History or Arcana 10 Player recognises encoded-document conventions — this is a substitution or positional cipher, not a random text
Identify the “every Nth word” structure Investigation 12 Confirms positional pattern; player identifies N=3 as the likely interval
Identify N=3 and assemble the full phrase Investigation 14 (if trying without counting manually) Decodes in one action; otherwise let players count manually (no roll)
Speed decode (trained cipher-breaker / arcane specialist) Arcana or History 10 Player recognises this as a standard archival cipher used in restricted research environments — was common practice before 50 years ago

No roll is required for a player who counts carefully and tries different N values. The puzzle is solvable through pure attention; the rolls simply speed up or confirm the process.

26.14.5 GM Note: The Real Riddle

The puzzle mechanic is not the point. The point is what Theron did — he wrote down the most dangerous thing he knew, in a form designed to eventually be found, and then he locked it in a drawer and waited for someone to look. He told himself this was caution. He knows it was something else.

This is worth saying explicitly, as a GM aside, after the scene resolves — not to the players, but to yourself before you run it:

Theron is not here to be stopped. He is here to be heard.

🎬 GM Notes — Scene 2

26.14.6 Pacing

This scene breathes after Scene 1. Let it. The combat was loud and urgent; this is quiet and deliberate. If players try to rush Theron, he slows down naturally — he has been waiting eleven years, he is not going to skip anything.

The puzzle should take 10–20 minutes of real table time. If players get it in five minutes, great — move to Scene 4 early. If they are genuinely stuck after fifteen minutes, have Theron say “Every word has a position. Count.” and let them try one more time. If still stuck at twenty minutes, have him reach for the slips (triggering the choice) and let the choice carry them forward.

26.14.7 What Not to Do

  • Do not have Theron smile when they get it. This is not a satisfying puzzle reveal. This is a man who has been alone with a terrible truth for eleven years and has just watched someone else find it.
  • Do not rush past “Yes.” Give the table a full beat of silence after Theron confirms it. Let the players sit with what they just read. Some of them will not have fully processed it yet.

26.14.8 Connects Forward

  • The answer (THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM) is the foundation of Scene 4’s confession. Do not restate it in Scene 4 — let it echo.
  • The slips themselves become documents the players carry. If Harran sees them in Scene 5, he recognises their significance.
  • Theron’s behaviour during the puzzle — reaching for the slips, then sitting down when players hold them — is his first real moment of agency in this session. Note which player he looks at when he says “Yes.” That player will matter to him.

26.15 Scene 3 — The Puzzle: The Resonance Box

PURPOSE STATEMENT: This scene provides physical evidence — not encoded, not secondhand, but Corven’s original handwriting saying what he did and why. The box has been sitting on Theron’s shelf for eleven years, unopened by him. The players open it. That inversion — that Theron brought them here to do the thing he could not — is the scene’s emotional core.

EMOTIONAL TARGET: The precision of dread. The players now know what the documents say in coded form; the box gives them the original language. Extinguished is not in this scene — that word lives in Theron’s mouth in Scene 4. But the phrase “the completion requires their release” is, and attentive players will understand what release means.

DRAMATIC QUESTION: What has Theron been protecting by leaving this unopened for eleven years?

DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT: The box opens. The lid rises. Players see Corven’s handwriting on the top document — and at the bottom of the stack, a midwife’s record with ten tally marks and the words “All healthy. All wrong.”

🌍 Environment & Setup

26.16 Location: Theron’s Private Study — Upper Archive Floor

The staircase behind the reference desk leads to the upper floor — a small landing with three doors. Two are reading rooms for supervised rare-document access. The third, northward-facing room, is Theron’s private study.

It is a small room. Desk, cleared of papers — he cleared it specifically before barricading himself downstairs, the first sign of preparedness in a man who presents as perpetually unprepared. Floor-to-ceiling shelves on the north and west walls. One window, east-facing, overlooking the Archive courtyard — the amber lanterns of the courtyard visible through the glass, a faint orange smear in the permanent dusk.

The shelves are full on all sections except one: a single shelf at eye level on the north wall, approximately 6 ft from the desk, holds nothing except the box.

26.16.1 The Box

Small. Dark wood, almost black — the kind of color that comes from age and oil, not stain. Brass corners and hinges, green-tinted with use. The three indentations on the lid are arranged in an equilateral triangle, each roughly an inch in diameter, worn smooth at the center as though many fingers have touched them without pressing. Warm to the touch even in the cold of the upper floor. Not supernaturally warm — the warmth of a contained resonance, like the wood holds onto the memory of what it was sealed for.

When a correct name is spoken while a hand presses the corresponding indentation: that indentation glows, faintly amber, for as long as the hand is pressed. The glow fades when the hand lifts, but the state is held — a correctly triggered indentation does not require re-pressing if the sequence continues within 60 seconds.

When all three are correct: the lid releases with a sound like a single low bell note, struck once and absorbed immediately. The sound is felt more than heard.

26.16.2 Five Senses — The Study

Sight: - A small, ordered room — the desk cleared, the shelves full except for the one shelf that holds only the box. That cleared space is obvious. It was meant to be. - The box itself, dark wood and brass, sitting on a shelf of its own. Nothing else on that shelf. The single lamp sconce adjacent to it creates a glow that makes the brass corners catch light. - Through the east window: the Archive courtyard below, amber lanterns on posts, a cobblestone yard empty at this hour. The city going about its half-dark evening below them.

Sound: - Quieter than the reading room — the upper floor absorbs sound differently. No fireplace. No open door. Just the building settling and the distant city noise through the window glass. - When Theron speaks up here, his voice is at normal conversation volume and it sounds measured. Down in the reading room, everything had to carry. Up here, it does not.

Smell: - The study has its own smell: old paper like the archive below, but underneath it something personal — the particular combination of the same coat worn to the same room for eleven years, ink, and the very faint mineral-warm scent of the closed resonance box, which is not identifiable as anything but is distinctly present.

Touch: - The box itself: the surface polished smooth from handling, the brass corners slightly rough where the protective lacquer has worn. The indentations worn smooth in the center. The wood warm. - The room’s chill: Theron has not been up here today, and the room has been closed. Cold air from around the window frame.

Taste: - No smoke up here. Clean cold air, old wood. The Archive’s base note without the fire.

26.16.3 Transition from Scene 2

Theron leads them upstairs after the riddle resolves — or after he decides to tell them plainly. He does not explain where they are going. He takes the staircase. If players follow, they follow. If they ask, he says: “There is something upstairs you should see.”

When he enters the study, he goes directly to the shelf. He looks at the box. He does not pick it up. He says:

“I’ve had this for eleven years. I’ve held it. I’ve never spoken those names to it.”

He turns to face the players.

“I found the names in the third document bundle. The colophon of Corven’s final notation — the witnesses. Their names are on the signatures page.”

He stops.

“I almost burned the bundle myself when I found them. I didn’t. I don’t know whether that was the right choice.”

He is telling them where the names are. He will not say the names himself. That is their job now.

🧩 Puzzle

26.17 The Resonance Box — Full Mechanics

26.17.1 Finding the Names

The three names — CORVEN, SALI, WREN — appear in the colophon signatures of Corven’s final notation document, which is in one of the bundles saved from Scene 1. Specifically:

  • The document is titled “Notation: Final Disposition of the Dawn Mechanism” — it is the third document in the relevant bundle (the first two are supplementary observations and a site record from the Ashring).
  • On the final page, the colophon lists three witnesses in signature order, left to right: Archmagister Corven, Scholar Sali Orthen, Scholar Wren Duvash.
  • No roll is required to read the names — the document is legible, the names are prominent. A DC 10 Investigation check identifies the connection between these names as “ritual witnesses” vs. authors; DC 12 connects them to the three-indentation pattern on the box.

If one bundle was burned (T2): This document survived in the other bundles — it appears in two of the three bundles as a copy. The names are available unless two bundles were burned.

If two bundles were burned: The colophon document is in whichever single bundle survived. Players have the names.

If all bundles were burned: Players have no documentary source for the names. Theron knows all three — he found the colophon originally. He will tell them if directly asked. He has just never been willing to say them to the box himself.

26.17.2 The Solution

Step 1 — Press left indentation, speak: CORVEN. → Left indentation glows amber. Hold.

Step 2 — Press center indentation (while left is held or within 60 seconds of it), speak: SALI. → Center indentation glows amber. Both glow.

Step 3 — Press right indentation, speak: WREN. → All three glow simultaneously. Bell note. Lid releases.

Order matters. Any name in the wrong position triggers a dull harmonic tone and resets that indentation (the others remain lit for 60 seconds). A wrong third attempt when two are correct causes all three to reset.

26.17.3 Partial Success (Players have incomplete names)

If players know two names and are missing one:

  • Two indentations light correctly. The third remains dark.
  • Theron, watching: “There were three witnesses. I have told you where the document is.”
  • If they press him directly — “What is the third name?” — he closes his eyes, pauses for a moment, then says it. He does not explain why he paused.
  • Alternatively: DC 14 Investigation on surviving documents, or DC 12 History to recall that Corven’s rituals required two verification witnesses by Archival law, and any two-name list implies a third who was senior (i.e., the third name is Corven himself, who was also listed as a witness though he authored the notation).

26.17.4 Forced Opening

Thieves’ Tools, DC 20: The box opens. The seal fractures — a crack runs along the wood between two brass corners, and the tone is wrong, a dissonant split-second scrape instead of the single bell note.

The documents inside are readable. The edges are scorched — not burned, but the paper carries the stain of broken containment, as though the energy the seal was holding had to go somewhere and went through the paper’s edges.

Theron’s expression when he sees them is not anger. It is the look of someone watching the last physical evidence of their guilt get hurt. He does not say anything. He does not need to.

GM note: This is not a failure state. The documents are readable. The emotional cost is paid by Theron, not by the players — but that cost is real and Theron will carry it into Scene 4.

26.17.5 Contents of the Box

1. Corven’s Original Ritual Redirection Notes Seven pages, Corven’s handwriting throughout — a precise, forward-slanting academic hand. The documents are Corven’s working notes from the night of the ritual, written in sequence as the ritual progressed and then as it changed course.

Key passage (page 3 of 7):

“The anchors are not lost. The light did not die. It redistributed into ten living vessels born at the moment of inversion. The ritual is incomplete, not failed. The completion requires their release.”

Players who spend ten minutes reading the full seven pages learn: - The ritual was designed to redirect the existing solar energy trapped in the dome mechanism, not to generate new light. - Corven knew the inversion was happening as it happened — he made a choice to let it continue rather than abort. - He does not explain in the notes why he made that choice. There is a gap between page 4 and page 5 where a section appears to have been excised — not burned, but carefully removed with a clean cut. Two sentences on page 4 end mid-thought.

2. Fragment 2 of the Notation Key A small folded card in Corven’s hand, containing the cipher key for the second set of compressed notation marks in his main journal. Combined with Fragment 1 (from Session 1, Scene 3), players can now decode approximately 60% of Corven’s journal entries. The remaining 40% requires Fragment 3, which is in Session 3.

3. The Midwife’s Record A single sheet of lighter parchment, different hand — looped, practical, the handwriting of someone who wrote many things quickly and legibly. Dated with a calendar mark that corresponds to the night of the ritual, fifty years ago.

The record reads:

“Night delivery, unusual circumstances — summoned to Ashring district. Ten births, three-hour window. No complications in any. All presenting healthy. Midwife Orell, attending.”

At the bottom, below the signature, in smaller script:

“All healthy. All wrong.”

The last line is in different ink — darker, added later. The handwriting is the same, but the pressure is heavier. This was written after the fact. Possibly much after.

26.17.6 Theron During the Opening

When the box is correctly named open: He watches. He does not move toward it. He stands at the side of the room while the players look at the contents. When they reach the midwife’s record, he speaks, quietly:

“I found that on the same day I decoded the rest. I’ve thought about whether the midwife knew, afterward. Whether she went back and added that line because she eventually worked it out, or because she simply knew in the room that something was different about those ten births and didn’t have a word for it yet.”

A pause.

“I’m very sorry for what I’m about to tell you.”

This is his transition line into Scene 4. After he says it, do not move on immediately — let it land.

Theron’s Full Dialogue — Scene 3:

  • “I’ve had that box for eleven years. I’ve held it. I’ve never spoken those names to it.”
  • “I found the names in the third bundle. I almost burned the bundle myself when I found them. I didn’t. I don’t know whether that was the right choice.”
🎬 GM Notes — Scene 3

26.17.7 Pacing

Scene 3 flows directly from Scene 2. The momentum from the riddle carries into the box. Let the naming sequence be deliberate — players should say each name clearly, and you should respond to each with the indentation glowing. This is a small ceremony. It should feel like one.

The contents of the box should be read slowly. Give players time to look at each document. The key passage on page 3 may need to be read aloud twice — “the completion requires their release” is carrying enormous weight, and players need time to absorb it before Scene 4 unpacks it.

26.17.8 What Not to Do

  • Do not tell players what “release” means. Not yet. Let it sit.
  • Do not have Theron explain the box’s mechanism. He doesn’t know how it works mechanically — Corven made it. He knows the names, and he knows the box has been on that shelf for eleven years.
  • Do not rush past the midwife’s record. “All healthy. All wrong.” is the scene’s emotional peak. Let it breathe.

26.17.9 Connects Forward

  • The Notation Key Fragment 2, combined with Fragment 1, gives players significant access to Corven’s journal in Sessions 3 and 4. Track which fragments they have.
  • The excised pages from Corven’s notes (pages 4–5 gap) are a thread that does not resolve until Session 4. Do not explain them now. Plant them: “There is a gap. Pages 4 and 5 — the beginning of page 5 picks up mid-sentence. Something was removed. Cleanly, with a cutting edge, not torn.”
  • The midwife’s record becomes important in Session 3 when players meet Sera Voss, who has a copy of the same record.
  • “I’m very sorry for what I’m about to tell you.” — Theron’s last line in this scene — is the bridge to Scene 4. Do not let any other dialogue follow it. Close the scene here and move.

26.18 Scene 4 — The RP Scene: Theron’s Confession

PURPOSE STATEMENT: This scene exists for one sentence: “The technical term in Corven’s notation is extinguished.” Everything before it is the weight that makes that word land. Everything after it is the players deciding what kind of people they are. This is the session’s pivot — the moment from which nothing in the campaign can be undone.

EMOTIONAL TARGET: The particular quality of being told something you had already half-worked-out, by someone who has been carrying it for eleven years, in a cold room at night. Not shock exactly — the shock was the riddle. This is confirmation. Confirmation is heavier.

DRAMATIC QUESTION: Who decides what to do with the truth — and does deciding make you responsible for it?

DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT: Theron says “extinguished.” The GM stops. The room is quiet. Nothing happens until a player speaks.

🌍 Environment & Setup

26.19 Location: Theron’s Private Study — Same Room, After the Box

The study has not changed. The box is open on the shelf or on the desk between them, depending on where players moved it. Corven’s documents are spread out or stacked, depending on how carefully players handled them. The window shows the courtyard below, amber lanterns, the cobblestone yard.

Theron does not sit behind the desk.

This is the key physical detail of the scene: he comes around to the front of it. He sits in the plain visitor’s chair — the one that faces the desk, normally for the people who come to him with archive requests, the chair that positions the sitter as a supplicant or a petitioner. He is putting himself on that side of the desk deliberately. He is not the authority here. He has information he has been holding for eleven years, and he is surrendering it.

He sits. His hands are in his lap. He is not looking at the box or the documents but at the players — specifically, at whoever is closest.

The room is quiet. Wherever the Varenhold city noise is, it is below them, muffled by the building and the closed window. The archive settles around them. Occasional creak of a shelf shifting.

26.19.1 Five Senses — Scene 4

Sight: - Theron in the visitor’s chair. Coat on. Hands in his lap. Not gesturing. Not fidgeting. The very specific stillness of someone who has rehearsed a thing many times and has arrived at the point of saying it. - The spread of documents on the desk or table between them — Corven’s notes, the midwife’s record, the notation key fragment. The physical evidence arrayed like a case file. - The box, open, on the shelf or on the desk. The amber glow of the indentations has faded. It is just a box now. - The east window: dark courtyard below, amber lanterns making halos in the permanent dusk. Whatever time it is, it is late.

Sound: - The building’s quiet. Occasional deep creak of the archive structure — wood contracting in the night cold. - The distant bell sequence from the Spire Quarter marking the hour, muffled by glass. - Theron’s breathing, which is steady. He has decided. He is not afraid of saying it. He is afraid of what happens after he says it, but that is different.

Smell: - Old paper and the cold of the study. No fire up here. Theron’s ink-cuff smell, present if players are close. - Faint scorched-seal smell if the box was forced open — it fades slowly.

Touch: - Cold air from the window frame on the back of the neck for players near it. - If a player is holding Corven’s documents while Theron speaks: the weight of the paper, the slight roughness of a fifty-year-old page. What their hands are holding is what Theron is about to explain.

Taste: - Clean cold. No smoke. The Archive at rest.

🎭 RP

26.20 Theron Waide — Full NPC Profile

26.20.1 OGAS

Occupation Master Archivist, Varenhold Civic Archive. Has held the position for sixteen years. Was a research archivist for twelve years before that — he has been in this building for twenty-eight years. He knows where everything is.
Goal To be heard. To stop carrying this alone. To be forgiven, though he will not ask for it — not because he is too proud, but because he genuinely believes he may not deserve it and does not want to have that confirmed by asking.
Attitude Deliberate. Careful with words in a way that looks like precision but is actually guilt — every sentence chosen to be technically true, because if any part of it is untrue he has compounded the original wrong with a further one. He has maintained this standard for eleven years. It is exhausting. It shows.
Secret He has been destroying his own annotations for eleven years. The original documents — Corven’s notes, the midwife’s record, the notation key fragments — are intact. His own thinking about those documents is gone. Marginal notes, separate analysis sheets, the personal journal he kept for eight months after decoding the ritual — all destroyed, one document at a time, over years. He has been erasing the record of his own knowledge to make it easier to pretend he never had it. The documents survived because they were not his to destroy. His understanding of them nearly did not.

26.20.2 Voice

Theron speaks in complete sentences. He does not trail off. He does not use “you know” or filler phrases. He pauses between thoughts — real pauses, long enough to be slightly uncomfortable at a table, because he is not filling space, he is thinking. The pauses are the honesty. When he speaks, he means exactly what he says.

He is not monotone. There is feeling in his voice — it is simply feeling that has been carefully managed for eleven years and has learned to be contained. Occasionally a sentence will be shorter than expected. That is where the containment is tightest.

26.20.3 The Seven Beats of the Confession

Deliver these beats in order. Do not rush between them. After each one, let players respond before continuing. If players do not respond, Theron continues after a breath — he has prepared for silence.

Beat 1 — The Preamble:

“I found it eleven years ago. I decoded the notation. I found the midwife records. I understood what Corven wrote.”

A pause — long enough for players to say something if they want to.

“I told no one because once I said it, someone would have to decide something. And I didn’t want to be the one who made that happen.”

Beat 2 — The Waiting:

“I’ve been hoping someone would come who could do what I couldn’t. So I waited. I arranged the fragments where they could be found. I left the box where it could be seen.”

He is looking at them. He does not break eye contact.

“I’m very sorry for what I’m about to tell you.”

Beat 3 — The Truth:

Deliver this as Theron. Speak directly to the players, not past them. Not as read-aloud — as Theron in the chair across from them, telling them something he has been waiting eleven years to say.

“The Dawnborn are the ritual. The ten people living in this city — the ones running the food halls and tending the sick and teaching children and keeping the peace — they are the ritual. They have been holding fifty years of redirected solar energy in their bodies since the night they were born.”

A pause. Long enough to be uncomfortable.

“Completing the ritual means releasing that energy simultaneously.”

A shorter pause.

“The technical term in Corven’s notation is extinguished.”

Beat 4 — The Silence:

Stop.

Do not add anything. Do not clarify. Do not move the scene forward.

The word extinguished has been said. Let it sit in the room. This is the moment the session exists for, and it will only happen once. Give the table the full weight of it before anyone speaks.

However long the silence is — let it be that long.

Beat 5 — The Question:

When the silence has run its course, ask the table directly. Speak as the GM, not as Theron. Make eye contact around the table if you are at one:

“What does your character do with this information in the next five minutes?”

This is not rhetorical. This is not an invitation to monologue. Ask every player, in order, to answer in some form — in character, out of character, one sentence or five. The scene does not proceed until each player has said something.

If a player says “I don’t know” — that is an answer. Theron says: “Neither do I. Neither have I. For eleven years.”

Beat 6 — The Accounting:

Players will have questions. These are Theron’s answers.

“Why didn’t you go to the Council?” > “I tried. Once. Before Ostenveld took office — before Keseph Vyne was in his current position. The response was two weeks in a holding room and my documents returned to me damaged. Not destroyed. Damaged. Whoever reviewed them wanted me to know they had been read. The city did not want to know.”

“Why didn’t you tell someone? The Dawnborn themselves?” > “Tell them what? That they are going to die for the city’s sun — possibly by their own decision if given a choice, possibly not, possibly before the question is even put to them? I thought about it. Day 4,017 was when I stopped counting. That was when I realised it didn’t matter how long I waited. Nothing was going to make this easier to say.”

“Are you a coward?”

Theron considers this.

“I don’t think I’m a coward. I think I told myself I was being careful. The difference feels smaller every year.”

“Can the ritual be done another way? Do the Dawnborn have to die?”

Theron goes quiet. Then:

“There is a researcher. Her name is Isolde Maren. She had a laboratory at the Spire until three years ago. Her work was put into institutional review. She is still working, I believe — somewhere in the Ashring district. Her theory was that the energy could be transferred rather than released. I cannot tell you whether her method functions. I know Keseph Vyne reviewed her proposal and filed it. That is all I know.”

He pauses.

“I should have told you this at the beginning.”

Beat 7 — Theron’s Personal Reckoning:

If players have not asked about alternatives, or after the Isolde information is given, Theron says one more thing — quietly, not as additional information but as something he has been building to:

“Sera Voss came here eight years ago. She asked to see Corven’s general historical notes — not the restricted documents, the public ones. I helped her. She thanked me. She was very polite. She did not tell me she had already worked it out.”

He stops.

“I’ve wondered since then whether she knows what I know, and whether she has been making the same choice I have — not for the city’s sake, but for her own. Because looking at the people who might have to die, and deciding not to tell them, is a very specific kind of cowardice that feels like protection until you look at it directly.”

26.20.4 Extended Dialogue — Optional Use

These lines are available if conversations go in specific directions. Use as needed, not in sequence.

On Corven: > “His name was Archmagister Corven. He died the night of the ritual, at the Ashring. He knew it would happen. He wrote his notes in full before he went. He could have stopped the ritual before the inversion completed. He didn’t. I’ve spent eleven years trying to understand whether that makes him a murderer or a martyr. I don’t think either word fits.”

On his own annotations: > “I’ve been destroying my working notes for eleven years. My marginal notations on Corven’s documents. My analysis papers. A journal I kept for eight months after decoding the ritual. Gone. I told myself this was protecting the original documents from my own interpretive contamination. That was not the reason. The reason was that reading my own handwriting about what the documents said was the closest I came to admitting I knew. I couldn’t do it.”

On the Reckoning: > “Harran Lecht has known for two years. I don’t know how he found out — not from me. Possibly from Keseph Vyne. Possibly from his own archivists. The Reckoning’s approach to the problem is straightforward and I understand it even as I find it terrible.”

On waiting: > “The worst day was year three. I remember exactly: it was the Festival of Continuance, the Dawnhall was full, I could hear singing from the street below the Archive window. I had my hand on the door to go downstairs and tell someone — Theron, the archivist, who has been sitting on this — and I thought: but who? And I sat back down. That was year three. There were eight more.”

🎬 GM Notes — Scene 4

26.20.5 Pacing

This scene has no wrong pace. Let players drive it after Beat 5. Some tables will want to talk for thirty minutes; some will be silent for a long time and then ask one question. Both are correct.

The only pacing rule is Beat 4: after “extinguished,” do not add anything. Do not gloss it. Do not say “so basically, the Dawnborn would die.” Theron said it. Players heard it. Let it work.

If players seem stunned into silence, wait. If they are still silent after a minute, quietly ask: “What is your character doing right now? Even physically — are they standing, sitting, looking at what?” This is not a prompt to speak. It is permission to be still.

26.20.6 What Not to Do

  • Do not let Theron perform his guilt. He is not crying. He is not breaking down. He is delivering information he has been holding for eleven years with the particular precision of someone who has rehearsed this conversation in his head thousands of times and has no energy left for theatre.
  • Do not skip Beat 5. Every player should say something before the scene moves forward. The question “what does your character do in the next five minutes” is load-bearing — it forces each player to make a choice about who their character is in response to the worst thing they have heard this campaign.
  • Do not have Theron offer comfort. He is not in a position to comfort anyone. He is the reason they are in this room knowing this thing. The best he can do is be honest, and he has been.
  • Do not give the Isolde Maren information unless players ask about alternatives. It is the campaign’s first lifeline — it should feel like something found, not handed over.

26.20.7 What to Confirm Before Running This Scene

  • You know the names of all ten Dawnborn the players have met or will meet. When Theron says “the ten people running the food halls and tending the sick and teaching children” — those are people the players know by name. If they have met Sera Voss, Tomas Wen, Lira Shan: those names are in that sentence. Let that land.
  • You know whether the players told any Dawnborn anything before this scene. If they have, Theron’s confession is not the first time the truth is in the room — but it is the first time it has been spoken aloud with full documentation.

26.20.8 Connects Forward

  • The name Isolde Maren is the campaign’s first alternative-path thread. Players who pursue her in Session 3 find someone who has a method that might work — and a reason she has been stopped from using it.
  • Keseph Vyne’s name appears here for the first time as a named obstacle. He is the bureaucratic mechanism that suppressed both the archive access and Isolde’s research. Players will encounter him directly in Sessions 3–4.
  • Theron’s mention of Sera Voss arriving eight years ago is a gift to sharp players. If they go back to Sera and ask her directly: “Do you know what you are?” — that conversation changes everything about Sessions 3–5.
  • Every conversation with a Dawnborn from this point forward is different. Track this. The players are now carrying a truth the Dawnborn do not have, and every interaction is a choice about whether to give it.

26.21 Scene 5 — Closing: Harran’s Arrival

PURPOSE STATEMENT: This scene exists to put a face on the faction that has already decided. Harran Lecht is not the villain — he is the city’s math made human. He knows what the players found. He has been waiting for them to find it. He wants to know if they are going to do anything that will slow down the only solution he can see. This scene plants the shape of Sessions 3–5: not the Reckoning as enemy, but the Reckoning as a deadline.

EMOTIONAL TARGET: The cold of being seen. The players leave the Archive carrying something enormous, and there is a man in the alley who already knows. He is not surprised. He is not angry. He is recalibrating.

DRAMATIC QUESTION: What is Harran willing to do if the players take too long to decide?

DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT: Harran’s exit line. “When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it.” He means it. He is not performing. He turns and walks into the dark and the soldiers go with him and the players are alone in the alley with documents and a question that has no clean answer.

26.22 Location: Archive Rear Alley / Spire Quarter Street

The players are leaving the Archive. The front entrance on the south face is the direct route, but after what happened inside, a rear exit or a side street is plausible. Harran anticipated both. He is at the Archive rear alley — a narrow cobblestone passage running along the building’s north face, between the Archive’s external wall and a row of locked storage buildings. Three Reckoning soldiers are already positioned: one at each end of the alley, one between Harran and the Archive’s rear door.

The alley is approximately 10 ft wide and 60 ft long. Two lamp posts — iron, similar to the Lowmark style but newer, Spire Quarter maintenance. One at each end. Between them: darkness and cobblestone and Harran.

He is standing in the center. Coat open, no weapon drawn. Hands at his sides. He is not performing casual — he has simply decided that drawing a weapon would be the wrong message, and so he has not drawn one.

Night now. Players have been in the Archive for three or more hours. The city is in its deep amber evening — the perpetual dusk, but late in it, the streets quieter than earlier.

26.22.1 Five Senses — The Alley

Sight: - The alley’s narrowness — two people wide at most. The lamp posts at each end casting light inward that doesn’t quite reach the center, where Harran stands in the half-dark. - Three soldiers — one at each alley mouth, one between Harran and the Archive door. They are armed but not actively drawing. Their hands are on their weapons’ hilts, not grips. The difference is intentional. - Harran himself: 50s, lean in the way that suggests a younger body that has been maintained through discipline rather than ease. His Reckoning coat is not the field grey of his soldiers — it is darker, the commander’s variant. He carries no visible shield. He carries a longsword on his left hip but his right hand is hanging open at his side. - Document bundles in the players’ hands or bags — Harran’s gaze goes to them immediately and stays there for one beat before moving to the players’ faces.

Sound: - The city at distance — the Spire Quarter is quieter than the Lowmark, less residential foot traffic at this hour. Somewhere a cart wheel. A distant bell sequence, the hour being marked. - Cobblestones underfoot — the players’ own footsteps as they enter the alley, which Harran hears and uses to track their approach. He has been standing still. They have been moving. He timed his position to be here when they arrived. - Harran’s voice when he speaks: measured, low — pitched for the alley, not for carrying. A voice that knows it does not need to be loud to be heard.

Smell: - Night air, stone cold, the faint oil of the lamp posts. Nothing dramatic. The alley is clean — this is the Spire Quarter, maintained. - The smoke from inside the Archive on the players’ clothes, distinguishable to anyone standing close.

Touch: - The cold of the alley between buildings — air channeled and caught, colder than the open street. - The weight of the document bundles, if players are carrying them. They are suddenly very present.

Taste: - Cold air. The Archive smoke still at the back of the throat. The mineral dryness that is Varenhold’s constant.

26.22.2 Three Read-Aloud Variants

A — Players leave by the rear door:

The Archive’s rear door opens onto a narrow alley — cobblestones, locked storage buildings on the far side, two lamp posts. Night air, cold. You’re three steps out when you see him: a man in a dark coat standing in the center of the alley with the particular stillness of someone who has been waiting long enough to stop expecting you to hurry. Three soldiers. One at each end. One close. No weapons drawn. The man in the center’s eyes go to what you’re carrying. Then they come back to your faces.

B — Players leave by the front and are intercepted:

The Archive’s front entrance opens onto the Spire Quarter road. You’re a dozen feet out when movement catches: figures stepping from a side passage. Three soldiers flanking a man in a Reckoning commander’s coat, who has positioned himself between you and the open street. His weapon is sheathed. His hands are down. He is looking at what you are carrying with the very focused attention of someone who already knows what it is. He does not say “stop.” He simply stands there, and the weight of what he knows is in his posture.

C — Players spot him before he speaks (high Perception):

You see him before he wants you to — or perhaps exactly when he wants you to; it is not entirely clear. A Reckoning commander in the Spire Quarter night, three soldiers placed at clean angles, himself in the center of a space you need to cross. He is watching you the way someone watches a thing they have been tracking for a long time and have finally seen clearly. When you stop, he says: “Stay where you are. I’d like to see what you’re carrying.” Calm. Unhurried. A man who has already done the math.

26.22.3 Pre-Scene Setup

Harran arrived approximately twenty minutes before the players — long enough to position his soldiers but not so long he had to wait at ease. The soldiers at the alley mouths are there to contain the space, not to attack. The soldiers between him and the Archive door is there to prevent players from retreating inside.

What Harran knows before speaking to them: - The players went into the Archive. Soldiers he sent emerged without completing their document-destruction order. - The players are coming out with documents. - Theron Waide survived (he knows Theron by name; Theron’s survival either confirms or complicates his assessment).

What Harran does not know yet: - Whether the players read Corven’s key passage. - Whether they decoded the resonance box. - Whether Theron told them about Isolde Maren.

These unknowns are what the Assess action (see combat block) is trying to fill in.

26.23 Scene 5 — Harran Lecht’s Arrival

Design intent: This is a negotiation scene with a combat backing. Harran is not here to fight and does not want to fight. He is here to calibrate what the players know, to deliver one message, and to leave. The soldiers are containment, not aggression. The secondary objective — keeping the document contents out of Harran’s direct line of sight — rewards players who have been thinking about information management throughout the session.

This scene can resolve entirely through roleplay. If players attack, it becomes combat. If players handle the documents well and talk to Harran clearly, he may leave them with something unexpected: a sense of what kind of opponent he actually is.

26.25.3 What Happens If Harran Reads the Documents

If Harran succeeds on Assess and reads Corven’s key passage or the riddle slips:

  • He confirms that the players have found the same material he has.
  • His Session 3 response is more aggressive: he initiates contact directly rather than waiting, and the Reckoning’s timeline becomes explicit rather than implied.
  • He may say (quietly, to himself, as Tactical Read): “They have the third page. And the midwife’s record, from the weight of the bundle.” Let the players hear this.

If Harran fails to read the documents (players conceal them well):

  • He knows they have something but cannot confirm specifics.
  • He remains in assessment mode in Session 3: watching, not acting.
  • He exits with his line but does not add the follow-up observation about Corven’s notes.

26.26 Round-by-Round Guide (If Combat Occurs)

26.26.1 Round 1 — Harran Does Not Draw

He uses Assess this round (action) and Command (bonus action) to reposition one soldier. He does not attack.

If players attack him first: he defends with Parry reaction and draws on Round 2.

26.26.2 Round 2+ — If Attacked

He goes to Multiattack. He uses Dagger (thrown) against the fastest-moving player. He does not use Shield Bash — he has no shield. He fights efficiently and without anger.

26.26.3 Exit Condition

Harran withdraws when he has confirmed or failed to confirm document contents — typically after two rounds of Assess. Or when reduced to half HP. Signal word: “Out.” All soldiers disengage simultaneously.

He does not need to be at risk to leave. He came to see. He has seen enough.

26.27 Harran Lecht — Full NPC Profile

26.27.1 Physical Description

50s. Lean jaw, watchful eyes that move constantly without appearing to. Carries himself with the specific economy of someone who has decided everything in advance — no wasted motion, no unnecessary expression. The Reckoning commander’s coat is darker grey than field issue. He has a scar on his right forearm, visible when his sleeve catches in movement. He does not draw attention to it.

26.27.2 OGAS

Occupation Commander, the Reckoning — the city’s emergency enforcement arm, technically dormant since Ostenveld’s administration but operationally active under Vyne’s authorizations.
Goal Assess what the players found. Determine whether they are going to act voluntarily, delay indefinitely, or attempt a third path. Decide, based on that assessment, whether the Reckoning needs to move before the players can introduce a variable he cannot control.
Attitude Professional calibration. He is not hostile to the players as people. He is hostile to delay. He has done the math on Varenhold’s food stores and he knows what six months of partial recovery looks like. He is not wrong about the number. He may be wrong about the method.
Secret He knows about Isolde Maren’s transfer research. He has not disclosed it publicly because it creates a delay — the Dawnborn could choose to wait for a safer method indefinitely, and Varenhold cannot afford indefinitely. He suppressed the research through Vyne, not because it is impossible, but because it removes the urgency that keeps the city from looking away.

26.27.3 Want

Confirmation that the players are not going to attempt to circumvent the ritual completion through a method that will buy weeks or months but ultimately fail — or that will succeed and eliminate a solution the city needs now.

26.27.4 Fear

That the players will find Isolde Maren, that her method will work, and that by the time it is implemented the food system will have collapsed enough that the restored sun arrives too late to matter. He is not wrong to fear this. He may be wrong about the timeline.

26.27.5 Lie

“I’m protecting the city.” True — but not the city’s Dawnborn. He has drawn a line and put the ten people who are the ritual on the wrong side of it. He tells himself this is math. He knows it is also a choice. He has not examined the difference closely.

26.27.6 Harran’s Dialogue

On arrival: > “Stay where you are. I’d like to see what you’re carrying.”

If players comply partially (show documents but conceal some): > “I see. So it’s confirmed.”

He says this without surprise. It was already confirmed for him before they came out. He wanted to see whether they would show him willingly.

If players refuse entirely: > “That tells me what I need to know.”

Also without surprise. Also not angry. Both responses are the same information to him.

If players ask what he already knows: > “I know what is in Corven’s notes. I have known for two years. The question is not what they say. The question is what you intend to do with the information — and how much time you think you have.”

If players challenge his authority: > “The Reckoning’s authority is emergency provision under Charter. It was invoked. I did not invoke it. I was asked to.”

He will not say Vyne’s name here. He uses “the administration” or “the Council” or simply “I was asked.”

If players attempt to negotiate: > “You want to find a third way. I understand the impulse. I would like you to tell me its timeline.”

If players cite Isolde Maren or mention a transfer method, his expression does not change, but there is a pause before his next sentence that was not there before. “That research is under institutional review. It has been for three years.” He will not confirm that he knows why.

If cornered or when choosing to leave:

“When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it.”

This is not a threat. It is not contempt. It is the sentence of a man who has done the arithmetic and arrived at a number he did not choose and cannot argue away, and who is trying to make other people understand that the arithmetic does not care about their principles.

He means it. It should land that way.

If attacked: > “This is not the conversation I wanted to have.”

He defends himself fully. He does not hold back. He is not interested in dying to demonstrate restraint.

26.27.7 Attitude Shift Triggers

Reduces escalation (Harran reassesses players as serious actors, not obstacles): - Players explicitly acknowledge the food timeline and engage with it rather than dismissing it. - Players mention Isolde Maren without being told about her — this signals independent research and shifts his assessment from reactive to proactive. - Players ask Harran what he believes, personally, about the Dawnborn as people — he pauses, which is itself the answer. - Players do not attempt violence and engage the substance of what he is saying.

Increases escalation (Harran marks them as a timeline threat): - Players threaten him or his soldiers. - Players claim they have already told the Dawnborn the truth (he will verify this; if true, his Session 3 timeline accelerates). - Players invoke the Chancellor’s authority against him — he has working authorization from Vyne and knows he outranks a Chancellor’s investigation commission. - Players mention trying to stop the ritual entirely without a replacement solution — this moves them from delay to obstruction in his classification.

🎬 GM Notes — Scene 5

26.27.8 Pacing

This scene should be short. Players have just come through the Confession — they are emotionally loaded. Harran should feel like a cold splash of water: everything Theron said in the warmth of the study is now standing in the alley in a coat, asking what they are going to do about it. The scene is five to fifteen minutes, not thirty.

If players want to talk at length, let them — but Harran stays efficient. He does not give long speeches. He asks one or two questions. He waits for answers. He takes his information and leaves.

26.27.9 The Document Secondary Objective

Track carefully whether Harran succeeds on his Assess action. This is a quiet mechanical note with large downstream consequences:

  • If he reads the documents: his Session 3 move is confident and direct.
  • If he fails: his Session 3 move is careful and probing — he will send a contact rather than coming himself.

Players who actively manage the documents’ visibility are making a smart choice that the campaign rewards. Acknowledge it.

26.27.10 What Not to Do

  • Do not make Harran cruel. He is not cruel. He is a man doing terrible arithmetic with genuine conviction. That is worse than cruelty and more interesting.
  • Do not let the soldiers attack unprovoked. They are containment. If players move toward an alley exit without attacking, soldiers move to block — that is not an attack, it is blocking.
  • Do not give Harran more than one speech. The exit line is his. Let it be the last thing he says before he turns and goes.

26.27.11 Harran’s Exit

When Harran leaves, do not describe him hurrying. He turns. He says one word to his nearest soldier — “Out.” — and all four of them disengage and walk. Not quickly. Not urgently. As people who have completed the task they came to do.

The players are left in the alley. They have the documents. They have Theron’s confession. They have a name — Isolde Maren — if Theron gave it. And they have Harran’s exit line ringing in the cold.

End the scene there. Do not transition directly into the End of Session debrief. Let there be one beat of the players simply standing in the alley, in the Varenhold night, with what they now know.

26.27.12 Connects Forward

  • Harran’s Tactical Retreat at half HP (if combat occurs) is the campaign’s first sign that the Reckoning treats the players as a serious factor. This shifts everything in Session 3.
  • “When the city collapses in six months” — track the number. Six months is Harran’s internal clock. Players who find ways to extend this timeline (food stores, alternative rituals, political pressure) change Harran’s urgency. This is a resource they can work.
  • Harran’s knowledge of Isolde Maren (if players mention her) is a thread. His suppression of the research is the second major bureaucratic obstruction they will trace back to Vyne.
  • If players managed to hide the documents completely: Harran’s assessment of them upgrades. He stops treating them as the Chancellor’s investigators and starts treating them as independent actors. That distinction matters in Sessions 3–5.

26.28 End of Session

26.28.1 What Players Now Know

  • Completing the ritual as designed means releasing that energy simultaneously. The technical term in Corven’s notation is extinguished.
  • Harran Lecht knows they know. The Reckoning is not going to wait indefinitely for a voluntary solution.
