[RECKONING OPERATIONAL ORDER — VARENHOLD ARCHIVE]

Objective:   Retrieve — CR-44-09, CR-44-11, AST-48-Y (ritual text)
Secondary:   CR-44-17 (Corven correspondence, complete set)
Tertiary:    Any notation key materials found on premises

If retrieval impossible: destroy in place. Do not allow
Chancellor's investigators to leave with originals.

Regarding the Archivist: he is not to be harmed. He is
a resource. If he resists, restrain. Marsh has authority
to detain him for transfer.

The Sealed Drawer (Shelf 4-17-3): DO NOT OPEN.

ask why. Do not attempt it. Leave it sealed.

The Sealed Drawer order appears twice in Reckoning documents. They know it exists. They were told not to touch it. Someone who outranks Marsh gave that order.

The Chancellor’s seal appeared on Session 1 agents. These orders don’t carry her seal. The Reckoning’s command structure has a different source.

Found on the Reckoning Infiltrator if captured or searched.

[FIELD OBSERVATION — DAWNHALLS, WEEK 3]

Interview questions (approved list):

   something is holding you?

impossible by standard methods.

Ward-emitter unit 3 deployed under kitchen floorboards.

The Reckoning is running multiple ward-emitters across the Dawnhalls. Session 1’s unit was one of four.

“Phase 2” is undefined. “Extraction” means they were prepared to take Petra by force.

They need the Dawnborn’s subjective experience of being a Lux Anchor. Why? That’s the question Session 3 answers.

Theron’s Journal — Day 4,017

Theron produces this himself at the Dawnhalls. He doesn’t hide it this time.

[A small leather journal, much-handled. Open to a recent entry. The handwriting is careful but tired.]

Day 4,017. Still counting. Still unable to tell them.

The mathematics of love and duty: I know the answer, and I cannot say it aloud. The day I found out — the day I should have gone to them immediately — I told myself I needed more time. That was 4,017 days ago.

What kind of scholar am I?

The Aldwyne family wrote again from Solenne. The younger son. He found my address somehow. He wanted to know if I had ever found what I was “close to understanding.” He doesn’t know what he’s asking. He doesn’t know that his father and sister are dead because I said I was close.

I did not write back.

I am still counting.

4,017 days = approximately 11 years.

Day 1 was an estate sale — the dispersal of Corven’s second assistant’s private papers. Theron attended. He bought the knowledge. He has been sitting on it since.

He is not a villain. He is someone who knew a terrible thing and found a thousand reasons, one per day, not to say it.

Edoran’s Letter — handed over at Scene 3 or found on Jaret (Session 1 carry-over)

If not already given in Session 1 — Edoran produces it himself when he finds the party.

The ritual’s completion is possible. The cost is real. I have been waiting eleven years for someone willing to face both without flinching. The Chancellor knows part of what I know. She chose not to finish the sentence. I will finish it.

I am not your enemy. I am not the Reckoning. The difference matters.

If you want answers rather than comfortable ignorance, you know where to find me.

— E.

“I will finish the sentence she wouldn’t.”

He did. Theron did too, tonight.

The Chancellor chose not to know. Two men chose not to say. Everyone was waiting for someone else to go first. That was the party.

The Three Parchment Slips — give all three simultaneously, Scene 2

Theron removes these from his locked desk drawer after the combat. Set them on the table. Hand this page to players — they work on it directly. Do not read them aloud sequentially; let players see all three at once.

SLIP ONE

The parchment is small, folded so many times the creases are deep as scars.

The Dawnmark rises when the anchor holds. The vessel must be willing. The energy of the third circuit does not disperse; it dwells.

SLIP TWO

Same hand as Slip One. Slightly different ink — written at a different time.

Ask the archivist what lives within the amber light. The answer is not in the records. Within them is the truth of the ritual’s end.

SLIP THREE

The handwriting matches but the parchment feels marginally different — recopied at some point.

Every third scholar agreed: the Dawnborn are the mechanism. The energy lives in willing vessels. The ritual them waits still.

The pattern: read every third word across all three slips in sequence.

Answer: THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM

Theron encoded this for himself, not against the players. The real question is why someone would write a secret in a form designed to be found.

Slip Three was recopied — a player who examines it closely (DC 15 Insight) notices very slightly different ink. Theron went back and corrected a transcription error three years ago. He did not examine why he did that.

Corven’s Ritual Redirection Notes — from the Resonance Box

Hand this after the Resonance Box is opened successfully. This is in Corven’s handwriting — the same hand as the colophon, the same hand as the notation key. Four pages. The passage below is from the third page.

[The handwriting is steady for the first two pages. On the third page it changes — smaller, more compressed, as if he knew he was running out of room or time.]

The anchors are not lost. The light did not die. It redistributed into ten living vessels born at the moment of inversion. The ritual is incomplete, not failed. The completion requires their release.

[Below this, in the margin, the same hand, different ink — added later:]

The mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed. Willing release is not the same mechanism as forced release. This is not a philosophical observation. It is an engineering specification.

[The fourth page is blank except for a single line at the bottom:]

No one should be forced.

“Their release.” The technical term Theron uses when he explains what this means is: extinguished.

Corven wrote the consent clause into the ritual because he understood it was load-bearing, not decorative. The ritual does not function the same way with unwilling anchors. The Reckoning either does not know this or has decided it does not matter.

The fourth page’s final line was added at a different time. It is in the same hand but the ink has aged differently — written years after the ritual, when Corven was still alive and still thinking about what he had built.

Fragment 2 — Corven’s Notation Key

Found inside the Resonance Box alongside the redirection notes. Same size and format as Fragment 1 from Session 1. Hand to players after the box is opened.

[A single sheet, cipher text on one side, two plain-language glosses on the other. The cipher matches Fragment 1’s style — same notation system.]

CIPHER TEXT (one side):

⊕⬠⬟⬟⬠⬡◆  ⬡◆⬠⬟  ◆⬠⬡⬟⊕  ⬡⬠◆⬟⊕⬡
⊕◆⬠⬡⬟  ⬠⬡◆⬟⊕  ⊕⬡◆⬠⬟  ◆⬡⬠⬟⊕⬡
⬡⊕⬠⬟◆  ⬠⊕⬡◆⬟  ◆⊕⬡⬠⬟  ⬟⊕⬠⬡◆⬡

PLAIN-LANGUAGE GLOSSES (other side):

“The vessel is not the container. The vessel is the carrier. The distinction is the mechanism.”

“The completion mechanism is not separate from the vessel. The vessel IS the mechanism. To separate them is to destroy both.”

Fragment 2 combined with Fragment 1 (Session 1) gives players partial decoding access to Corven’s journal notation. This grants advantage on the DC 14 Arcana check for the Transfer Apparatus in Session 3 and on the Astronomical Activation Sequence in Session 4.

Fragment 3 is in Session 3. All three together allow full notation decoding — which makes the Inversion path visible on the ritual diagram at Tier 3.

The second gloss is the key: “The vessel IS the mechanism.” This is what Theron means when he says the Dawnborn are not participants in the ritual. They are the ritual.

Tomas — The Long Preparation [player of Tomas’s closest PC only]

Play this when Tomas arrives mid-revelation at the Dawnhalls.

He reads the room before he reaches you. You can see him doing it — the way someone reads a situation who has been expecting this situation for a long time.

He sits down. He asks three questions. Each one is precise. Each one suggests he already knows the general shape of the answer and is confirming the details.

After the third question, he is quiet for a moment.

Then: “I saw a secondary notation in a ritual diagram once. About seven years ago — a copy of a copy, badly transcribed. I didn’t know what I was reading at the time. I filed it as ‘notation anomaly, unclear.’ I looked for the original twice. Never found it.”

He looks at the diagram in your hands.

“I think I found it now.”

He does not look afraid. He looks like someone who has been carrying a low-grade dread for a very long time and has just been told the thing he was dreading is real. That is, in a specific way, a relief.

What do you say to him?

Lira — The Hard No [player of Lira’s closest PC only]

Play this at the branching point, when Lira is told.

She goes still first. Not cold — still. The way someone goes still when they need a moment before they respond because the response that is coming without the moment would be the wrong one.

Then: “No.”

She looks around the room. At Sera. At Petra. At the wall.

“Find another way.”

She walks out.

She is not finished with this conversation. She is finished with it right now, in this room, with these people, while she is still deciding whether to trust any of you. She has a daughter the party may not know about yet. She has been saying no to the city’s needs since she was nineteen years old and her name started appearing on Dawnborn registry documents.

She will come back. She will want to know if there is actually another way.

If someone follows her: she stops walking. She doesn’t turn around. She says: “Give me an hour. If you have found an alternative, it will still be true in an hour.”

Do you follow her?

Kaelen — Reading the Reckoning [Moustaffa only]

Play this during either combat — Archive or Dawnhalls. No roll required.

You’ve fought organised units before. You know the difference between zealots and soldiers.

The Reckoning fights like soldiers.

Not fanatically. Not sacrificially. They have assigned roles — one to restrain, one to strike, one to control the exit. They have a priority hierarchy: documents first, Theron second, the party third. When Marsh takes a hit he doesn’t react emotionally. He recalculates.

These people have been training for this specific operation. Someone drilled them on the Archive layout. Someone told them where the restricted section was and what was in it.

The Reckoning isn’t a cult running on ideology. It’s a paramilitary unit running on an objective.

Who gave them the objective? Who funded the training?

You don’t have an answer. But you know what a well-resourced military unit looks like. This is one.
