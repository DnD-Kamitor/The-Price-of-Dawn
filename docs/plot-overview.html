19  The Price of Dawn — Plot Overview

GM reference. Not for players.

Logline: Ten people are carrying the sun inside them. The city needs it back. Whose choice is that to make?

World state: Varenhold has been in permanent artificial twilight for 50 years. Grey sickness spreads. Food stores are failing. 3–5 good harvests left.

The central question: Who gets to decide what someone’s life is for?

SESSION 1 — What you need right now

The Job

Chancellor Mira Ostenveld hires the players to investigate why the Ritual of Eternal Dawn failed 50 years ago. She has chosen strategic ignorance — she suspects the answer is terrible and has arranged not to know it directly. Players take the job before understanding what it is. This mirrors the Chancellor’s own choice exactly. That symmetry is intentional.

The Three Scenes

Scene 1 — Lowmark Junction (combat) Restorer zealots intercept the players. Not trying to kill them — warning them off. The Enforcer carries a sealed note from Brother Edoran: “The willing heart gives freely what the bound heart cannot.” Edoran himself watches from the east alley. The Restorers are a theological movement, not a gang. They believe the Dawnborn’s deaths would be meaningful rather than wasted. The intercept was designed to slow the players, not stop them. It won’t.

Scene 2 — The Archive Door (puzzle) Theron Waide (Master Archivist) meets them at the Civic Archive. He has known the truth for 11 years and said nothing — guilt, not malice. He has been slowly destroying his own notes. The restricted stacks door has a riddle carved in Archmagister Corven’s private notation. Answer: CONSENT. Theron knows the answer. He will not say it. He has been waiting for someone else to open the door so the knowledge becomes theirs, not only his.

Scene 3 — The Restricted Stacks (puzzle) Star-wheel mechanism: 3 brass rings on a central column, set to Vael’s Crown / Shepherd’s Eye / Trailing Light (Dawnmark constellation, west-to-east rise order). The star chart on the east wall contains the solution in plain language. Shelf 4-17-3 opens.

What players retrieve: - Corven’s public summary: uses the phrases “distributed sympathetic anchors” and “living conduit mechanism” without defining them - Fragment 1 of Corven’s private Notation Key: cipher text + two plain-language glosses, including: “The weight borne willingly is not the same weight as the weight imposed. This is not a philosophical observation. It is an engineering specification.”

Session ends here. Players have documents they cannot fully decode yet. The phrase “living conduit” is the last thing in their heads. They don’t know what it means. They will.

What Players Know at Session 1 End

  • The ritual used “distributed sympathetic anchors” and a “living conduit mechanism”
  • Corven built consent into the design as an engineering requirement
  • Theron has been sitting on something for 11 years
  • Someone (Edoran) tried to warn them off — which means they’re getting close to something

What Players Don’t Know Yet

  • What “living conduit” means (= people)
  • That the Dawnborn are the anchors
  • That completing the ritual requires the Dawnborn to die
  • Who Edoran actually is
  • That Theron has been destroying his own notes
THE TRUTH — Reveal this in Session 2

What Actually Happened 50 Years Ago

The Ritual of Eternal Dawn didn’t fail. It redirected.

Corven’s design required 10 “distributed sympathetic anchors” — fixed points in the world to hold the ritual’s energy. On the night of the ritual, 10 children were born in perfect health under impossible circumstances. They became the anchors instead.

The Dawnborn are the ritual. They’ve been holding the sun’s energy inside their bodies for 50 years.

To complete the ritual — to bring the sun back — that energy has to be released. Releasing it means they die.

The Consent Question

Corven knew consent was an engineering specification:

“The mechanism will not function as designed if the distinction is collapsed.”

Whether the original 10 anchor-candidates genuinely consented is the buried question. Whether each Dawnborn consents now is the active one.

Why Theron Said Nothing

He decoded enough of the Notation Key 11 years ago to understand what the documents would say. He could not bear to be the one who condemned 10 people. He has been waiting for someone with enough courage — or ignorance — to investigate seriously.

His confession in Session 2 is the campaign’s first gut-punch. He says: “I’ve been waiting for someone with enough courage — or ignorance — to investigate seriously.”

THE FACTIONS — Three answers to the same question

The Restorers — Brother Edoran

Position: The Dawnborn were always going to die for this. The twilight is killing everyone slowly. Better a meaningful death than a slow one.

Who Edoran is: Former Auris priest. His daughter died of grey sickness at 17. He organized his grief into a theological position over 20 years. He is not wrong about the math. He is complicated about consent.

His lie: He presents the Restorer position as philosophy. It is grief.

His crack: “If the Dawnborn should choose for themselves — why did your daughter not get to choose?” This does not change his mind. It stops him arguing for the rest of the scene.

His role in Session 1: Watching from the east alley during the intercept. He sent the zealots. He is not impulsive — he has been building this movement for 20 years. He’s the most dangerous antagonist because he’s partly right.

The Reckoning — Commander Harran Lecht

Position: The Dawnborn don’t get a vote. The city’s survival takes precedence over 10 lives.

What he’s hiding: He knows about Isolde’s transfer method (which would let the Dawnborn survive). He doesn’t offer it because it gives the Dawnborn leverage to stall.

His encounter beat: When defeated or captured, he says: “When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it.” He is not wrong that there is a clock. He is wrong about who gets to decide when it runs out.

His role by session: Followers visible in Session 1 background → first direct confrontation Session 2 → forces the Session 4 Ashring confrontation.

The Amber Compact — Warden Keseph Vyne

Position: The ritual must remain in institutional limbo indefinitely.

Why: He is funded by Solenne — Varenhold’s grain creditor. Varenhold self-sufficient = Solenne loses 20 years of leverage over the city’s debt infrastructure. Every bureaucratic delay Keseph creates is deliberate. He has been receiving ~800 amber script per year through the Compact’s Solenne liaison, justified as “diplomatic continuity fees.”

His method: Players will feel Keseph before they meet him. Archive access “pending institutional review” (pending for 11 years). Isolde’s lab “reassigned.” A competing access claim filed 3 hours after players enter the Spire. He is invisible until Session 4.

His lie: “I’m protecting the Dawnborn from a rushed decision.” Partly true. The most dangerous kind of lie.

When players meet him: Session 4-5. He is courteous, regretful, procedurally immovable. The moment that breaks him: showing him that Isolde’s method would have worked three years ago. He goes quiet. That quiet is worse than an argument.

The Chancellor — Mira Ostenveld

Not an antagonist. A person who made a defensible choice and lives inside its consequences.

She has the answer. She chose not to look at it directly. She hired the players to find it so she doesn’t have to be the one who found it. Players will recognize this — it’s the same thing they did in Session 1 when they took the job before asking what it actually was.

THE DAWNBORN — Ten individuals, ten different answers

The Dawnborn are not a unified group. Each has arrived at their own position. Every yes and no is different. None of them are simple.

Name Role Position Notes
Sera Voss Runs the Dawnhall healing centre Willing The most public-facing Dawnborn. Her faith is genuine and costs her something. Players meet her in Session 1. She assumes competence and goodwill until proved otherwise — which is somehow the most disarming thing imaginable.
Tomas Areth Scholar, ritual historian Willing Has done the math and is writing his will. His coded notebook (Asymmetry Journal) is a key Session 2–4 discovery.

Five “surge-phase” Dawnborn carry double the energy load: Sera, Tomas, Ysel, Lira, Petra. Relevant to Tomas’s Asymmetry path (Session 3).

SESSION 3 — The three alternative paths

This is where the campaign becomes a real moral problem rather than an investigation. Three paths, none clean.

Path 1 — Isolde’s Transfer Method

Move the anchor energy from the Dawnborn into constructed vessels. The ritual fires. Dawnborn survive.

Cost: 15–20% chance that one or two Dawnborn die anyway despite the transfer. Science instead of hope.

Obstacle: Warden Keseph has suppressed Isolde’s research via institutional review. Her lab was “reassigned.” She’s working from personal notes in a rented room in the Ashring district.

Path 2 — Tomas’s Asymmetry

The five surge-phase Dawnborn (Sera, Tomas, Ysel, Lira, Petra) carry double the energy load. Five could theoretically complete the ritual, protecting the other five.

Cost: Requires identifying which five and asking them. Lira is in the surge group.

Source: Tomas’s coded Asymmetry Journal — legal-case notation disguising ritual energy calculations. DC 14 Investigation to decode.

Path 3 — The Inversion Pathway

Revealed by the Primer Stone in Session 4. If all 10 Dawnborn freely consent — genuinely, without coercion or pressure — the ritual inverts. The sun returns. No one dies.

Cost: Requires every single Dawnborn. Including Lira. Including Cormac (wavering). This is the “good” ending. It’s significantly harder than letting the Reckoning do it, because every individual has to choose.

What makes it hard: You cannot convince people into genuine consent. You have to earn it. Across multiple sessions. Through real relationship, not persuasion mechanics.

SESSION 4 — The convergence

What Arrives Simultaneously

Everything the players built in Sessions 1–3 lands at once. Track consequences, not successes.

  • The Primer Stone reveal: Hidden inversion pathway. All 10 willing simultaneously. This is the route to the ending where no one dies.
  • The Chancellor’s ultimatum: Seven-day clock. Food stores at 35%. When rationing begins, the moderate wing of the Desperate faction collapses.
  • Keseph activates: Six Solenne mercenaries deployed to Ashring perimeter. A formal challenge filed to the ritual proceeding “without institutional oversight.”
  • The Reckoning strikes: Harran forces the Ashring confrontation. Secondary objective: keep Aldric Stone at his stone — not defeating Harran. A player who only fights misses why this matters.

Cormac’s Window

If players haven’t reached Cormac yet, this is the last session to do so. His stone stays dim at the Inversion Circle without genuine re-consent. Sitting with him and asking again costs 30 minutes. Proceeding without him collapses the inversion to Ending A.

SESSION 5 — The ritual and the endings

Corven’s Letter

Six minutes. He spent them writing this. “No one should be forced.” In his own hand. Players who think about what that means — that the architect of this situation spent his last six minutes on a consent clause — are doing the work.

The Inversion Circle

Not a puzzle about magic. All 10 Dawnborn must stand at their Primer Stones simultaneously within a six-second window. The coordination problem is logistical — who gives the signal, how it reaches all 10 positions, how players manage the Dawnborn who aren’t confirmed willing. No skill check solves this. Only conversations that happened in Sessions 1–4.

The Last Stand

Whoever hasn’t been neutralised arrives now: Reckoning, Desperate faction, or Keseph’s mercenaries. Three secondary objectives: keep Dawnborn at their stones, protect the central dais, get the activation signal out.

The Endings

# Name Conditions What Happens
A The Willing Sacrifice Consenting Dawnborn complete ritual Sun returns. Some die. Both things are true simultaneously.
B The Inversion All 10 freely consent — no coercion Sun returns. No one dies. Hardest path. Requires earning Lira.
C The Transfer Isolde’s method completed in time Sun returns. 15–20% chance one or two die anyway. Workable.
D The Refusal Players protect Dawnborn over ritual Sun stays dark. Grey sickness continues. Valid ending.
E Cascade Failure Reckoning forces unwilling Dawnborn Cooperative resonance breaks. All 10 die. Ritual fails. Sun doesn’t return.
F The Long Dark No path pursued; time runs out Inaction is a choice. It has consequences.

Most campaigns blend two or three endings.

The Debrief (not optional)

The 15 minutes after the final scene are part of the experience. Ask directly: “When the right choice and the good choice were different — which did your character make? Do they know?”

Varenhold does not wait. Apply between sessions.

THE ANTAGONIST INTERACTION MAP — When factions collide

The three antagonist layers don’t operate as a unified faction. Let them conflict.

If players try to… Edoran Harran Keseph
Delay the ritual Patient — time is on his side Escalates — the clock is his argument Quietly approves — delay is his business model
Expose Spire interference Interested and angry Irrelevant — he doesn’t care about Spire politics Catastrophic alarm — acts immediately
Prove the inversion works Genuine consideration Skeptical — consent is a delay tactic Desperate obstruction — a successful ritual ends his income
Gain the Dawnborn’s trust Grudging respect Contempt for the process Panic

When two antagonist layers conflict directly (Harran arrests Edoran; Restorers attack Keseph’s mercenaries) — let it happen. Players should not feel like the only force in motion.

THEMATIC SPINE — What every scene is asking

Primary theme: Consent — who gets to decide what someone else’s life is for.

YES — the community decides: Ten people cannot hold thousands hostage. The ritual was always the Dawnborn’s purpose. To refuse is to privilege the few over the many in a city that cannot afford that luxury.

NO — the individual decides: No city, no calculation gives anyone the right to define another person’s death as their purpose. Consent is not a courtesy — it is the precondition that makes the sun worth returning. A dawn built on coercion is not a dawn.

Both answers are correct. Both are incomplete. Every NPC is answering this question differently. The players are the only ones in the room with enough information to hold both answers at once.

The players aren’t hired to find the truth. They find the truth in the first two sessions. They are hired to do something with it. That is the whole game.

Tone anchor: Le Guin’s The Ones Who Walk Away from Omelas — the moral weight without the clean resolution. Honest tragedy, not punishing tragedy.
