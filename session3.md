# Session 3 — The Watched City (Level 6)

*Dilemma: What does it mean to choose something when choosing is itself a kind of violence?*

---

**GM Note — Faction naming:** The antagonist faction is called the "Dusk Faction" throughout this chapter. Different NPCs name them differently: Osindra calls them "the necromancer faction" (their method), Ellan calls them "the darkness faction" (their goal). They are the same group. Use whichever name fits the NPC's voice; this document uses "Dusk Faction" as the canonical term.

---

## Scene 1 — RP: The Divination Tower

**Five senses:**
- Sight: A tower narrower than expected, no signage, windows filled with slowly rotating orreries that don't match any sky the players have seen. A brass speaking tube by the door, polished to a mirror finish.
- Sound: A faint mechanical ticking from somewhere high above. The sound of pages turning, though no one is visible through the windows.
- Smell: Old ink and something sharper — ozone, like lightning held still. Faintly: tea, recently brewed. Exactly enough cups.
- Touch: The door handle is warm, as if someone just let go of it.
- Air: Still. The tower seems to be holding its breath.

**The Prescience Door (funny mechanic for Sephandrius):**

Before anyone can knock, the speaking tube crackles. A young apprentice's voice, slightly out of breath: *"We've been expecting you. All of you. The Divination Tower has three rules for visitors: no uninvited scrying past the second floor, no astral projection above the fourth, and—"* sound of shuffling papers *"—Sephandrius, your paper on temporal recursion was cited incorrectly in the Aldshire Compendium. Third footnote. Magistra Osindra would like to discuss that separately."*

The door opens before anyone touches it.

Inside, a teenage apprentice with ink-stained fingers and a stack of notes looks up from a desk covered in observation logs. *"She's been watching your approach for forty minutes. She says to tell you she saw the beacon go off, she's cross about it, and to please wipe your feet."*

The apprentice's notes, if players glance at them, contain a detailed log of their journey across the city — including: *"Sephandrius paused twice to adjust appearance before arrival. Noted."*

**Magistra Vell Osindra** — Divination Tower Master

- **Want:** Remain neutral. She trades in information; sides are overhead costs she'd rather avoid.
- **Fear:** The Dusk Faction has a seer of their own — and she suspects theirs may be more accurate than hers.
- **Lie:** She has already received an approach from the Dusk Faction. An offer. She has not refused it yet.
- **Voice:** Quiet, precise, never repeats herself. Addresses the group as "investigators." Addresses Sephandrius by first name, as if they've argued before.

She doesn't look up from the shallow basin of black water when they enter: *"The beacon lit four scrying pools simultaneously. Whatever was in that room — three parties now have your description. Sit down."*

**Osindra and Sephandrius:** She knows his work. She's read his published research and has opinions. If players make it obvious that Sephandrius is a diviner, she pauses and actually looks at him: *"You're better suited for this than I'd like you to be. Don't let it go to your head."* If pushed on her neutrality: *"I'm not neutral. I'm careful. There's a difference."*

**The Sending:**

Osindra offers the use of the tower's amplified sending focus — a silver rod mounted in a resonance ring that extends range and reduces distortion. With it, the message reaches Sera clearly. Without it, the message still works but the response may be fragmented (50% chance of partial static: one random word of Sera's response is lost).

Sephandrius casts Sending. Sera's 25-word response:

> *"They knew my name before the beacon. Ironbell tannery, third lane. Come quiet. No magic in the streets — they have a seer. Lira's already coming."*

**GM Note — "They knew my name before the beacon":** This is deliberate unsolved mystery for future sessions. If players press Sera later, she doesn't know how they identified her either. Do not resolve this in Session 3.

**What Osindra tells them:**

She gives them three pieces of intelligence, freely, because she wants them moving before anyone connects the party to her tower:
1. The Dusk Faction has operated in Varenhold for at least twenty years. Not new. Not improvised.
2. Their seer works through resonance, not line-of-sight. Arcane signatures in the streets act like footprints.
3. *"The Ironbell district has a dead zone for scrying — old clay pipes in the walls, copper-lined. Use them."*

What she doesn't say: she knows more about the Dusk Faction's leadership than she's sharing. The offer they made her. The name they used.

**GM Note — Osindra / Session 4 betrayal hook:** If Osindra accepts the offer she's been made, she becomes an intelligence asset for the Dusk Faction and knows where the party went after leaving her tower. Decide between sessions whether she takes the offer — if she does, Session 4 can open with the party discovering their movements were leaked. Flag this in your Session 4 prep.

**Scene ends** when she says, without looking up: *"I'd appreciate it if no one saw you leave."*

## Scene 2 — Skill Challenge: Through the Watched Streets

**Five senses (the Ironbell approach):**
- Sight: Narrower streets than the civic quarter — buildings lean together overhead, closing the sky to a grey strip. Loading docks, a tanner's market with hanging hides, a water channel running close to the wall. People who don't make eye contact.
- Sound: Iron bells, distant and irregular — the district's namesake, used as shift markers by the tannery workers. Underfoot: cobbles give way to fitted stone over the copper-pipe infrastructure — a different sound when you tap it.
- Smell: Tanning chemicals from three directions. Oak bark. Something biological and old.
- Touch: The walls here are cooler than the open streets. The copper pipes behind the plaster radiate a faint chill.
- The dead zone: When players enter the copper-pipe alley Osindra described, every spellcaster feels a sudden silence — the arcane signature doesn't propagate outward. Like casting into a room with no echo. Osindra was right. It's oddly peaceful.

The Dusk Faction seer reads arcane signatures. Every spell cast in the open streets is a flare. The city is also physically watched — the faction has moved people into key intersections within the last hour.

**Stakes:** 3 successes before 2 failures. Failure consequences accumulate silently and carry into Scene 3.

| Roll | Skill | DC | What it represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spot the watcher | Perception / Insight | 15 | Identify which figure at the corner is reading them, not just standing |
| Lose the tail | Stealth / Deception | 13 | Cut through the tanner's market, change silhouette, double back through a loading dock |
| Detect the scry-ping | Arcana (no spell required) | 16 | Recognize the faint directional pull of an active scry attempt before it locks |
| Back-route through Ironbell | History / Athletics | 12 | The copper-pipe district has a service passage Osindra mentioned — finding it requires knowledge of the city's old infrastructure |
| Misdirect a watcher | Performance / Deception | 14 | Plant a false trail toward the Conjuration district |

**Failure consequences (track, don't announce):**
- 0 failures: Arrive clean. Surprise round in combat. Sera has time to prepare.
- 1 failure: Tailed. No surprise. One watcher has their location — a runner has already been sent.
- 2 failures: Tailed and flagged. Reinforcements arrive end of round 2 of combat (1 additional Dusk Cultist).

## Scene 3 — Combat: The Tannery Ambush

**Five senses:**
- Sight: The tannery is shut, shutters cracked, one amber lamp visible inside through a gap. Three other figures visible in the lane — too still, too positioned.
- Sound: The chemical smell of curing leather hits before the building does. Under it: something sharper, less explicable. Iron bells in the distance.
- Touch: The lane cobbles are damp. Fresh rain, or deliberate — harder to run on.
- Air: The door has been opened recently. The dust disturbed in a specific arc.

**Why Lira is here:** Sera didn't call Lira through magic — Lira came looking when she heard two days ago that Sera had gone into hiding. She found the tannery this morning through a shared contact. She left an hour ago to retrieve a medical kit from someone three streets over and is returning now. She doesn't know how bad it is yet.

Sera Voss is inside, crossbow across her knees, sitting against the far wall. Not frightened. Waiting. When she sees them: *"Took longer than I expected."*

She has two days of information to give. She doesn't get the chance.

**The ambush hits when players enter or signal entry.** Three figures in the lane move simultaneously — coordinated, not panicked. The door behind the players closes. Two more agents were already inside.

**Secondary Objective (state before first initiative roll):** The Priest's goal is to extract Sera alive, not kill her. Players must fight and protect Sera simultaneously. If Sera drops to 0 HP (captured via Hold Person + drag), the Priest signals immediate withdrawal — and Session 4 changes significantly.

**GM Note — If Sera is captured:** The Dusk Faction holds her as leverage. Session 4 opens differently: players must locate where she is held before pursuing Ellan Voss. The faction will attempt to use Sera — a willing Dawnborn — to pressure the others. This is a harder, darker path. Note it in your Session 4 prep.

**Encounter XP:** 3,800 adjusted XP — Hard encounter for 4 level-6 characters. With 2 skill challenge failures (reinforcement arrives round 2): ~4,200 adjusted — Deadly. Warn players with secondary objective stakes, not numbers.

---

**Tannery Environment:** Level-6 players will interact with the space. Key features:

- **Tanning vats** (3 ft deep, acid/chemical solution): Creatures pushed in take 2d6 acid damage per turn; climbing out costs full movement. Shove action can push adjacent creatures in.
- **Hanging hides on wooden racks**: Soft cover (−2 to ranged attack rolls through them). Moving through costs extra movement (difficult terrain). Can be pulled down with DC 11 Athletics to create a 10-ft area of difficult terrain.
- **The amber lamp** (only light source): Smashing it (ranged attack, AC 10) plunges the tannery into natural darkness — Dusk Cultists see normally with darkvision; players without darkvision are blinded. Risky if players do it accidentally.
- **Barrels of tanbark**: Athletics DC 13 as a bonus action to push 10 ft — can block doorways or pin creatures into a corner.

**Tactical Priority (run in this order each round):**

**Round 1:** Cast *Darkness* (2nd-level) centred on the amber lamp — plunges a 15-foot radius into magical darkness. The Priest and all Dusk Cultists have Devil's Sight and see normally inside it. Players without Devil's Sight or Truesight have disadvantage on attacks against targets in the sphere and cannot see into it at all. Sera is inside the zone — finding her requires action or Blindsight.

*If Darkness is Counterspelled:* Priest uses Toll the Dead on the highest-HP visible player (no slot, no concentration). Cultists move to flank — plan shifts from control to aggression. Priest repositions with Shadow Step to a dim-lit corner and repeats Toll the Dead until a safer Hold Person opportunity opens.

**Round 2:** Cast *Hold Person* targeting Sera (WIS save DC 13). If she fails, two cultists immediately move to her position and begin dragging (10 ft per round, costs their movement). If she succeeds: Priest uses Toll the Dead on the highest-HP player.

**Withdrawal trigger:** If 3 of 4 Cultists are downed OR the Priest drops below 20 HP — the Priest signals withdrawal with a specific hand gesture. All remaining Cultists disengage simultaneously (no opportunity attacks, Shadow Step away). The Priest casts *Misty Step* to reach the exit, then Dashes. They do not fight to the death.

**Bestow Curse / Shadow of Moil:** Only used if the fight runs longer than 5 rounds (unlikely). Bestow Curse on the most active player (disadvantage on WIS saves — makes Hold Person harder to resist). Shadow of Moil is the Priest's last-resort below 20 HP: 4d12 necrotic to adjacent attackers per turn, heavily obscures the Priest.

**Post-combat — the Sera/Lira confrontation:**

Both women are in the same room. Both are breathing hard. Neither is injured badly enough to stop the conversation that's been waiting.

**Sera wants:** Players to understand that she has already decided — freely, clearly, without coercion — to proceed. She decided months ago. The beacon, the cryptics, the Dusk Faction: none of this changes the calculation.

**Sera fears:** That her willingness will be used as a reason to pressure the unwilling.

**Lira wants:** Players to understand that she has also decided — freely, clearly, without coercion — to refuse. She has a daughter. That is the entire argument.

**Lira fears:** That Sera's willingness makes her own refusal morally irrelevant — and she hates herself for thinking about it that way.

**The lie:** Neither is lying. This is what makes the scene unresolvable.

**Five senses (post-combat):**
- Sight: Amber tannery lamp, acid and adrenaline in the air, a child's handkerchief falling from Lira's medical kit when she opens it. Lira picks it up without comment.
- Sound: The argument never gets loud. That's worse.

**The Encounter Beat:** Lira says, quietly, to Sera: *"You want to die for them. I understand that. I have a daughter. You don't."*

Sera does not answer. She has no answer. The two women look at each other across fifty years of friendship and the gap is visible. Do not resolve this. Let it be real. Players who intervene cannot fix it. They can only be present.

**Sample dialogue:**
- Sera (to players, before Lira speaks): *"Someone has to make a decision. We can't wait for a perfect answer that might not exist."*
- Lira (when pushed): *"I have a daughter. That's the whole argument. I don't have another one."*
- Sera (quietly, to Lira, after a long pause): *"I know."*

**How it connects forward:** Players now know the consent problem is not theoretical. Two Dawnborn, same room, diametrically opposite positions, both freely chosen. Any path that requires unanimous agreement must reckon with this moment.

## Scene 4 — Puzzle: The Paired Cryptics

After the tannery, somewhere safe — a Divination Tower safe house, Sera's backup location, or wherever players establish as secure.

**Five senses (the safe house):**
- Sight: Two cryptic documents spread side by side on the table. Part A (library): Theron's precise hand, 24 symbols in 6 rows of 4. Part B (archive box): older parchment, slightly yellowed, same 24 symbols in 4 rows of 6. The ritual drawings from the box lie open beside them — a circle, ten marked positions, lines of notation around the rim. A candle stub is the only light.
- Sound: Distant iron bells. Papers sliding on wood. Someone breathing through their nose while they think.
- Smell: Old ink. The ritual drawings carry a faint mineral smell — stone dust, chalk. The same smell as old Ashring.
- Touch: Part A is crisp, written within the last twenty years. Part B is much older — edges softened, parchment slightly translucent when held to light.
- The discovery: Holding Part B over Part A against the candle, symbols from Part A show through — shifted in a specific, non-random pattern. This is not wear. This is the mechanism.

**The setup:**

They have both parts of Theron's cryptic. Neither decoded fully alone — they've been treating them as two separate documents. They are one system. Theron encoded the message across both, requiring both simultaneously. Part A alone produces fragments of ritual notation — meaningful but incomplete. Same for Part B. The message exists in neither document individually.

**Finding the method:**

*DC 15 Arcana:* "These documents use the same base notation but complementary encoding. The symbol frequency in Part A is the mathematical inverse of Part B — they were designed as a paired key. One cannot be read without the other."

*DC 13 Investigation (no Arcana required):* Count the symbols. Part A: 24. Part B: 24. Same count, different arrangement, same notation system, same author. That is not coincidence.

*Sephandrius:* Advantage on either check. Paired-key encoding is used in prophetic ciphers to prevent partial readings — a diviner recognises the structure immediately.

*No roll needed if players try the physical method:* Holding Part B over Part A against the candle causes certain symbol pairs to align — the double layer darkens them visibly. The aligned symbols, read in sequence, are the message. If players physically hold the documents up to the light, they find this without rolling anything.

**Once the method is found:**

DC 12 Investigation to work through the cross-reference systematically. This is methodical, not hard — the difficulty was the method, not the execution. On failure: they extract the name and district but not the street number. DC 14 History fills the gap from knowledge of Ashring's old layout, or Sephandrius can ask Osindra.

**The ritual drawings connect here:** The decoded name corresponds to the ninth marked position in the circle diagram. That position has a mark beside it that none of the others have — a single line through it, added in a different ink, at a different time. Someone crossed out that participant after the ritual ended. Theron, Year 43. The line was added in grief or in guilt — it is impossible to tell which from the pressure of the pen.

**What the paired reading produces:**

The aligned symbols translate from Corven's notation into old Varenhold script:

> *Ellan Voss. Ashring. [Street name]. [Door number].*
> *Ninth position. Withdrew Year 42.*
> *She is still alive.*

No letter. No sentiment. Compressed data — hidden across two documents for fifty years. Theron didn't write feelings here. He wrote a location and a fact and trusted that whoever stood in this room needing it would know what to do.

**Three-clue fallback (if they cannot decode it):**
1. **Osindra:** Has observed the Dusk Faction watching a specific Ashring street for three years. DC 14 Arcana or direct, well-framed roleplay prompts her to share it.
2. **Theron directly:** He knows the address. DC 13 Persuasion via Sending or in person.
3. **Sera + legwork:** Heard rumours of an old woman the Dusk Faction protects. Knows the street, not the door. DC 14 Investigation on foot narrows it to three houses.

## Scene 5 — Riddle + RP: The Ashring Door

**Getting there:** Ashring is the district built around the original ritual site. The stone circle — Ashring Plaza — sits at its center, permanently cordoned. The streets around it are older than the rest of the city. The architecture is pre-darkness.

**Five senses:**
- Sight: A narrow row of stone houses, older than anything in Lowmark. One door has no knocker. No lock visible. Instead: a stone panel set into the wall beside the frame, carved with text.
- Sound: The stone circle is audible from here — a low, irregular resonance, barely below hearing. Not wind. Something else.
- Smell: Old stone and something faintly mineral. The smell of places that have been waiting.
- Touch: The carved letters on the panel are worn but legible. Someone has traced them recently — the dust in the grooves is disturbed.
- Air: Warmer than the lane. Inexplicably.

**The Riddle:**

Carved into the stone panel beside the door. No preamble. No instruction. Just this:

> *I was present when the sun stopped.*
> *I am absent now that it has not returned.*
> *I am not carried by the willing or the unwilling.*
> *I am not held by the living or the dead.*
> *I am not time, though I outlast it.*
> *I am not truth, though I contain it.*
> *I am not silence, though I am what remains when speaking ends.*
>
> *Ten held me fully.*
> *Nine still hold me.*
> *The one who withdrew me was made whole again — and broke everything else.*
>
> *I cannot be divided. I can only be present or absent.*
> *When I am absent from even one, I am absent from all.*
>
> *The door does not open for the word.*
> *The door opens for the act.*
> *Perform me — do not name me — and enter.*

**The Answer: Consent.**

The door does not respond to players saying "consent." This is the trap for smart players — they will solve the riddle quickly, then be baffled when nothing happens.

The mechanism: **each player character must state, aloud, why they are here.** Not the tactical reason. The real one. The door's ward reads intention, not vocabulary. It was built by someone who understood that consent cannot be claimed, only given.

**What the ward detects — GM guidance:** The ward does not detect honesty. It detects whether the speaker has actually asked themselves the question. Specificity and self-awareness count; nobility does not. Examples:

- *"We need to speak with her"* — panel stays cold. That is a task, not a reason.
- *"To save the city"* — panel stays cold. That is a mission, not consent.
- *"Because I don't know if I would make the same choice she did, and I need to understand it"* — panel clicks.
- *"I am afraid of what comes next if I don't"* — panel clicks.
- *"I follow my companions"* — panel stays cold. Obligation is not consent. This player must find their own reason.
- *"I am afraid of the dark and I cannot explain why this matters, but it does"* — panel clicks. Vulnerability counts.

If a player cannot find a personal reason: the panel holds them — not locks them out. The others can enter without them. The ward does not require unanimous entry; it requires genuine individual statement. A player who stands outside while the party enters has performed a kind of consent too: the door remains open.

**Hint timing:** Let players work for at least ten real-world minutes before giving any hint. When they are genuinely stuck — not just impatient, but out of ideas — Sera (if present) reads the last three lines again slowly, then says: *"It's not asking you to say the word. It's asking you to do it."* If they're still stuck after another five minutes: Lira looks at the panel for a long time, then says, quietly, to no one in particular: *"I am here because I have a daughter and I need to know if there is another way."* The panel clicks.

After each player speaks their real reason, the panel slides aside. One compartment. One wax-sealed letter, addressed: *"To whoever finds this after I'm gone — I expect I will be."*

**About this letter:** It was placed in the external door panel by a courier acting on written instructions Theron left with his Archive assistant — to be delivered if he did not return. The panel is accessible only from the street side; Ellan cannot reach it from inside and has never known it was there.

**Ellan Voss answers the door before they can finish reading the letter.**

She is in her nineties. She moves slowly, without assistance, and looks at them with the particular calm of someone who has been waiting for a specific knock for a very long time.

*"Theron finally sent someone. Good. Come in."*

She does not elaborate. She makes tea. When they sit:

*"The ritual didn't fail because the cost was wrong. It failed because I withdrew my consent at the last moment. I was the ninth. I said no. And because I said no, it ended."*

She pauses. Sets down her cup.

*"I have spent fifty years making sure no one tried again. The darkness faction found out I existed approximately three years ago. They have been keeping me alive because I am useful to them. If the ritual proceeds, it requires my consent. I will not give it. Not because I am afraid. Because I am the only person in this city who has seen what happens when someone changes their mind at the last second inside the circle."*

Another pause.

*"It was not a small thing."*

**Ellan Voss — NPC**

- **Want:** For someone to ask her the right question. She has been rehearsing the answer for fifty years and no one has come until now.
- **Fear:** That when she finally says yes — and she knows she will — it will be too late. She is in her nineties. The Dusk Faction keeps her alive but not protected; she understands the distinction.
- **Lie:** *"I will not give it. Not before."* This is her public position, not her private truth. She wants to say yes. She has been waiting for someone to give her a reason that outweighs what she saw inside the circle. Her refusal is a door held open, waiting for someone to walk through it the right way. She will not say this unprompted. Players who read her carefully — Insight DC 15 — sense it.

**What players can ask:**
- Why did you withdraw? *(She answers directly: "I was afraid. I was nineteen. I chose to live. I have lived with that choice for fifty years.")*
- What did you see? *(She answers directly: "The others — the nine who stayed — I heard them. The ritual had already begun. Withdrawing did not just stop it. It reversed. Partially. For a moment, I watched the light begin and then un-begin. That is not a word. But it is what happened.")*
- Are you a Dawnborn? *(No. She was a ritual participant, not a Dawnborn. She is mortal. She is old. She chose to withdraw and has been dying slowly ever since, like everyone else.)*
