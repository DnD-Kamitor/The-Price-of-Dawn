# Session 3 — The Watched City (Level 5)

*Dilemma: What does it mean to choose something when choosing is itself a kind of violence?*

---

<details>
<summary>📖 The Story — What Is Happening & Why</summary>

**What this session does:**
Session 2 ended with the Dawnborn's true nature revealed and Theron Waide's eleven years of silence exposed. Session 3 moves from investigation to pursuit. The Concordance of the Withdrawn — who have been watching for years — now act. Players are tracked through the city, ambushed at the tannery where Sera is hiding, and ultimately reach the woman at the center of everything: Ellan Voss.

If Iza is in the party, this session ends with a revelation that changes everything for her character.

---

**The Concordance of the Withdrawn:**

Founded within a decade of the twilight. Rooted in a heretical reading of Morthis theology: the sun completed its passage through Morthis's door. The darkness is not a wound — it is what follows. Restoring the sun would drag a soul back from beyond the threshold: cosmic necromancy, a violation of the god of passage himself.

They are not evil by their own theology. They are preventing a second violation of the cosmos. This coherence is what makes them genuinely dangerous.

*Naming:* Osindra calls them "the Concordance" (formal, intelligence-work). Ellan calls them "the Withdrawn" (personal; she knows she is their patron saint and despises it). Cultists say "the Concordance" or simply "we."

*Hierarchy (ascending):*
- **The Unlit** — initiates; field operatives; some are The Returned (reanimated)
- **Dusk Priests** — ordained operatives, corrupted Morthis liturgy; the Priest in this session holds this rank
- **The Unmoved** — the leadership council; not encountered until a later session

*Mark:* A sealed-door tattoo on the inner wrist — a horizontal bar crossed by a vertical strike. Inverted from Morthis's open-door symbol. The Unlit wear it small. Priests wear it prominently on the back of the hand.

*Patron saint:* Ellan Voss — "the One Who Held." She does not know they venerate her. When she finds out, it will not be a comfort.

*The Returned:* The Concordance practices what it preaches. Initiates who die in service are sometimes returned as Ghasts by the Dusk Priests — proof of the theology made flesh. Death is not the end of the passage; it is the passage. They bear the sealed-door brand on their wrists, glowing faint violet. They do not speak. They do not retreat without the Priest's signal.

*The invocation (spoken before operations):* "The door has closed. The passage is complete. We are the passage now."

---

**Theron Waide — his actual role:**

Theron is the Master Archivist who held Corven's documents for eleven years. He is NOT the ritual's designer — Corven was (Archmagister Corven, deceased). Theron was a young archive assistant when the ritual failed.

But Theron's guilt runs deeper than eleven years of inaction.

Ellan Voss had a daughter — stillborn. Theron was present. He used ritual energy from Corven's research to revive the child at terrible personal cost — the nature of that cost is not in any record. The child survived. That child is Iza.

Theron raised Iza or arranged her upbringing, telling her she was the result of a research procedure — which was not entirely a lie. He could not tell her the truth because the truth required telling her she had a living mother, and that mother was the person who held the key to the ritual, and he had spent eleven years unable to act on what he knew.

The sealed letter at Ellan's door is his. He placed instructions with his archive assistant to deliver it if he never returned. He did not expect to send investigators who included the very child he revived.

**What players should understand:** Theron's role is keeper and witness, not maker. His weight is not the guilt of authorship — it is the guilt of knowing, acting once in a way he cannot fully account for, and then doing nothing for eleven years while time ran out.

---

**Iza — for the GM:**

Coordinate with Iza's player privately before this session. What Iza knows: she was in a research facility studying her own Dawnborn-adjacent properties; she was told she was the result of a procedure; she has no family that she knows of.

What Iza does not know: Ellan Voss is her biological mother. She was stillborn. Theron revived her. Her Dawnborn properties may be the result of this revival — a transfer of ritual energy at the moment of return, with consequences that have not yet been fully understood. She may be a functional equivalent to a Dawnborn participant in the ritual. This is a question for a later session; do not force it now.

The reveal at Scene 5 should not be scripted. Seed it and let the scene find it.

<summary>📁 Scene 1 — RP: The Divination Tower</summary>

<details>
<summary>📄 Five Senses & Arrival</summary>

**Five senses:**
- *Sight:* A tower narrower than expected, no signage, windows filled with slowly rotating orreries that don't match any charted sky. A brass speaking tube by the door, polished to a mirror finish.
- *Sound:* Faint mechanical ticking from somewhere high above. Pages turning, though no one is visible through the windows.
- *Smell:* Old ink and something sharper — ozone, like lightning held still. Faintly: tea, recently brewed. Exactly enough cups.
- *Touch:* The door handle is warm, as if someone just let go of it.
- *Air:* Still. The tower seems to be holding its breath.

**The Prescience Door — mechanic for Sephandrius:**

Before anyone can knock, the speaking tube crackles. A young apprentice's voice, slightly out of breath: *"We've been expecting you. All of you. The Divination Tower has three rules for visitors: no uninvited scrying past the second floor, no astral projection above the fourth, and—"* sound of shuffling papers *"—Sephandrius, your paper on temporal recursion was cited incorrectly in the Aldshire Compendium. Third footnote. Magistra Osindra would like to discuss that separately."*

The door opens before anyone touches it.

Inside, a teenage apprentice looks up from a desk covered in observation logs: *"She's been watching your approach for forty minutes. She says to tell you she saw the beacon go off, she's cross about it, and to please wipe your feet."*

The apprentice's notes, if players glance at them: a detailed log of their journey across the city — including *"Sephandrius paused twice to adjust appearance before arrival. Noted."*

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 Osindra — NPC</summary>

**Magistra Vell Osindra** — Divination Tower Master

- **Want:** Remain neutral. She trades in information; sides are overhead costs she'd rather avoid.
- **Fear:** The Concordance has a seer of their own — and she suspects theirs may be more accurate than hers.
- **Lie:** She has already received an approach from the Concordance. An offer. She has not refused it. She is deciding between sessions.

**Voice:** Quiet, precise, never repeats herself. Addresses the group as "investigators." Addresses Sephandrius by first name, as if they've argued before.

She doesn't look up from the shallow basin of black water when they enter: *"The beacon lit four scrying pools simultaneously. Whatever was in that room — three parties now have your description. Sit down."*

**Osindra and Sephandrius:** She knows his published work and has opinions. If it becomes clear Sephandrius is a diviner, she actually looks at him: *"You're better suited for this than I'd like you to be. Don't let it go to your head."* On her neutrality: *"I'm not neutral. I'm careful. There's a difference."*

**Intelligence she offers freely** (she wants them moving before anyone connects the party to her tower):
1. The Concordance has operated in Varenhold for at least twenty years. Not improvised.
2. Their seer reads through resonance, not line-of-sight. Every spell cast in the open leaves an arcane signature — a footprint readable in real time.
3. *"The Ironbell district has a dead zone for scrying — old clay pipes in the walls, copper-lined. Use them."*

**What she doesn't say:** She knows the Concordance leadership's name. The offer they made her includes information she doesn't have yet.

**GM Note — Session 4 betrayal hook:** Decide before Session 4 whether Osindra accepts the offer. If yes, Session 4 can open with the party discovering their movements after this session were leaked.

**Scene ends** when she says, without looking up: *"I'd appreciate it if no one saw you leave."*

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 The Sending + GM Notes</summary>

**Sending to Sera:**

Osindra offers the tower's amplified sending focus — a silver rod mounted in a resonance ring. With it, Sera's response arrives clean. Without it: 50% chance one word is replaced by static.

Sera's 25-word response:

> *"They knew my name before the beacon. Ironbell tannery, third lane. Come quiet. No magic in the streets — they have a seer. Maerin's already coming."*

**GM Note — "Maerin's already coming":** Maerin Voss is a priest the players met in Session 0.5. Same surname as Sera — she is family. She heard two days ago that Sera had gone dark. She's been moving toward Ironbell on her own.

**GM Note — "They knew my name before the beacon":** Deliberate unsolved mystery. Do not resolve in Session 3. If players press Sera later, she doesn't know how they identified her either.

**GM Note — The Sending triggers The Watcher:** The arcane signature of a Sending spell is large, clean, and distinctive. The Concordance seer reads it immediately. The Watcher — bound to track Sending signatures — is dispatched within minutes. It cannot enter the copper-pipe dead zone. It will be waiting.

<summary>📁 Scene 2 — Skill Challenge: Through the Watched Streets</summary>

<details>
<summary>📄 Five Senses & Setup</summary>

**Five senses (Ironbell approach):**
- *Sight:* Narrower streets than the civic quarter — buildings lean together overhead, closing the sky to a grey strip. Loading docks, a tanner's market with hanging hides, a water channel close to the wall. People who don't make eye contact.
- *Sound:* Iron bells, distant and irregular. Underfoot: cobbles give way to fitted stone over the copper-pipe infrastructure — a different sound when you tap it.
- *Smell:* Tanning chemicals from three directions. Oak bark. Something biological and old.
- *Touch:* The walls here are cooler than the open streets. The copper pipes behind the plaster radiate a faint chill.
- *The dead zone:* When players enter the copper-pipe alley, every spellcaster feels a sudden silence — the arcane signature doesn't propagate outward. Like casting into a room with no echo. It's oddly peaceful.

**The threat:** The Concordance seer reads arcane signatures in real time. Operatives are positioned at key intersections. And at the edge of the copper-pipe zone: a shape that isn't moving with the crowd. The Watcher is here.

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 Challenge Table + Consequences</summary>

**Stakes:** 3 successes before 2 failures. Track failures silently — announce only what players can observe.

| Roll | Skill | DC | What it represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spot the watcher | Perception / Insight | 15 | Identify which figure is reading them, not just standing |
| Lose the tail | Stealth / Deception | 13 | Cut through the tanner's market, change silhouette, double back |
| Detect the scry-ping | Arcana (no spell required) | 16 | Recognize the pull of an active scry before it locks — casting to detect makes it worse |
| Back-route through Ironbell | History / Athletics | 12 | Find the copper-pipe service passage Osindra mentioned |
| Misdirect a watcher | Performance / Deception | 14 | Plant a false trail toward the Conjuration district |

**No spells in the open streets.** Each spell cast outside the copper-pipe zone grants the Concordance seer a position fix. Additionally, The Watcher moves 30 ft toward any arcane source as a free action. Casting during the skill challenge = automatic 1 failure, no roll.

**Outcome tiers:**

| Result | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Full success (3/0):** Arrive clean. | Surprise round. Sera has had time to prepare a position. The Returned start clustered. |
| **Partial (3/1):** Tailed once. | No surprise. The Watcher is at the tannery perimeter — visible through shutter cracks. Watching. |
| **Hard partial (3/2):** Tailed and flagged. | No surprise. The Watcher enters through the lane-threshold gap at the start of Round 1. Reinforcement (1 additional Ghast) arrives end of Round 2 via the shuttered east window. |
| **Full failure (2 failures before 3 successes):** Seer locks on before they reach Ironbell. | Sera is captured before they arrive. Chase / rescue scenario replaces the combat. Session path changes significantly — see GM note below. |

**GM Note — Full failure:** Players arrive at the tannery to find a fight has already ended. Sera is gone. One unconscious Unlit is left behind (extraction team moved fast). <summary>📄 The Watcher — First Appearance</summary>

At the edge of the copper-pipe zone, Passive Perception 14+: a shape at the alley mouth that doesn't move like people move. It stands exactly at the boundary and does not cross it. <summary>📁 Scene 3 — Combat: The Tannery Ambush</summary>

<details>
<summary>📄 Five Senses & Setup</summary>

**Five senses:**
- *Sight:* The tannery is shut. Shutters cracked. One amber lamp visible inside. Three figures in the lane — too still, too positioned. Their wrists glow faint violet. They are not alive.
- *Sound:* The tanning chemicals hit before the building does. Under that: silence. The figures in the lane make no sound at all. Iron bells in the distance.
- *Touch:* The lane cobbles are damp. The door has been opened recently — dust disturbed in a specific arc.
- *The uncanny:* One figure's wrist has a sealed-door brand, glowing softly. No breath vapour. They have been standing here for a long time.

**Sera Voss** is inside, crossbow across her knees, sitting against the far wall. Not frightened. Waiting. When she sees them: *"Took longer than I expected."*

She has two days of intelligence to give. She doesn't get the chance.

**The ambush hits when players enter or signal entry.** The three figures in the lane move simultaneously — coordinated, not hurried. The door behind the players closes. Two more were already inside.

**Secondary Objective (state before first initiative roll):** The Priest's goal is to extract Sera alive. Players must fight and protect Sera simultaneously. If Sera drops to 0 HP (captured via Hold Person + drag), the Priest signals withdrawal — Session 4 changes significantly.

**GM Note — If Sera is captured:** Session 4 opens with the party needing to locate where she is held before pursuing Ellan Voss. The Concordance will attempt to use Sera — a willing Dawnborn — as leverage. This is a harder, darker path.

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 Tannery Environment</summary>

Approximately 40 ft × 30 ft. Low ceiling. One amber lamp — the only light source.

- **Tanning vats ×3** (3 ft deep, acid/chemical solution): Creatures pushed in take 2d6 acid damage per turn; climbing out costs full movement. Shove available to adjacent creatures.
- **Hanging hides on wooden racks**: Soft cover (−2 to ranged attacks through them). Difficult terrain. Athletics DC 11 to pull down — creates 10-ft area of difficult terrain.
- **The amber lamp** (only light source): Smashing it (AC 10) plunges the tannery into darkness. The Returned and the Priest operate freely in darkness; players without darkvision are blinded. Dangerous.
- **Barrels of tanbark**: Athletics DC 13 as a bonus action to push 10 ft — can block doorways or pin creatures.
- **Copper-pipe infrastructure**: The tannery itself is inside the dead zone. The Watcher cannot enter through the walls. If players move into the lane during combat, The Watcher is there.

**The Stench:** The tannery's chemical smell is extreme. At the start of combat, all creatures within the tannery must make an initial DC 11 CON save or be Poisoned for the first round. The Returned are immune. This is separate from the Ghasts' own Stench ability.

</details>

<summary>📁 Scene 5 — Riddle + RP: The Ashring Door</summary>

<details>
<summary>📄 Getting There + Five Senses</summary>

**Getting there:** Ashring is the district built around the original ritual site. The stone circle — Ashring Plaza — sits at its centre, permanently cordoned off by the city. The streets around it are older than the rest of Varenhold. The architecture is pre-darkness. The Watcher cannot follow here: the entire Ashring district sits on a natural resonance-null layer, and Ellan's door bears a ward that would destroy The Watcher if it crossed. Players who need to permanently deal with The Watcher can lure it here.

**Five senses:**
- *Sight:* A narrow row of stone houses, older than anything in Lowmark. One door has no knocker. No lock visible. Instead: a stone panel set into the wall beside the door frame, carved with text in old script.
- *Sound:* The stone circle is audible from here — a low, irregular resonance, barely below hearing. Not wind. Something else.
- *Smell:* Old stone and something faintly mineral. The smell of places that have been waiting.
- *Touch:* The panel is warm when touched. Not lamp-warm — something inside the stone.
- *Air:* Warmer than the lane. Inexplicably.

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 The Door Ward — D&D Mechanics</summary>

**What the ward is:**

A 7th-level Abjuration construct, ritually built into the stone frame by Corven himself. Not a standard Glyph of Warding — the trigger mechanism is Divination-adjacent, and the ward predates most published Abjuration systems by fifty years. Corven built it specifically for this door, based on his own research into consent as a magical mechanism — the same principle built into the ritual itself.

**Sephandrius can identify it (Arcana DC 14):** *"7th-level Abjuration. Not a Glyph — the trigger is Divination-adjacent. It doesn't read what you say. It reads the intention signature behind what you say. Corven built this to distinguish performed compliance from actual consent. That's extraordinary work for any mage. For one mage working alone in the year it was built, it's nearly impossible."*

Higher result (DC 18): *"The ward is permanent — or as close to permanent as magic gets. It requires no renewal. It was built into the stone during original construction. Corven spent real time on this door. Whatever was behind it mattered to him."*

**What the ward does:** The carved panel is the ward's focus. Speaking to it activates the trigger. The ward reads the speaker's intention — not whether they're being honest (that is Zone of Truth's domain), but whether they have genuinely examined what they mean. Performed nobility reads as empty. Tactical justification reads as empty. Self-awareness and genuine reason trigger the latch.

**The ward also:** Permanently destroys The Watcher if it attempts to cross. The ward does not allow The Watcher to approach voluntarily — but if players lead it here, the ward activates and destroys it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>📄 The Riddle + Door Mechanic</summary>

Carved into the stone panel. No preamble:

> *I was present when the sun stopped.*
> *I am absent now that it has not returned.*
> *I am not carried by the willing or the unwilling.*
> *I am not held by the living or the dead.*
> *I am not time, though I outlast it.*
> *I am not truth, though I contain it.*
> *I am not silence, though I am what remains when speaking ends.*
>
> *Ten held me fully.*
> *Nine still hold me.*
> *The one who withdrew me was made whole again — and broke everything else.*
>
> *I cannot be divided. I can only be present or absent.*
> *When I am absent from even one, I am absent from all.*
>
> *The door does not open for the word.*
> *The door opens for the act.*
> *Perform me — do not name me — and enter.*

**The answer:** Consent.

**The trap for smart players:** They will solve the riddle quickly. Then stand at a cold panel waiting for something to happen when they say "consent." Nothing happens. This is intentional.

**The mechanism:** Each player character must state, aloud, why they are here. Not the tactical reason. The real one. The ward reads intention, not vocabulary.

Examples:
- *"We need to speak with her"* — cold. That is a task, not a reason.
- *"To save the city"* — cold. That is a mission, not consent.
- *"Because I don't know if I would make the same choice she did, and I need to understand it"* — click.
- *"I am afraid of what comes next if I don't"* — click.
- *"I follow my companions"* — cold. Obligation is not consent. This player must find their own reason.
- *"I am afraid of the dark and I cannot explain why this matters, but it does"* — click. Vulnerability counts.

**Individual, not unanimous:** The ward does not require everyone to enter together. A player who cannot find their real reason stands outside while the others enter. The door remains open. Standing outside is its own kind of answer.

**Hint timing:** Let players work for ten real-world minutes before any hint. When genuinely stuck: Sera (if present) reads the last three lines again slowly: *"It's not asking you to say the word. It's asking you to do it."* Another five minutes later, if still stuck: Sera looks at the panel and says quietly: *"I am here because I decided yes, months ago, and I need to know if someone else did the same."* Click.

**After each player speaks their real reason:** The panel slides aside. One compartment. One wax-sealed letter, addressed: *"To whoever finds this after I'm gone — I expect I will be."*

And, beneath the letter, tucked against the back of the compartment: a small card in Corven's hand. One sentence. No date.

<details>
<summary>📄 Ellan Voss — NPC + Dialogue</summary>

She answers the door before they finish reading the letter.

She is in her late sixties. She moves slowly, without assistance, and looks at them with the calm of someone who has been expecting a specific knock for a very long time. She is weathered — fifty years of waiting has a cost — but her eyes are clear and her hands are steady.

*"Theron finally sent someone. Good. Come in."*

*"The ritual didn't fail because the cost was wrong. It failed because I withdrew my consent at the last moment. I was the ninth. I said no. And because I said no, it ended."*

She sets down her cup.

*"I have spent fifty years making sure no one tried again. The Concordance found out I existed approximately three years ago. They have been keeping me alive because I am useful to them. If the ritual proceeds, it requires my consent. I will not give it. Not because I am afraid. Because I am the only person in this city who has seen what happens when someone changes their mind at the last second inside the circle."*

*"It was not a small thing."*

---

**Ellan Voss — NPC**

- **Want:** For someone to ask her the right question. She has been rehearsing the answer for fifty years and no one has come until now.
- **Fear:** That when she finally says yes — and she knows she will — it will be too late. The Concordance keeps her alive but not protected; she understands the distinction.
- **Lie:** *"I will not give it. Not before."* This is her public position, not her private truth. She wants to say yes. She has been waiting for someone to give her a reason that outweighs what she saw inside the circle. Insight DC 15: sense this. She will not say it unprompted.

**What players can ask:**

- *What did you see?* → *"The others — the nine who stayed — I heard them. The ritual had already begun. Withdrawing did not just stop it. It reversed. Partially. For a moment, I watched the light begin and then un-begin. That is not a word. But it is what happened."*
- *Are you a Dawnborn?* → *"No. I was a ritual participant, not a Dawnborn. I am mortal. I am old. I chose to withdraw and have been dying slowly ever since, like everyone else."*
- *What does the Concordance want from you?* → *"They want me to keep refusing. As long as I refuse, the ritual cannot be unanimous. They know this. They are protecting their asset."*
- *Will you say yes now?* → *Long silence. Then: "Ask me again when you've found all ten of them. Ask me again when they've all had the chance to choose freely. Then ask me. Not before."*
