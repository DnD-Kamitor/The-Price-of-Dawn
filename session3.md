# Session 3 — The Watched City (Level 6)

*Dilemma: What does it mean to choose something when choosing is itself a kind of violence?*

---

## Scene 1 — RP: The Divination Tower

**Five senses:**
- Sight: A tower narrower than expected, no signage, windows filled with slowly rotating orreries that don't match any sky the players have seen. A brass speaking tube by the door, polished to a mirror finish.
- Sound: A faint mechanical ticking from somewhere high above. The sound of pages turning, though no one is visible through the windows.
- Smell: Old ink and something sharper — ozone, like lightning held still. Faintly: tea, recently brewed. Exactly enough cups.
- Touch: The door handle is warm, as if someone just let go of it.
- Air: Still. The tower seems to be holding its breath.

**The Prescience Door (funny mechanic for Sephandrius):**

Before anyone can knock, the speaking tube crackles. A young apprentice's voice, slightly out of breath: *"We've been expecting you. All of you. The Divination Tower has three rules for visitors: no uninvited scrying past the second floor, no astral projection above the fourth, and—"* sound of shuffling papers *"—Sephandrius, your paper on temporal recursion was cited incorrectly in the Aldshire Compendium. Third footnote. Magistra Osindra would like to discuss that separately."*

The door opens before anyone touches it.

Inside, a teenage apprentice with ink-stained fingers and a stack of notes looks up from a desk covered in observation logs. *"She's been watching your approach for forty minutes. She says to tell you she saw the beacon go off, she's cross about it, and to please wipe your feet."*

The apprentice's notes, if players glance at them, contain a detailed log of their journey across the city — including: *"Sephandrius paused twice to adjust appearance before arrival. Noted."*

**Magistra Vell Osindra** — Divination Tower Master

- **Want:** Remain neutral. She trades in information; sides are overhead costs she'd rather avoid.
- **Fear:** The necromancer faction has a seer of their own — and she suspects theirs may be more accurate than hers.
- **Lie:** She has already received an approach from the darkness faction. An offer. She has not refused it yet.
- **Voice:** Quiet, precise, never repeats herself. Addresses the group as "investigators." Addresses Sephandrius by first name, as if they've argued before.

She doesn't look up from the shallow basin of black water when they enter: *"The beacon lit four scrying pools simultaneously. Whatever was in that room — three parties now have your description. Sit down."*

**Osindra and Sephandrius:** She knows his work. She's read his published research and has opinions. If players make it obvious that Sephandrius is a diviner, she pauses and actually looks at him: *"You're better suited for this than I'd like you to be. Don't let it go to your head."* If pushed on her neutrality: *"I'm not neutral. I'm careful. There's a difference."*

**The Sending:**

Osindra offers the use of the tower's amplified sending focus — a silver rod mounted in a resonance ring that extends range and reduces distortion. With it, the message reaches Sera clearly. Without it, the message still works but the response may be fragmented (50% chance of partial static: one random word of Sera's response is lost).

Sephandrius casts Sending. Sera's 25-word response:

> *"They knew my name before the beacon. Ironbell tannery, third lane. Come quiet. No magic in the streets — they have a seer. Lira's already coming."*

**What Osindra tells them:**

She gives them three pieces of intelligence, freely, because she wants them moving before anyone connects the party to her tower:
1. The necromancer faction has operated in Varenhold for at least twenty years. Not new. Not improvised.
2. Their seer works through resonance, not line-of-sight. Arcane signatures in the streets act like footprints.
3. *"The Ironbell district has a dead zone for scrying — old clay pipes in the walls, copper-lined. Use them."*

What she doesn't say: she knows more about the necromancer faction's leadership than she's sharing. The offer they made her. The name they used.

**Scene ends** when she says, without looking up: *"I'd appreciate it if no one saw you leave."*

## Scene 2 — Skill Challenge: Through the Watched Streets

The seer sees arcane signatures. Every spell cast in the open streets is a flare. The city is also physically watched — the necromancer faction has moved people into key intersections within the last hour.

**Stakes:** 3 successes before 2 failures. Failure consequences accumulate silently and carry into Scene 3.

| Roll | Skill | DC | What it represents |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spot the watcher | Perception / Insight | 15 | Identify which figure at the corner is reading them, not just standing |
| Lose the tail | Stealth / Deception | 13 | Cut through the tanner's market, change silhouette, double back through a loading dock |
| Detect the scry-ping | Arcana (no spell required) | 16 | Recognize the faint directional pull of an active scry attempt before it locks |
| Back-route through Ironbell | History / Athletics | 12 | The copper-pipe district has a service passage Osindra mentioned — finding it requires knowledge of the city's old infrastructure |
| Misdirect a watcher | Performance / Deception | 14 | Plant a false trail toward the Conjuration district |

**Failure consequences (track, don't announce):**
- 0 failures: Arrive clean. Surprise round in combat. Sera has time to prepare.
- 1 failure: Tailed. No surprise. One watcher has their location — a runner has already been sent.
- 2 failures: Tailed and flagged. Reinforcements arrive end of round 2 of combat (1 additional Cult Fanatic).

**The copper-pipe dead zone:** Any player who casts a spell in the marked alley near the tannery feels a sudden silence — the arcane signature doesn't propagate. Osindra's information was accurate. This is reassuring and slightly eerie.

## Scene 3 — Combat: The Tannery Ambush

**Five senses:**
- Sight: The tannery is shut, shutters cracked, one amber lamp visible inside through a gap. Three other figures visible in the lane — too still, too positioned.
- Sound: The chemical smell of curing leather hits before the building does. Under it: something sharper, less explicable.
- Touch: The lane cobbles are damp. Fresh rain, or deliberate — harder to run on.
- Air: The door has been opened recently. The dust disturbed in a specific arc.

Sera Voss is inside, crossbow across her knees, sitting against the far wall. Not frightened. Waiting. When she sees them: *"Took longer than I expected."*

She has two days of information to give. She doesn't get the chance.

**The ambush hits when players enter or signal entry.** Three figures in the lane move simultaneously — coordinated, not panicked. The door behind the players closes. Two more agents were already inside.

**Secondary Objective (state before first initiative roll):** The Priest's goal is to extract Sera alive, not kill her. Players must fight and protect Sera simultaneously. If Sera drops to 0 HP (captured via Hold Person + drag), the Priest signals immediate withdrawal — and Session 4 changes significantly.

**Encounter XP:** 3,800 adjusted XP — Hard encounter for 4 level-6 characters. With 2 skill challenge failures (reinforcement arrives round 2): 4,600 adjusted — Deadly. Warn players with secondary objective stakes, not with numbers.

**Actions**

**Tactical Priority (run in this order each round):**

**Round 1:** Cast *Darkness* (2nd-level) centred on the amber lamp — plunges 15-foot radius into magical darkness. All Dusk Cultists have darkvision and can Shadow Step through it. Players without darkvision have disadvantage on attacks and the Priest has advantage on attacks against them. Sera is inside the darkness zone. Finding her requires action or Blindsight.

**Round 2:** Cast *Hold Person* targeting Sera (WIS save DC 13). If she fails, two cultists immediately move to her position and begin dragging (10 ft per round, costs their movement). If she succeeds: the Priest uses Toll the Dead on the highest-HP player.

**Round 3+:** If Sera is Held and being dragged: the Priest uses Shadow Step to flank any player blocking the extraction route. If Sera is free: repeat Hold Person (uses 2nd slot). If both 2nd slots are spent: Inflict Wounds on the nearest player, use cultists as a screen.

**Withdrawal trigger:** If 3 of 4 Cultists are downed OR the Priest drops below 20 HP — the Priest signals withdrawal with a specific hand gesture. All remaining Cultists disengage simultaneously (no opportunity attacks, Shadow Step away). The Priest dimension-doors if possible; otherwise disengages and runs. They do not fight to the death.

**Bestow Curse / Shadow of Moil:** Only used if the fight goes longer than 5 rounds (unlikely). Bestow Curse on the most active player (disadvantage on WIS saves — makes Hold Person harder to resist). Shadow of Moil is the Priest's last-resort when below 20 HP: 4d12 necrotic to adjacent attackers per turn, heavily obscures the Priest.

**Lira Anwick arrives** approximately 2 rounds into combat — she was already coming, as Sera's Sending message revealed. She enters through the roof hatch with medical kit and no weapons. She can stabilize, she can drag, she cannot fight. Her presence creates a secondary protection problem.

**Post-combat — the Sera/Lira confrontation:**

Both women are in the same room. Both are breathing hard. Neither is injured badly enough to stop the conversation that's been waiting.

**Sera wants:** Players to understand that she has already decided — freely, clearly, without coercion — to proceed. She decided months ago. The beacon, the cryptics, the necromancers: none of this changes the calculation.

**Sera fears:** That her willingness will be used as a reason to pressure the unwilling.

**Lira wants:** Players to understand that she has also decided — freely, clearly, without coercion — to refuse. She has a daughter. That is the entire argument.

**Lira fears:** That Sera's willingness makes her own refusal morally irrelevant — and she hates herself for thinking about it that way.

**The lie:** Neither is lying. This is what makes the scene unresolvable.

**Five senses (post-combat):**
- Sight: Amber tannery lamp, the smell of acid and adrenaline, a child's handkerchief falling from Lira's medical kit when she opens it. Lira picks it up without comment.
- Sound: The argument never gets loud. That's worse.

**The Encounter Beat:** Lira says, quietly, to Sera: *"You want to die for them. I understand that. I have a daughter. You don't."*

Sera does not answer. She has no answer. The two women look at each other across fifty years of friendship and the gap is visible. Do not resolve this. Let it be real. Players who intervene cannot fix it. They can only be present.

**Sample dialogue:**
- Sera (to players, before Lira speaks): *"Someone has to make a decision. We can't wait for a perfect answer that might not exist."*
- Lira (when pushed): *"I have a daughter. That's the whole argument. I don't have another one."*
- Sera (quietly, to Lira, after a long pause): *"I know."*

**How it connects forward:** Players now know the consent problem is not theoretical. Two Dawnborn, same room, diametrically opposite positions, both freely chosen. Any path that requires unanimous agreement must reckon with this moment.

## Scene 4 — Puzzle: Theron's Second Cryptic

After the tannery, somewhere safe — a Divination Tower safe house, Sera's backup location, or wherever players establish as secure.

They have two cryptics. The first (the library cryptic, found in the sealed room) has been partially decoded. The second is Theron's — he built it himself, in Year 43, and hid it in the Archive deliberately. His is different. Not a cipher. A mechanism.

**The Object:** A nested wooden box, palm-sized, with three rotating outer rings. Each ring is engraved with seven symbols — the original ritual notation Corven used. The rings can be set to any combination. A small aperture in the center will open when the correct configuration is set.

**Visual clue (DC 12 Investigation — free to attempt, no time pressure):** One symbol on the outermost ring has a faint scratch beside it. Not damage — deliberate. Theron's personal notation mark, the same one he uses in his archive cataloguing. Players who handled any of Theron's papers recognize it with DC 10 History.

**The three-ring puzzle:**

*Ring 1 (outermost):* The scratch marks the correct symbol. Its meaning: **the moment before**. In Corven's ritual notation: the symbol for threshold — the instant between states.

*Ring 2 (middle):* The inscription on the lid of the box reads: *"What comes before the ritual, after the silence, and beneath the agreement."* The answer, in Corven's notation: the symbol for **consent**. Players who studied the ritual documents (Sessions 1–2) have advantage on DC 14 Arcana to identify it. Without that background: DC 17.

*Ring 3 (innermost):* Self-referential. The third ring's answer is encoded in the second cryptic's own cipher notation — the original library cryptic contains, if fully decoded, a single symbol that Theron embedded as a forward reference. This requires the players to cross-reference both cryptics. DC 16 Investigation with both cryptics present, or DC 14 Arcana for a player specializing in divination or encryption. If Sephandrius examines it: advantage.

**If players attempt to force it:** The box has a secondary lock — setting the wrong configuration on all three rings simultaneously causes the aperture to seal permanently (a thin metal bar drops across it internally). Only one wrong attempt allowed across all three rings simultaneously. Individual rings can be adjusted freely.

**What's inside:** A folded letter, Theron's handwriting, addressed to no one. Three lines:

> *The ritual failed because the agreement was not whole.*
> *One withdrew. All broke.*
> *The one who withdrew is still alive. She lives in Ashring. Her name is Ellan Voss.*
> *She has been preventing it from being tried again for fifty years.*
> *She does not know I know.*

Below that, a district and a street. A door number.

## Scene 5 — Riddle + RP: The Ashring Door

**Getting there:** Ashring is the district built around the original ritual site. The stone circle — Ashring Plaza — sits at its center, permanently cordoned. The streets around it are older than the rest of the city. The architecture is pre-darkness.

**Five senses:**
- Sight: A narrow row of stone houses, older than anything in Lowmark. One door has no knocker. No lock visible. Instead: a stone panel set into the wall beside the frame, carved with text.
- Sound: The stone circle is audible from here — a low, irregular resonance, barely below hearing. Not wind. Something else.
- Smell: Old stone and something faintly mineral. The smell of places that have been waiting.
- Touch: The carved letters on the panel are worn but legible. Someone has traced them recently — the dust in the grooves is disturbed.
- Air: Warmer than the lane. Inexplicably.

**The Riddle:**

Carved into the stone panel beside the door. No preamble. No instruction. Just this:

> *I was present when the sun stopped.*
> *I am present now that it has not returned.*
> *I am not carried by the willing or the unwilling.*
> *I am not held by the living or the dead.*
> *I am not time, though I outlast it.*
> *I am not truth, though I contain it.*
> *I am not silence, though I am what remains when speaking ends.*
>
> *Ten held me fully.*
> *Nine still hold me.*
> *The one who reclaimed me made me whole again — and broke everything else.*
>
> *I cannot be divided. I can only be present or absent.*
> *When I am absent from even one, I am absent from all.*
>
> *The door does not open for the word.*
> *The door opens for the act.*
> *Perform me — do not name me — and enter.*

**The Answer: Consent.**

The door does not respond to players saying "consent." This is the trap for smart players — they will solve the riddle quickly and then be baffled when the word does nothing.

The mechanism: **each player character must state, aloud, why they are here.** Not the tactical reason. The real one. The door's ward reads intention, not vocabulary. It is testing whether those who enter do so with genuine purpose — not coercion, not mission, not obligation. It was built by someone who understood that consent cannot be claimed, only given.

**GM guidance:** Let players work at it. When they're genuinely stuck, Sera (if she came with them) reads the last three lines again slowly, then says: *"It's not asking you to say the word. It's asking you to do it."* If they're still stuck, Lira looks at the panel for a long time and then says, quietly, to no one in particular: *"I am here because I have a daughter and I need to know if there is another way."* The panel clicks.

After each player speaks their real reason, the panel slides aside. One compartment. One wax-sealed letter, addressed: *"To whoever finds this after I'm gone — I expect I will be."*

**Ellan Voss answers the door before they can read the letter.**

She is in her nineties. She moves slowly, without assistance, and looks at them with the particular calm of someone who has been waiting for a specific knock for a very long time.

*"Theron finally sent someone. Good. Come in."*

She does not elaborate. She makes tea. When they sit:

*"The ritual didn't fail because the cost was wrong. It failed because I withdrew my consent at the last moment. I was the ninth. I said no. And because I said no, it ended."*

She pauses. Sets down her cup.

*"I have spent fifty years making sure no one tried again. The darkness faction found out I existed approximately three years ago. They have been keeping me alive because I am useful to them. If the ritual proceeds, it requires my consent. I will not give it. Not because I am afraid. Because I am the only person in this city who has seen what happens when someone changes their mind at the last second inside the circle."*

Another pause.

*"It was not a small thing."*

**What players can ask:**
- Why did you withdraw? *(She answers directly: "I was afraid. I was nineteen. I chose to live. I have lived with that choice for fifty years.")*
- What did you see? *(She answers directly: "The others — the nine who stayed — I heard them. The ritual had already begun. Withdrawing did not just stop it. It reversed. Partially. For a moment, I watched the light begin and then un-begin. That is not a word. But it is what happened.")*
- Are you a Dawnborn? *(No. She was a ritual participant, not a Dawnborn. She is mortal. She is old. She chose to withdraw and has been dying slowly ever since, like everyone else.)*
- What does the darkness faction want from you? *(They want her to keep refusing. As long as she refuses, the ritual cannot be unanimous. They know this. They are protecting their asset.)*
- Will you say yes now? *(Long silence. Then: "Ask me again when you've found all ten of them. Ask me again when they've all had the chance to choose freely. Then ask me. Not before.")*

**The sealed letter** (Theron's, from the compartment):

> *Ellan —*
> *I have known for seven years. I did not come to you because I was afraid you would say no again, and I needed more time to find a different question to ask.*
> *I think I have one now. I think someone else will bring it.*
> *If they've read this far: ask her what she saw. Not what she did. What she saw.*
> *It matters.*
> *— T.W.*

**Cliffhanger:** As they leave, Ellan says — not dramatically, just factually: *"The darkness faction will know you came here. They watch this street. They have for three years."*

She closes the door.

## End of Session

**What players now know:**
- Sera is Dawnborn, has been hunted for months, has decided yes.
- The necromancer faction has a seer and has been operating in Varenhold for at least twenty years.
- The ritual failed fifty years ago because Ellan Voss withdrew consent at the last moment.
- Ellan Voss is alive, in her nineties, and being kept alive by the necromancer faction specifically to prevent the ritual.
- Her consent is required for the ritual to proceed. She has not given it. She has conditions.
- The darkness faction now knows the players visited her.

**What they don't know yet:**
- Who leads the necromancer faction.
- Whether Osindra took the offer she was given.
- What Ellan Voss actually saw in the moment she withdrew — and why Theron thought it mattered.

**What they carry forward:** Lira's face when she said *"I have a daughter."* And Ellan Voss saying *"I was afraid. I was nineteen."* Two women who said no for completely different reasons.

**Session debrief question:** *"If the only thing standing between darkness and light is one person's consent — does that one person owe the world a yes?"*

Wait for an answer. Don't accept a quick one.
