# Session 2 — The Weight of Light (Level 5)

*Dilemma: Do you have the right to decide someone else's fate, even to save thousands?*

---

## Scene 1 — Opening Combat: The Reckoning at the Archive

<!-- SCENE HEADER BLOCK -->

---

**PURPOSE STATEMENT:** This scene exists to establish that the Reckoning is not reacting to the players — it is ahead of them. The soldiers arrived before the players did. The documents were already being moved toward the fire. The scene opens mid-action, not at the start of a confrontation, and that displacement should feel deliberate and disquieting. The secondary objective (saving the document bundles) reframes combat as a triage decision: players cannot simply fight their way through this scene without also managing the clock on the documents.

**EMOTIONAL TARGET:** Urgency first, then cold realisation. The soldiers are burning evidence — not hiding it, not securing it, burning it. That is different. Someone has already decided these documents should not exist.

**DRAMATIC QUESTION:** Can you stop the fire and learn who ordered it, or do you have to choose between the two?

**DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT:** Round 2. A soldier has a bundle in both hands and is walking steadily toward the fireplace, not looking at the players, not flinching when someone shouts at him. He is completing a task. He will walk into crossbow range. He will keep walking. The players must physically stop him or the bundle is gone.

## Tactical Map Description

### The Archive Ground Floor Reading Room — ~60×40 ft

The Varenhold Civic Archive's ground floor reading room is the longest room in the building: a wide rectangular hall with the main entrance on the south face, shelves along three walls reaching nine feet high, and a large functional fireplace built into the east wall. The room is used for supervised research access — long oak tables run parallel to the shelves, with high-backed chairs and individual reading stands. The lighting is amber lantern sconces every eight feet along the upper shelves, warm and steady. In the permanent twilight of Varenhold, this room is always lit.

The players have been here before — Session 1, Scene 3. They know the layout: the main door south, the staircase to the upper floor behind the reference desk at the room's north end, the librarian's standing desk near the door, Theron's reading alcove partition built flush into the north wall.

Tonight, the room has been altered in three specific ways:
- The fireplace is lit and stoked high — it has been burning long enough to be hot, not just lit.
- Three document bundles from the restricted archive are spread across the central reading table — they were pulled in haste, some still in their binding-twine, laid flat.
- Reckoning soldiers are already in the room, moving with the particular efficiency of people who have done this before.

### Five Senses — Full

**Sight:**
- The familiar amber reading room, shelves high on three walls, the long oak table with its reading stands — but the atmosphere is wrong. The fire is too big. Research rooms don't need fires this large.
- Three cloth-wrapped bundles on the central table, binding twine still on two of them, the third already loose where someone pulled documents out to check they had the right material.
- A soldier walking with steady deliberate steps toward the fireplace, a bundle under both arms. No hesitation. No glance at the players. The door being open meant he heard them arrive; it hasn't changed anything.
- The Lieutenant's face when he turns: not surprise. A scheduling adjustment. He was expecting to be done. He is not.
- Four dark-coated soldiers in Reckoning livery — the grey coat with the closed-eye emblem on the left breast — positioned across the room with the particular dispersal of people who planned where they would stand.

**Sound:**
- Crackling from the fireplace, louder than a reading room fire should be — someone has been feeding it. A low, persistent roar of a fire built for purpose.
- The scrape of the soldier's boots on the stone floor as he walks, unhurried, toward the east wall. That sound — steady, rhythmic, not stopping — is the combat clock made audible.
- Paper rustling from S3's hand on bundle T3. He is not lifting it yet. He is waiting to see what happens.
- The Lieutenant's voice when he speaks, or the silence if he does not.
- Beneath everything: the building's own quiet, which is the wrong kind of quiet for a library — no creak of a librarian's footstep, no turn of a page. The Archive is empty of everything except the Reckoning and what they came to destroy.

**Smell:**
- Old paper, leather bindings, archival dust — the room's permanent base note. The smell of preserved knowledge.
- Over it now: wood smoke from the fireplace, sharper and more present than it should be in a reading room. Not unpleasant, but wrong.
- A faint scorched-cloth smell from the direction of the fireplace — S4 has already fed one document into it before the players arrived. One bundle is already gone.

**Touch:**
- The door handle warm from recent contact — someone touched it minutes ago, coming in.
- If players move to intercept S1: the close warmth radiating from the fireplace before they reach the east end, the air getting drier and hotter.
- The reading room's typical chill at the south entry — the doors being open have let the Varenhold night in.

**Taste:**
- Woodsmoke at the back of the throat, the thin edge of it that reaches all the way to the doorway when the fire is this size.
- The mineral dryness of the Archive itself — dust and old paper absorbed into the air.

## Initiative & Surprise

### Surprise?

The doors are open. The players can hear the fire. There is no surprise in either direction — this is a room-entry into an active situation, not an ambush. Roll initiative normally at the threshold.

**Exception:** If players spent time outside the building listening or observing through a window, they may have identified S1's movement before entry. In this case, they get one free action before initiative — a player may declare one preparatory move (draw weapon, call a specific command) that happens before the first initiative roll resolves.

## The Document Clock

### Three Bundles — Status at Round Start

| Bundle | Position | Soldier Assigned | Round Reaches Fire |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | Central table, south end | None — closest to players' entry | Never, unless S2 or S3 reassigned |
| **T2** | In S1's hands, 20 ft from fire | S1 (actively carrying) | End of Round 1 (movement + action = 20 ft + drop in fire) |
| **T3** | Table, north end — S3's hand on it | S3 (not yet lifted) | End of Round 2 if S3 acts normally |

**Fireplace consumption:** A bundle in the fire burns for 2 full rounds before the documents inside are unrecoverable. A player can reach into the fire (DC 12 Dex, action, takes 2d6 fire damage on failure or half on success) to retrieve a bundle that has been in the fire for 1 round. After 2 full rounds, the documents are ash.

### Intercepting a Bundle-Carrier

A player who gets adjacent to a soldier carrying a bundle may:
- **Grab the bundle** (contested Athletics: player vs. soldier +4). On success, player has the bundle and the soldier's Document Priority action is spent. On failure, soldier keeps it and the player takes a Hold the Line reaction attack.
- **Tackle the soldier** (Shove, Athletics vs. Athletics +4). On success, soldier is knocked prone; they cannot move the bundle that turn. Bundle is still in their hands.
- **Simply block the path** (stand adjacent). The soldier must either attack the player (ending its bundle-carry movement this turn) or pass through the player's space (requires Shove — Athletics DC matching player's Str).

## Round-by-Round Guide

### Round 1 — "They Were Here First"

**Initiative count 20 (top of round):** Describe the room's state plainly: *"S1 has twenty feet to the fire. S3's hand is on bundle T3. The Lieutenant is between you and the table."*

**S1:** Uses full movement (30 ft) toward fireplace. If he began 20 ft away, he reaches it and can use his action to place the bundle in the fire. **This is the first crisis point.** Any player who acts before S1 in initiative can intercept if they move immediately.

**S2:** Engages the most forward player — Multiattack. His job is to hold the south end of the room and slow the players' movement into the space.

**S3:** Picks up bundle T3 (action) and begins moving toward the fireplace. With 30 ft movement, he ends Round 1 about 10 ft from the fire.

**S4:** Positions himself at the fireplace, hand torch ready. He does not attack players unless adjacent. His role is to receive the bundles. If S1 delivers T2 this round, S4 uses his action to push T2 deeper into the fire (ensuring 2-round consumption starts this round).

**Lieutenant (Round 1):** Does not attack immediately. He uses Command as his bonus action to direct S3 (move 15 ft extra toward fire). He spends his action on Longsword attacks against the first player to step past him toward the table. He is the gate. He expects to buy S1 the one round he needs.

---

### Round 2 — The Second Bundle Decision

**If T2 is in the fire at this point:** The bundle has been burning for 1 round. One round left before it's gone. A player can still retrieve it (2d6 fire damage attempt), but must weigh this against T3 still in motion.

**S3:** If not intercepted, reaches the fire and begins feeding T3. S4 assists.

**S2:** Continues engaging front-line players. Uses Multiattack each turn.

**S1:** Bundle delivered. He draws his weapon and engages the nearest player — Multiattack this round.

**Lieutenant:** Now switches to full offense. Two Longsword attacks against the player who most threatens the operation. Uses Shield Bash bonus action to push that player away from the fireplace side of the room.

---

### Round 3+ — Endpoint Conditions

**The combat ends when ONE of these is true:**
1. The Lieutenant is incapacitated or captured (grappled and restrained; he does not yield at half HP the way the Session 1 Enforcer did — he is more committed than the Restorers).
2. Two or more bundles are in the players' secure possession (not just grabbed — held away from the fireplace or removed from the room). At this point the soldiers withdraw on a sharp two-word command from the Lieutenant if he is still standing: *"Out. Now."* They disengage and leave through the main entrance in good order.
3. All soldiers are incapacitated.

**If the Lieutenant is captured:** He gives information under pressure (see NPC dialogue below). He does not give Harran's name — he uses "the Commander." He is professional.

### Soldier Priority (in order)
1. Move bundle toward fireplace.
2. If adjacent to a player blocking path: Hold the Line reaction, then continue movement.

<summary>🎬 GM Notes — Scene 1</summary>

### Pacing

The scene should feel rushed — it opens mid-action and never fully slows down until the Lieutenant is dealt with. Do not let the players take a breath between entering and the first decision about S1 and the bundle. The urgency is the scene.

**Do not** describe the room fully before initiating. Give the read-aloud, call for initiative, and let the tactical picture develop through play. Players who want to know exactly where everything is can ask, and you answer, but do not front-load the spatial information — the confusion of entering mid-crisis is correct.

### Secondary Objective Guidance

The bundle clock is your pacing tool. If the scene is running slow (players spending too many actions on careful positioning), have S3 pick up T3 and narrate him walking: *"Two of them now. Moving."* This should prompt a response.

Do not remind players that T1 is still on the table and unattended — let them discover it or not. T1 is the safety net for a party that loses T2 and T3. If they save T1 and T2, they have enough for Scene 3. If they save only T1, Scene 3 becomes harder. Track the outcome; do not manage it.

### What Not to Do

- Do not let the Lieutenant be verbose. He is not a monologue villain. He is a manager doing a job. If he talks, it is brief.
- Do not make the soldiers hesitate to burn documents because the players look intimidating. They have an order. They complete it unless physically prevented.
- Do not have the soldiers fight to the death for the last bundle once the Lieutenant is down. At that point they withdraw — that is their training.

### Theron's Emergence

After the scene ends and the players have secured whatever bundles they have, pause for one beat of silence. Then:

> "From behind the north wall — the section that looks like shelving — a sound. A latch. The panel swings open. Theron Waide steps out with ink-stained cuffs and the very specific composure of someone who has been standing completely still behind a locked door for fifteen minutes."

Do not rush Theron. Let the players react to him before he speaks.

### Connects Forward

Everything in this scene feeds Scene 2:
- The bundles the players saved contain the colophon signatures for the resonance box in Scene 3.
- The Lieutenant's line about "the Commander watching for three months" confirms the Reckoning knew about Corven's documents before the players did — which means someone in the administration told them, which Theron will corroborate in Scene 4.
- Theron's emergence from hiding is his first appearance this session. His survival of the raid is important to him — he waited behind that door not because he was afraid, but because he was protecting the alcove's locked drawer. The parchment slips were in it the whole time.

## Scene 2 — The Riddle: Theron's Encoded Message

<!-- SCENE HEADER BLOCK -->

---

**PURPOSE STATEMENT:** This scene exists to give players the first clear statement of what the documents mean — not through an NPC monologue, but through a puzzle they solve themselves. Theron has been carrying this encoded message for eleven years. He wrote it for himself, not for them. The fact that it is solvable is the most honest thing about it. By the time players read the answer aloud, they have earned the horror of knowing.

**EMOTIONAL TARGET:** Slow dread, then the specific stillness of understanding something you cannot un-understand. The moment the answer is spoken aloud should be followed by silence at the table, not clarification.

**DRAMATIC QUESTION:** What kind of person writes down the most dangerous truth they know, in a code they leave solvable?

**DESIGNED PEAK MOMENT:** A player reads the decoded message aloud for the first time. Theron looks at them. He does not deny it. He says: *"Yes."*

## Location: Archive Ground Floor — Reading Room to Private Alcove

The scene begins in the reading room, which now has the aftermath quality of interrupted destruction: chairs shifted, the fireplace burning lower now that no one is feeding it, document bundles spread across the central table in whatever state the players managed. The soldiers are gone.

Theron is standing near the alcove doorway. He has not moved far from it — he can see the room, the state of the bundles, the bodies if there are any. He takes stock. He does not say anything immediately.

After thirty seconds of player action — gathering documents, checking the room, asking questions — Theron goes to the reference desk. He takes a key from his coat. He unlocks the top right drawer.

He removes three parchment slips. Not from a folder, not in a box — loose, slightly bent from being carried flat in the drawer for years. He sets them on the reading table in front of the players without speaking first. He straightens them once, aligning their edges. Then he steps back.

---

### Physical Description — The Three Parchment Slips

Each slip is approximately 6 inches by 3 inches, cut from larger parchment with a clean edge. The writing is Theron's hand — neat archival script, the handwriting of someone who has transcribed thousands of documents. No ornamentation. Each slip has a single sentence.

**The slips are not labeled. They are not numbered. They are not in any indicated order. Theron places them left-to-right on the table: First Slip, Second Slip, Third Slip.**

### The Riddle — Three Parchment Slips

> **First slip:** *"The Dawnmark rises when the anchor holds. The vessel must be willing. The energy of the third circuit does not disperse; it dwells."*

> **Second slip:** *"Ask the archivist what lives within the amber light. The answer is not in the records. Within them is the truth of the ritual's end."*

> **Third slip:** *"Every third scholar agreed: the Dawnborn are the mechanism. The energy lives in willing vessels. The ritual them waits still."*

### The Pattern

Reading every third word across all three slips in sequence, starting from word 3 of the first slip and counting across slips continuously:

**First slip word 3:** *rises* — **word 6:** *anchor* — **word 9:** *willing* — **word 12:** *third* — **word 15:** *not* — **word 18:** *dwells*

**Continue into second slip word 3:** *what* — ...

The cleaner reading is every third word from the beginning of each slip, taken in order across all three slips:

> Slip 1: **The** Dawnmark *rises* when the **anchor** holds. The **vessel** must be willing. The **energy** of the third **circuit** does not **disperse**; it **dwells.**
>
> Slip 2: **Ask** the archivist **what** lives within **the** amber light. **The** answer is **not** in the **records.** Within **them** is the **truth** of the **ritual's** end.
>
> Slip 3: **Every** third scholar **agreed:** the Dawnborn **are** the mechanism. **The** energy lives **in** willing vessels. **The** ritual **them** waits **still.**

Reading only the **capitalised words** (the pattern Theron used — every third word starting from the first word of each slip, in sequence across all three):

**THE · ANCHOR · VESSEL · ENERGY · CIRCUIT · DWELLS · ASK · WHAT · THE · THE · NOT · RECORDS · WITHIN · THEM · TRUTH · RITUAL · EVERY · AGREED · ARE · THE · IN · THE · THEM · STILL**

The relevant phrase — what Theron was encoding — is assembled from the word that begins every third grouping, starting at word 1 of the full sequence:

> **THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM**

*For GMs who prefer a clean puzzle mechanic:* The simplest version is this: read only the words that occupy positions 1, 4, 7, 10, 13, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28 across the three slips read left to right as continuous text. Those words, in order, form the sentence. The slips were designed so a scholar would find it eventually. Theron is a scholar.

## The Riddle — Full Mechanics

### Giving It to the Players

Set the slips in front of the players physically if possible — print or write them on separate pieces of paper, or read each aloud slowly and write them on an index card. The riddle must be visible and rereadable; players should be able to point at words. If running digitally, paste the text into the shared space where players can see it.

Do not tell players there is a pattern to find. Do not say "it is a cipher." Simply: *"Theron places three parchment slips on the table in front of you."* Then read the three texts. Then: silence. Theron says nothing.

---

### What Theron Does During the Puzzle

Theron watches. He does not pace. He does not give hints unless prompted, and even then his hints are minimal — he is measuring whether they can arrive at the answer, because he has spent eleven years arriving at it himself and the fact that it can be found is the only thing about it he still finds comforting.

**If players are working:**
Theron is silent. If asked *"Is this a code?"*, he says: *"Yes."* If asked *"What kind?"*, he says: *"The simplest kind I could devise."*

**If players hit a wrong answer (first attempt):**
> "Keep trying. The pattern is simpler than it looks."

He does not confirm the method. He will confirm when they have the right approach: *"That direction. Keep going."*

**If players are stuck after two wrong attempts:**
> "Every word has a position. Count."

This is the most explicit he will be. Count positions. Every Nth word. He will not say which N.

**If players fail three times or attempt to stop:**
Theron reaches for the slips. *"Perhaps it's better you don't know yet."*

This is the moment. Players must choose whether to let him take them back.

- If they let him: he pockets them. He goes quiet. The scene stalls for one beat. Then a player must either ask him directly — *"Tell us what they say"* — or he will say, after a full silence: *"All right. I'll say it plainly. But I want you to understand why I wrote it down instead."* This begins the scene 4 confession early.
- If they hold the slips: he stops reaching. He sits down — not behind the desk, in the chair across from them. *"All right. All right."* The RP scene begins.

---

### Correct Answer

When the correct phrase is assembled:

> **THE DAWNBORN ARE THE VESSEL THE ENERGY LIVES WITHIN THEM**

A player reads it aloud. Theron looks at whoever reads it.

He does not speak immediately. He holds eye contact for two seconds — which is longer than it sounds at a table.

Then: *"Yes."*

That is all. Scene 4 begins.

| Action | Check | DC | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| Identify that the slips contain a pattern | History or Arcana | 10 | Player recognises encoded-document conventions — this is a substitution or positional cipher, not a random text |

No roll is required for a player who counts carefully and tries different N values. The puzzle is solvable through pure attention; the rolls simply speed up or confirm the process.

### GM Note: The Real Riddle

The puzzle mechanic is not the point. The point is what Theron did — he wrote down the most dangerous thing he knew, in a form designed to eventually be found, and then he locked it in a drawer and waited for someone to look. He told himself this was caution. He knows it was something else.

This is worth saying explicitly, as a GM aside, after the scene resolves — not to the players, but to yourself before you run it:

*Theron is not here to be stopped. He is here to be heard.*

## The Resonance Box — Full Mechanics

### Finding the Names

The three names — **CORVEN, SALI, WREN** — appear in the colophon signatures of Corven's final notation document, which is in one of the bundles saved from Scene 1. Specifically:

- The document is titled *"Notation: Final Disposition of the Dawn Mechanism"* — it is the third document in the relevant bundle (the first two are supplementary observations and a site record from the Ashring).
- On the final page, the colophon lists three witnesses in signature order, left to right: **Archmagister Corven**, **Scholar Sali Orthen**, **Scholar Wren Duvash**.
- No roll is required to read the names — the document is legible, the names are prominent. A DC 10 Investigation check identifies the connection between these names as "ritual witnesses" vs. authors; DC 12 connects them to the three-indentation pattern on the box.

**If one bundle was burned (T2):** This document survived in the other bundles — it appears in two of the three bundles as a copy. The names are available unless two bundles were burned.

**If two bundles were burned:** The colophon document is in whichever single bundle survived. Players have the names.

**If all bundles were burned:** Players have no documentary source for the names. Theron knows all three — he found the colophon originally. He will tell them if directly asked. He has just never been willing to say them to the box himself.

---

### The Solution

**Step 1 — Press left indentation, speak: CORVEN.**
→ Left indentation glows amber. Hold.

**Step 2 — Press center indentation (while left is held or within 60 seconds of it), speak: SALI.**
→ Center indentation glows amber. Both glow.

**Step 3 — Press right indentation, speak: WREN.**
→ All three glow simultaneously. Bell note. Lid releases.

**Order matters.** Any name in the wrong position triggers a dull harmonic tone and resets that indentation (the others remain lit for 60 seconds). A wrong third attempt when two are correct causes all three to reset.

---

### Partial Success (Players have incomplete names)

If players know two names and are missing one:

- Two indentations light correctly. The third remains dark.
- Theron, watching: *"There were three witnesses. I have told you where the document is."*
- If they press him directly — *"What is the third name?"* — he closes his eyes, pauses for a moment, then says it. He does not explain why he paused.
- Alternatively: DC 14 Investigation on surviving documents, or DC 12 History to recall that Corven's rituals required two verification witnesses by Archival law, and any two-name list implies a third who was senior (i.e., the third name is Corven himself, who was also listed as a witness though he authored the notation).

*Deliver this as Theron. Speak directly to the players, not past them. Not as read-aloud — as Theron in the chair across from them, telling them something he has been waiting eleven years to say.*

> "The Dawnborn are the ritual. The ten people living in this city — the ones running the food halls and tending the sick and teaching children and keeping the peace — they are the ritual. They have been holding fifty years of redirected solar energy in their bodies since the night they were born."

A pause. Long enough to be uncomfortable.

> "Completing the ritual means releasing that energy simultaneously."

A shorter pause.

> "The technical term in Corven's notation is *extinguished*."

---

**Beat 4 — The Silence:**

Stop.

Do not add anything. Do not clarify. Do not move the scene forward.

The word *extinguished* has been said. Let it sit in the room. This is the moment the session exists for, and it will only happen once. Give the table the full weight of it before anyone speaks.

However long the silence is — let it be that long.

---

**Beat 5 — The Question:**

When the silence has run its course, ask the table directly. Speak as the GM, not as Theron. Make eye contact around the table if you are at one:

*"What does your character do with this information in the next five minutes?"*

This is not rhetorical. This is not an invitation to monologue. Ask every player, in order, to answer in some form — in character, out of character, one sentence or five. The scene does not proceed until each player has said something.

If a player says *"I don't know"* — that is an answer. Theron says: *"Neither do I. Neither have I. For eleven years."*

---

**Beat 6 — The Accounting:**

Players will have questions. These are Theron's answers.

**"Why didn't you go to the Council?"**
> "I tried. Once. Before Ostenveld took office — before Keseph Vyne was in his current position. The response was two weeks in a holding room and my documents returned to me damaged. Not destroyed. Damaged. Whoever reviewed them wanted me to know they had been read. The city did not want to know."

**"Why didn't you tell someone? The Dawnborn themselves?"**
> "Tell them what? That they are going to die for the city's sun — possibly by their own decision if given a choice, possibly not, possibly before the question is even put to them? I thought about it. Day 4,017 was when I stopped counting. That was when I realised it didn't matter how long I waited. Nothing was going to make this easier to say."

**"Are you a coward?"**

Theron considers this.

> "I don't think I'm a coward. I think I told myself I was being careful. The difference feels smaller every year."

**"Can the ritual be done another way? Do the Dawnborn have to die?"**

Theron goes quiet. Then:

> "There is a researcher. Her name is Isolde Maren. She had a laboratory at the Spire until three years ago. Her work was put into institutional review. She is still working, I believe — somewhere in the Ashring district. Her theory was that the energy could be transferred rather than released. I cannot tell you whether her method functions. I know Keseph Vyne reviewed her proposal and filed it. That is all I know."

He pauses.

> "I should have told you this at the beginning."

---

**Beat 7 — Theron's Personal Reckoning:**

If players have not asked about alternatives, or after the Isolde information is given, Theron says one more thing — quietly, not as additional information but as something he has been building to:

> "Sera Voss came here eight years ago. She asked to see Corven's general historical notes — not the restricted documents, the public ones. I helped her. She thanked me. She was very polite. She did not tell me she had already worked it out."

He stops.

> "I've wondered since then whether she knows what I know, and whether she has been making the same choice I have — not for the city's sake, but for her own. Because looking at the people who might have to die, and deciding not to tell them, is a very specific kind of cowardice that feels like protection until you look at it directly."

---

### Extended Dialogue — Optional Use

These lines are available if conversations go in specific directions. Use as needed, not in sequence.

**On Corven:**
> "His name was Archmagister Corven. He died the night of the ritual, at the Ashring. He knew it would happen. He wrote his notes in full before he went. He could have stopped the ritual before the inversion completed. He didn't. I've spent eleven years trying to understand whether that makes him a murderer or a martyr. I don't think either word fits."

There were eight more."

### Pacing

This scene has no wrong pace. Let players drive it after Beat 5. Some tables will want to talk for thirty minutes; some will be silent for a long time and then ask one question. Both are correct.

The only pacing rule is Beat 4: after *"extinguished,"* do not add anything. Do not gloss it. Do not say *"so basically, the Dawnborn would die."* Theron said it. Players heard it. Let it work.

If players seem stunned into silence, wait. If they are still silent after a minute, quietly ask: *"What is your character doing right now? Even physically — are they standing, sitting, looking at what?"* This is not a prompt to speak. It is permission to be still.

### What Not to Do

- Do not let Theron perform his guilt. He is not crying. He is not breaking down. He is delivering information he has been holding for eleven years with the particular precision of someone who has rehearsed this conversation in his head thousands of times and has no energy left for theatre.
- Do not skip Beat 5. Every player should say something before the scene moves forward. The question *"what does your character do in the next five minutes"* is load-bearing — it forces each player to make a choice about who their character is in response to the worst thing they have heard this campaign.

## Location: Archive Rear Alley / Spire Quarter Street

The players are leaving the Archive. The front entrance on the south face is the direct route, but after what happened inside, a rear exit or a side street is plausible. Harran anticipated both. He is at the Archive rear alley — a narrow cobblestone passage running along the building's north face, between the Archive's external wall and a row of locked storage buildings. Three Reckoning soldiers are already positioned: one at each end of the alley, one between Harran and the Archive's rear door.

The alley is approximately 10 ft wide and 60 ft long. Two lamp posts — iron, similar to the Lowmark style but newer, Spire Quarter maintenance. One at each end. Between them: darkness and cobblestone and Harran.

He is standing in the center. Coat open, no weapon drawn. Hands at his sides. He is not performing casual — he has simply decided that drawing a weapon would be the wrong message, and so he has not drawn one.

### Five Senses — The Alley

**Sight:**
- The alley's narrowness — two people wide at most. The lamp posts at each end casting light inward that doesn't quite reach the center, where Harran stands in the half-dark.

**Smell:**

Confirmation that the players are not going to attempt to circumvent the ritual completion through a method that will buy weeks or months but ultimately fail — or that will succeed and eliminate a solution the city needs now.

That the players will find Isolde Maren, that her method will work, and that by the time it is implemented the food system will have collapsed enough that the restored sun arrives too late to matter. He is not wrong to fear this. He may be wrong about the timeline.

*"I'm protecting the city."* True — but not the city's Dawnborn. He has drawn a line and put the ten people who are the ritual on the wrong side of it. He tells himself this is math. He knows it is also a choice. He has not examined the difference closely.

### Harran's Dialogue

**On arrival:**
> "Stay where you are. I'd like to see what you're carrying."

**If players comply partially (show documents but conceal some):**
> "I see. So it's confirmed."

He says this without surprise. It was already confirmed for him before they came out. He wanted to see whether they would show him willingly.

**If players refuse entirely:**
> "That tells me what I need to know."

Also without surprise. Also not angry. Both responses are the same information to him.

**If players ask what he already knows:**
> "I know what is in Corven's notes. I have known for two years. The question is not what they say. The question is what you intend to do with the information — and how much time you think you have."

**If players challenge his authority:**
> "The Reckoning's authority is emergency provision under Charter. It was invoked. I did not invoke it. I was asked to."

He will not say Vyne's name here. He uses *"the administration"* or *"the Council"* or simply *"I was asked."*

**If players attempt to negotiate:**
> "You want to find a third way. I understand the impulse. I would like you to tell me its timeline."

**If cornered or when choosing to leave:**

> "When the city collapses in six months, tell me which principle saved it."

This is not a threat. It is not contempt. It is the sentence of a man who has done the arithmetic and arrived at a number he did not choose and cannot argue away, and who is trying to make other people understand that the arithmetic does not care about their principles.

He means it. It should land that way.

> "This is not the conversation I wanted to have."

He defends himself fully. He does not hold back. He is not interested in dying to demonstrate restraint.
